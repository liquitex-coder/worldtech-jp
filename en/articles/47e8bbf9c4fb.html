Ground-Truth-First Benchmarking of Reinforcement Learning via Converse Optimality: From Smooth, Fully Observed Systems to Non-Smooth or Partially Observed Tasks

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/10/24
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30954

arXiv:2610.30954v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Benchmarking in reinforcement learning is usually relative: algorithms are ranked by returns on environments with unknown optima. Distance to optimality remains unobservable, while conclusions may depend on reward design, stochasticity, hyperparameters, environment selection, and simulator details. We develop a ground-truth-first framework based on converse optimality, constructing each task with a certified optimal value function and policy withheld from learners and used only for evaluation. The framework unifies two modes: sensitivity benchmarking samples certified environments from continuous-time generators to assess robustness to task variation, while oracle-referenced comparison uses native discrete-time generators to evaluate policies via optimality gap, regret, and policy error. Continuous-time models support physically interpretable sensitivity studies but introduce dependence on the simulator step ($\Delta t$) and numerical scheme. Native discrete-time models align the environment, oracle, and learner on the same transition law, eliminating external integration choices as a confounder. We reproduce quantitative results covering optimizer sensitivity and oracle-referenced comparisons on serial planar arms, dynamically extended nonholonomic vehicles, and quadrotor-inspired tasks. The methodology extends beyond smooth affine Gaussian systems: virtual-input pullback handles non-affine raw-action interfaces and finite-covariance non-Gaussian disturbances. A non-smooth converse optimality formulation enables certified benchmarks with kinked value functions and switching surfaces. For partially observed evaluation, an information-state formulation reduces benchmarking to converse optimality on a sufficient statistic and provides an information-penalty reference. Code, fixtures, and diagnostics are available on the public project page.
