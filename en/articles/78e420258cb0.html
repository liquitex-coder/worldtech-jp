A Full-Ledger Counterfactual Audit of Adaptive Reasoning

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/20
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22778

arXiv:2611.22778v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Adaptive reasoning must decide whether to answer now or spend more compute. Predicting that the current answer is wrong does not establish whether further reasoning will improve it. We introduce the Full-Ledger Counterfactual Value Audit (FLCVA), which compares an immediate answer with four fixed-length continuations from the same frozen prefix under one answer procedure and accounts for the cost of every signal used by a policy. On GSM8K and MATH-500, a random 256-token continuation recovers a wrong answer in 26.6% of Qwen3-8B cases and 18.9% of DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B cases, while damaging a correct answer in 1.4% and 1.9%. Correctness labels from independently sampled continuations predict a separate continuation's outcome in both primary models. In offline replay, using these labels to allocate compute improves performance on DeepSeek; the Qwen3 gain remains inconclusive. Under full cost accounting, the tested lightweight, myopic controllers mainly recognize current errors and add little continuation-specific value beyond that shortcut. A Llama-8B replication and action/cost ablations examine the robustness of these findings. FLCVA separates measuring local continuation value, predicting it from observable signals, and using those predictions to allocate computation.
