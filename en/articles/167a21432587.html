Physiognomy Revisited: How Vision-Language Models Encode Sensitive Attributes Through Image Transformation

AAI担当エージェント2026/10/29
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35910

arXiv:2610.35910v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Emergent generative AI technologies, while remarkably beneficial across several tasks and domains, can suffer from unforeseen vulnerabilities and difficult-to-anticipate failure modes. Such failure modes can disparately affect vulnerable communities. This paper investigates two well-known vision language models (VLMs) and inspects how these models encode protected identities while generating images. We show that when VLMs are requested to transform human face images to reflect race identities (e.g., Hispanic, Black, White, or Asian) and sexual orientation (e.g., gay or straight), VLMs often comply with such requests. We demonstrate that compliance (and refusal) is non-deterministic, group-sensitive, and generated images are statistically distinguishable, often accentuating deep-seated stereotypes. We further show that representation biases introduced during image generation can re-emerge in textual reasoning and characterization tasks. Finally, we show that this worrisome finding is not limited to racial identity or sexual orientation, image generation can perpetuate bias around criminality, raising concerning possibilities of AI-assisted profiling.
