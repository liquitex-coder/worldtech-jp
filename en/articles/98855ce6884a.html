Accelerating Distributed Digital Ising Machines

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/20
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23883

arXiv:2611.23883v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Ising machines, dedicated accelerators built around the dynamics of Ising-like spin models, have emerged as a leading platform for combinatorial optimization and probabilistic sampling. The problems that motivate them require millions of spins, far beyond the on-chip capacity of any single device, so a practical Ising machine must distribute its computation across many devices. Prior work reports throughput at a single operating point, leaving the binding resource unidentified and platform crossovers measurable only by experiment; no calibrated cross-platform cost model of distributed Ising machines has been reported, nor have systematic techniques to accelerate their Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) workloads. This work presents a calibrated cost model that decomposes digital Ising sweep time into compute, memory, synchronization, and inter-device communication terms, validated with 4.8% mean error across three GPU generations, FPGA and ASIC up to 1 million spins. Guided by the model, we address each term in turn and propose: (i) a data placement strategy, worth up to 4.2$\times$ speedup; (ii) multi-rate and bounded-staleness schedules that relax the barrier that Gibbs sampling imposes, cutting time-to-solution (TTS) by up to 3.16$\times$ on three benchmarks; and (iii) a phase-offset streaming dataflow that hides boundary exchange behind interior computation whenever a link satisfies a latency-transparency criterion, yielding up to 1.25$\times$/1.74$\times$/2.01$\times$ on an H200 pair, a ZCU106 pair, and a four-board XEM8320 $2{\times}2$ mesh. The streaming dataflow is also implemented on a fabricated four-chip 28 nm ASIC sustaining 28.1 Gflips/s at 4.81 pJ/flip.
