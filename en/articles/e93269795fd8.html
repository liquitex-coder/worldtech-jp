RePT: 3D Geometry Emerges from 2D Relational Pretraining

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31601

arXiv:2611.31601v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Learning spatial representations often relies on explicitly predicting geometric quantities, but how physical geometry is organized within learned representations remains less understood. We ask whether 3D structure can emerge from learning relations across 2D observations, without 3D prediction targets. We introduce RePT, which learns compact relations between distinct physical points through cross-view 2D point prediction. The learned relation space develops a geometric organization in which magnitudes, angles, and neighborhoods reflect physical 3D displacement. A single linear readout fitted on 16 scenes recovers displacement length and angle on 39 held-out scenes without refitting (Pearson r = 0.72 and 0.78). The ARKitScenes-trained checkpoint also retains strong geometric organization on ScanNet++, showcasing generalizability. In controlled comparisons, distinct-point relational prediction consistently yields stronger geometric organization than matched same-point correspondence learning. This organization depends on additive relations and adaptive scene context, and higher point-transfer accuracy does not by itself produce stronger organization. More broadly, our results suggest that 3D geometry need not be an explicit prediction target and can instead emerge as a structural property of representations trained to relate 2D observations, pointing to relational prediction as a complementary route to learning the representations that spatial intelligence builds on.
