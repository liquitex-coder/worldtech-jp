Self-Improving Long-Horizon Reasoning by Learning Summaries from Local Continuations

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31621

arXiv:2611.31621v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Reasoning models degrade as their context grows, so longer reasoning traces do not by themselves give longer effective horizons. One way to extend the horizon is to compress the reasoning history into a compact state from time to time and continue from the state. Existing methods train the state with the final-answer reward or distill it from a stronger model. Neither signal is tailored to measure the contribution of the state alone. We observe that a reasoning model that degrades over long trajectories still behaves reliably in short contexts. We aim to preserve this short-context behavior when compressing the reasoning history. Motivated by this, we train a separate summarizer with Delta-KL, a reward computed from the frozen reasoning model's token-level predictions on sampled continuations, and run inference by truncating the trace, summarizing it, and continuing from the summary (TruSuCo). Training uses a few thousand questions with 4k-token reasoning prefixes and does not need answers or external teachers. Under a fixed 32k-token budget, the trained summarizer improves on vanilla reasoning and on an untrained summarizer in all nine settings across mathematical benchmarks and Reasoning Gym puzzles, by up to 7.7 points and 12 points, respectively. The improvement over vanilla reasoning grows with the budget. At 128k tokens, our method outperforms it by 10 points on AIME 2024--2026. Analyses show that truncation prevents the collapse of vanilla reasoning and that the trained summaries retain more of the reasoning history.
