Long-Distance Real-World Navigation of the Legged-Wheeled Robot Go2-W Using Deep Reinforcement Learning

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21387

arXiv:2606.21387v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Legged-wheeled robots have been extensively studied for their potential to combine the efficient flat-ground mobility of wheels with the rough-terrain adaptability of legs. However, their application to long-distance autonomous navigation in real environments remains limited. This paper reports our efforts to build a deep reinforcement learning-based locomotion controller and an autonomous navigation system for the commercially available Go2-W legged-wheeled robot. The study focuses on the integrated system and the practical challenges associated with its sustained operation over long distances in real-world environments. For locomotion control, we extended our proprioception-only policy, previously developed for quadruped robots, to Go2-W, a 16-degree-of-freedom legged-wheeled robot. We additionally found that wheeled locomotion concentrates the load on the hip joints, resulting in heat concentration that hinders sustained travel. To address this issue, we developed a policy with periodic foot-lifting motion that distributes the joint loads and mitigates heat concentration. We evaluated the integrated system at the Tsukuba Challenge 2025. The robot autonomously traversed an approximately \SI{2.8}{\kilo\meter} route that included sidewalks, a park, and stairs without stopping owing to overheating.
