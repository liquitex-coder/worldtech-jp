Model-Aware Generation of Safety-Critical Scenes for Robust End-to-End Driving via Distilled Planner Surrogates

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/10/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35448

arXiv:2610.35448v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: End-to-end driving models perform well on standard benchmarks but collide frequently in safety-critical scenarios. We present a failure-mode analysis on NeuroNCAP which shows that 95.9% of DiffusionDrive's collisions are planning failures despite reliable detection, pinpointing the bottleneck in planning rather than perception. We attribute this to a fundamental data imbalance, as safety-critical interactions are rare in real driving logs, so planners are hardly ever trained on them. We close this gap with a novel model-aware framework that generates safety-critical training scenes against a specific planner. Each planner is attacked at the bird's-eye-view (BEV) level through a differentiable surrogate distilled from its own planning head. This keeps the expensive video renderer out of the attack loop. We extract 50 scenes per planner, each pairing an attack with a safe escape trajectory that serves as the fine-tuning label. A fully automated quality-diversity search generates both the attacks and the escapes, so that their variety is not specified by hand. Only these scenes are rendered to fine-tune the planner, which reduces the computational cost significantly. Fine-tuning on these scenes roughly halves the closed-loop collision rate of two planners on NeuroNCAP (SparseDrive 28.0% to 12.2%, DiffusionDrive 37.5% to 19.2%) while largely preserving normal driving. A cross-planner transfer experiment shows that the gain comes from targeting the specific planner. Each planner improves most on the scenes generated against itself, and DiffusionDrive fine-tuned on SparseDrive's scenes retains only 6.7 of its 18.3-point collision-rate reduction.
