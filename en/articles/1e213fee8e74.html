HOG-WAM: Improving Visual Robustness through Future Gradient Prediction

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34811

arXiv:2611.34811v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: World-action models jointly predict future observations and actions, yet visual corruption can disrupt the resulting policies. We introduce HOG-WAM, which adds future Histograms of Oriented Gradients (HOG) as structural supervision derived directly from demonstration images. An auxiliary HOG diffusion transformer (DiT) module reads RGB features through asymmetric attention, allowing its objective to shape the visual representation used for control. The module is used only during training and removed at inference, preserving FastWAM's RGB-to-action architecture. We evaluate four controlled observation corruptions through a visual-corruption extension of RoboTwin, termed RoboTwin-Plus; HOG-WAM achieves the highest success among the compared policies under all four. Mixed-noise success increases from 78.86% to 82.86% relative to FastWAM, with comparable performance on clean scenes. On LIBERO Plus, overall success increases from 74.49% to 75.26%, with the largest gain under sensor noise, from 74.14% to 78.89%. Across four Franka tasks, average success increases from 40% to 59% under clean observations and from 17% to 24% under noisy observations. A representation ablation favors combining RGB observations with auxiliary HOG prediction over replacing RGB with HOG. These findings support future-gradient prediction as complementary supervision for visually robust control, with larger absolute gains on physical tasks than in simulation. Code is available at https://github.com/iPPLab/HOG-WAM.
