EPPC-CARE: Staged Hierarchical Annotation of Communication Behaviors in Secure Patient-Provider Messages

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34539

arXiv:2611.34539v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Objectives: Patient portal messages provide invaluable information about communication dynamics between patients and healthcare providers, but extracting it manually is labor-intensive. We developed and evaluated EPPC-CARE, an automated framework for identifying communication behaviors together with the supporting text for each classification. Materials and Methods: From 867 messages, we obtained 5,516 reference standard annotations. Each annotation is a triplet of communication-related text (span), a broader communication category (Code), and a specific subcategory (Sub-code). We evaluated EPPC-CARE's identified triplets across six open-weight large language models (LLMs) against supervised fine-tuning and communication-based models such as EPPC-OASIS. We also compared EPPC-CARE with human expert annotations on 100 messages. Results: EPPC-CARE improved performance over EPPC-OASIS across all six models. EPPC-CARE's Triplet F1 improved by 2.90 percentage points on average, with a best score of 64.4%. On the human expert evaluation subset, EPPC-CARE and the human expert showed comparable agreement with the reference standard, with differences favoring EPPC-CARE. Discussion: These findings suggest that identifying triplet components (span, code, subcode) sequentially can improve performance across model families while preserving the supporting text behind each identification. EPPC-CARE links the communication code and subcode with the source text (span), supporting human verification of automated annotations in clinical informatics. EPPC-CARE may therefore make larger-scale studies of patient-provider communication more feasible and provide a foundation for human-in-the-loop annotation workflows. Conclusion: EPPC-CARE offers a more reliable and reviewable approach for automated characterization of communication behaviors in secure patient-provider messaging.
