Draft Calibration for Speculative Decoding: Accelerating LLM Serving by Matching Draft and Target Distributions

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33111

arXiv:2611.33111v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Speculative decoding accelerates large language model (LLM) inference by employing a lightweight draft model to propose draft tokens and allowing the target model to verify the new tokens in parallel to improve inference-time compute utilization. The speedup relies on the acceptance probability of draft tokens in target verification, which is directly related to the gap $\|p-q\|_1$ between the target distribution $p$ and the draft distribution $q$. In this paper, we study draft calibration of speculative decoding to improve the speedup of speculative decoding, where we employ post-hoc methods to calibrate the draft distribution to mitigate the distribution gap. We observe that though current community open-source draft models may learn the top candidate token correctly, they are generally underconfident compared to the target model, suggesting a consistent distribution mismatch. To mitigate the effect of this mismatch on the acceptance rate of the draft models, we try various post-hoc calibration methods, including standard temperature scaling and token-wise calibration. Our results suggest that a standard temperature scaling applied to the draft distribution improves the acceptance length by up to approximately 10\% with almost no additional cost across different tasks. Throughput analysis further verifies that this improvement in serving efficiency. Analysis of more general calibration methods reveals further potential room for improvement.
