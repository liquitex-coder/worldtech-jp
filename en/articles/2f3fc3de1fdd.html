Find the Clue, Not the Scene: Rethinking Reasoning for Entity Recognition in Vision-Language Models

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34681

arXiv:2611.34681v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Answering a knowledge-based visual question requires first recognizing the entity in the image and then recalling knowledge about it. Thinking vision-language models (VLMs) reason for thousands of tokens before answering, yet it remains underexplored how well such thinking VLMs perform each of these two steps. We therefore analyze thinking VLMs on this task and make two key observations. (1) Recognition is the bottleneck, since 76.3% of errors across four models on ViQuAE stem from misrecognizing the entity, and 84.9% of these failures are resolved once the entity name is provided in the question. (2) Recognition is driven by entity-recall clues rather than by general descriptions of the image. An entity-recall clue is a distinctive visual detail that helps the model recall the entity, such as keys, a traditional attribute of Saint Peter. Based on these findings, we propose ClueRL, a reinforcement learning approach that extends Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with an advantage localized to the entity-recall clue in the thinking trace. Rather than injecting new entity knowledge, ClueRL changes how VLMs think: it leads them to attend more closely to visual details and spot these clues, helping them use the knowledge they already have. Consequently, it outperforms both GRPO and a perception-reward baseline on three entity recognition benchmarks. Moreover, this clue-seeking behavior generalizes beyond entity recognition, the only task used for training. Since recognition is the bottleneck, the gains carry over to knowledge-based visual question answering. They even extend to general multimodal reasoning, raising Qwen3.5-4B by 4.4 and 4.0 points on the ScienceQA and MMMU subsets of MMStar, respectively.
