PhiJEPA: Denoising Latent Predictions for Visual Representation Learning

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/24
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27516

arXiv:2611.27516v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Can a predictor trained to infer hidden image regions still help a world model plan when part of an object disappears from view and the visible context is blurred? We introduce PhiJEPA, a joint-embedding predictive architecture that adapts PhiNet's two-stage prediction to masked Transformers. From sparse, photometrically corrupted context, a Transformer block predicts hidden tokens of an independently corrupted teacher view. A token-wise MLP then maps these predictions to clean teacher tokens. Controlled ablations show that the combined objective improves dense features over the tested one-stage alternatives. Pre-trained on ImageNet-1K with $5.6\times10^{19}$ training FLOPs, PhiJEPA achieves 69.3 mIoU on linear PASCAL VOC segmentation, exceeding the evaluated I-JEPA and DSeq-JEPA checkpoints in segmentation accuracy, with 25\% fewer training FLOPs than I-JEPA. It also improves hidden-region segmentation over Simple I-JEPA (49.1 vs.\ 44.3 mIoU). As an application, we use the frozen encoder in DINO-WM on PushT, and reuse the pre-training predictor, which JEPAs usually discard, to complete occluded tokens without fine-tuning. In open-loop planning with simultaneous object occlusion and Gaussian blur ($\sigma=2$ pixels), predictor completion raises success from 33\% to 65\%, compared with 25\% for Simple I-JEPA with its predictor and 40\% for iBOT with pixel inpainting. Together, these results support two-stage latent prediction as an efficient approach to learning visual representations that remain useful under photometric degradation and support completion under partial observation.
