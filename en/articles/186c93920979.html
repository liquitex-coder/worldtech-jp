Motion Equivariant Convolutional LSTM: When Motion Becomes a Gauge Symmetry

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/10/23
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28771

arXiv:2610.28771v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Objects move, cameras pan, and weather drifts with the wind: much of what changes in sequential data is motion. Motion changes where things are, not what they are, and is therefore a symmetry of the data. Yet standard recurrent networks are not equivariant to it: their memory stays put while the input moves on. Flow equivariant recurrent networks first brought this symmetry into sequence models, for motions of constant velocity, or flows, by enumerating a bank of velocities. We extend this idea in three directions: from simple recurrent networks to convolutional LSTMs, from flows to motions whose velocity changes in time, and from enumerating velocities to estimating them. Treating a sequence as a field over time, we show that motion becomes a gauge symmetry: a global symmetry made local in time. Built on this view, our motion equivariant convolutional LSTM (MELSTM) uses its velocity model as the gauge field and is provably equivariant to every motion. Across future-frame prediction, classification of digits visible only through their motion, and action recognition under camera shake, MELSTM outperforms a convolutional LSTM and its flow equivariant counterpart with the same number of parameters: it is more accurate, generalizes to longer horizons and to unseen motions, and carries a fraction of the flow equivariant model's state.
