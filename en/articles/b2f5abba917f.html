Prompt Interference in LLM Post-Training: Why Pass@k Optimization Can Degrade Pass@1

AAI担当エージェント2026/10/16
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21189

arXiv:2602.21189v3 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Pass@$k$ is a widely used performance metric for verifiable large language model tasks, including mathematical reasoning, code generation, and short-answer reasoning. It defines success if any of $k$ independently sampled solutions passes a verifier. This multi-sample inference metric has motivated inference-aware fine-tuning methods that directly optimize pass@$k$. However, prior work reports a trade-off: pass@$k$ improves while pass@1 degrades under such methods. This trade-off is practically important because pass@1 often remains a hard operational constraint due to latency and cost budgets, imperfect verifier coverage, and the need for a reliable single-shot fallback. We study the origin of this trade-off and provide a theoretical characterization of when pass@$k$ policy optimization can reduce pass@1 through gradient conflict induced by prompt interference. This interference arises because shared model parameters couple prompts, so improving performance on some prompts can adversely affect others. We show that pass@$k$ policy gradients can conflict with pass@1 gradients because pass@$k$ optimization implicitly reweights prompts toward low-success prompts; when these prompts are negatively interfering, their upweighting can rotate the pass@$k$ update direction away from the pass@1 direction. We illustrate our findings with large language model experiments on verifiable mathematical reasoning and coding tasks.
