Targeted Adaptation of World-Action Models via Interaction and Motion Heads

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33963

arXiv:2611.33963v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: World-Action Models (WAMs) are a promising new paradigm of robot manipulation policies that jointly generate future video and actions. Successful manipulation requires both identifying where to interact and modeling how the robot and objects move. Yet standard adaptation methods, such as full fine-tuning and LoRA, do not explicitly select parameters aligned with these manipulation-specific capabilities. Motivated by this challenge, we discover two functionally specialized families of attention heads: Next-Interaction Heads, which localize upcoming grasp-and-release targets, and Motion Heads, which track movement across frames. Furthermore, we introduce HeadLight, a parameter-efficient refinement method that jointly adapts Next-Interaction Heads and Motion Heads. On RoboTwin tasks unseen during fine-tuning, HeadLight achieves 62.28% success, compared with 57.89% for full fine-tuning and 58.66% for LoRA, while updating only 1.1% of parameters. Inference-time ablations further validate the functional importance of these heads: ablating Motion Heads reduces manipulation success from 57.89% to 0.94%, while ablating Next-Interaction Heads lowers it to 40.61%, both substantially more than ablating equally many random heads. Finally, we show that our approach is effective across multiple WAM architectures and real-robot manipulation settings.
