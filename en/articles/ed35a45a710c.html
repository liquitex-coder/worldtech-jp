Poisoning Self-Evolving Security Policies in LLM Agents

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33385

arXiv:2611.33385v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Self-evolving defenses improve LLM-agent security by learning reusable policies from observed attacks. However, we demonstrate that this learning process itself can become an attack surface. Using SED (Self-Evolving Defense) in the AgentDojo benchmark, we construct a single prompt-injection episode that causes the defense to learn an over-broad policy and subsequently refuse legitimate requests. Across 10 Nemotron runs, every poisoned run over-refuses at least one legitimate task, while unrelated-task performance remains unchanged and the original prompt-injection defense remains effective. The attack also transfers to gpt-oss-120b; however, its poisoning mechanism depends on a successful initial prompt injection and therefore fails to activate when the target agent resists the underlying injection. In contrast, integrity-oriented attacks targeting SED's judge, policy synthesis, retrieval, and organization mechanisms fail in our evaluated settings, identifying over-refusal as the primary exploitable failure mode observed in our experiments. We further identify self-poisoning from false-positive harm judgments without an attacker. Finally, contrastive policy synthesis and an automatic broadness-check gate substantially reduce, but do not eliminate over-refusal, without compromising security in our evaluated settings.
