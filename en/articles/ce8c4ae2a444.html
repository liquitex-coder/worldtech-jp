Can a Frontier VLM Serve as a Robot Policy for Memory-Dependent Manipulation?

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/10/20
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17350

arXiv:2610.17350v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: We use a frozen frontier vision-language model, GPT-6-Astra, directly as the robot policy on RoboMME, a benchmark of 16 memory-dependent manipulation tasks spanning temporal, spatial, object, and procedural memory. The model keeps the whole episode in context and outputs every end-effector waypoint and gripper command, with no RoboMME-specific training. On the 800 test episodes it succeeds in 92.1%, above the previously reported human score of 90.5%, which was obtained with an oracle skill interface, and far ahead of the best leaderboard entry (60.8%) and of a hierarchical system that pairs the same model with a RoboMME-trained policy (79.1%). It matches or exceeds the human score on 13 of the 16 tasks. Its 63 failures concentrate in two tasks and are mostly premature completions and mistimed or unintended button presses. These results blur the usual division between long-horizon planning, memory and end-effector control, and raise the question of how much of this stack a single general-purpose model can take over in embodied systems. Project website: https://embodied-astra.github.io. Code, prompts and all episode logs: https://github.com/jackbjed/embodied-astra-robomme.
