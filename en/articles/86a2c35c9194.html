JECT: Addressing the Context Shortcut in Joint-Embedding Predictive Learning for Chest CT

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31849

arXiv:2611.31849v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Joint-embedding predictive architectures learn by predicting features of masked regions from visible context. In chest computed tomography, the findings that decide a diagnosis, such as a small nodule, are often not predictable from the surrounding anatomy. A hidden finding is then best predicted toward normal anatomy, and the prediction loss stops rewarding the encoder for representing it. We call this the context shortcut. Conditioning the predictor on the radiology report repairs the prediction, but in our setup the encoder gains little over image-only pretraining: the report supplies what the encoder never had to learn. JECT therefore gives the report two roles: a condition for the predictor and, through masked section-text prediction, a target for the encoder, which must predict the report's anatomical sections from the unmasked scan. A hierarchical three-dimensional encoder that predicts at every stage keeps this clinical content at every scale. In ablations, the text target improves classification and localization, while conditioning adds little to the encoder. Trained from scratch on 173k volumes, the recipe reaches the level of current computed tomography foundation models in classification, retrieval and nodule malignancy, and of task-specific models in lung-cancer risk prediction after fine-tuning. Conditioning still matters: its predictor can be reused, with no extra training, to show where a report sentence acts, changing most at the finding the sentence describes. Code and pretrained weights are available.
