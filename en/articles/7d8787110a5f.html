Evaluating Aviation Safety Knowledge Extraction with SmolLM2: From Zero-Shot to Retrieval-Grounded Prompting

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34432

arXiv:2611.34432v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Aviation accident narratives contain safety knowledge that is difficult to capture using structured occurrence fields alone. This study evaluates prompting-based knowledge extraction across four dimensions: concept accuracy, verbatim evidence grounding, structural compliance, and directed relation extraction. SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct was evaluated under zero-shot, one-shot, fixed few-shot, and retrieval-grounded prompting using 300 National Transportation Safety Board narratives from 2000-2020, containing 3,763 reference concepts and 467 directed relations. Evaluation combined structural checks, verbatim evidence matching, expert-reviewed semantic matching, and paired bootstrap confidence intervals. No prompting strategy performed best across all dimensions. Retrieval-grounded prompting achieved the highest concept micro-F1 of 32.27%, although its advantage over fixed few-shot prompting was uncertain (95% CI: -0.92 to 2.40 percentage points). One-shot prompting achieved the highest relation F1 of 6.95%, identifying directed relation extraction as the principal SmolLM2 bottleneck. Compared with fixed few-shot prompting, retrieval improved verbatim grounding (59.45% vs. 54.02%) but reduced complete structural compliance (33.3% vs. 42.7%). Verbatim matching establishes source presence rather than evidential relevance. An exploratory comparison using 35 benchmark narratives showed that Qwen3-8B achieved higher concept micro-F1 across all prompting conditions and higher relation F1 under demonstration-based prompting, reaching 51.37% and 34.62%, respectively, under one-shot prompting. The small subset limits generalization. These findings support evaluating semantic correctness, source traceability, structural reliability, and directed relation accuracy separately.
