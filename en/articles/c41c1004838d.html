Simulation-Based Testing of RCM-Constrained Robotic Software via Task Manifolds

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/24
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27144

arXiv:2611.27144v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: System-level testing of surgical robots requires generating executable test scenarios that exercise diverse, safety-critical behaviors. However, remote-center-of-motion (RCM) surgical manipulators operate within tightly constrained workspaces governed by coupled task geometry, inverse kinematics, and motor limits. Direct sampling in motor-command or Cartesian spaces frequently wastes testing budgets on physically unfeasible configurations. To address this limitation, we present a manifold-centered testing framework that decouples feasible test-domain construction from downstream test-generation strategies. Candidate inputs are generated directly within this constructed feasible domain and mapped to lower-level joint commands for simulation execution. Offline trajectory safety oracles and a spatial reference grid then evaluate failure yield and coverage. We compare our task-manifold representation against Cartesian XYZ and principal component analysis (PCA) under random testing, systematic traversal, and genetic search strategies. Results demonstrate that the feasible task domain improves test-generation efficiency and enables systematic reference-cell coverage. Crucially, random and genetic search strategies detect a broader spectrum of failure patterns than baseline representations, revealing that complete reference-cell coverage alone does not exhaust intra-cell behavioral dynamics. Furthermore, differential testing between defective and patched controller versions reveals that targeted evolutionary search detects aggregate failure-rate shifts from software repairs. Finally, a cross-platform study on a 7-DoF Kinova Gen3 arm in MoveIt 2 confirms workflow transferability to kinematically redundant manipulators. Together, these findings highlight the necessity of combining feasibility-aware domain modeling with complementary search strategies for systematic testing of robot software.
