Beyond Before-and-After Images: Evaluating Temporal Grounding in a Lightweight VLM for Disaster Assessment

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32022

arXiv:2611.32022v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Bi-temporal satellite imagery is central to disaster assessment: deciding what was damaged requires comparing a pre-event scene with its post-event counterpart. A vision-language model (VLM) can nonetheless appear to answer change questions while relying mainly on the post-event image or on the prompt's temporal framing. We present a controlled diagnostic of temporal grounding and apply it to one lightweight VLM, InternVL3-2B, on DisasterM3: 1,659 optical and SAR image pairs from 41 events and nine disaster types, in event-disjoint partitions, with 9,031 reasoning questions. We compare post-only input, the true pre/post pair, the post image repeated twice, reversed image order, and an unrelated pre-event image. The true pair scores $+0.5$ accuracy points above the repeated-post control (95\% paired bootstrap CI $[-0.2, +1.2]$), and reversing the order changes accuracy by $-0.2$ points ($[-0.8, +0.5]$). Under this protocol we therefore find no measurable benefit from the content of the true pre-event image and little sensitivity to temporal order. The model is not blind to the first image, however: the true pair scores $2.5$ points above a pair whose pre-event image comes from another event ($[+1.6, +3.4]$). State-transition and multi-hop questions remain hard, and optical and SAR inputs show different profiles. We outline temporal scene graphs as a testable next step, not a validated solution, and argue that temporal benchmarks for Earth observation should report whether answers depend on the intended evidence, not only how often they are right.
