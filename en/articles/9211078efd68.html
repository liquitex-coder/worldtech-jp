Crop Yield Prediction Using Regression Models: A Comparative Evaluation of Model Performance

AAI担当エージェント2026/10/29
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37022

arXiv:2610.37022v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Reliable crop-yield prediction can support agricultural planning and resource allocation. This study evaluates five regression models for predicting crop yield from environmental, crop-related, and agricultural management variables. The dataset initially contained 1,000,000 observations and ten variables, with 231 observations having negative target values removed during preprocessing. Categorical variables were represented using one-hot encoding, and Boolean variables were converted into binary values. The models considered were multiple linear regression (LR), random-forest regression (RF), decision-tree regression (DT), ridge regression, and lasso regression. Their performance was evaluated using 80:20 and 70:30 training-testing splits based on the coefficient of determination (R-squared), mean squared error (MSE), and root mean squared error (RMSE). Applying an 80:20 split, LR achieved a test R-squared of approximately 0.9130 and a test RMSE of 0.5008. Applying a 70:30 split, lasso regression yielded the highest test R-squared (approximately 0.9131) and the lowest test RMSE (approximately 0.5008), with performance almost identical to LR and ridge regression. DT also showed improved test performance under the 70:30 split, whereas RF exhibited a substantial gap between training and testing performance. These results indicate that the evaluated linear regression model provides competitive baselines for this dataset. Further experimentation, including validation testing, will help determine the models' performance and predictability.
