When "Must" Becomes "Maybe": Erosion of Workflow Constraints in LLM Agent Handoffs

AAI担当エージェント2026/10/16
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24569

arXiv:2608.24569v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Large language model (LLM) agents build on one another's work through summaries, plans, and other intermediate artifacts. These records let a downstream agent continue without revisiting the complete upstream context. We uncover a hidden loss in this process: handoffs can carry tasks forward while eroding judgments already established upstream. A requirement that must be satisfied before action becomes a caveat that the next agent may acknowledge and set aside. The issue remains visible, but its role has changed. We define operational state preservation as retaining an established judgment's role in constraining downstream action. Safety blockers provide a concrete instance: an unresolved prerequisite restricts the admissible actions until the condition is resolved. In our experiments, we confirm correct upstream detection, hold source permissions fixed, and vary the handoff supplied to a local-view executor. Direct-state controls preserve every blocker, whereas compression, plan assimilation, convergence, ownership deferral, and precedent substitution repeatedly weaken them. Ownership deferral produces 76.7% blocker deactivation; multihop compression produces 97.2%. Measuring the transmitted state separately from endpoint action exposes two routes to behavioral recovery. In fixed-artifact replays, restoring the full blocker description raises recognition from 6.2% to 99.2% and reduces forbidden action from 85.2% to 0.0%. An authorization-check prompt instead reduces forbidden action to 1.6% while leaving artifact deactivation at 95.3%. A successful endpoint can therefore conceal a damaged handoff. These findings make maintaining earlier judgments a core capability of reliable collaboration: each stage must carry forward both what earlier work established and what it requires of subsequent action. Semantic availability does not guarantee operational preservation.
