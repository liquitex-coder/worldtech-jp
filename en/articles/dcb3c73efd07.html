Acoustic Respiratory Phase Detection on Mobile Devices

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/15
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16710

arXiv:2611.16710v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Breathing monitoring is important for well-being, stress regulation, and guided breathing exercises, yet existing approaches often rely on contact-based sensors or controlled environments. This paper presents a non-invasive framework for respiratory phase detection using acoustic signals recorded on commodity smartphones. The proposed hybrid CNN-Transformer architecture combines local spectral feature extraction with long-range temporal modeling to capture the cyclic structure of breathing. The model is evaluated using a Leave-One-Subject-Out (LOSO) protocol on the Foundation Dataset and on an independent unseen test set comprising over 1300 breathing cycles and 2 hours of continuous recordings. The proposed 3-class formulation achieves 72.85% frame-level accuracy and 96.6% cycle-level exhalation detection efficiency in the LOSO evaluation, while achieving 76.94% frame-level accuracy, a Cohen's Kappa score of 0.65, and 99.2% cycle-level exhalation detection efficiency on the unseen test set. Results suggest that accurate respiratory monitoring may be achievable under data-constrained conditions, indicating that appropriate temporal modeling can learn transferable respiratory representations and generalize to previously unseen participants. An end-to-end ONNX deployment pipeline enables privacy-preserving on-device inference with latency below 20 ms, supporting real-time monitoring on mobile and wearable devices.
