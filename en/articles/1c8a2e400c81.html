Not All Interpretation Methods Are Created Equal: Tiered Evaluation of AI Interpretability

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31833

arXiv:2611.31833v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: As AI transitions to sociotechnical deployments, uninterpretable models pose serious threats to reliability, public trust and contestability. However, not all interpretation methods are created equal: for interpretability to enhance deployment safety and effectiveness, interpretation methods must be evaluated in a tiered system to differentiate evidence levels of trustworthiness, rather than validated through isolated interpretation instances. Our core vision is that elevating the evaluation of interpretation methods to a core AI assessment metric will drive robust knowledge discovery from data (KDD) and ensure safe, contestable sociotechnical deployments of AI. To this end, we propose TRUST, a tiered evaluation framework that builds upon the Predictability-Computability-Stability (PCS) principles for Veridical Data Science (VDS) and the Predictive, Descriptive and Relevant (PDR) foundations. To differentiate interpretation methods effectively and systematically, TRUST grades interpretation across five tiers: Task-Aligned PDR Foundations, Rigorous Epistemic Grounding, Unifying Semantic Understanding, Scope Transferability, and Targeted Prospective Validation. We present a start-to-finish case study of the iterative Random Forest (iRF) family under TRUST and contrast it with other LLM interpretation methods that struggle to clear specific tiers. Finally, we conclude with brief initial thoughts on how TRUST principles can extend in autonomous agentic frameworks. Ultimately, the TRUST framework fosters contestability and epistemic grounding as foundational evaluation principles for human-machine workflows and, eventually, autonomous agentic workflows.
