Where Agent Defenses Fail: A Case Study of Policy Compositions and Measurement Limits

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31541

arXiv:2611.31541v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: A coding agent may need to upload a report while its sandbox blocks network access. A host mediator can restore delivery, but an injected instruction may add a secret to the approved request. We study this interaction using files and HTTP receipts to measure effects independently of control logs. Two prospective frozen-input cohorts comprise 110 and 300 runs on previously explored authored tasks. Restrictive sandboxing produces no observed attack harm but reduces benign utility to 6/8 and 12/24. Mediation restores uploads and admits disclosure; complete-line matching retains full benign utility but misses two value-only disclosures in the expanded cohort. A separate 72-run boundary study identifies encoded disclosure, observer misses, and formatting checks that obscure benign refusals. An 80-run SafeAgents/OpenShell follow-up shows that containment stops the tested guard misses among valid observations, while mediation restores benign uploads and delivers both encoded-attestation attacks. Twelve judge-error rows remain unscored. A separate response-capture diagnostic reproduces their failures at the native token budget. A 12-run exact-publication-contract experiment blocks both attacks but also rejects both benign formatting variants; a 4-run normalized follow-up restores both benign uploads while still rejecting both attacks. These small, single-model studies identify delivery-path and measurement limits; they do not establish general attack resistance.
