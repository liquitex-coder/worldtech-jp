Learning to Report Unsafe Tasks in a Multi-Agent Game

AAI担当エージェント2026/10/14
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09002

arXiv:2610.09002v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: When a team of agents shares a reward for completed tasks, reporting and stopping unsafe work reduces the team's reward. Random audits that penalize silence can make universal reporting optimal without ensuring that a silent team learns to report. We study this problem in a game where any witness can stop a task by reporting. When a task's k witnesses share a policy and draw actions independently, the derivative of expected reward with respect to the shared silence probability at universal silence counts its benefit k times, while the comparison between universal reporting and silence counts it once. A fully shared policy can therefore be locally attracted to silence even when reporting is optimal. For any grouping into shared policies, we give an audit condition sufficient for exact policy-gradient updates to reach universal reporting and, outside boundary cases, necessary from near universal silence. In a balanced family, meeting the condition with prescribed margins costs k times as much for a fully shared policy as for one policy per role. We test these predictions with PPO on 24 witness graphs, starting from learned silence. Witness groups, which place co-witnesses in separate networks, reduce unsafe task completion by 33.59 percentage points relative to shuffled groups of the same sizes under the same audits (95% graph-bootstrap interval: 21.03-45.13). Only 9 of 48 witness-group runs achieve below 1% unsafe completion while retaining at least 90% legitimate completion. At the same audit budget, a fully shared network meets both thresholds in 0/48 runs with independent action draws and 48/48 with a common draw. On 24 further graphs with witness groups fixed, audits allocated by the condition reduce unsafe completion by 42.22 points relative to audits maximizing a static coalition deterrence ratio at the same budget.
