The Transformer Revolution, Part 1: Dynamic Processing through Output-Weight Interconnections

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03921

arXiv:2608.03921v4 Announce Type: replace Abstract: We reinterpret Transformer inference by developing a functionally equivalent mechanical-structural description of its functional architecture. Parameterized transformations of token representations are identified with simple neural networks organized through output-input and output-weight interconnections. This reconstruction makes explicit a constitutive feature of ordinary Transformer inference: outputs generated from the current input determine the weights, and hence the transformations, of other networks. We show that the resulting mechanical-structural organization is functionally equivalent to the standard matrix description and gives rise to Sequence-level Interactive Dynamic Parallel Processing (SIDPP). We then quantify the dynamic component of inference. For a fixed Transformer architecture, the number of dynamic parameters generated by a prompt grows linearly with prompt length, with a proportionality constant determined by the model architecture, whereas the number of static parameters remains constant. We call the resulting structural dependence of processing on the prompt strong prompt sensitivity. GPT-4.5's recent Turing test success provides a striking behavioral phenomenon for which this framework offers a viable explanatory route. Finally, we discuss implications for interpretability, control, predictability, and neuromorphic construction. We argue that known biological mechanisms support the in-principle neural constructibility of a form of SIDPP, and on this basis formulate Conjecture T as a conditional research hypothesis concerning cortical language processing.
