Passable for Whom? Few-Shot Learning for Personal Calibration of Sidewalk Accessibility Perception for Human-Centered Autonomous Driving

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/20
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19839

arXiv:2611.19839v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Whether a sidewalk is passable depends on who is trying to pass: people who use the same mobility aid disagree about the same image, so one label per scene, or per aid, is wrong for many of them. We introduce few-shot personal accessibility perception: from K answers of a person never seen before, predict their judgment of sidewalk scenes never seen before. PassCal separates a shared description of the scene from the person's interpretation of it. The description comes from the internal state of a frozen vision-language model (VLM) and from barrier type, severity and context properties predicted by a detector trained on 14.9k public audits; the interpretation, a calibration of the person's response style and a weighting of the properties, is fit from their answers. On 8,883 judgments in which each of 135 verified mobility-aid users rated the same 50 sidewalk scenes, with people and scenes both held out, eight answers lower the Brier score from 0.550 to 0.479. The same VLM prompted with those answers reaches 0.562, and the same calibration without the scene 0.521. Conformal risk control turns predictions into passable, blocked or "depends on you" statements with a bounded expected rate of wrong statements over people; at 5%, eight answers raise the share of decisive scenes from 27% to 42%. Audit-supervised properties improve all sixteen vision and VLM encoders we test and outperform attributes from a sidewalk navigation VLM.
