ExpGuard: Guarding Long-Horizon LLM Agents against Corrupted Experience

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32085

arXiv:2611.32085v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Long-horizon large language model (LLM) agents distill past trajectories into reusable experiences. Misleading or adversarial guidance in source trajectories can corrupt these experiences, allowing harmful instructions to persist across tasks and influence future agent actions. Assessing the risks of potentially corrupted experiences poses three challenges: (1) modeling provenance and execution dependencies before experience reuse, (2) learning task-conditioned risk from trajectory outcomes, and (3) repairing risky experiences at online inference. To address these challenges, we present ExpGuard, a framework for retrieval-triggered experience risk assessment and online repair. Specifically, to tackle challenge (1), ExpGuard integrates experience semantics with provenance and execution dependencies in a task-conditioned heterogeneous graph to predict attack and task-failure risks before experience reuse. To enable task-conditioned risk supervision and tackle challenge (2), we conduct matched counterfactual replays of each original experience and its minimally neutralized counterpart from the same task and environment checkpoint, deriving separate risk labels from differences in attack success and legitimate task completion. To tackle challenge (3) at inference time, ExpGuard repairs candidate experiences flagged as high-risk by the graph-based predictor at retrieval and re-predicts their risks before they enter the agent's active experience context. Extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of ExpGuard in identifying risky experiences and defending agents against experience corruption.
