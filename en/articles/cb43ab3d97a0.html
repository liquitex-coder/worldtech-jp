Evaluating Language Models as Spatial Priors for Object-Goal Navigation

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18123

arXiv:2611.18123v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Semantic exploration methods for object-goal navigation use large language models (LLMs) to score how strongly a target object is associated with a room type or with a nearby object. This paper considers obtaining from the LLM a spatial prior instead: a distribution over the position of the target in a room. We evaluate whether two open-weight LLMs, Qwen3.5-27B and Gemma-4-31B-it, can provide such a prior. Using real room layouts from CubiCasa5K and ScanNet, we measure the likelihood the models assign to real and perturbed layouts, and the layouts they generate. The object frequencies and room sizes of the generated rooms follow the room type, but the proximity of the objects to the walls does not follow the differences between room types found in real rooms. The likelihood distinguishes real layouts from perturbed ones partly through their geometry and partly through the agreement between the coordinates and a field that states the wall each object touches; for the movable furniture of ScanNet, it is higher for layouts with all objects piled near the room center than for the real layouts in most rooms. When an object is given in the prompt, the models place a related object relative to it. When the wall of each object is given, they place most objects against that wall, but do not keep all objects inside a given room rectangle. As a prior for object search, the generated placements reduce the searched area relative to a uniform prior, but by less than a third of the reduction obtained with a prior built from real layouts, and by less than two thirds when both priors are spatially smoothed. These results support the use of LLMs for object-room associations and for writing coordinates under constraints determined by another component, followed by a geometric check, but not as a source of placement priors where real layouts are available.
