EvoTER: Evolving Embodied Agents via Tool-Use Experience and Reflection

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/24
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27797

arXiv:2611.27797v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Long-horizon embodied tasks require agents to ground instructions in changing observations, coordinate heterogeneous tools, and recover from failures that can propagate through downstream operations. Although existing LLM-based agents retain experience and refine reusable skills, successful local repairs do not by themselves establish complete-task gains over the current shared procedure library. We present EvoTER, an experience-driven framework that enables embodied agents to self-evolve tool-use skills without updating model parameters. EvoTER operates through a three-role evolution loop: Explorer records tool-use trajectories; Diagnoser distills successful and failed executions into skills for tool selection, parameter computation, postcondition checks, and recovery; and Validator retains candidate revisions only when they improve complete-task success in fresh sessions. In simulation, EvoTER improves overall task success from a pre-evolution baseline of 53.60% to 80.40%. In real-world experiments, simulation initialization reduces the attempts required to acquire short-horizon skills, while continued real-world evolution improves long-horizon success from 20% to 80%-100% across tasks and enables the evolving skill library to incorporate new tool functionality introduced after simulation development. Project page: https://evoter-project.github.io/
