Motion-Aware Reward Modeling for Instruction-Guided Video Editing

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/19
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22495

arXiv:2611.22495v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Reward models for instruction-guided video editing can favor temporal consistency at the expense of motion. We study this failure, termed \emph{motion collapse}, using controlled motion-suppression variants of real edits. On VE-Bench, the evaluated composite reward prefers a motion-suppressed candidate in $97\%$ of cases. Learned video rewards and a VLM judge also struggle with mild suppression, while latent optimization and reward-guided fine-tuning exploit this weakness to increase reward as motion declines. A linear probe of our base reward backbone performs near chance on mild suppression, motivating an explicit inter-frame dynamics input. We propose a motion-aware reward model that combines an RGB frame-difference stream with preference training against motion-suppressed and fabricated-motion negatives. A distillation anchor preserves alignment with human quality judgments. On the VE-Bench motion ladder, our model reduces the rate of selecting motion-suppressed candidates to $0\%$ while achieving a Spearman correlation of $0.57$ with human quality ratings. It also detects unseen suppression variants and preserves motion during reward-guided fine-tuning on two editing pipelines. These results support explicitly modeling motion in rewards for video editing.
