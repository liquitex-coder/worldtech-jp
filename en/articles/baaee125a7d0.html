Stranded Compute in AI Inference Workloads: Recovering Value Through Demand Aggregation

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31654

arXiv:2611.31654v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: AI inference infrastructure is commonly analyzed as an energy business. Under full total cost accounting it is not: in the reference deployment modeled here, capital recovery is 87.3% of fully loaded cost per token and electricity 3.7%. Because capital accrues per unit time irrespective of use, accelerator value is destroyed by non-utilization rather than by consumption. Yet, SLO qualified goodput reaches only 59.6% of nameplate throughput, and the decomposition of the loss chain shows that 25.2 of the 40.4 lost percentage points arise from scheduling idleness and stranded burst reserve. Both are properties of a tenant's demand portfolio rather than of its silicon, and neither is diversifiable by a single tenant acting alone. Modelling reserve and idle capacity as diversifiable risk, pooling demand across eight imperfectly correlated tenants (rho = 0.3) recovers 7.3 pp of goodput, approximately $0.75M per annum per megawatt of IT capacity, or between 2.8x and 4.9x the entire annual interconnection spend of the deployment that produced it. The implication for neutral colocation and interconnection providers is that the switching and interconnection layer aggregating tenant demand functions as an instrument of capital productivity while being priced as transport.
