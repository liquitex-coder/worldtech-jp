PatternMiner: Automated Mathematical Discovery Through LLM-Guided Training and Interpretation of Small Models

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33966

arXiv:2611.33966v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: There are several approaches to AI-driven mathematical discovery. One of these involves the use of large language models (LLMs) to resolve well-specified conjectures via text-based reasoning, while another uses narrow models and interpretability for more open-ended, human-in-the-loop exploration using object-based mathematical data. The latter is limited in its ability to move beyond simple empirical pattern detection while the former is not well-designed for direct, open-ended exploration of large mathematical datasets. In this paper we introduce PatternMiner, a system that combines these two paradigms. In PatternMiner, an LLM agent generates mathematical datasets based on an initial broad question, trains narrow machine learning models on them, uses interpretability techniques to extract patterns from these trained models, and uses these patterns to formulate conjectures. Once an initial conjecture has been formulated, PatternMiner instructs the agent to search for counterexamples using calls to mathematical software. If a set of counterexamples can be found, these are used as feedback for conjecture refinement or another round of training and interpretability. The conjecture progressively evolves through this iterative sequence with the agent allowed to try to prove it at any point. We describe the results of applying PatternMiner to five well-known positivity problems in algebraic combinatorics. PatternMiner proves several new theorems related to Schubert positivity and projection, a character-row evaluation formula, a restricted version of the Kazhdan--Lusztig combinatorial invariance conjecture, and Schur positivity for a family of immanant coefficients. We end by analyzing the most common routes to conjecture generation that are used by PatternMiner in our case studies as well as documenting the most common failure modes.
