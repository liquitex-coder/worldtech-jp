Budget, Readout or Reasoning? Where Small Reasoning Models Lose Value in Auction Bidding

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34068

arXiv:2611.34068v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Ad platforms and marketplaces show bidders a bid landscape, the estimated chance of winning at each candidate bid, and increasingly let agents built on reasoning language models choose the bid. We give six open reasoning models of 4B to 14B parameters such landscapes, with win curves from eBay closing prices and from a simulated advertising market (AuctionNet), and score their bids on held-out auctions against an exact calculator. Because the agent is handed the estimates, the lost value can be split by cause. Two failures belong to the models. They pass over the best option even when the values written in their own trace already rank it first, and their wrong answers lean toward the highest bid on the menu. The lean is about the bid's size, not its place in the list, and it survives sampling. At 768 forced tokens on eBay, under pay-your-bid pricing, the wrong answers cost bidders 7-17% of the surplus the calculator's bids earn. Arithmetic errors are rare. Two further losses come from operator choices. A token cap cuts traces short: allowed to stop on their own within 4,096 tokens, five models recover 91-100% of the calculator's lead over a model that does no reasoning. Asking for the answer while the reasoning block is still open misreads it: closing the block first lifts the weakest model from 42% to 84% on eBay and from 24% to 81% on AuctionNet. Finally, an audit that flags bids disagreeing with the trace's own numbers, and overrides them with the calculator's bid, raises the share of the calculator's lead recovered at 768 forced tokens from 42-85% to 84-100% on eBay under nested cross-validation, and the same reader settings transfer from eBay to AuctionNet.
