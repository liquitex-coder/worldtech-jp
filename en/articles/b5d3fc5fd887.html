Reliability of Language-Model Judges for Quantitative Scientific Question Answering: Evidence from Perovskite Photovoltaics

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31269

arXiv:2611.31269v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Evaluating retrieval-augmented language models increasingly relies on automated model judges, yet their reliability on quantitative scientific synthesis remains largely unmeasured. Here, we evaluate automated grading on a quantitative benchmark for perovskite photovoltaics, where all 755 ground-truth answers are deterministically computed from 45,441 experimental device records. We evaluate a frontier judge (Claude Opus 5.5) alongside two local open-weight judges (Qwen3-14B and Qwen3-8B) across 4,530 free-text answers from six retrieval architectures under three regimes: reference-augmented (with the computed key), evidence-augmented (with retrieved passages and query logs), and unassisted (question and answer alone). Equipped with the reference key, Claude Opus reproduces ground truth near-perfectly (Cohen's $\kappa = 0.982 \pm 0.003$) and preserves exact architectural rankings ($\tau = 1.00$). Withholding the reference causes evaluation fidelity to collapse: frontier agreement drops to $\kappa = 0.557 \pm 0.022$, and providing retrieved literature passages yields virtually no improvement ($\kappa = 0.576 \pm 0.024$). Without reference keys, local judges degrade to near-random agreement ($\kappa = 0.087$ and $0.112$), inverting the leaderboard to rank a hallucinating closed-book baseline first ($\tau \le 0.20$). Furthermore, SQL-augmented generations induce severe evaluative blind spots: absent a reference, the frontier judge accepts 46.6% of erroneous SQL answers, overestimating architecture accuracy as 66.4% against its true 52.6%. These findings demonstrate that model-based evaluation in the physical sciences cannot rely on unstructured text inspection; rigorous benchmarking requires reference ground truth precomputed from structured measurement records.
