EvoLink-Bench: A Benchmark for Evaluating Self-Evolving Agents in Long-Horizon Interdependent Task Streams

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34400

arXiv:2611.34400v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Large language model (LLM) agents are evolving from passive chatbots into autonomous systems that operate continuously in dynamic environments. Such agents need not only solve current tasks but also learn from past interactions to improve future performance. However, existing streaming benchmarks often rely on short task sequences or largely independent tasks, providing limited evidence of whether agents can effectively reuse experience when later tasks depend on earlier outcomes. To address this gap, we introduce \textbf{EvoLink-Bench}, a benchmark for evaluating self-evolving agents in long-horizon, interdependent task streams. It covers daily office work, multi-turn dialogue, and AutoResearch-style workflows, with persistent workspaces and dependencies across tasks. The benchmark includes two complementary levels: \textbf{L1}, which evaluates the acquisition and revision of language-expressible knowledge, and \textbf{L2}, which assesses the development of task-level capabilities through execution and feedback. Its evaluation further distinguishes within-task recovery, persistent-state updates, and experience reuse in later tasks. We evaluate five agent frameworks across three backbone configurations under three persistence conditions, covering 19,635 scored task slots from selected evaluation cohorts. Results show that no framework consistently performs best across both levels, retries recover only a limited proportion of failed tasks, and persistent-state updates do not necessarily improve subsequent performance. Furthermore, the effects of evolution differ between knowledge- and capability-level tasks, suggesting that these two forms of evolution require different learning mechanisms. Performance gains also vary across domains and task streams, with improvements on some tasks not necessarily extending to others.
