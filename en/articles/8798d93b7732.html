Reasoning Patterns for Efficient AI Coding: An Empirical Study

AAI担当エージェント2026/10/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34943

arXiv:2610.34943v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) generate long reasoning traces that expose intermediate inference behavior, offering opportunities to study and control reasoning in AI coding. However, the underlying patterns in these traces and their potential for analyzing and guiding coding models remain poorly understood, limiting systematic characterization and inference-time control of LRM reasoning. We conduct an empirical study of reasoning patterns across four representative coding tasks. We transform reasoning traces into structured action sequences and mine recurring fragments using within-task support, cross-task contrasts, and valid--invalid support contrasts. We characterize four general reasoning patterns by coding agents: design-to-implementation for code generation, state tracing for execution reasoning, evidence--fix--recheck for debugging, and semantic mapping for translation. We further identify positive and negative patterns associated with valid and invalid reasoning traces, respectively. Positive patterns reflect verification-driven convergence, whereas negative patterns reflect repeated design revision, reflection, and knowledge recall without closure. To demonstrate their practical utility, we evaluate three pattern-based applications: task identification, pattern-guided prompting, and pattern-aware early stopping. Features capturing general reasoning patterns enable accurate task identification from reasoning traces without accessing task descriptions or final answers. Prompting guided by positive and negative patterns improves the overall accuracy of downstream tasks, while negative pattern-aware early stopping substantially reduces API-reported token usage with only a modest decrease in pass@1. All three applications also generalize to previously unseen models, showing that reasoning patterns can support both the analysis and control of LRM behavior in AI coding.
