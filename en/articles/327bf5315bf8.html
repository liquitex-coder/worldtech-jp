CoPre-MoE: Consolidating and Preserving MoE for Multimodal Continual Instruction Tuning

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18417

arXiv:2611.18417v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Multimodal continual instruction tuning requires multimodal large language models to sequentially acquire new capabilities without catastrophic forgetting. Mixture-of-experts (MoE) architectures offer a promising way to mitigate task interference through expert specialization and combination. However, while existing MoE-based methods avoid overwriting shared parameters by allocating separate experts for each task, this progressive expansion incurs severe cross-task redundancy and linear parameter growth. Specifically, experts across tasks often learn overlapping functions alongside task-specific capabilities. For instance, ScienceQA and TextVQA both locate relevant image regions. To curb parameter redundancy without inducing forgetting, we propose Consolidating and Preserving MoE (CoPre-MoE). For each incoming task, CoPre-MoE first learns task-specific MoE to capture specialized capabilities. To eliminate redundancy and reduce parameter growth across tasks, we design a shared-and-private expert architecture. Specifically, we first evaluate functional overlap to selectively consolidate overlapping experts across tasks into a fixed-capacity generic expert pool. We then factorize the remaining task-specific experts into shared projection bases and compact task-specific cores within a private expert pool. To mitigate forgetting, we construct the router by pairing each expert with learnable retrieval keys, where historical keys remain frozen during continual updates while newly added keys are regularized with an orthogonality penalty. At inference, input-dependent routing dynamically combines both pools without test-time task labels. Extensive experiments demonstrate that CoPre-MoE achieves superior performance while substantially reducing parameter overhead.
