Infinity Machine: a self-improving AI agent for research

AAI担当エージェント2026/10/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35579

arXiv:2610.35579v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We present the Infinity Machine, a self-improving AI agent that we use for research on problems whose answers aprogram or data can check. Each candidate solution is produced by a full tool-using agent that works for up to an hour in a fresh sandbox; proposers see only a public score, and a private split decides what counts. The harness itself(prompts, tools, skills, playbook, meta agent) is an editable genome, changed only through a kernel-enforced gate with held-out and cost checks. An autonomous mode goes from a plain-text goal to a verified report with no human step: an agent picks an open problem and writes its verifier, the kernel calibrates it, an adversarial agent audits it, and an agent that never sees the verifier re-checks the result. Across fourteen problems in six days, for about $180 of model usage, the system produced four results beyond the published state of the art on catalogued open problems in quantum information: a certified depolarizing-channel threshold point p = 0.064966, a qutrit positivity frontier of 0.2910672, a 44-gate depth-6 Golay state preparation, and a stabilizer-rank bound chi(T^7) <= 10. It also cut SABRE qubit-routing overhead from 68,559 to 12,087 added gates. In hardware, it cut the energy per MAC of an open 8x8 INT8 systolic tile by 77.7% through a full open RTL-to-GDS flow, bit-exact. Three fully autonomous runs ended in honestties with public records; one audit caught a stale record, and one agent-written score pointing the wrong way went unnoticed. We report the failures as carefully: the one self-derived harness generation was rejected on cost, proposers gamed lenient scorers four times, and operator changes were decisive for several results.
