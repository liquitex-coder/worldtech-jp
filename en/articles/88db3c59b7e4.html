Are Web Agents Still Detectable? Stress-Testing Bot Defenses with Coordinated Evasion

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31962

arXiv:2611.31962v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: AI web agents can autonomously navigate websites and complete various tasks. As their capabilities and adoption expand, evaluating bot defenses requires adversarial stress tests. Although prior work demonstrates that existing agents can remain detectable, it remains unclear how effective bot defenses are against adversaries that coordinate evasion across browser fingerprints, automation controls, and interaction behavior. To investigate this threat, we introduce DarkAgent, an autonomous web agent that combines a forked Chromium browser that conceals automation artifacts and generative models that produce human-like mouse and keyboard traces. To address the limitations of existing keyboard mimickers, our keyboard model jointly generates realistic key events and their timings while reliably reproducing the target text. Released agent detectors accept our generated behavior as human, and trained classifiers find it substantially harder to distinguish from human traces than existing mimickers. DarkAgent achieves an 89% page-access bypass rate across a third-party stealth benchmark, exceeding the strongest published baseline by 12 percentage points. To further enhance the measurement of agents, we introduce a benchmark that measures browser fingerprints, interaction behavior, and network activity to assess each channel's contribution to detecting adversarial agents such as DarkAgent. On this benchmark, browser and behavioral modifications are complementary: either alone remains detectable, and only their combination evades all tested checks. Together, our findings expose the gap between passing existing bot defenses and reproducing human behavior, and point to timing between actions as a remaining signal for defenders.
