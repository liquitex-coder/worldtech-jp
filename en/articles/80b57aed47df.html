On Evaluating User Simulators for Interactive Agents

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/20
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24892

arXiv:2611.24892v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: User simulators offer a scalable way to evaluate interactive agents, but how to assess their quality remains underexplored. In particular, it is unclear whether existing user simulator evaluation frameworks reliably detect meaningful failures in simulated user behavior. This uncertainty makes it difficult to interpret simulator evaluation. To understand what a high benchmark score tells us about simulator quality, we (meta-)evaluate the metrics themselves. To instantiate this meta-evaluation, we deliberately perturb simulated replays to induce failures such as altered goals, omitted constraints, premature stopping, and acceptance of broken work, then test whether the metrics penalize these failures. Evaluation methods from three prior studies detect only 5-6 of the 10 perturbations; their combined scores even favor populations instructed to accept broken work over unmodified controls. Motivated by these gaps, we propose a new evaluation suite that combines utility of user simulators for evaluating agents and two measures of fidelity to real users: population-level fidelity assesses collective interaction patterns, while trace-level fidelity assesses the preservation of individual users' goals, constraints, and persona. Under the same detection criterion, our evaluation suite detects all 10 perturbations. Evaluating six user simulators on 351 real multi-turn sessions in which 348 distinct users across 23 languages and agents work together to create and refine artifacts such as full-stack web apps, games, design assets, and automated workflows, we find that population-level measures can miss when a simulated user pursues the wrong goal, ignores a constraint, or behaves out of character, highlighting the need to assess fidelity at comprehensive granularities.
