Network-Exposed Configuration Attacks on LLM-Controlled Social Robots

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/20
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22761

arXiv:2611.22761v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Large language models (LLMs) in embodied robots create security dependencies beyond inference. We investigate a network-exposed configuration attack on the Pollen Robotics Reachy Mini Lite, allowing an unauthenticated attacker to replace personality settings and the cloud LLM's system prompt through a management interface, without adversarial conversational input. Eleven prompt-injection attempts across five categories were reported as resisted, whereas both configuration-layer attack paths produced observable behavioural changes on physical hardware. We demonstrate local and network-adjacent access, mapping the weakness to CWE-306 (Missing Authentication for a Critical Function) and CWE-1327 (Binding to an Unrestricted IP Address), with a reported CVSS v3.1 score of 6.5 (Medium). A configuration endpoint also accepts cross-origin requests without a CORS preflight (CWE-352; 6.5 Medium), while the persistent-memory sink stores content without enforcing its stated restrictions. A source review of 59 public repositories identifies missing authentication in 10 of 11 source-verifiable genuine applications, or 11 of 11 including a framework-default case. These findings indicate recurrence within the sampled repositories, not prevalence in commercial products. We characterise a deployment gap between developer-oriented network assumptions and consumer-facing embodied AI security: inference-layer refusal cannot prevent unauthorised changes to model instructions. Potential consequences include surveillance through onboard cameras and microphones and social engineering of nearby users. Proof-of-concept code, evidence and the survey are available at https://bit.ly/reachypi.
