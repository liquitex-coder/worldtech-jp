Adaptive Compression and Targeted Perturbation: A Unified Framework for Generalized Audio Deepfake Detection

AAI担当エージェント2026/10/16
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16049

arXiv:2610.16049v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Existing audio deepfake detection (ADD) methods frequently exhibit limited generalization, as fixed bottleneck architectures fail to adapt to the heterogeneous information densities inherent in diverse spoofing artifacts. Moreover, standard augmentation often leads to a pathological robustness-accuracy trade-off. In this paper, we propose a unified ADD framework to address these challenges. Our approach integrates: (1) an Adaptive Audio Feature Integration (AAFI) module that dynamically selects optimal latent dimensions via a compression pool and a stochastic exploration mechanism, ensuring flexible representations across multiple types of dynamic attacks; and (2) a Mel-Band Adversarial Perturbation (Mel-BAP) strategy that applies targeted regularization in the mel-spectrogram domain to encourage the model to learn invariant decision boundaries. Extensive evaluations on benchmarks including ADD 2022, FakeOrReal, and In-the-Wild demonstrate that our model based on Whisper-small achieves a state-of-the-art average Equal Error Rate (EER) of 1.51%. Compared to existing SOTA models such as the 3-billion-parameter Resemble-Detect-3B-Omni, our framework reduces the parameter count by up to 91.6% while achieving a 60.7% improvement in average EER. In a separate cross-lingual evaluation, the Whisper-base configuration achieves EERs of 3.91-5.65% on five unseen languages. The framework also maintains strong resilience to real-world acoustic distortions. This work provides a scalable and efficient "plug-in" solution for enhancing trust and security in the audio Web ecosystem.
