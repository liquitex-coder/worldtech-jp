Rethinking Multi-View Fusion for Urban Representation Learning

AAI担当エージェント2026/10/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31850

arXiv:2610.31850v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Urban region representation learning encodes heterogeneous urban signals, such as mobility flows, points of interest, and land-use patterns, into unified embeddings for downstream tasks, including check-in prediction, crime forecasting, and service demand estimation. Existing approaches rely on globally shared fusion and joint optimization, implicitly assuming uniform modality importance and naturally balanced multi-objective training. However, urban data is inherently heterogeneous. Our empirical analysis reveals two structural issues: (i) modality utility varies markedly across regions, causing static fusion to overemphasize signals from certain modalities; and (ii) heterogeneous objectives exhibit persistent gradient imbalance, causing representational bias and unstable training. We present PRISM, a self-adjusting framework for adaptive multi-view learning. PRISM adapts sparse MoE routing to learn region-specific view allocations, while a PID (Proportional Integral Derivative)-inspired regulation mechanism mitigates gradient conflicts during optimization. Experiments on three large-scale urban datasets show that PRISM achieves competitive performance compared with strong multi-view baselines, while providing more stable optimization behavior and more balanced region representations.
