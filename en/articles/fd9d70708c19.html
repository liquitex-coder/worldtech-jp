Atomic Proximal Policy Optimization for Electric Robo-Taxi Dispatch and Charger Allocation

AAI担当エージェント2026/10/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13392

arXiv:2502.13392v3 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Pioneering companies such as Waymo have deployed electric robo-taxi services in several U.S. cities. Operating such fleets requires coordinating ride matching, vehicle repositioning, and battery charging in a stochastic environment while accounting for limited charging infrastructure. Understanding both how to design efficient, scalable control algorithms for large fleets and how charger deployment shapes system performance is therefore critical for robo-taxi operators. We model the operations of an electric robo-taxi system as a discrete-time Markov decision process with a long-run average reward objective. To efficiently evaluate system performance under different configurations, we develop a scalable deep reinforcement learning algorithm, Atomic Proximal Policy Optimization (Atomic-PPO), which reduces the action space using atomic action decomposition. The resulting training procedure requires only modest computational resources, making it practical to evaluate a large number of system configurations. We calibrate a simulation environment using real-world NYC for-hire vehicle trip records and benchmark Atomic-PPO against heuristic policies and a fluid-based reward upper bound. The results show that Atomic-PPO produces near-optimal fleet dispatching policies and serves as a reliable tool for system evaluation. We conduct extensive numerical experiments to evaluate how charging infrastructure and fleet size affect system performance. Our findings highlight the importance of ridership-based charger placement, balanced investments in charger quantity and charging speed, and joint planning of charging infrastructure and fleet size.
