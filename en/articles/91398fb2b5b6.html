BioAgent: Self-Directed Skill Acquisition and Capability Growth for Long-Horizon Laboratory Protocols

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/03
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41383

arXiv:2610.41383v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Executing an unfamiliar laboratory protocol requires a robot to identify missing capabilities and determine whether each operation remains valid as the experiment progresses. This letter presents BioAgent, an embodied agent that addresses these coupled challenges of capability sufficiency and experimental validity. It matches human-reviewed requirements to validated skills and task packages, selects tool-based implementations for missing behaviors, and requests human teaching when needed during learning. Learned skills undergo sequential task-package validation to check outcomes and compatibility between successive operations. During execution, precondition, outcome, and experimental-state checks govern progression, autonomous recovery, or termination. Execution evidence guides tool selection, while accepted experience supports offline policy updates and task packaging. On the second task, BioAgent reuses 17 skill types unchanged, revises two existing implementations, and acquires five new types. On two dual-arm laboratory tasks with 20 and 31 ordered skill invocations, BioAgent achieves complete-task success rates of 66\% and 58\% with autonomous recovery over 50 runs per task. Across 25 atomic skills evaluated over 50 autonomous trials each with recovery disabled, success rates range from 84\% to 100\%, with 17 skills achieving 50/50.
