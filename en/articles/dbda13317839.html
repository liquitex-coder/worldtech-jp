Discharge Summary Generation Is Not Just Summarization: A Reality Check for Evaluation

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/15
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14823

arXiv:2611.14823v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Discharge summaries combine a retrospective account of hospitalization with discharge-time decisions. Physician-written references therefore contain both facts supported by prior clinical notes and reference-only content, potentially reflecting clinical synthesis, handoffs, or discharge planning. Reference-only evaluation can conflate failure to recover available information with failure to reproduce content absent from the model input. We introduce FairSumEval, an evidence-aware, fact-level framework that assesses whether each reference and generated fact is supported by the model's evidence and captured by the other summary. The resulting outcome map separates matches, omissions, and additions. Two complementary metrics, Soft IoU-S and Soft IoU-NS, measure reference-generation agreement for facts with and without identified evidence support. On 200 MIMIC-III admissions, 18.8% of physician reference facts have identified support in the prior evidence, compared with 65.4% and 63.5% of facts generated by a general-purpose and a medically specialized model. Overlap is substantially higher for supported content (0.255 and 0.236) than for content without identified support (0.030 and 0.026). The two scores are weakly correlated across admissions (rho = 0.22 and 0.17), and conventional metrics do not consistently capture both dimensions. FairSumEval distinguishes evidence-grounded summarization from agreement on content without identified support, revealing what models omit and add in each category.
