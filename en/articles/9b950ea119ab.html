JEPA for astronomy: multi-survey spectral alignment puts stellar parameters on the leading principal axes

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/20
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23492

arXiv:2611.23492v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Spectra from different surveys carry different systematics and are not calibrated against one another. Multi-survey foundation models for stellar spectra are then built by aligning embeddings of the same star observed across surveys. Most in astronomy follow the recipes established for language and vision, aligning with a contrastive loss that separates every star from the others. However, this does not ensure that the stellar physics is encoded in a form that a simple read-out can recover. Holding the cross-survey architecture fixed and varying only the alignment loss, we measure what an objective encodes from the principal-component geometry of the Gaia BP/RP (XP) spectra and from a linear probe of the stellar parameters. In a properly aligned embedding, the leading two principal components form a plane that recovers an analog of the Kiel diagram, the third component tracks [Fe/H], and [alpha/Fe] varies coherently across the plane, such that the chemically defined Galactic disks and accreted populations occupy distinct loci. We show that legibility, the ease of reading the information out, is highest for a training objective that never pushes stars apart, and instead constrains only the overall distribution of each survey's embeddings (JEPA). JEPA also exceeds the contrastive alternative at the same architecture, and the performance improves further as more surveys (Gaia XP+LAMOST LRS+DESI+APOGEE) are included. Predicted abundances for 8.0 million XP giants reproduce the main [alpha/Fe]-[Fe/H] structure of the Milky Way. These results indicate that the choice of alignment objective, not alignment alone, determines whether stellar labels are linearly recoverable.
