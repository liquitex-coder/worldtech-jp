Before the Choice: An Evidential Framework for Studying Prospective Representations in Language Models

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/21
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25170

arXiv:2611.25170v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: An observable decision reveals the selected action but does not determine which alternatives were internally represented. Drawing on prospection research and hippocampal forward-sweep observations, we develop Before the Choice, an evidence-oriented framework distinguishing future-directed content, prospective computation, and causal guidance. The framework organizes four experimental contrasts and makes their alternative explanations explicit. Its first test case concerns unchosen trajectories in deterministic symbolic environments. A preregistered low-rank probe evaluated once on a sealed TEST did not meet its criterion (paired accuracy 0.020 versus 0.195 for a text-and-observed-choice baseline; 293 admissible pairs). Exploratory H1b exposed a reconstruction-by-elimination problem: identifying the chosen candidate can determine the unchosen target. In H1c, matched worlds varied an unchosen continuation while keeping candidate keys bitwise invariant. With specified positive controls passed, decoding in the late-key condition remained at 0.521 (p = 0.18; 141 contributing families). These observations do not identify prospective simulation or establish an absence of prospective representations. They illustrate why identifiability, sensitivity, and a dependence-aware null model must constrain inferences from decodability. PREM is proposed as an organizational framework and is not validated by these results. We offer it as a methodological contribution to empirical AI welfare research, which will require such evidential distinctions. Code and protocol records: https://github.com/besionisrael/before-choice
