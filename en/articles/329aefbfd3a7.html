EffectWAM: Learning Action-Grounded Effects for Robot Manipulation

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32053

arXiv:2611.32053v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: World-action models connect visual prediction with executable robot control. We present EffectWAM, which develops this connection through three complementary components: Effect, camera positional encoding (CaPE), and Action Grounding. Effect represents visual change as the difference between independently encoded future observations and a shared current-observation anchor. CaPE conditions endpoint pooling on relative camera pose when reliable extrinsics are available, while sharing the same learned parameters with pose-free pooling for uncalibrated trajectories. Action Grounding trains the visual representation to retain measured changes in end-effector pose and gripper state. An exponential moving average of the observation encoder supplies Effect targets. A shared generation expert learns to jointly predict Effect and an executable action chunk through flow matching, and reuses the sampled pair to generate future video on demand. This allows the controller to execute actions without waiting for video synthesis. We train on more than 20,000 hours of heterogeneous embodied data, excluding unused chain-of-thought data, and accommodate dataset-specific joint, end-effector, and hand-pose representations. Our evaluation uses the Small (8B) configuration on all five benchmarks and additionally evaluates the Large (26B) configuration on RoboDojo. Experiments examine closed-loop manipulation across complementary benchmarks, and comparisons across multiple baseline policies show consistent improvements from Action Grounding.
