Who Should Be on the Panel? Task-Conditioned Persona Selection for LLM Trading Agents

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18150

arXiv:2611.18150v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Multi-agent LLM systems are built by fixing a cast of roles and then designing how the cast coordinates. Which agents should make up the group for a given task is left to the designer, and the same cast is reused for every task. Financial trading makes the cost of that choice visible: stocks trade differently, and the trading styles suited to one need not suit another. We study task-conditioned persona selection, composing the group from the characteristics of the task it will face, and instantiate it with FinPanel, an LLM multi-agent trading system in which a recruiter selects a panel of named trader personas for each stock from a direction-blind statistical sketch of how the stock trades, each persona gives an independent daily call in its own style, and a manager commits the trade after reading the panel's arguments and their desk-kept, hierarchical track records. Because nothing is returned to the traders, the panel stays the panel that was selected, and it can trade a new stock from an empty record. On eight US stocks over the out-of-knowledge year 2025, against three prior LLM trading agents re-implemented in the same framework and against rule-based, predictive and reinforcement-learning baselines, the selected panel is ahead of a fixed panel of the same size and of the prior agents run as their papers prescribe, with a shallower drawdown than buy-and-hold on every stock; it keeps its performance with no warm-up and with no record at all, whereas the memory-augmented agents' returns swing with their warm-up; and neither more seats nor the manager's reading of the arguments substitutes for selecting the right ones. Who is in the group is a design decision to be made per task, before how the group coordinates.
