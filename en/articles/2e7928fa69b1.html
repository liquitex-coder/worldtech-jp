Assessing Vision-Language Models for Early Prediction of Unsafe Situations in Robotics

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/20
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24146

arXiv:2611.24146v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Vision-Language Models (VLMs) process visual information and textual instructions and, by using their ability to understand and reason about context, are well-suited for predicting robot failures before they occur. However, evidence supporting this remains limited. To address this, we present an empirical evaluation of VLMs for predicting robot failures. In particular, we experiment with Qwen, InternVL, and LLaVA-OneVision across three robotic case studies (HuRoN, Pal Robotics, and CrowdBot), analyzing both video and odometry data for early failure warning (i.e., before an unsafe situation occurs). Specifically, we evaluate their performance in classifying situations as safe, potentially unsafe, and unsafe, together with uncertainty quantified using various metrics, and study the relationship between uncertainty and performance. Our results showed that Qwen provides the strongest overall classification and prediction-reliability performance, while InternVL shows stronger early-warning capability in several configurations. The results further show that the effect of robot-state information and temporal history depends on the model and evaluation objective. Overall, our findings indicate that general-purpose VLMs can support early robot-safety monitoring, but the most suitable configuration depends on whether the priority is accurate assessment of safety-state, reliable confidence and uncertainty, or timely warning before unsafe events.
