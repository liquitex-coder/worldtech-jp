Multistage Contextual Optimization for Vehicle Repositioning

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/20
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22330

arXiv:2611.22330v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Ride-hailing platforms reposition idle vehicles to improve spatial supply-demand balances. This problem is prediction-driven and multistage: at each stage, the platform forecasts future demand, executes current decisions, and uses the resulting state to initialize the next stage. We formulate a multistage linear program (LP) with a macroscopic fundamental diagram (MFD) linking accumulation to trip completion. We then develop a multistage contextual optimization approach that uses decision performance to train the prediction model. The learning problem combines multiple coupled stages, context that depends on earlier decisions, and predictions that affect the objective, the constraint matrix, and the right-hand side of the LP. We derive the exact gradient, which has three parts: parameters change forecasts and hence decisions; earlier decisions change the current decision through the inherited state; and this state also changes forecasts as context. An equivalent adjoint uses reverse vector-Jacobian products, avoiding full solution and state-parameter Jacobians. Finally, forward propagation solves many stage LPs one after another, which makes training slow. We propose a parallel block evaluation algorithm that solves blocks of consecutive stages in parallel and repeatedly feeds each block output to the next block. We prove that the algorithm recovers the serial trajectory within a finite number of passes and that its gradient error remains bounded when it stops early. On New York City ride-hailing data, multistage contextual optimization yields 7.7% to 9.1% more business benefit than the same predictor trained by mean squared error, outperforms all compared baselines from five paradigms, and even outperforms realized demand fed to the same policy. With eight workers, parallel block evaluation shortens training by a factor of 1.86 without loss of decision performance.
