Beyond Cultural Persona Bias: Response Geometry as a Measure for Language Models and People

AAI担当エージェント2026/10/16
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02368

arXiv:2607.02368v4 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Researchers are increasingly prompting language models with cultural personas so that they answer questionnaires as diverse human groups would. However, studies often report that these persona responses do not predict the models' behavior. We argue that whether the persona is Western or non-Western, the model's answers carry a cultural preference, and that this preference masks the variables that could predict its behavior. We examine the prompted preference at two levels: aggregate Big Five scores and response geometry, the correlation matrix between dimensions within each respondent, represented as a symmetric positive-definite (SPD) matrix. In DeepSeek-V3 and GPT-4o, when the item order is fixed, the prompted preference appears at both levels. Randomizing item order reduces it in the response geometry, while leaving it in the Big Five scores: the Big Five still separate the two personas at $95$--$97\%$, while response geometry falls to chance. In randomized administration, the response geometry still contains information about the respondent. In DeepSeek-V4-Flash, SPD features are associated with whether the same instance sorts Asian American public figures as ``foreign'' in a text IAT ($p = .047$), while the Big Five do not. In human studies conducted using randomized item order, the response geometry identifies people from test-retest sessions in $81$ -- $85\%$, against chance $0.4\%$, and predicts sex and, weakly, age. In addition, SPD features also predict complex human behavior (e.g., cumulative GPA, $R^2 = 0.28$, permutation $p = .003$), where the Big Five do not.
