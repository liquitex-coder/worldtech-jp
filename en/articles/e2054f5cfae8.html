Evidence sufficiency assessment enables cost-effective review allocation in on-premise clinical AI agents

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/24
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27390

arXiv:2611.27390v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: On-premise clinical AI agents must auto-accept high-confidence diagnoses, deferring others to clinician review, yet token probabilities and self-reported confidence miss errors, while more reliable five-run consistency incurs fivefold compute. We propose training-free decision-point evidence sufficiency assessment and a three-tier sequential triage protocol. Masking reasoning and diagnosis, the same 4B model scores sufficiency from objective evidence via prior-debiased probabilities over five ordered levels. Calibration freezes a nominal top-50% threshold, selecting single-run, five-run consistency, or full-deferral tiers by whether the one-sided 95% Wilson lower confidence bound of pass rate among accepted cases meets the 95% clinical target line. Across 1,605 MIRA-v2, CDM, VivaBench, and in-hospital liver malignancy primary cohort cases, MIRA-v2 single-run top-50% pass rate reached 98.91%, matching five-run consistency, saving ~80% compute; CDM achieved 96.00% test attainment by escalating to the five-run consistency tier; cross-specialty, missing-imaging, and combined enriched cohorts entered the full-deferral tier, capping calibration false-certification rate at 0.00%-3.50%.
