Redefining Instance Matching: A Unified Framework for Part-Aware Matching in Panoptic Segmentation Evaluation

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/24
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31094

arXiv:2605.31094v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: The Panoptic Quality metric is the standard for jointly evaluating instance and semantic segmentation. Its original definition matches predicted and ground truth segments only if their intersection over union exceeds 0.5, a threshold that guarantees a One-to-One matching that is both optimal and unique. Below this threshold, the uniqueness guarantee is lost and multiple matching strategies emerge in a poorly explored problem space. We systematically elucidate this space by recasting segment matching as a constrained bipartite assignment problem. Independently bounding the prediction- and ground-truth-side degrees yields four matching strategies: One-to-One, Many-to-One, One-to-Many, and Many-to-Many. We show that the first three yield interpretable instance-level assignments, whereas Many-to-Many entangles over- and under-segmentation and admits no consistent attribution of errors. These strategies become relevant when instances are fragmented, adjacent objects are difficult to delineate, or annotations are noisy. Central to our framework is a vertex-based accounting of TP, FP, and FN, anchored to ground truth and predicted segments rather than to matching edges. We further show that the framework extends naturally to part-aware panoptic segmentation and illustrate this extension conceptually on biomedical examples. Across configurable case studies we report how different combinations of thresholds and matching strategies behave in practice.
