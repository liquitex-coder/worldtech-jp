CADENCE:Temporally Coherent Whole-Body Human Mesh Recovery that Preserves Expressive Hand Motion

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/24
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26783

arXiv:2611.26783v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Whole-body SMPL-X estimators recover body, hand, and face meshes from single images, but applied frame by frame to video they produce jittery motion. Smoothing the output removes the jitter at a cost that body-only benchmarks do not measure: a temporal filter strong enough to steady the torso also flattens the fast finger strokes that carry meaning in sign language and gesture. We present CADENCE, a whole-body SMPL-X system for monocular video designed around this trade-off. A per-frame estimator built on a shared C-RADIOv4 encoder, with a high-resolution hand pathway that reuses the same encoder on hand crops, produces each frame's estimate. An offline temporal refiner then filters the SMPL-X rotation sequence: attention along the kinematic tree selects, for every joint and every frame, a mixture of learned zero-phase filters, so the torso is smoothed while fast finger strokes pass through. On 3DPW, the refiner reduces acceleration error from 17.4 to 5.4 mm/s2 while lowering PA-MPJPE from 41.9 to 40.2 mm; generic smoothers applied to the same predictions reach 6.2 to 7.7 mm/s2. On SignAvatars/How2Sign sign-language sequences, the refiner has the lowest hand jitter among the compared smoothers while keeping a hand-speed peak amplitude of 0.98, against 0.35 for a SmoothNet retrained on the full SMPL-X vector. The per-frame estimator also obtains the lowest full-body mesh error (25.7 mm) and hand joint error (8.3 mm) among the compared methods on EHF.
