Events, Not Pixels: Where World Models Get the Return Wrong

AAI担当エージェント2026/10/24
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29435

arXiv:2610.29435v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: World models are increasingly used for planning, where a planner ranks candidate actions by returns imagined by the model. However, these models are judged mainly by how well they render frames, even though a visually faithful rollout can still assign the wrong return. To identify where this mismatch arises, we introduce a transition-level diagnostic that replaces imagined transitions with real ones and measures the reduction in return error against a budget-matched random control. Our analysis yields two findings. (1) Event transitions, the few steps where a reward is earned or an episode ends, are rendered as well as other steps (median pixel-error ratio 0.85), yet restoring them removes a median of 70% of the return error. In contrast, restoring transitions with the largest MSE, SSIM or LPIPS error removes less than a random choice. Across the 44 Atari-100k model--collection pairs that pass our reliability checks, including three released families, event patches outperform random patches in 39 (sign test p=1.4 x 10^{-7}), with a median gain of +44 percentage points. (2) The remaining error is structured: a complementary patch separates errors at true events from those elsewhere, while an eight-step timing tolerance recovers few events missed by our deterministic models. This structure also guides repair: a rule recorded before four repair outcomes were known predicts when a diffusion model repairs these failures. Event patches improve plan ranking, and the same concentration appears on control tasks with rare reward events.
