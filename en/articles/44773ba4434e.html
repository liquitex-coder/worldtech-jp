Agent Collectives Should Not Detect Their Own Imposters: A Chess Case Study

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09027

arXiv:2605.09027v4 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Collectives of AI agents can outperform any single agent on complex tasks, yet remain vulnerable to imposters that deliberately mislead their peers. Because even one such imposter can sabotage the collective, reliable detection is essential. We examine two strategies: embedding detection inside the participating agents, or employing a dedicated external detector that observes the deliberation without joining it. On a deliberately small but challenging chess-based testbed, we find that simply warning agents of possible imposters is counterproductive. It degrades decisions even in the absence of adversaries, triggers unhelpful social dynamics, and leaks information to the imposter. An external detector avoids these pitfalls, yet must continually adapt as imposters evolve new strategies. We show that a lightweight meta-trained detector can rapidly recalibrate to unseen attacks from only a handful of examples, far more efficiently than conventional fine-tuning. To enable continued study of robust multi-agent systems under adaptive adversaries, we release the accompanying benchmark of labeled deliberations spanning a wide range of evolved imposter strategies. Code and data are available at https://anonymous.4open.science/r/gambit.
