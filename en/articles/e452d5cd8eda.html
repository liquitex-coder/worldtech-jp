When the Knowledge Runs Out: A Part-Level Pilot Evaluation of How Language Models Handle Partially Covered Questions

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33428

arXiv:2611.33428v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Assistants that answer from supplied reference material often receive material that covers some parts of a user's question and not others. Existing evaluations of insufficient context mostly score one answer per question, and often treat partially sufficient context as simply insufficient. We describe a small, open evaluation that scores each part of a three-part question separately, with a verdict for parts that are answered correctly from the material but carry an added unsupported claim. On 30 cases (five scenarios, six coverage patterns, five samples each) we test claude-haiku-4-5 and llama3.1:8b. Without an explicit instruction to stay within the material, both models fill most gaps from their own knowledge (unsupported-claim rates of 0.71 and 0.90). One added sentence instructing them to answer only from the material brings these rates to 0.00 and 0.06, consistent with prior work. What remains is less visible: answers that strengthen the material, for example turning "many job sites prohibit bots" into a flat "no". We also report on the judge. Three rounds of blind hand labelling show that this added-claim verdict is the hardest to apply, for the judge and the author alike; RAGAS faithfulness is unsuitable for this task because it penalises correct withholding; and a difference between the two models that is clear under one judge disappears when the judge model is replaced. The dataset, code, hand labels and per-run figures are public.
