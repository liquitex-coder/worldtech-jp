Tune for Retention, Not Just Gain: SFT Forgets Far Less than You Think

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34579

arXiv:2611.34579v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Catastrophic forgetting, the drastic loss of previously acquired capabilities when learning new ones, is a central challenge in language model fine-tuning. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) is widely believed to be prone to such forgetting. Recent works have attributed this to the off-policy nature of SFT, arguing that on-policy learning is key to continual learning. In this paper, we challenge this premise. We show that while maximizing new-task performance can induce catastrophic forgetting, alternative hyperparameter choices (lower learning rate and batch size) can yield similar gains with little to no forgetting. With hyperparameters selected jointly for gain and retention, vanilla SFT retains prior capabilities far better than previously suggested and can be competitive with Self-Distillation Fine-Tuning (SDFT), an on-policy approach designed to mitigate forgetting. While SDFT can be more robust to hyperparameter choices, it also requires a retention-aware criterion to identify hyperparameter configurations that avoid catastrophic forgetting. Our results challenge the view that on-policy training is what enables learning with retention and raise broader questions about what drives catastrophic forgetting during fine-tuning.
