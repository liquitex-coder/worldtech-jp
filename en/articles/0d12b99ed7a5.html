When can an interpretability claim be trusted? Stability and certification of feature dimensionality, ridge leverage and the feature recovery rate

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/15
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16190

arXiv:2611.16190v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Interpretability tools increasingly support safety claims, such as that a sparse dictionary has recovered most of a model's features. Such claims rest on measurements with arbitrary thresholds, and small changes to the model weights, a different training seed or a different threshold can reverse them. We study two widely used measurements in toy models and synthetic dictionaries with known ground truth: the feature dimensionality of Elhage et al. and the feature recovery rate (FRR) with a cosine threshold. We characterize exactly where feature dimensionality is discontinuous and replace it with a regularized (ridge) version with sharp stability bounds: the ridge leverage score changes by at most $3\sqrt{3}/(8\sqrt{\lambda})$ times the operator-norm change of the weights, and this constant cannot be improved; for the regularized dimensionality the stability constant grows like $\rho/4$ in the scale $\rho=R/\sqrt{\lambda}$, with explicit upper and lower bounds. We also prove certificates stating when a claim "feature $i$ is recovered" must survive a given change of the dictionary. The certificates held in every test and are informative for small perturbations, but certify no feature non-trivially across independently trained dictionaries; at the standard threshold, whether a feature counts as recovered changed with the training seed for 55 percent of features. Experiments use toy models only.
