VLAN: Vision-Language Accessible Navigation

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18346

arXiv:2611.18346v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Accessible mobility is user-conditioned rather than scene-intrinsic, as walkable paths for one embodiment are inaccessible to another. Yet, existing benchmarks evaluate traversability under a fixed and limited embodiment. We present Vision-Language Accessible Navigation (VLAN), containing 1,000 scenes, 20K navigation trajectories, and 160K keyframes across diverse lighting, environmental conditions, scene semantics, and path geometries. For each trajectory, VLAN provides embodiment-specific actions and accessibility features for legged and wheeled agents, including humanoid and quadruped robots and people with visual or mobility impairments, while everyday obstacles and accessibility labels are categorized as Passable, Bypass, HighRisk, and Blocked. Unlike goal-only evaluation, our benchmark measures physical safety and alignment with user-specific constraints via goal-reaching, collision-aware, and accessibility-aligned metrics. We benchmark modern vision-language models and find they often fail to consistently adapt obstacle relevance, path selection, and safety criteria across user profiles, suggesting accessibility-aware navigation requires dedicated datasets, tasks, and metrics beyond conventional embodied navigation evaluation. All data and code open-source at https://github.com/InSAI-Lab/VLAN.
