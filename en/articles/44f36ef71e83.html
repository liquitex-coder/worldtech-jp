BRAG: Bayesian Retrieval Augmented Generation

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31656

arXiv:2611.31656v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) ranks memory chunks by semantic similarity to the query. Many methods improve on this by learning from feedback on what was retrieved, but each is a separate heuristic, with no account of when it is the right choice. We introduce BRAG, a generative model of retrieval with use. Each chunk generates the queries it answers from a mixture of von Mises-Fisher components anchored at its original embedding, and each chunk has a latent utility that drifts over time. The model gives the optimal inference, a closed-form filter that stores a few keys and two scalars per chunk and updates them online from the labels of a curator, and it frames retrieval as a control problem. It also shows when existing methods are optimal. Query averaging, Rocchio-style feedback and frequency reranking are special cases with fixed parameters, NUDGE is the case of one key per chunk with no dynamics, and LADER the limit of one key per past query. On two public long-term memory benchmarks, BRAG matches these methods on queries asked before, as expected, because when nothing changes over time the assumptions behind these special cases hold. On harder problems, where a new chunk makes an old one obsolete, BRAG outscores them. They all stay at plain-search level (Recall@5 0.67-0.71), while BRAG retrieves the new chunk 0.80-0.86 of the time. We also ran BRAG on three of NUDGE's seven text benchmarks, and it matches or beats NUDGE on all three. On nine months of a personal agent's memory (20,640 chunks), BRAG raises Recall@5 on held-out queries from 0.75 to 0.98 and Recall@1 from 0.44 to 0.97, with little change on chunks never queried.
