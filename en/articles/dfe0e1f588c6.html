Perceive Once, Escalate on Demand: Handing Off Visual KV Memory from Small to Large VLMs

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/24
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26902

arXiv:2611.26902v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: An embodied agent cannot afford a large vision-language model (VLM) at every step of a long video, so a cascade runs a small VLM continuously and escalates only uncertain decisions to a large one. Each escalation, however, forces the large model to re-encode a history the small model has already perceived, and this dominates its cost. We present Baton, which hands the large model the small model's visual memory, its key-value (KV) cache over the history. Baton rests on one finding: the large model needs a usable memory, not a faithful copy of its own. A map trained to reproduce the large model's cache matches it most closely, yet brings no gain over the small model alone. Baton instead distills a per-layer linear map, without labels, from the large model's own answers, with the large model encoding only the current frame. Interventions on the map trace the gain to one direction, carried by the middle third of the large model's layers. On RoboMME, Baton beats the small model alone, a pruned or shortened re-watch, and a text memory; as the escalation channel of a cascade it matches re-watching to within about one point at 28-47% less compute, and 8x less per escalation at 64 frames. Its gain grows with the large model, from 8B to 32B, tracks the large model's headroom across eight model pairs from four families, and transfers zero-shot to OpenEQA.
