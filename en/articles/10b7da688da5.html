What Matters for Cross-Modal Alignment

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/19
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20000

arXiv:2611.20000v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: The Platonic Representation Hypothesis posits that models trained on different modalities converge toward a shared representation. Related work aligns frozen image and text encoders on natural images without paired data. We test zero-pair alignment on five datasets across four modalities: natural images, environmental audio, chest radiographs, and 3D computed tomography (CT). It succeeds only on COCO and fails on the other four. We analyze the failures and find that alignment must first locate the subspace where the modalities co-vary and then orient the mapping within it. Consistent with previous studies, medical imaging is the hardest case: its shared subspace carries the smallest fraction of the variance. When this fraction is small, alignment needs image-text pairs, and random-matrix theory predicts how many pairs are needed. These findings motivate LightAlign: a few pairs let a ridge regression between two frozen encoders locate and orient the mapping simultaneously, and a classifier fitted only on report text then predicts findings from the mapped images. On 3D CT, LightAlign surpasses models trained end to end on image-report pairs without finding-level labels: with only 200 pairs, LightAlign outperforms CT-CLIP on CT-RATE (0.754 vs. 0.733 AUROC), and with only 100 CT-RATE pairs, it does so on RAD-ChestCT, an out-of-distribution dataset from a different hospital. On CheXpert, it reaches 0.862 mean AUROC with only about 5% as many pairs as models trained on MIMIC-CXR.
