ALLMTS: Automated LLM-based Medical Triage System

AAI担当エージェント2026/10/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32469

arXiv:2610.32469v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Telephone triage is a safety critical task: from a brief intake note, a nurse must decide whether a caller needs an ambulance, an emergency department, an appointment, or self care at home. Queried directly, large language models (LLMs) are unreliable: they over-escalate routine cases and cannot justify their recommendations against an auditable clinical standard. We present the Automated LLM-based Medical Triage System (ALLMTS), which grounds every disposition in the same commercially maintained telehealth guidelines nurses use. Rather than asking an LLM to triage, ALLMTS makes it execute the guideline: it selects the appropriate guideline and evaluates its triage assessment questions. The disposition follows deterministically from the first positive answer, using the same traversal rule a nurse follows. We evaluate ALLMTS on 566 synthetic, expert-validated scenarios spanning 14 dispositions and designed to test specific guideline questions. ALLMTS achieves 0.95 accuracy, compared with 0.47 for a raw-LLM baseline. On a curated 50-scenario subset released as an open benchmark, ALLMTS reaches 0.98 accuracy, on par with the 0.944 correct disposition rate that a five nurse expert panel recorded on those same scenarios. Critically, on that subset the baselines have over-triage rates of up to 0.50 (0.44 on the full dataset), whereas ALLMTS reduces the over-triage rate to at most 0.02 while maintaining low under-triage. These results support coupling LLM language understanding with executable clinical guidelines for triage from intake notes.
