Measuring the Value of One Shared Image at Robot Handoffs in Vision-and-Language Navigation

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33865

arXiv:2611.33865v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: When the lead robot cannot continue, what should it send the teammate that must finish the route? We replay a frozen receiver at each contact under alternative messages. Across 3,920 autonomous departures in 106 environments, adding the view before the lead's last action block to its executed actions raised success by 7.51 percentage points (pp) per RxR handoff and 8.54 pp per SAGE handoff. Per departure the gains were 2.23 and 1.44 pp, respectively. The lead continuing in simulation defines the empirical full-history ceiling (Solo). At contact, the lead had accumulated a median of 41 frames. Sending one frame when available recovered 24% of the full-history advantage over actions alone in SAGE and 30% in RxR. Over an estimated progress cue in SAGE, the image rescued 100 routes and reversed 54 successes. Near-goal receivers finished outside less often (63.1% to 51.7%, post hoc). In secondary comparisons the earlier view outperformed the contact view by 6-7 pp per handoff. Exact-route specificity and image gains at controlled starts with mostly unknown progress or outside the goal region remained unresolved. Gains were detectable from about 7 kB per handoff. Random transmission matched within size bins nearly equaled the learned sender's team success. At a required handoff, a view of the approach to the last maneuver helps the receiver finish.
