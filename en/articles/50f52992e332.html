Rapid Exploratory Maps and their potential role in supporting evidence-to-policy dialogues and processes

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31693

arXiv:2611.31693v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Producing useful evidence syntheses requires clearly formulated research questions linked to an understanding of their optimal scope. When policymakers (or other end users) commission or steer the production of systematic reviews or other evidence syntheses to fulfil a demand for evidence, these parameters are rarely specified sufficiently for production to proceed. Instead, these details are typically developed via a collaborative dialogic process involving evidence producers and users to ensure the resulting synthesis will meet users' needs. This process is informed by scoping and (more-or-less formally) mapping the existing evidence base, which can take many weeks or months, slowed by applying mostly manual processes to identify and characterise the evidence. Recent developments in artificial intelligence (AI) technologies have opened up new opportunities for more rapid, iterative, and responsive mapping of relevant evidence to support such early-stage evidence-to-policy dialogues and processes. In this article, we outline and characterise this emerging approach, termed Rapid Exploratory Maps (REMs). We also present two illustrative case studies drawn from first-hand experiences within a programme of evidence reviews production to inform government policy in England. While this approach could be transformative for the mapping process, its broader potential is as yet largely unassessed, and it confers important challenges and likely risks, as well as benefits. Determining whether REMs could usefully support more efficient ways of working with end users when developing and designing evidence syntheses will require continued development and careful evaluation.
