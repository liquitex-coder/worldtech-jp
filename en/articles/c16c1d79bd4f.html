From Temperature-Limited to Battery-Limited Legged Locomotion

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/19
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22231

arXiv:2611.22231v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: On legged robots, sustained locomotion often ends in thermal shutdown rather than battery depletion: the heat of walking concentrates in a few joint motors, which overheat long before the battery is empty. We train thermally aware locomotion policies by reinforcement learning without ever observing temperature, and demonstrate this on a Unitree Go2 quadruped. We first identify a coupled 13-node lumped RC thermal network, fitted on two runs of a single robot and calibrated against the measured electrical power, that predicts all twelve motor temperatures with a mean RMSE below 1 $^\circ$C across different robot instances, gaits, payloads, and speeds. Under a sustained gait, the temperatures the motors will settle at follow directly from the gait-averaged squared torques and joint speeds. Our core contribution is a reward that drives down the hottest predicted asymptotes: thermal time constants exceed the credit-assignment horizon of reinforcement learning by orders of magnitude, but predicted asymptotes respond to torque and joint speed within a single gait cycle, so the policy learns to lower and spread heat with no temperature input. On the robot, the policy turns sustained locomotion from temperature-limited to battery-limited: with a 5 kg payload at 1 m/s, a learned baseline reaches thermal shutdown after 19 minutes (1.1 km), whereas our policy runs the battery down after 174 minutes (10.0 km) with its hottest motor 14 $^\circ$C below the shutdown threshold in a 26 $^\circ$C room; on an unloaded factory circuit with stairs and ramps, the baseline overheats after 25 laps (6.2 km), while our policy completes 47 (11.2 km) on one battery with 22 $^\circ$C of margin.
