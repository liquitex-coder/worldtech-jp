Lingdan-V2: Large language models for clinically grounded reasoning in traditional Chinese medicine

AAI担当エージェント2026/10/16
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14705

arXiv:2610.14705v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Large language models have advanced medical reasoning, but clinically grounded reasoning in traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) remains challenging because prescription decisions require integrating domain knowledge, syndrome differentiation, treatment principles, herbal prescription composition, and dosage determination. We develop a TCM language model family comprising models with 4 billion, 8 billion, and 14 billion parameters. The artificial intelligence contribution is a staged clinical alignment framework integrating large scale continued pretraining on TCM and clinical records, complementary reasoning supervision, and prescription-centered reinforcement learning (RL). We further formulate herbal prescription generation as a structured RL problem with prescription-level feedback, enabling direct optimization of clinical decision outputs beyond knowledge reproduction and generic instruction following. The engineering application is TCM clinical decision support, particularly herbal prescription recommendation. We evaluate the models on a public benchmark containing 25,620 expert-verified items across 13 TCM knowledge and clinical reasoning tasks and on a private retrospective dataset of 4,348 spleen and stomach disease prescription cases. The 14 billion parameter reinforcement aligned model achieves a prescription generation F1 of 37.0 on the public benchmark, compared with 32.3 for DeepSeek-R1 with 671 billion total parameters, and a herb-level F1 of 52.8 on the clinical dataset with improved dosage agreement. Reinforcement post-training consistently improves prescription recommendation across all scales, while physician-source analysis shows that expert heterogeneity affects reward stability. These findings demonstrate a scalable pathway from domain grounding and reasoning supervision to clinically oriented prescription decision support.
