Certify What You Plant: Shortcut-Free Proof Depth in Logical Reasoning Data

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33303

arXiv:2611.33303v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Procedurally generated logic problems are used to both evaluate and train reasoning in language models. Their difficulty is usually set by planting a proof, that is, by sampling a proof of the desired depth and stating its premises as the problem. However, a planted proof is only one possible proof. A problem can also have shorter proofs, or several unrelated ones, and its stated depth then overstates what solving it requires. We measure what a problem actually requires, relative to a stated set of inference rules. For each problem, we compute the steps every proof must make, the shortest proof, the number of minimal proofs and the indispensable premises, and export certificates that an independent checker verifies. Audits of eight datasets find depth that is forced (ProofWriter, Blocksworld), problems labelled depth 5 that require about two specific rule applications (FLDx2), and answers that need no particular step (ZebraLogic). We then construct problems whose every proof has a requested structure, using a catalog of shortcuts proved once per rule set. Every constructed problem is checked in full and comes with its own certificate. In training, the best data mixes depths and problem types. At equal size, such mixtures of certified problem types, including model-checking problems that forward generation does not produce, transfer better than FLDx2-style derivation data for Qwen3 models from 0.6B to 8B.
