Personalization of Basic and Moral Values Guiding LLM-Controlled Assistant Robots

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18230

arXiv:2611.18230v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Personal assistant robots powered by large language models (LLMs) are likely to enter everyday life within the next decade, but the values that should guide their behaviour remain largely unspecified. Current alignment practice steers a single model toward an aggregate of human values, ignoring that individual users may prefer a robot whose value profile differs from their own. We administer parallel self- and robot-targeted versions of three established psycho-metric instruments - the simplified Schwartz Values Survey (SVS), the Portrait Values Questionnaire (PVQ-21), and the Moral Foundations Questionnaire (MFQ) - to potential future users (N=22), capturing both their personal values and the values they would want an assistant robot to embody. We compare the three instruments as personalization tools using the UEQ-S and benchmark fifteen contemporary LLMs against the resulting profiles. Participants wanted their robot to score markedly lower than themselves on personal-focus values and higher on social-focus values; moral profiles were nearly identical. LLMs prompted to act as robots shifted only weakly toward the desired profile, indicating that default model behaviour and prompt engineering are no substitute for explicit value personalization.
