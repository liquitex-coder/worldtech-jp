MUSE: Multi-Task Uncertainty-Aware Self-Supervised Encoder for mmWave ISAC

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34122

arXiv:2611.34122v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) allows a wireless system to use the same waveform for communication and environment sensing. At mmWave frequency bands, ISAC enables diverse human sensing tasks, such as localization, gesture recognition, and pose estimation, with high precision. However, each task requires training a dedicated model, typically from scratch on a task-specific dataset, with no mechanism to flag unreliable predictions. This hurts the scalability, transferability, and reliability of mmWave ISAC models. In this work, we propose MUSE (Multi-task Uncertainty-aware Self-supervised Encoder), a unified framework for mmWave human sensing. MUSE leverages a self-supervised transformer model with per-dataset tokenization and is trained via masked autoencoding across five datasets. During fine-tuning, MUSE uses evidential classification heads that enable uncertainty quantification. MUSE replaces N separate encoders with a single shared transformer encoder, an N-times backbone parameter reduction, while achieving a 9.7 percentage points improvement in classification performance compared to the task-specific model, showing that learning jointly across tasks improves per-task generalization. Across four classification tasks, MUSE produces reliable uncertainty estimates that enable selective prediction, improving accuracy by abstaining on the least confident 20% of predictions. We further improve the reliability of the model's confidence estimates using temperature scaling and conformal prediction. Finally, MUSE's uncertainty signal remains reliable even under cross-user deployment, assigning higher uncertainty to wrong predictions than to correct ones 78% of the time, and recovering 9.2 percentage points of accuracy through selective prediction, with no additional retraining on the unseen user.
