SHAPE: Discrete Supervision and Parallel Continuous Streams for Learning Dexterous Manipulation from Egocentric Human Data

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/20
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24585

arXiv:2611.24585v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Egocentric human recordings are far cheaper than dexterous robot demonstrations, promising stronger pretraining and direct skill learning without task-specific robot data. Yet humans and robots differ in morphology and kinematics, so human recordings lack the joint positions and actuator commands that supervise robot policies, providing only retargeted end-effector (EEF) actions. This raises two questions: how human data should supervise the policy, and how a dexterous robot should read out actions at deployment. We address both in SHAPE (Supervision from Human Action tokens with Parallel Executable streams), a vision-language-action (VLA) model whose backbone predicts discrete action tokens of EEF chunks as an auxiliary task, while a shared flow-matching expert outputs two executable streams, EEF and joint. On a bimanual robot with 22-DoF hands, a matched study shows that discrete supervision helps learn from human data, driving backbone gradients and shaping action-informative representations across domains. Under standard control pipelines at deployment, direct joint readout excels at fine-grained control with robot demonstrations, scoring 0.80 across six tasks against 0.63 for {\pi}0.5, whereas IK-based EEF readout excels at human-to-robot transfer. Pretrained with human data and discrete tokens, SHAPE learns new tasks from human demonstrations without task-specific robot data.
