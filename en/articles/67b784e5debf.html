Contrastive Bootstrap Representation Learning for Label-Efficient Agricultural Image Classification

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/15
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15610

arXiv:2611.15610v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Expert annotation remains a major bottleneck in agricultural image classification, where collecting images is often easier than obtaining reliable labels from domain specialists. Self-supervised learning can reduce this dependence by pretraining transferable representations on unlabelled imagery. Contrastive and bootstrap methods provide two established but distinct training signals, motivating an empirical study of a formulation that applies both signals within one representation path under severe downstream label scarcity. We present Contrastive Bootstrap Representation Learning (CBRL), which jointly optimises SimCLR-style NT-Xent contrastive learning and bidirectional BYOL-style exponential-moving-average target prediction within a shared online projection space. Using a ViT-L/16 backbone and 10% of the downstream labels, we report single-seed point estimates on four primary agricultural test benchmarks plus an exploratory 33-image New Plant Diseases evaluation. CBRL records the highest macro-F1 point estimate on the four primary benchmarks. On DeepWeeds, its 79.77% test macro-F1 is 32.29 points above the random-initialisation, label-only baseline and 15.78 points above the next-highest comparison point estimate; on PlantVillage, its 98.14% macro-F1 is 10.43 and 0.43 points higher, respectively. On PlantDoc, however, all methods fail to reach practically useful performance. These results identify the evaluated joint formulation as a promising candidate for label-limited transfer, but they do not establish objective synergy, statistical superiority, or robustness across stochastic runs.
