On the Granularity of Causal Effect Identifiability

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/07/25
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.16703

arXiv:2510.16703v3 Announce Type: replace Abstract: The classical notion of causal effect identifiability is defined in terms of treatment and outcome variables. In this paper, we consider the identifiability of state-based causal effects: how an intervention on a particular state of treatment variables affects a particular state of outcome variables. We demonstrate that state-based causal effects may be identifiable even when variable-based causal effects may not. Moreover, we show that this separation occurs only when additional knowledge -- such as context-specific independencies -- is available. We further examine knowledge that constrains the states of variables, and show that such knowledge can improve both variable-based and state-based identifiability when combined with other knowledge such as context-specific independencies. We finally propose an approach for identifying causal effects under these additional constraints, and conduct empirical studies to further illustrate the separations between the two levels of identifiability.
