EndoRSI: Physical-Evidence-Driven Recursive Improvement of Endoscopic Robot Policies

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/10/13
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13376

arXiv:2610.13376v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Artificial intelligence is beginning to automate scientific discovery, yet physical robot-policy research still relies on scientists and engineers to interpret experimental failures and decide how controllers should be revised. We present EndoRSI (Endoscopic Recursive Self-Improvement), a human-governed, self-improving robotic research system in which the embedded EndoForge coding agent iteratively develops and refines endoscopic robot policies using evidence from physical experiments. Physical execution remains isolated within a safety-constrained runtime under human authorisation. In motorised endoscopy, validation evidence first caused the agent to reject an initial action representation, while subsequent physical experiments falsified an offline-supported insertion strategy by revealing hazardous closed-loop behaviour absent from the source data. Failed physical rollouts were then incorporated into policy revision; insertion success subsequently increased from 2/21 in the first generation to 17/21 in the fourth. The frozen final policy retained competence across visual and hardware shifts and was the best performing autonomous policy across six independent ex-vivo porcine specimens. These results show that physical robot interaction can serve as experimental evidence for agent-driven robot policy research.
