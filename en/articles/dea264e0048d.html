Gaussian Approximation for Composition and Subsampling of Differentially Private Algorithms

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33333

arXiv:2610.33333v3 Announce Type: replace Abstract: The privacy analysis of differentially private stochastic gradient descent (DP-SGD) relies on bounds for composition of subsampled mechanisms. Its privacy is bounded by that of a pair of product distributions, whose privacy loss is a sum of independent terms and has been analyzed via a variety of concentration results. Yet, existing closed-form bounds either lose a logarithmic factor or require restrictive conditions on the noise scale. Further, while sums of independent random variables are approximately Gaussian, existing CLT-based techniques give an approximation with a large error in $\delta$. We show that the privacy loss of any composition is close to a Gaussian random variable with the same mean and variance as long as each step has a small privacy loss except with small probability. Specifically, the privacy profile is bounded from above and from below by that of the Gaussian, up to an additive error $\beta$ in $\delta$ and a shift of $\varepsilon$ that grows only as $\ln(1/\beta)$. This shift does not depend on the number of steps, and thus the composition typically satisfies Gaussian differential privacy up to lower-order shifts. For $t$ steps of a mechanism with Poisson subsampling at rate $\lambda$, the variance of the Gaussian is essentially $t\lambda^2$ times the $\chi^2$ divergence of its dominating pair. This gives a new simple analysis of subsampled composition. We extend it to random allocation (balls-and-bins sampling). For the Gaussian mechanism with either sampling method at rate $1/t$, we obtain the central limit rate for $\sigma\gtrsim\sqrt{\ln(1/\delta)}/\ln(t)$, whereas the standard R\'enyi-divergence analyses of Poisson subsampling need $\sigma\gtrsim\sqrt{t\ln(1/\delta)}/\ln(t)$. We also show that for bounded privacy losses the approximation explains the high accuracy of the connect-the-dots discretization of privacy loss distributions.
