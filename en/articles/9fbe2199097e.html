Lost in Delegation: Studying the Effectiveness of Task Orchestration in Generative Search

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/15
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16526

arXiv:2611.16526v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: LLMs have quickly evolved from models used in isolation to agentic systems that delegate tasks to external tools and other LLMs to solve complex assignments. This agentic augmentation has led to impressive capability gains. However, when and how delegation improves performance remains poorly understood. For example, it is unclear whether delegation helps when tasks involve post-cutoff information that the orchestrating LLM cannot reliably resolve from its own knowledge. In this work, we use purpose-built datasets with pre- and post-cutoff splits to compare various delegation modes. We find that delegation provides no consistent accuracy improvement over non-delegated setups, with effects varying across models and tasks. When tasks involve knowledge unavailable to the orchestrator, delegation can substantially degrade accuracy, while direct tool use with the same LLM performs much better. Our analysis identifies distorted delegated queries, inefficient tool use, and failures to preserve and use information between agents as recurring failure modes. These findings suggest that effective orchestration requires both well-calibrated delegation decisions and effective exchange of information between agents.
