TACT-Bench: Testing Adaptive Conversational Threats against Tool-Use Agents

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31756

arXiv:2611.31756v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The growing autonomy of AI agents in using external tools and making decisions has created an urgent need for comprehensive safety benchmarks. Existing benchmarks test compromised tool channels or adaptive multi-turn attacks, though they may not capture the unique capabilities of policy-governed tool-use agents, which must resist unauthorized requests without abandoning the legitimate work embedded in the same interaction. To fill this gap, we introduce TACT-Bench, a stateful benchmark built around scenario-specific stories, personas, manipulation tactics, and response-adaptive escalation. Each adversarial task targets an action explicitly prohibited by the policy provided to the assistant, while the policy remains hidden from the attacking user. TACT pairs each adversarial task with at least one benign counterpart, and its adversarial tasks retain authorized ground-truth actions when a compliant resolution remains available. We define these base tasks as TACT-Base. We additionally introduce TACT-TrustPrime, which places an independent benign task before an unrelated attack in the same conversation. We also propose Paired Selective Trustworthiness (PST), which combines explicit safe refusal with demonstrated capability on matched benign tasks, to analyze an agent's overcautiousness and practicality. Averaged across 6 different models as assistants, TACT-Base raises Attack-Induced Failure Rate (AFR) by 17.53% over four compatible attack baselines, and TACT-TrustPrime raises it by a further 17.01%. The two evaluated defense methods reduce AFR by only 8.35%--9.41% on average and do not consistently improve PST, underscoring the need for stronger policy-enforcement methods.
