EgoLoAF: Learning Humanoid Loco-Manipulation from Egocentric Demonstrations with Hand-Object Affordances

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/26
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32260

arXiv:2611.32260v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Training humanoid vision-language-action (VLA) models relies heavily on costly real-robot teleoperation. Recent efforts convert egocentric human demonstrations into humanoid training data. However, most methods still require wearable motion trackers and leave important hand-object interaction cues implicit. Many also mix in real-robot teleoperation data for VLA post-training. These dependencies limit scalable data collection, while implicit interaction cues make precise contact harder to learn. To overcome these limitations, we propose EgoLoAF, the first paradigm to derive all training data for humanoid loco-manipulation directly from egocentric demonstrations without wearable motion trackers. We post-train the VLA on the converted data alone, without real-robot teleoperation demonstrations, and use explicit hand-object interaction cues to guide action generation. Specifically, to obtain humanoid action targets, we estimate torso motion from recorded camera poses and reconstruct both hand trajectories from video. From the same recording, we use each hand's current pose relative to the object to represent hand-object affordances. During post-training, we inject these affordances into the action expert through cross-attention while keeping the vision-language backbone frozen. Additionally, we release 128,400 egocentric demonstration episodes spanning 1,000 hours and 1,284 tasks, together with converted humanoid action trajectories and rendered robot-view observations. Across four real-world tasks, EgoLoAF nearly achieves teleoperation performance (73.1 vs. 74.4 in mean Task Progress Score), while achieving a mean 4.03 times improvement in data-collection efficiency.
