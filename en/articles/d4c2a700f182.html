Acoustic and Lexical Signals for Native Language Identification of Language Learners

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/10/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32382

arXiv:2610.32382v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Spoken native language (L1) identification for second language learners has traditionally relied on acoustic attributes of speech. Comparatively little attention has been paid to analysing speech transcripts for L1 traces. In this paper, we present a study of L1 identification using both speech and corresponding transcripts from a spoken learner English corpus. We compare a WavLM-based audio L1 classifier with a zero-shot GPT-4o classifier operating on transcripts and analyse their predictions across proficiency levels using a CEFR-balanced evaluation set. Our experiments show that the audio classifier outperforms the transcript-based approach overall when full transcripts are available, although their relative strengths vary with learner proficiency. Audio performs better at A1, whereas text performs better from A2 to C1 with full transcripts. Transcript-based accuracy generally increases with proficiency, while acoustic performance remains comparatively stable. Restricting transcript length substantially reduces text-based accuracy, whereas audio is less sensitive to input length and performs relatively well with limited input. These findings provide insights into L1 identification from acoustic and textual representations of learner speech and how performance varies with proficiency and the amount of available language material.
