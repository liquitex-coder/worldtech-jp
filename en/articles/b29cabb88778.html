Large Language Model-Empowered Intelligent Agent for Adaptive Fast-Charging Control of Lithium-Ion Batteries

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18140

arXiv:2611.18140v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Fast charging of lithium-ion batteries is a multi-objective control problem that requires reducing charging time and sustaining high energy throughput while suppressing accelerated degradation and safety-critical electrochemical reactions. Conventional protocols are easy to implement but have limited ability to adapt to variations in battery chemistry, operating conditions, and aging state. Model-based optimization and conventional data-driven control often require accurate models, costly iterative search, or task-specific policies. We develop a large language model (LLM)-empowered intelligent agent for adaptive battery fast-charging control. In this closed-loop framework, the agent interprets structured battery states, generates executable charging actions, observes the resulting battery response, and continuously adjusts subsequent decisions. We construct a large-scale simulation corpus with PyBaMM across five battery chemistries and 11 parameter sets and convert the resulting state-action-response trajectories into structured process data for agent training. Our two-stage strategy combines supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). SFT establishes battery-control semantics and feasible decision priors from simulation-derived demonstrations. GRPO aligns the agent with a multi-objective control objective covering energy throughput, degradation mitigation, and operational constraints. Closed-loop simulations show that, under matched energy-throughput requirements, the agent achieves longer cycling life than conventional charging baselines and optimization-based strategies across heterogeneous battery configurations. This study provides a simulation-grounded proof of concept for using LLM agents as sequential decision makers in battery management and takes a step toward language-model-enabled control of physical energy systems.
