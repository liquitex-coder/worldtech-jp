A Capability Framework for Evaluating Agentic AI Platforms in Regulated Enterprises

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/21
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24039

arXiv:2611.24039v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Agentic AI platforms-systems in which LLM agents plan, invoke tools, retain memory and coordinate to change the state of enterprise systems-are being procured faster than they can be measured. Public agent benchmarks score models in fixed harnesses; enterprises buy integrated platforms whose orchestration, memory, governance and tooling layers dominate outcomes, and they buy them largely on the strength of vendor demonstrations. We report an industrial evaluation programme ("bakeoff") run by the innovation laboratory of a global bank in which ten platforms-spanning open-source frameworks, proprietary products and hyperscaler ecosystems-were assessed by a cross-functional panel on nine capability dimensions across three financial-services workloads: counterparty credit risk, retrieval-augmented generation and process automation. Scores separate the field into three tiers that are stable under alternative weightings, and surface workload-specific strengths that aggregates hide. A secondary analysis, however, finds Cronbach's alpha = 0.98 across nominally distinct dimensions and rank correlations >= 0.93 with the total for eight of nine: a halo effect indicating that demonstration-based scoring measures presentation as much as capability. From these lessons we propose a gate-then-score evaluation protocol with four layers (capability, execution reliability, governance gates, business value), trace-level reliability metrics estimated over repeated runs, an autonomy-readiness scale and a reproducibility protocol. We state precisely what the present evidence supports, and what a controlled harness must add to turn enterprise platform evaluation from structured judgement into measurement.
