Make the Adversary Spend More: Stateful Deception as a Cost-Exchange Primitive for AI-Era Cyber Defense

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34770

arXiv:2611.34770v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Large language models (LLMs) are transforming the economics of intrusion by reducing the attacker's cost to scan and adapt, while tempting defenders into unbounded compute. We argue that cyber deception should be judged by the cost exchange it imposes, not by realism or detection, and that the primitive making that exchange favorable is adversary-specific persistent state. Rather than a honeypot that resets or merges sessions, each recognized adversary re-enters the world it believes it has compromised, kept coherent with its own past actions and isolated from every other adversary, while the language model acts only as a constrained renderer of explicit state, never as its store. We formalize the trade-off as a Deception Exchange Rate (DER), relating mission-weighted adversary effort burned to defender resources spent, and instantiate it in an SSH honeypot pairing Cowrie with a local LLM. In this paper, we focus on the defender-side evaluation of that exchange, while specifying, but not yet measuring, per-adversary state and the adversary side of the DER. Across five open-weight models, deterministic-layer and prompt optimizations cut per-session defender cost by around 63% against a naive all-LLM decoy.
