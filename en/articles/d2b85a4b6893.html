Unimodal Shortcuts and Joint Scene-Intent Dependence in Vision-Language Safety Representations

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/24
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27339

arXiv:2611.27339v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: The safety of a request to a vision-language model often depends on the image: asking how to serve a dish is harmless for fresh food and harmful for spoiled food. High accuracy on situational safety benchmarks does not tell us whether a model's safety representation encodes both the scene and the user's intent, because a benchmark can often be solved from one modality alone. Studying frozen vision-language safety representations, we audit five public situational benchmarks and find that each admits a unimodal shortcut: images alone predict the labels, and on same-image prompt pairs a probe that never sees the photograph reaches 0.9645 AUROC. We therefore build a controlled evaluation that crosses safe and hazardous scenes with observation and action requests, so that no single-modality rule can exceed 0.500 group-ranking accuracy. On this control, linear probes on intermediate states of Qwen3-VL-8B and SmolVLM2-2.2B reach 0.958 and 0.930, showing that the scene-intent conjunction is linearly decodable; concatenating separately encoded image and text states already recovers most of this signal, so decodability is not evidence of a fusion mechanism. The same probes screen cheaply but generalize poorly to unseen images and other benchmarks. Reliable multimodal safety evaluation therefore needs controls that rule out unimodal shortcuts before accuracy is read as evidence of joint understanding.
