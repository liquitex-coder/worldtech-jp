LLM GerryMandering: Separating Interactions from Additive Effects in LLM Audits

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/24
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26538

arXiv:2611.26538v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Intersectional bias arises when a model treats people who hold several marginalised identities at once differently from what their separate identities would predict. Auditing such intersectional subgroups remains a challenge: their number grows quadratically, and there is no agreed baseline for what independent attributes would predict. Without such a baseline, many existing audits flag a subgroup when it exceeds each attribute alone, a condition that attributes acting independently already satisfy. We test this standard criterion on 150 synthetic datasets built with no interaction. With the shuffle null, it flags 66.1 false subgroups per dataset on average, and at least one in every dataset. When we plant interactions of known size into these datasets, a repaired null lets the criterion recover only 32% of them. We introduce ADDIS (Additive Intersectional Screen), which fits an additive model over all attributes, scores each subgroup by its Additive Residual Distance (ARD) from that model's prediction, and tests it against a Freedman-Lane null. On our synthetic benchmark, ADDIS flags 0.09 false subgroups per dataset and recovers 84% of the same planted interactions. We then build IndiPersona, 10,000 synthetic Indian personas with six identity attributes, and audit 13 open language models. The standard criterion flags 7.1% of the 738 tested subgroups on average; ADDIS flags 3.0%. Gender identity crossed with sexual orientation is the largest pairing in every model, and counterfactual personas confirm it and additionally reveal a caste x class interaction in all but one model that the observational test lacked the data to detect. Much of what audits report as intersectional bias can thus arise from independent single-attribute effects alone, yet a small core of interactions survives.
