Learning Martial Arts Motion Skills for Animating Opponents in VR Sparring Games

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/20
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23825

arXiv:2611.23825v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Animating virtual opponents is an important yet challenging task in game development. In martial arts games, opponent motions must not only be physically plausible but also exhibit appropriate technique, timing, transitions, and target-directed intent. Traditional game animations often rely on handcrafted clips, state machine transitions, and procedural adjustments, which can be labor-intensive and may limit the behavioral diversity of non-player characters. This paper presents a martial arts motion skills learning framework for character animation in virtual sparring games. Inspired by Tang Soo Do, we collected a martial arts motion capture dataset containing isolated attacking and defending techniques, technique combinations and transitions, and contextual sparring variations. We first pretrained a reusable repertoire of martial arts motion skills from the captured data using reinforcement learning (RL) with adversarial style rewards, and then adapted these skills with task-specific rewards for target-directed striking and threat-aware evasion. We evaluated the motions generated by the learned policy through a user study, in which participants assessed their physical plausibility, smoothness, and martial arts form. To demonstrate their applicability to interactive gameplay, we developed a VR sparring prototype for Meta Quest headsets. The prototype combines policy-generated motion clips with lightweight state-based control to support interactive opponent behavior. Together, these findings support the feasibility of using a learned martial arts skill repertoire as a motion foundation for animating opponents in VR sparring games.
