FPTR: Feasibility-Preserving Transactional Refinement for Deadline-Constrained Joint Beam and Resource Allocation

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/06
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06491

arXiv:2611.06491v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Short-interval wireless schedulers must return a complete, feasible beam-and-resource allocation before each execution deadline, and a late or invalid output gives no utility in the current control cycle. Searching longer can increase transmitted traffic, but it may also miss the deadline or leave only an infeasible intermediate solution. We propose Feasibility-Preserving Transactional Refinement (FPTR), which always keeps a validated feasible allocation, the incumbent. Each refinement stage builds a separate candidate and leaves the incumbent unchanged. A candidate replaces the incumbent only if it is complete, passes the feasibility validator before its stage cutoff, and strictly improves the objective; otherwise it is discarded. Four refinement stages use this rule: buffer-aware resource selection, compatibility-group (CG) user sharing, beam-mask rebuilding from remaining demand, and joint reassignment of two resources. On 150 fixed synthetic instances, the full pipeline (Full) raises traffic by 3.83% on average over a lightweight beam-first reference and by 1.79% over its Base constructor. In a separate suite with matched inputs, Full exceeds six independent search baselines (ALNS, tabu search, GA, simulated annealing, iterated local search, and GRASP) by 6.68-8.35%, where outputs later than 100 ms receive zero utility. All 750 Full runs in the internal suite returned feasible allocations, and none exceeded 100 ms. Separating mandatory output construction from optional refinement lets spare computation time improve the allocation without endangering the incumbent. The open-source implementation supports independent reproduction.
