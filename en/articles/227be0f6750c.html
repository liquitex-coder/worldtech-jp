DECODE: Diagnosing Interpretability Failures in Automated Interpretation Process

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17856

arXiv:2611.17856v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Tools for interpreting the internal representations of Large Language Models (LLMs) increasingly rely on LLMs themselves to both generate and evaluate explanations, creating a self-referential automated interpretation process with minimal human oversight. This automation raises significant reliability concerns, which are exacerbated by the absence of ground truth. In practice, it is difficult to validate explanations or to attribute interpretability failures to their source -- whether they originate from the tool's extracted representations or from the LLM used in the process. To address these challenges, we present DECODE, a visual analytics system that reintroduces human oversight into the automated interpretation process. Our approach decomposes the diagnostic workflow into three progressive stages, isolating failure sources ranging from structural defects to explanation-level misalignments. Drawing inspiration from inter-coder consensus in qualitative analysis, we utilize consensus among independently generated explanations and their evaluation metrics as a proxy for reliability. To further scale human inspection, we employ a three-phase active seeding protocol that strategically recommends high-priority instances for manual review. Finally, we demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach through a case study on 16,384 Sparse Autoencoder features, showing that it can efficiently identify and attribute interpretability failures at scale.
