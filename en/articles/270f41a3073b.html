Multi-CONDOR: Learning Multistable Motion Primitives from Demonstration

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/19
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21059

arXiv:2611.21059v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Dynamical-system-based motion policies provide a framework for learning reactive robot motions with stability and convergence properties. Many manipulation tasks, however, admit several distinct goals or a continuous set of acceptable outcomes, requiring motion primitives that preserve these alternatives. Accommodating both goal structures within a unified framework with continuous, autonomous dynamics remains an open challenge. We propose a CONDOR-based framework, multi-CONDOR, for learning motion primitives with either multiple discrete attractors or a continuous attracting goal contour. The approach combines imitation learning with losses that encourage agreement between the learned task dynamics and latent reference systems encoding the desired attraction structure. We provide a Lyapunov-based analysis of the convergence properties of the learned motion dynamics. We evaluate the framework on simulated benchmarks with discrete goals, qualitative examples with continuous goal contours, and robotic grasping experiments. In simulation, multi-CONDOR reduces trajectory RMSE by approximately 72% compared with separately learned single-goal motion primitives selected by a state machine. For tasks with two and three goals, respectively, it reduces the fraction of evaluated initial states ending outside the goal tolerance from StableNODE's 0.31% and 7.29% to 0% in both settings. Experiments with a Franka Emika robot arm demonstrate successful grasping of a container by either of its two handles using motions learned from demonstrations. Together, these results show multi-CONDOR combines motion reproduction with the flexibility to converge to alternative valid outcomes.
