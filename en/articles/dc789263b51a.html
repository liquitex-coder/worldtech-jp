The Date You Didn't Set: The System-Prompt Date as a Hidden Variable in LLM Evaluation

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31254

arXiv:2611.31254v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: The system prompt of many instruction-tuned models carries a date, usually today's, filled in automatically for conversational and agentic requests alike, so an unchanged evaluation can send a different prompt each day. In this paper, we explore how much this hidden input changes evaluation outcomes. Across six instruction-tuned models and four benchmarks, with prompts, decoding, and configuration fixed, every model is affected: two plausible dates (2024--2026) switch a median $6.6\%$ of test items between correct and incorrect. Even rerunning the evaluation one day later flips about as many items as changing the batch size, although nothing in the setup was changed. In addition, using a grammar-constrained variant of Greedy Coordinate Gradient (GCG) to search dates of any year, we find dates that cost the Llama-3.x models up to $36.5$ points of held-out accuracy. Beyond accuracy, switching to another plausible date alters LLM-judge verdicts and undoes $15$--$32\%$ of reproduced jailbreak successes on Llama-3.2-3B. Yet none of the eleven evaluation stacks we audit pins the date. With the date and the rest of the setup fixed, reruns reproduce byte-for-byte; we therefore recommend pinning, logging, and reporting the date.
