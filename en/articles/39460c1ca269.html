Astrocyte Biocomputing: Astrocytic Contributions to Neural Network Computation

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17532

arXiv:2611.17532v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Conductors are critical for the performance of an orchestra even though they do not emit sound. Basic research and clinical studies in the last decade are revealing that astrocytes may be the "conductors" of the "orchestra" that is the brain network: astrocytes make up approximately $50\%$ of all brain cells but do not emit electrical signals. Nevertheless, they may play critical roles in important cognitive brain functions, specifically learning, memory, and information processing. Here we consider the implications of these discoveries for brain-inspired machine learning (ML), where analogy is typically drawn between neurons and their electrical interactions. We highlight specific algorithms for two key astrocyte characteristics. The first characteristic is the ability to link to and modulate thousands of synapses simultaneously. A model based on this characteristic yields faster training and makes the network more robust to noise, providing a supervised learning algorithm that can be applied to any existing neural network. The second characteristic we focus on is that the non-electrical communication among astrocytes includes slow, rhythmic dynamics. The rhythmic sharing algorithm we developed based on this premise yields rapid anomaly detection adaptation and prevents catastrophic forgetting. Overall, we find that astrocyte-derived algorithms outperform neurocentric algorithms significantly and remediate critical weaknesses of current state-of-the-art ML models.
