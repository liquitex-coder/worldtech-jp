AI-Voyager: A Public-Interest System for Human-AI Civic Reasoning to Contest and Shape the Rules That Govern AI

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17564

arXiv:2611.17564v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Governments are writing rules for artificial intelligence that decide what counts as an automated decision or a harm, often before the people they affect have a chance to question them. We introduce AI-Voyager, a public-interest system for human-AI civic reasoning, in which people and AI work together to understand, contest and shape the rules that govern AI. A resident can find their city's rules on AI in hiring or upload their country's AI strategy, read it as an affected worker would, open the passage behind any answer, and draft a revision. Its architecture turns 3,653 governance documents from city, state, federal and national governments into passages that keep their page and edition, and retrieves evidence through bounded agentic search that verifies each statement. On 360 new questions that require finding the relevant documents, it reached 83.0% macro recall, against 80.5% for the strongest baseline, 73.7% without iterative search and 99.1% on a benchmark with fixed documents. In a study with 24 participants new to two AI bills, most proposed changes asked how provisions would apply. Participants chose lenses for the kind of explanation they wanted, and citations signaled grounding but rarely led them to check the source. By binding AI assistance to the exact text of public documents, this work introduces a grammar of operations and a design space that inform how such systems are designed and evaluated, and how people take part in governing AI.
