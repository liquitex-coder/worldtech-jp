Gaze World Model

arXiv:2611.31836v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Where a person looks indicates their focus of attention, and how their gaze shifts between targets reveals how that attention responds to a changing scene. Existing gaze target estimation methods largely operate frame by frame and do not model these shifts. Gaze shifts are often driven by dynamic changes in the scene, which is the kind of information world models are designed to encode. This relationship is subtle, however, since an event far across the frame may produce only a slight movement of the person's head or eyes. We therefore introduce the Gaze World Model (GWM), which co-trains latent scene prediction with person-conditioned gaze supervision on a frozen V-JEPA 2.1 encoder; in our ablations, the latent prediction objective improves both shift detection and gaze localization. Given a video clip and the person's head box, GWM estimates gaze locations and detects gaze shifts in the observed clip, and forecasts future gaze and shifts from predicted scene features. To train and evaluate these tasks, we augment VideoAttentionTarget and ChildPlay with event-level gaze-shift annotations. GWM achieves state-of-the-art shift detection on these benchmarks, about 10 points higher in four-class AP than the strongest baseline, while remaining comparable to the strongest image-based models in gaze target estimation, and outperforms the compared baselines on future gaze localization and shift forecasting.
