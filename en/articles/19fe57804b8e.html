Semantic Prism: Generative Segmentation with an Explicit Semantic-Image Interface

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11537

arXiv:2608.11537v3 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Conditional image generators can render segmentation maps as semantic RGB images, but color drift and boundary mixing can undermine direct decoding. We present Semantic Prism, a framework that combines an explicit semantic-image interface with hierarchical refinement for road-scene segmentation. A generator adapted from a diffusion-distilled backbone produces a semantic RGB image with a single denoising update per crop. Distances to a fixed class-color codebook define interface logits and pixelwise class probabilities. Hierarchical Generator Evidence Alignment (HGEA) combines spatially aligned multilevel features from the VAE encoder of the generator with the input image, generated semantic image, and interface probabilities to predict additive corrections to these logits. Contextual Interface Hierarchy Disagreement (C-IHD) combines pointwise uncertainty, local context, and interface-refinement disagreement in a fixed score for ranking pixel errors, without an auxiliary predictor or an additional network pass. On the 500-image Cityscapes validation set, Semantic Prism achieves 72.07% mean mIoU, improving on direct-interface decoding by 11.39 percentage points. Extensive capacity-matched ablations show higher mean mIoU for multilevel refinement than for flat and single-level alternatives. The Cityscapes-trained model transfers to ACDC Dataset, achieving 46.89% mIoU without target-domain adaptation. Beyond Cityscapes, on BDD100K dataset, a separately trained model achieves 62.22% mIoU on its full 1,000-image validation set. Across the 3 evaluated datasets, C-IHD improves pixel-error area under the AUPR over uncertainty derived from maximum softmax probability, with segmentation predictions held fixed for each comparison. In particular, on ACDC, AUPR increases from 0.6580 to 0.7557, demonstrating that Semantic Prism presents more effective error ranking under adverse-condition domain shift.
