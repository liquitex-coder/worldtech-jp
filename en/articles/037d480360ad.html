Lifelong Learning of Scheduling Rules with Surrogate-assisted Genetic Programming for Dynamic Flexible Job Shop Scheduling

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/04
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41158

arXiv:2610.41158v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Genetic programming (GP) has been widely used to learn scheduling rules for dynamic flexible job shop scheduling (DFJSS). However, trained rules tend to only generalise to tasks of similar scales and distributions, and when task characteristics change, learned rules often suffer significant performance degradation. Moreover, fine-tuning pre-trained rules on new tasks often leads to catastrophic forgetting, where prior knowledge is lost. This motivates the need for GP methods that can adapt to new tasks while preserving past knowledge, leading to a lifelong learning paradigm. However, lifelong learning in GP for DFJSS remains largely under-explored. To address this gap, this paper proposes a surrogate-assisted GP method for lifelong learning to handle sequential heterogeneous DFJSS tasks with job or batch arrivals. The framework includes three key components: an adaptive two-stage training strategy to balance plasticity and stability, a task-similarity-based weighting mechanism to regulate knowledge reuse, and an improved surrogate model with distance constraints to enhance estimation accuracy. Experimental results show that the proposed method effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting while maintaining strong adaptability, outperforming several compared methods and demonstrating the potential of lifelong GP in dynamic scheduling environments.
