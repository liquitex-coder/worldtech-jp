From Questions to Decisions: Benchmarking How LLM Agents Acquire Decision-Relevant Information from Human Beings

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/12
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13015

arXiv:2611.13015v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: LLM agents are increasingly asked to make plans for people, such as scheduling drivers or assigning nurses. However, people's requests are often incomplete, and the supporting information for decision-making may be outdated, requiring agents to ask for clarification. Existing benchmarks typically provide fully specified problems or rely on uncalibrated LLM-simulated users, leaving this ability insufficiently tested. We introduce Ask2Decide, a benchmark of 43 planning tasks that evaluates whether agents ask for missing information that matters for their decisions. An operator holds facts absent from the agent's request and records, and optimization solvers determine which facts change the optimal plan. We also include similarly worded facts that leave the optimal plan unchanged to test whether agents distinguish useful information from irrelevant details. We collect 79 interaction sessions from 19 participants and find that, after an incorrect plan, human replies omit the correcting fact far more often than replies from an uncalibrated simulated user (43% versus 7%). Using these interactions, we calibrate a simulated operator that more closely matches human reply patterns. Evaluating four frontier LLMs, we find that their initial plans are infeasible on 58-98% of tasks when information is missing, compared with 5-28% when all facts are provided upfront. Agents uncover facts that change the optimal plan only slightly more often than facts that leave it unchanged (39-52% versus 32-41%). These results show that obtaining decision-relevant information through interaction remains a major bottleneck for LLM planning. This is ongoing work and will be updated.
