D'BEAST: Logged Bad Experiences as Explanation for Autonomous Driving Agents

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/19
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20019

arXiv:2611.20019v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Reinforcement learning agents learn from mistakes, yet their decisions can be difficult to trace back to those experiences. We investigate whether a tabular agent's decisions can be explained by its bad experiences, defined as the training transitions associated with safety or task events. A fixed training log is replayed from the same initial Q-table under three conditions. FULL retains all updates, SKIP omits bad-experience updates, and NEUTRALIZE retains those updates without their event penalties. We illustrate the method on six classic Markov decision processes and evaluate it in closed-loop driving in CARLA. On the full driving task, FULL reached the goal from 33 of 40 test starts, whereas SKIP and NEUTRALIZE reached it from none, indicating that the bad experiences carry decision-relevant information. At the local level, for 47 decisions shortly before a failure of SKIP or NEUTRALIZE, removing or neutralizing a small group of bad experiences, with a median size of one, caused the trained agent to select the failing policy's action in 46 cases. For this agent, the results indicate that its decisions can be explained by its bad experiences.
