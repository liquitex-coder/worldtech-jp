LeanWAM: Learning Latent World Action Models from Frozen Video Models

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/26
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31740

arXiv:2611.31740v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: World action models (WAMs) typically adapt large pretrained video generators to jointly predict future observations and robot actions, incurring substantial training and deployment costs. However, it remains unclear which future representations a policy actually needs and how compactly they can be represented. Our probing study shows that complementary future representations can be compressed into a few tokens while retaining most of their action-decoding performance. Motivated by this finding, we introduce LeanWAM, a latent WAM that transfers video priors from a frozen video generator to a lightweight policy via two-stage training. In the first stage, a Future Extractor compresses observed transitions into 32 predictive tokens. These tokens condition the frozen generator and are jointly shaped by action, RGB, semantic, and geometric supervision, while a compact readout of the generator's hidden states supplies complementary world tokens. The second stage distills both representations into the policy, which learns to predict them from current observations while generating actions. With 1.4B parameters and no large-scale embodied pretraining, LeanWAM outperforms strong vision-language-action (VLA) and WAM baselines on LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, and RoboTwin~2.0. It predicts an action chunk in 25.1\,ms on an A100 GPU, 12.3--41.5$\times$ faster than representative WAMs. These results demonstrate that compact predictive supervision can transfer video priors to robust and efficient control.
