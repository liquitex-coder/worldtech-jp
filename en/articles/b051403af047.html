From Letter-of-Credit Requirements to Executable Specifications: Evaluating Verification Accuracy and Completeness

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/24
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26988

arXiv:2611.26988v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: In letter-of-credit (LC) document examination, a correct final decision does not show that every requirement was examined. We therefore evaluate examination requirement by requirement and make each requirement executable. A language model converts the bill of lading (BL) and commercial invoice (IV) requirements of a credit into atomic conditions and logical structures, and a deterministic verifier retrieves the named evidence and computes each outcome, so every check can be compared with a gold encoding and traced to logged evidence. Because LC records are confidential, our on-premises 9B parser is fine-tuned only on synthetic pairs built from templates and human-reviewed public document values. On 345 real LC cases, final-decision accuracy proves misleading: a constant PASS rule has the highest raw accuracy while examining nothing. Although it never sees a real record during training, the fine-tuned parser detects 93% of the atomic requirements its ontology covers and reconstructs 86% of the covered requirements exactly. It verifies 82% of the evaluable requirements correctly, against 54% for a tool-using agent, and leads the agent on both satisfied requirements and discrepancies. It also completely examines 1.8 times as many cases as few-shot prompting of the same model, while direct prediction and the agent complete none. Executing the gold encodings with the same verifier shows that some of the remaining errors, missed discrepancies included, come from the operators and normalization, which a better parser alone cannot fix. Every rejection comes with a trace of the units and evidence behind it, which suits examiner-in-the-loop review.
