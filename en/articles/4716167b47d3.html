Event-SAMA: Language-Grounded Video Object Segmentation on Neuromorphic Event Streams

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/01
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39676

arXiv:2610.39676v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Event cameras offer microsecond temporal resolution, high dynamic range and no motion blur, but referring video object segmentation (RVOS) lacks event-stream datasets and models. We introduce Event-SAMA-239K, a synthetic-event conversion of the SAMA-239K grounded video-chat corpus and all additional SAMA training data: 30,650 video clips and 392,162 images. Optical-flow, FILM or saccade upsampling followed by a linear event generator preserves all mask, referring-expression and dialogue annotations. We also introduce Event-SAMA, adapting SAMA-4B with a two-channel polarity patch embedding initialised from pretrained RGB weights and a zero-initialised event adapter. End-to-end training yields 24.37 $\mathcal{J}\&\mathcal{F}$ on MeViS valid_u and 22.33 $\mathcal{J}\&\mathcal{F}$ on the converted ReVOS valid subset, establishing the first event-native RVOS baseline (RGB SAMA-4B: 55.4 on MeViS). Frozen RGB SAMA-4B with pseudo-RGB renderings or E2VID reconstructions of the same events scores 13.23 and 15.94 $\mathcal{J}\&\mathcal{F}$. Without retraining, Event-SAMA works on real event-camera recordings when the accumulation window matches their event density. A seed-controlled ablation with a measured noise floor of 0.47 $\mathcal{J}\&\mathcal{F}$ finds gains from diverse training sources over MeViS alone (+4.99), RGB-mean event-stem initialisation (+4.33), and a trainable SAM2 mask decoder (+2.76); the event adapter, partial ViT unfreezing and event polarity are within noise at this budget. An earlier, partially converted corpus snapshot trained with the identical recipe scores 1.38 $\mathcal{J}\&\mathcal{F}$ higher on MeViS and 1.12 higher on ReVOS. Both are single runs that also differ in schedule length, so we draw no conclusion about corpus scale.
