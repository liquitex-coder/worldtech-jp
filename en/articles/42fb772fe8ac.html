AhaBench: Do Agents Turn Experience into Reusable Insights? A Long-Horizon Benchmark for Continual Learning

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/24
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05435

arXiv:2609.05435v3 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Solving a task, even from a worked example, does not make an agent any better at the next one. As agents move from single tasks to long collaborations with people, turning experience into reusable insight becomes the capability that separates a tool from a collaborator that improves over time. AhaBench is a benchmark for this kind of continual learning. It simulates three patterns of long human-AI collaboration and holds a later test fixed while varying the experience before it: in Aha-Puzzle, the agent questions a partner who knows the answer to a new mystery after solving earlier ones; in Aha-Euler, a person teaches a computation and the agent applies it to new inputs; in Aha-Vending, the agent runs a delegated business for a simulated year. The learning mechanism is left open, so memories, skill libraries, and weight updates can be compared under common rules against a baseline of fixed-weight agents that see their experience in context. Our results show that today's frontier agents are not yet continual learners. Earlier Aha-Puzzle solutions help some models, most clearly Qwen 3.6 Plus, which gains +12.57 points over attempts on the same stories without earlier episodes, yet every model scores far below what it reaches with hints. On single-step Aha-Euler tasks, the same model solves 94.0% of requested held-out inputs when taught with an explanation and code but 6.0% when given only the question and answer, counting unanswered attempts as incorrect. In incident-free Aha-Vending operation, Doubao Seed 2.0 Pro ends the year at +495 USD, yet its profit averaged over the year is -10 USD because it recovers late. Exploring without help, reconstructing a method, and sustaining productive operation remain open problems, and AhaBench provides the paired tests a learning method must pass to solve them.
