Visual Similarity in Adult Word Extension and Artificial Word Learning

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14116

arXiv:2611.14116v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: When two objects share a name, how much does their visual similarity explain, and what do the teaching examples add? We studied word extension across ten visual representations: eight trained encoders, a randomly initialized network, and raw pixels. Reanalyzing trial-level data from a large adult word-extension study (Lewis & Frank, 2018; 520 people), we computed each test image's distance to the teaching examples. Within the taught domain, greater distance was associated with less extension for all eight trained encoders and for the untrained network, so the association cannot be attributed only to what the encoders learned; raw pixels showed no negative association. In post hoc models that let the distance slope vary across images, seven of the eight trained encoders retained a negative estimate and five had 95% intervals entirely below zero; the interval of the reference encoder included zero. Whether trial order changes the distance slope, the frozen primary question, remained inconclusive. Example composition changed extension at comparable distances: after three examples, adults extended a word to 1.1% of superordinate items when the examples were subordinate and to 77.5% when they were superordinate. In artificial learners, words joining more distant categories were learned less accurately for six of the eight trained encoders. Every trained encoder correlated with human similarity measures from three datasets, and so did the untrained network, although far more weakly than the trained encoders other than MAE on two of them. In an exploratory comparison on the same images, no common threshold on any trained encoder reproduced the adults' narrow-to-broad gain (at most 66.67 vs. 76.43 percentage points). Cosine distance to the centroid of the teaching examples predicts part of word extension, but does not capture the effect of example composition.
