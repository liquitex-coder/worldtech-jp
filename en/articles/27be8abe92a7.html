An In-Silico Audit of Topographic Organization in a Multimodal Brain-Response Model

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18105

arXiv:2611.18105v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: TRIBE v2 (d'Ascoli et al., 2026) predicts fMRI responses across the cortical surface from video, audio and text. Its authors validated it with category-selective localizers (faces, places, bodies, words) and language contrasts, but not with the classic topographic organization of sensory cortex: retinotopy, tonotopy, and motion selectivity in area MT+. There is a clear architectural reason to expect retinotopy to fail: TRIBE's video features are averaged over space before the response head, which should discard visual-field position. I present mapping stimuli, a fitting pipeline adapted from standard fMRI mapping analyses, and a stand-in model with known ground truth that serves as a positive and negative control, and apply them to TRIBE v2's predictions, comparing the fitted maps with human atlases using spin tests. TRIBE v2 shows a sharp split. There is no contralateral hemifield response and no polar-angle map, robustly across stimulus controls. There is no tonotopic gradient in primary auditory cortex: best frequencies span an interquartile range of only 0.14 octaves across core and belt, and the null persists with loudness-equalized tones. An eccentricity-like gradient appears only when the ring geometry changes, consistent with global image statistics rather than a positional code. In contrast, motion selectivity is strong and precisely localized: 22% of the 1% most motion-selective vertices fall in MT+, which covers 0.42% of cortex. In-silico experiments that depend on feature identity appear viable with TRIBE v2, while those that depend on visual-field or frequency position are not supported by the current model.
