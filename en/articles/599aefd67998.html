Imitation Bootstrapped Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18543

arXiv:2611.18543v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Demonstrations are an effective way to accelerate single-agent reinforcement learning (RL), but extending this idea to multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) is non-trivial as it is difficult and often impossible for a single person to teleoperate multiple agents simultaneously. Round-Robin Behavior Cloning (R2BC) addresses this by letting a single demonstrator control one agent at a time while the others execute their current policies. However, the resulting imitation policies are limited by the quality of the demonstrations. We propose Imitation Bootstrapped Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (IBMARL), a framework that extends Imitation Bootstrapped Reinforcement Learning (IBRL) to cooperative teams and uses R2BC to train the imitation policies. At each time step, every agent receives one action proposal from its imitation policy and one from its RL policy, and an ensemble of centralized critics selects among the per-agent combinations of the two. A gated imitation penalty, a soft Q-filtered behavior cloning loss, regularizes each RL policy toward its imitation policy only where the critics value the imitation action more, so that after training each agent executes its RL policy alone, without the centralized critics. On four cooperative multi-agent RL benchmarks, IBMARL surpasses its imitation policies $2.6$-$4.2\times$ faster than demonstration-based baselines on two tasks, is the only method to surpass it on the other two, and exceeds the demonstrator itself on two of the four. With real human demonstrations IBMARL still surpasses imitation learning alone, and does so more than twice as fast as the best baseline.
