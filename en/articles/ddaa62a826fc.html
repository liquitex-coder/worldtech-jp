Uncertainty-Driven Self-Play for Robust Multi-Agent Driving Policies

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/10/16
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19714

arXiv:2610.19714v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Learning robust driving policies that generalize beyond their training domain remains a central challenge in motion planning. We propose an uncertainty-driven self-play scheme, in which half of the agents are rewarded for increasing their neighbors' epistemic uncertainty, while the other half learn to drive safely as the exploring agents continuously seek out their weaknesses. Epistemic uncertainty indicates where the policy is least confident and serves as an automatic proxy for scenario difficulty. Exploring agents use this signal to seek out hard cases, generating a challenging curriculum without manual scenario design. The resulting policy outperforms standard self-play, achieving best-in-class zero-shot transfer to nuplan Val14 (99.12% vs. 97.97%) and Argoverse 2 (82.39% vs. 78.03%) without fine-tuning, while also improving performance over standard self-play on Waymo. Our diversity-driven approach achieves 85% higher LSTM hidden-state coverage than the baseline, indicating a much richer set of behaviors. Further analysis shows that epistemic uncertainty serves as a calibrated difficulty detector, with highest-uncertain agents exhibiting 14.8 times the combined failure rate across collision, off-road, and DNF, of lowest-uncertain agents, enabling automatic prioritization of challenging scenarios without ground-truth labels.
