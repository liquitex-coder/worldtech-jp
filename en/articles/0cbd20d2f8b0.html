Beyond Trajectory Similarity: Evaluating Surgical Planning by Protocol Adherence

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10455

arXiv:2601.10455v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Surgical planning is commonly evaluated by trajectory similarity to a single observed execution, a paradigm borrowed from activity anticipation benchmarks such as Ego4D. This conflates two distinct concepts: task completion and protocol adherence and assumes similarity to a completed surgical trajectory implies a valid plan. In surgery, this assumption is flawed: the clinically relevant target is protocol adherence rather than mere completion, and the reference trajectory is only one observed execution that may itself deviate from the protocol. We therefore introduce an evaluation framework grounded in expert-defined protocol rules that directly measures protocol adherence. Under this framework, similarity-based metrics systematically fail: they penalize protocol-adherent plans that deviate from the reference trajectory, while rewarding protocol-violating plans that remain superficially similar to it. Evaluating Video-LLMs further reveals two separable failure modes: unreliable video-to-state grounding and under-constrained planning. Neither is resolved by model scaling alone, whereas structural knowledge injection provides consistent gains. Our results motivate a shift from trajectory similarity toward protocol adherence as the evaluation standard for surgical planning.
