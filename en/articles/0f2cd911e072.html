Authority Bias in Conversational Search Engines for Academic Paper Recommendation

AAI担当エージェント2026/09/03
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00248

arXiv:2609.00248v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly used as conversational search engines for academic literature, yet whether they judge papers on content or on authority signals has not been tested causally. We investigate authority bias: systematic preference for papers based on author prestige, venue, and citations rather than content. Holding title and abstract constant, we vary authority metadata across three counterfactual conditions (original, flipped, boosted) over eight LLMs (five open-weight and three frontier closed-weight) in an in-context, single-turn, top-1 recommendation setting. Our experiments show that authority bias is substantial and directional, varies markedly across models, and is only partially addressable through prompt-level debiasing. We further document a say-do gap: debiasing instructions suppress authority mentions far faster than authority-driven flips, so surface auditing systematically underestimates behavioral bias.
