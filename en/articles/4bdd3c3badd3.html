Safe Personal RSI: Evaluating and Repairing Personal-Agent Updates

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/21
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23651

arXiv:2611.23651v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Kicker is a personal health assistant. Changes to an assistant's reusable instructions can improve task handling while changing what it discloses, which requests it follows, and how it reports actions. We study Safe Personal RSI, an update-evaluation component developed for Kicker. We restrict recursive self-improvement (RSI) to prompt updates, with model weights and runtime code fixed. The procedure tests proposed and current instructions, repeats failing cases, temporarily undoes changes to guide repairs, and checks safety and task performance before adoption. If no version passes, the current instructions stay in use. Our executable benchmark contains 29 email, calendar, health, and marketplace cases, with separate probes for authorization reuse, memory, and tool-output injection. Across three models and two deliberately risky edits, the procedure rejects 20 of 21 update attempts. In repair confirmation, an admitted GPT-5.6 Luna repair reduces attack violations from 10.7% to 0% and increases safe task completion from 89.7% to 100%; a broad handwritten authority rule matches these outcomes. Under stronger attacks, a repair transferred from Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct to Qwen3-14B reduces violations from 69.0% to 17.9% and increases safe task completion from 19.5% to 64.4%. Separate runtime probes examine memory-entry deletion and identify missing local OAuth scope checks in the evaluated snapshot.
