KiRA: Kronecker Input-Gated Rank Adaptation for Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning of Pretrained Models

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33236

arXiv:2611.33236v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Full fine-tuning of large pretrained models is expensive, and parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) is the standard alternative. Most PEFT methods use low-rank updates, whose rank lies far below that of full fine-tuning and is the same for every input. Input-adaptive variants vary the capacity per input but remain within the low-rank bottleneck, and whether a task benefits from input conditioning is not known before training. This paper introduces Kronecker input-gated rank adaptation (KiRA). Instead of the conventional low-rank product, the update $\Delta W$ is the Kronecker product of a frozen random $p\times p$ matrix $A$ and a trainable factor $M(x)$, which overcomes the rank ceiling: because rank is multiplicative under this product, $A$ multiplies the rank of $M(x)$ by $p$ without adding trainable parameters. The factor $M(x)$ is further decomposed as $B_1\,\mathrm{diag}(g(x))\,B_2$, where the input gate $g(x)$, computed once per sample by a network shared across all adapted layers, adjusts the rank of the update to each input in steps of $p$. Where input conditioning is unnecessary, the gate is folded into $B_1$ at deployment. Because the gate changes the rank only in discrete steps, the benefit of input conditioning can be predicted before training from a few hundred labeled inputs. Across six medical imaging and language benchmarks, KiRA attains the highest mean score against five published baselines with 8.7--9.7 times fewer trainable parameters than low-rank adaptation, and the pre-training criterion agrees with the measured benefit of input conditioning on all six tasks.
