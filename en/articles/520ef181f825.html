Language Models Make New Research Sound Old

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/21
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23546

arXiv:2611.23546v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Researchers are increasingly using large language models (LLMs) to validate research questions. We find that LLMs make open research questions sound partly studied and overstate what related papers establish. Models recognize most of these overstatements when asked to check the papers' full texts separately. On 40 questions drawn from newly accepted papers, GPT-6-Astra correctly judges 98% to be unstudied, but states that status plainly in only 8% of ordinary answers. All six frontier systems show reporting gaps of 30-90 percentage points. Across 22 models given identical papers, higher capability is associated with more accurate judgments and larger reporting gaps. This reporting failure is asymmetric: models correctly identify studied questions in 88-100% of ordinary answers when we supply the answering papers. To correct this, we build a fixed answer from each model's judgment and separate checks of cited papers. This raises Astra's clear reporting of open status from 8% to 95%, with improvements in all six systems. Finally, we predict reviewers' novelty grades by combining several models' written ratings with a rule learned from graded examples, reaching the performance range reported by Think-Probe-Respond. Unlike TPR, our approach needs no access to model weights or internal activations, so it also works with closed-weight frontier models.
