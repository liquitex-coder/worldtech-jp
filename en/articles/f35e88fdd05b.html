On the Origin and Evolution of Training-Inference Mismatch in LLM Reinforcement Learning

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/04
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40945

arXiv:2610.40945v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: On-policy reinforcement learning for large language models assumes that training responses are sampled from the current policy. In practice, an inference engine samples the responses and a separate trainer computes the gradients, and the \textbf{training-inference mismatch} between the two makes the training data off-policy. The mismatch has two sources, the numerics of the engine and the stale weights of asynchronous sampling, and a correction that stabilizes training under one source can fail under the other. \textit{We ask which property of a mismatch source decides the correction it needs.} Evaluating the trainer at the sampler's weights splits each sampled-token error exactly into a \textit{numerical discrepancy} of the engine and a \textit{policy lag} of the trainer, and we follow both through training. The numerical discrepancy stays approximately constant, so it biases the updates by a nearly fixed amount, and centering the score at each prefix removes its leading bias. The policy lag is produced by the trainer's own updates: the bias it leaves enters the next update, which enlarges the next lag and closes a feedback loop. Importance weighting cuts this loop only while the lag ratio stays concentrated, and on Qwen3-1.7B it fails at the step where the effective sample size of the ratio falls below one half. From this account we derive the \textbf{discrepancy-lag correction} (DLC), which centers the numerical discrepancy, clips the lag ratio against the sampler's weights and resynchronizes the sampler when this effective sample size falls below one half. Under a stale sampler, DLC keeps the KL divergence from on-policy training within twice the KL between two independent on-policy trainings on Qwen3-0.6B and Qwen3-1.7B, and its resynchronization prevents collapses that the per-token corrections leave and that a KL measured on the sampler's prefixes does not detect.
