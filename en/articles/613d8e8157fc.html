Statutory Article Retrieval from Arabic Judicial Narratives: Family Law and Law of Evidence in Saudi Arabia

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/01
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38336

arXiv:2610.38336v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Reliable legal information retrieval must ground legal reasoning in authoritative statutory sources. In Arabic judicial settings, long factual narratives must be mapped to concise articles that express abstract legal rules, creating a narrative-to-legal-rule abstraction gap. Using a benchmark of 200 synthetic Saudi Arabic judicial cases and 381 articles from the Family Law and the Law of Evidence, we compare word-level and char-4 BM25, zero-shot dense encoders, per-law retrieval, dense-sparse reciprocal rank fusion (RRF), and a proposed Decompose-and-Refine (DaR) pipeline. In DaR, a large language model splits each narrative into disputed legal issues and generates, for each issue, three retrieval channels: a statute-style paraphrase, legal keywords, and a candidate taxonomy chapter. On the whole corpus, the instructed Qwen3-Embedding-8B dense encoder reaches an MRR@10 of 0.849, the best result of any non-decomposed system. Under per-law scoring, this advantage reflects Family Law only: restricted to Evidence articles, the same encoder drops to an MRR@10 of 0.087, and fusion with BM25 raises it only to 0.207. DaR changes this picture, raising Evidence MRR@10 to 0.696 and Family MRR@10 to 0.864. Without decomposition, the two laws favor different strategies; under DaR, the same retriever achieves the best MRR@10 on both.
