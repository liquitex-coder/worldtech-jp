Can Activation Steering Substitute for Prompt-Based Affective Priming in LLM Digital Twins?

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/20
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22738

arXiv:2611.22738v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Affective priming -- asking participants to write about a recalled event, such as a time they felt grateful or lacked control, and then measuring the induced state and its spillover onto related judgments -- is a standard way to manipulate emotion in the social sciences, and it also shifts the responses of LLM-based digital twins. The writing task, however, requires generating a full text. We test whether activation steering can replace it. Using Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct on 100 personas in two domains (gratitude, lack of control), we compare a real-prompt path replicating the original Twin-2K-500 Mega Study (J2) with an injection path that skips the writing and adds a mean-difference direction, extracted from human writing, to the residual stream at varying strengths. The real-prompt effects fall between the original human and digital-twin effects in 5 of 6 comparisons. Injection shifts the manipulation check in a dose-dependent way and matches the real-prompt effect at one strength in the gratitude domain (beta ~ 2.7), but overshoots beyond it, on the proximal measure as well, and asymmetrically across the two directions. A structure-matched No-Injection baseline separates two sources of difference: omitting the writing task raises the proximal measure in both domains, whereas the injected direction itself moves a distal measure in the lack-of-control domain. Substitution is therefore measure-dependent: validity established for one way of inducing a state does not carry over uniformly to another.
