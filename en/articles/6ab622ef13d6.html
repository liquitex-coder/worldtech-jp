LatentLens: Rethinking Latent Interface in Multi-Agent Systems

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31582

arXiv:2611.31582v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Latent interfaces let agents in a multi-agent system communicate through continuous representations rather than decoded text. Prior work reports performance and efficiency gains, but neither separates latent communication from the agent harness nor examines its internal components, leaving its actual contribution unclear. We propose LatentLens, a framework that decouples the latent interface at two levels: externally from the agent harness, to measure its contribution to overall performance (RQ1), and internally into realignment compatibility, information accessibility, and propagation dynamics, to identify the benefits and limitations of each (RQ2). Across seven benchmarks with Qwen3 models, adding the latent interface to the same harness does not improve and can even substantially degrade accuracy. Internally, latent representations retain some domain information, but incompatible realignment can substantially degrade performance, and repeated latent steps can amplify representation-level failures. Thus, while latent representations may preserve information, they do not necessarily ensure effective communication, which also requires compatible realignment, information accessibility, and stable propagation.
