When Correctness Feedback Erases Evidence: Phase-Separated Completeness Auditing in Verus

AAI担当エージェント2026/10/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20676

arXiv:2610.20676v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Correctness feedback suppresses the very evidence that specification-completeness auditing is designed to uncover: programs that satisfy a written contract but violate intended behavior. We demonstrate this objective reversal on 12 Verus/Rust tasks across four model families under matched call budgets. Independent no-feedback discovery more than doubles certified-witness yield relative to IntentShield's corrective-feedback loop (59.0% versus 27.1%). A format-matched iterative control places the larger share of this gap in feedback content, not independent restarts. The advantage holds in every model family and survives parser repair. A separate phase-separated execution matches the aggregate yield without reusing responses; most certified witnesses also fail held-out cases. Preserving these witnesses delivers stronger specification repair: Phase-Revised contracts achieve 81.3% implementation success, versus 62.5% for IS-Revised, with Direct-Revised close behind. Both no-feedback witness sources substantially outperform weakened contracts. The effect does not reproduce in the available Dafny and Lean evaluations, establishing an empirical boundary around the studied Verus/Rust workflow. Completeness auditing and correctness repair have opposing immediate objectives. Preserve and certify the admitted failure before repairing it.
