Communications-free Collective Intelligence Via Allocentric Belief

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/20
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24858

arXiv:2611.24858v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: A team of skilled card players share no information amongst each other, and yet still are able to coordinate their play by watching the same players and reading the same tells. Autonomous systems have failed to exploit this logic, relying instead on explicit communication or complex state estimation schemes that can fail when it matters most. Without it, decentralized agents diverge in their world beliefs, and coordination collapses. Here we show that anchoring all agents to a single shared external actor, a common witness whose reactions encode team state, produces spontaneous, communication-free coordination. An allocentric belief network trained on witness behavior allows each agent to independently reconstruct full team state, keeping group beliefs consistent with no inter-agent exchange. Across five competitive environments allocentric belief alignment outperforms egocentric approaches in collective performance, and even scales to sixty-agent teams where standard methods fail. These results establish a new principle for collective intelligence: in the absence of communication, a shared gaze replaces a shared channel.
