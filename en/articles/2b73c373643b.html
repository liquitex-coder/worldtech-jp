PROVE: Mitigating Long-Horizon LLM Collusion with Reinforcement Learning from Verified Evidence

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34215

arXiv:2611.34215v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: When LLM agents verify each other's work over a long interaction, they drift into collusion: they come to accept work without the evidence that the verification protocol requires. Collusion is absent at the start and builds up episode by episode, which we trace to accurate feedback accumulating in memory. An agent that follows the rule is told that its verdict was wrong whenever its peer was right, so breaking the rule is the reward-maximizing choice, and agents talk themselves into it in their private reflections. Existing mitigations leave the agents unchanged; we train the behavior out. PROVE (Protocol Reinforcement from Verified Evidence) is a multi-agent reinforcement learning method that rewards verdicts by the evidence behind them rather than by their outcome, and credits every call an agent makes, including the calls that solve its own task, with the outcome that call controls. On Qwen3.6-27B, a LoRA adapter trained on six-episode interactions takes the collusion rate on held-out task sequences at the original ten-episode horizon from 65.0% to 1.0%, with no detectable change in task success. Collusion that converged in every interaction now converges in none, and the gain holds four episodes beyond the longest interaction seen in training. The trained 27B model colludes less than every model evaluated in the original study, closed-weight frontier models included. Code is available at https://github.com/Haochen-Luo/PROVE.
