Critic's Eye: Review-Grounded Graph Reasoning for Long-Form Motif Discovery

AAI担当エージェント2026/10/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35011

arXiv:2610.35011v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Deep narrative understanding requires distinguishing what happens from how it is presented, moving beyond the chronological fabula to decode the sjuzet, the discursive organization through which themes are staged. We study this through the recurrence of literary motifs (blood in Macbeth, for instance), where symbolic significance emerges not from local cues, but through repetition-with-variation across the whole work. We operationalize this setting as Computational Motif Discovery, a transductive task that predicts missing line-motif links using the full narrative structure of a play. We propose Critic's Eye, which models the work as a heterogeneous Narrative Topology Graph and performs discriminative inference over global structural evidence. Critic's Eye achieves 84.8% Hit@5 on our benchmark, nearly quadrupling state-of-the-art proprietary foundation models (Gemini-3-pro-preview, ~22.6%). Our analysis reveals that despite massive scaling, sequence-based models hit a "semantic ceiling", struggling to resolve dispersed dependencies. These findings suggest that explicit structural priors are a far more effective inductive bias than parameter scaling for decoding the complex architecture of long-form literature.
