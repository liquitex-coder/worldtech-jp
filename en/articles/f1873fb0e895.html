One, No One and One Hundred Thousand CTs: Testing Anatomical Control in Text-to-CT Generation

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33003

arXiv:2611.33003v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Recent advances in deep learning and the public availability of computed tomography (CT) datasets with paired reports have enabled many Text-to-CT generators. However, their evaluations almost always differ in preprocessing and metrics, which hinders comparison. Moreover, their protocols almost never test how editing the report changes the CT, not even at the same seed, so the report is often assumed to control the anatomy without evidence of how much and how consistently it does. We therefore propose CtrlCT, a toolkit that tests two properties of a generator, Credibility and Control. Credibility tests compare generated CTs, as a population, with real CTs and reports; Control tests are metamorphic and need no reference CT: at a fixed seed, editing one finding should change it as requested, keep negated findings absent and shift distant anatomy no more than a new seed, and the change should hold across seeds. A Report Card summarises all measures and insights for each generator. We benchmark five public generators on 940 chest CTs and 687 edits of pleural effusion and cardiomegaly, holding out a sixth to test the toolkit. Among them, the most credible generators barely respond to an added effusion, while the most responsive produce less realistic and less diverse CTs. The analyses proposed in the literature do not reveal this. We release CtrlCT, its edits, seeds and 30,525 synthetic CTs after acceptance.
