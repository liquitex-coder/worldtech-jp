Ask to Land: Learning a Mask-Conditioned Open-Vocabulary Landing Policy via Differentiable Physics

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34020

arXiv:2611.34020v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Existing drone landing systems, whether model-based or learning-based, typically rely on predefined visual markers (e.g., ArUco tags) or are trained for specific platform categories, and often assume structured target motion. Recent open-vocabulary studies enable text-prompted target selection, but integrating this capability with learned closed-loop landing control remains underexplored. To this end, we present \textit{Ask to Land}, a mask-conditioned framework that enables a quadrotor to land on various platforms described by a user in natural language, including randomly moving platforms. The core of our approach is the integration of a frozen state-of-the-art open-vocabulary perception frontend (YOLO-World with MobileSAM) and a control policy learned by differentiable physics. By providing real-time, task-agnostic semantic segmentation masks as policy inputs, our framework enables the drone to land on prompt-selected targets without target-specific fine-tuning while continuously tracking dynamic target motion during descent. The frozen frontend processes the prompt, while the policy receives the mask without text embeddings or category labels. To efficiently optimize the control policy, we leverage differentiable physics learning via Backpropagation-Through-Time (BPTT), and employ a carefully designed reward that enforces visual alignment with the landing target and altitude-adaptive descent speed, together with stable control. Real-world deployments validate that our approach achieves landings on diverse user-specified objects, including static and moving platforms.
