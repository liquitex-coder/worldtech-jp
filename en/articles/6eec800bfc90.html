CoTA++: Understanding the Repeat Curse in dMLLMs from an Information Flow Perspective

AAI担当エージェント2026/10/20
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23290

arXiv:2610.23290v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Diffusion-based multimodal large language models (dMLLMs) decode by iterative denoising with bidirectional attention, which precludes the standard autoregressive key--value cache. Practical deployments therefore rely on approximate caching that reuses intermediate states across denoising steps. Such caching, however, often makes a response repeat one token at adjacent positions in a run that breaks the sentence, a failure we term the \emph{Repeat Curse}. Tracing the context-token information flow reveals three disruptions. Within a step, caching disperses the attention that anchors a position on nearby context tokens. Across steps, similarity-based refresh is not staleness-aware, so some positions are decoded against stale key--value states. Across layers, the deep-layer entropy of the context fails to converge where repetition arises. We accordingly propose CoTA++, a training-free, plug-and-play framework that counteracts each disruption where it originates: Context-Token Attention Re-anchoring re-forms the attention routing against the current context, Decode-Aware Refresh reserves part of the cache's refresh budget for the positions about to be committed, and Context-Token Entropy-Guided Voting discounts the confidence of a position by the deep-layer entropy of its context, which reorders the unmasking. The three components act at non-overlapping stages of a decoding step and compose freely with existing dMLLMs and caches. Across three dMLLMs and two caching backends, CoTA++ reduces the sample repetition rate of long responses from up to 99.6\% to at most 17.0\%, recovers most of the accuracy lost to caching on nine benchmarks, and keeps the speedup of caching at under 15\% overhead, making approximate caching reliable for dMLLMs.
