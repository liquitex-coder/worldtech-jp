Provenance Loss and Repair in Multilingual Compaction

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31668

arXiv:2611.31668v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Conversational assistants compact older messages into short notes and evict the originals. When the note's language requires a distinction that English leaves open, such as Spanish adjective gender (agotado vs. agotada), messages that omit or explicitly state that attribute can yield the same note. After eviction, later turns cannot tell them apart: taking the inflected form literally asserts an attribute the user never gave, whereas discounting it as routine grammar discards one the user did give. We propose a training-free repair across writing, pre-eviction verification and reading. The writer marks unstated attributes with paired forms (e.g., agotado/a), a likelihood check before eviction rewrites unsupported single forms into paired ones, and the reader trusts remaining single forms as stated facts. On five Qwen3.5 and Gemma-4 models, eviction cuts recall of stated attributes from 90% to 34% in question answering (QA); our repair raises F1 (which penalizes both errors) on every model, by 9-53 points in QA and 12-40 points in agent handovers.
