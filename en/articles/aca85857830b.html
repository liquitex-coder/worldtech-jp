MedGuardBench: A Patient-Oriented Benchmark for Personalized Guardrail Development

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/20
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22069

arXiv:2611.22069v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Safety is personal: the same action can be safe for one person but unsafe for another. Yet current guardrails judge content without considering who is affected. Using medication safety, we introduce \textit{patient-conditioned medication guarding}, which predicts a safety verdict and 17 risk categories from a patient profile and prescription. We release \textbf{MedGuardBench}, a drug-disjoint benchmark of 5{,}001 scenarios across 87 medications. We find that standard guardrails rely on surface cues, such as wording and profile presence, rather than patient facts. To address this, we develop \textit{personalized reasoning guardrails} with three components. \textbf{Personalized Reasoning Distillation} links risks to relevant patient fields. \textbf{Context Unrolling (CU)} makes models check each field themselves, reducing false alarms on most backbones; controls show that providing the check does not yield the same benefit. A \textbf{rule-based reward (guided GRPO)} improves unsafe recall and complex-risk detection, including allergies and adverse reactions, on some backbones, but increases false alarms on others. Our models outperform base models and matched no-profile controls in accuracy, while reducing false alarms relative to no-profile controls. Profile removal and swapping confirm that gains come from patient information, not fine-tuning alone.
