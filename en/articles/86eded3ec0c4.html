A preference for local interaction between layers emerges during training

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/24
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27258

arXiv:2611.27258v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: A deep neural network is a stack of layers, and all layers are trained to lower one shared error. A change in one layer can therefore reach every other layer. Because the error is shared by all layers, one would expect the other layers to respond alike, whether they are next to the changed layer or far from it. We find that early in training the response is weak and its order by distance is not stable. As training proceeds, the response grows, and neighboring layers come to respond to changes in each other more strongly than layers further apart, in almost every network. This locality forms during training, although training links every layer to every other. We show this by pushing one layer of a copy of the network at many stages of training and measuring how much of the push reaches each of the other layers. The experiments use 512 five-layer networks trained on modular addition with each of two optimizers, AdamW and stochastic gradient Langevin dynamics.
