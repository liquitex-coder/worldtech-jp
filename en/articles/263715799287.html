Blaming the Machine, Billing the Victim? How Mock Jurors Reassign Fault from Generative AI Systems and Respond to Liability Cliffs in Tort

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32188

arXiv:2611.32188v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Generative AI complicates tort liability attribution when people blame a generative AI system that cannot itself bear legally relevant fault. We investigate where this blame goes when fault must be allocated among legal persons, and whether American discontinuous apportionment doctrines influence mock jurors' factual findings of fault. Such doctrines include Contributory negligence, Joint and several liability, and their 50% or 51% variations, all of which have a fault-percentage threshold or a "cliff" where the victim-entitled compensation (or a defendant's liability) jumps to zero (or to all compensatory damages). Our pre-registered human subjects experiment (N=199 Prolific participants) shows that as many U.S. mock jurors (36%) blame the AI system itself, the AI blame often "leaks" to the victim or to the AI user (in 27% to 73% of our AI-blaming records, depending on how leakage is calculated). Although mock jurors rarely change their blame assignment from one side of the liability cliff to the other, these participants do not acknowledge the legal doctrine or its monetary consequence when explaining their changes. Our empirical results illustrate how a narrow doctrinal exception in tort, carving out "joint and several liability" for those deploying or using Generative AI without reasonable care, might mitigate the impact of blame leakage on victims' deserved compensation without introducing significant jury nullification risks.
