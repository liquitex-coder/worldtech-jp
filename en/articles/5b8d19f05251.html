Multimodal Person Concept Model for Person Identification under Varying Observation Conditions

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17802

arXiv:2611.17802v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Service robots in domestic and office environments must identify users to provide personalized services based on their preferences and previous interactions. This is challenging because only a few observations may be available for learning, while observation conditions change owing to appearance, environmental noise, and utterance content. To address this challenge, this study proposes a multimodal person concept model that learns person concepts from a small number of observations, without person labels, and enables robust identification under changing conditions. The model employs a nonparametric Bayesian generative model based on the Chinese restaurant process and integrates visual, acoustic, and linguistic features extracted using Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training (CLIP) and Contrastive Language-Audio Pretraining (CLAP), followed by principal component analysis (PCA). We evaluated six participants under eight conditions in which appearance changes, additive noise, and transcription errors were introduced individually and in combination. Averaged over the eight conditions, the proposed model achieved an identification accuracy of 0.89 while inferring the number of person concepts from the observations, which is comparable to k-means clustering that was supplied with the true number of participants (0.88). We also examined PCA dimensionality, modality availability, and variation strength, and demonstrated the model on a Toyota Human Support Robot. These results show that multimodal person concept learning supports robust person identification under changing conditions, and that identification can succeed even when the number of concepts is not correctly recovered. The project page and demonstration video are available at https://okitamisan01.github.io/person_concept/.
