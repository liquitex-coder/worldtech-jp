AltRA-Test: An Alternative Annotator Test for LLM Judges Without Shared Annotators

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26073

arXiv:2611.26073v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used as judges and graders to replace human annotators, raising the question of when such replacement is statistically justified. To support such decisions, the Alternative Annotator Test (Alt-Test) was introduced for dense settings where all items are annotated by the same annotators. However, in many large-scale annotation settings, items are annotated by different subsets of annotators. We therefore propose the Alternative Annotator Test with Resampled Annotations (AltRA-Test), a statistical test that does not require that all items are annotated by the same annotators. AltRA-Test uses a bootstrap that resamples items, draws k human annotations for each item, and substitutes one of them with an LLM annotation. This produces paired all-human and mixed human-LLM panels. A replacement is supported when the inter-annotator agreement (IAA) of the mixed panel is statistically non-inferior to the IAA of the all-human panel, i.e., when the LLM preserves the signal that the human annotators share. We establish that AltRA-Test is a consistent test of asymptotic level alpha. We then evaluate it through simulations and real-world experiments in which we test 18 LLMs and 5 prompting techniques on 20 datasets across text, image, audio, and video. Across these evaluations, about 100 items often suffice for the test. In simulations, where we know whether the LLM is a better or a worse annotator than the humans, AltRA-Test makes more correct replacement decisions than existing baselines. In real-world data experiments, LLMs pass the test in about half of the setups, depending mainly on the task. Since replacement is not always justified, we encourage researchers to test it for their task.
