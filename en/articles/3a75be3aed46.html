GGDriver: Glance at What Is Shared, Gaze at What Differs for Perception-Free Planning

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/24
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27330

arXiv:2611.27330v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: A planner deciding whether to turn left or continue straight needs to inspect different parts of the scene for each maneuver. Yet many end-to-end driving planners evaluate every candidate trajectory against the same compressed scene representation. We let each candidate select the visual evidence used to refine and score it. A global glance first produces trajectory hypotheses from shared scene features. We then project a corridor around each trajectory into the camera views and use the resulting mask to attend to patch features at the backbone's native resolution. After refinement, the scorer projects each final proposal again and ranks it using evidence along its own path. This geometric selection requires only the predicted trajectory and camera calibration. On three planners with different scene representations (learned registers, a perception-supervised bird's-eye-view grid, and a coarse image-token grid), adding this gaze improves planning scores. In ablations, path-aligned patches outperform both a matched number of randomly selected patches and attention over all patches. Using the gaze for ranking yields larger gains than using it for refinement alone. With a ViT-S backbone and no perception labels, GGDriver achieves a rater feedback score of 8.110 on the WOD-E2E test set, 94.2 PDMS on NAVSIM-v1, 48.6 EPDMS on NAVSIM-v2, and a closed-loop Driving Score of 72.18 on Bench2Drive.
