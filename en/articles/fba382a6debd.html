Conformal Policy Selection: When to Deploy AI Decisions for Targeted Intervention

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/10/20
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22930

arXiv:2610.22930v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Artificial-intelligence (AI) policies are increasingly used to make consequential decisions in medicine, finance, education, and public services, yet deploying them responsibly requires knowing not only whether a policy helps on average, but for whom. Existing tools for off-policy evaluation and safe policy learning provide population-level guarantees, but some individuals may experience adverse treatment effects. We recast targeted deployment as a multiple-testing problem: for each candidate unit, test whether the outcome under the AI policy would exceed a user-specified threshold, and control the false discovery rate (FDR) among the selected set. Our method, Conformal Policy Selection, combines weighted conformal $p$-values that account for the distributional shift from behavior to target policy with a two-step selection-and-pruning procedure. We prove finite-sample FDR control and establish a doubly robust asymptotic guarantee, where FDR remains controlled if either the outcome model or the propensity model is correctly specified. Synthetic experiments verify these guarantees across four misspecification regimes, and a semi-synthetic study built on real electronic-health-record covariates illustrates how the same FDR control can act as a deployment safety gate, bounding the harmful treatments that a globally trained policy would otherwise administer to a high-risk subpopulation where it misgeneralizes. The framework offers a principled method for safe AI deployment in high-stakes applications.
