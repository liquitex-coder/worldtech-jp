Understanding the limits of snapshot-based fault diagnosis in distribution networks

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17998

arXiv:2611.17998v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Inverter-based resources weaken fault currents in distribution networks, and snapshot-based diagnosis, which classifies a fault from voltage phasors at a few buses at one instant, offers a cheap first-stage screen. Its published accuracies, however, rest on different noise assumptions and do not show whether the error comes from the classifier, the features, the measurement error, or the fault resistance. This paper separates these sources on three IEEE test feeders and a public data set with distributed generation, using four feature sets, thirteen classifiers, and measurement error defined by total vector error classes. The classifier is not the limiting factor, because the full phasor snapshot classifies every fault correctly without noise, and the remaining error comes from measurement error on high-impedance faults. Three voltage sensors classify 90-96% of faults at the 1% accuracy class and 96-99% with distribution micro phasor measurement units (micro-PMUs). A discriminability-margin placement selects them from simulations alone in under one second and matches the best of twelve placement methods. The results show when snapshot-based diagnosis can be trusted.
