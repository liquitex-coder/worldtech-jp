Probabilistic Machine Learning for Total Kinetic Energy Distributions in Neutron-Induced Fission

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33379

arXiv:2611.33379v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: The incident-neutron-energy dependence of post-neutron total-kinetic-energy (TKE) distributions, particularly their width in the multichance-fission regime, provides a sensitive test of fission calculations. We investigate how the General Description of Fission Observables (GEF) and probabilistic machine-learning models trained on GEF reproduce measured TKE distributions, emphasizing their width at high incident-neutron energies and extrapolation beyond the training range. Gaussian process regression (GPR), GPR with a white-noise kernel, a mixture density network (MDN), and a Bayesian mixture density network (BMDN) are evaluated on a common calculated dataset. An extended MDN/BMDN analysis uses 1,025 GEF spectra from 20 target nuclei and 60 measured distributions. Training ranges of up to 20, 40, and approximately 76 MeV are compared with matched-size controls extending to 30 or 40 MeV while preserving the number of spectra per target. Above 40 MeV, the BMDN width mean absolute error relative to experiment decreases from 5.63 MeV for training up to 20 MeV to 2.70 and 2.53 MeV for two matched-size 30-MeV controls and 1.77 MeV for the matched-size 40-MeV control. Predictive-interval coverage relative to GEF above 40 MeV rises from 52.3% to 92.3% between the 20-MeV and full training ranges. Agreement with measured mean TKE and widths is target dependent, with no uniform ordering among GEF, MDN, and BMDN. The results demonstrate the sensitivity of high-energy widths to training-energy coverage, with improvements already evident at 30 MeV. The comparatively slow variation of GEF widths at high energies limits the extent to which these improvements can be attributed to learning nontrivial energy dependence. Adequate training-energy coverage is critical for reproducing the calculated high-energy evolution, while agreement with experiment remains constrained by the underlying fission model.
