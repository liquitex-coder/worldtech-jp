Reproducer Synthesis for AI-Reported Bugs in the Linux Kernel

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34142

arXiv:2611.34142v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Agentic review systems are increasingly flagging suspected bugs in Linux kernel patches as textual reports. A maintainer who receives such reports needs further inspection to determine whether the report is true. The inspection requires understanding whether the bug can be triggered and, if necessary, manually constructing one. As a consequence, despite the excessive textual explanation, many reports go unanswered. In this paper, we argue that a runnable proof-of-concept (PoC) is the key information in such AI-generated kernel review reports. It is, however, challenging to automatically synthesize a valid and runnable PoC. We present ReproCue, a framework that generates PoCs for AI-generated Linux kernel reviews. ReproCue replays the historical kernel bugs and learns one reproduction finite-state-machine (FSM) per subsystem from successful runs. An FSM records the steps a reproduction goes through, together with the requirements observed at each step. Given a new review, ReproCue follows the FSM of that subsystem, adjusting the PoC and the environment as execution shows what is missing. In 100 recent AI-generated reviews, ReproCue writes PoCs for 49 of them, compared with 10 for the current agentic reproducer and 9 for the state-of-the-art directed fuzzing. Excitingly, with our generated PoCs, Linux kernel maintainers confirmed 69.4% of them, much higher than 9.3% from Sashiko where only textual explanations are included, making AI reviews more actionable in practice.
