The Qualitative Laboratory: Theory Prototyping and Hypothesis Generation with Large Language Models

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/15
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00797

arXiv:2601.00797v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: The use of Large Language Models (LLMs) to simulate social actors has garnered attention for its implications for the epistemic status of discourses generated through such means. This paper considers the more modest question of whether sociological role-playing can serve as a heuristic instrument in the generation of qualitative hypotheses. We take as a case study the development of four divergent fictional characters using a theoretically informed Matrix of Climate Reception. These characters are asked to respond to a series of discourse frames covering two topics: a proposed ban on the sale of new gasoline-powered cars, and the inclusion of climate justice in the school curriculum. We then analyse the 32 model-generated responses for their commonalities, situational differences and divergences from the presuppositions underlying the characters' profiles. The experiment illustrates how LLMs can be used to flesh out theoretical presuppositions into contextual narratives and to propose avenues of further inquiry. It also shows that role-playing can generate considerable similarity between characters and is sensitive to how prompts are constructed, warning against the confusion of discursive coherence with sociological data. Role-playing is argued to be just one of several possible ways to use LLMs for generating hypotheses, with the heuristic specificity of this procedure compared with other methods of direct LLM-based reasoning or with other techniques remaining an open issue. The qualitative laboratory is thus conceived of as a heuristic instrument for provoking reflection and refining questions, not for testing theories with artificial data.
