When Exact Token Importance Is Not Enough: Limits of Top-K Selection in LLM Attention

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32324

arXiv:2611.32324v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Long-context language models often retain only a limited set of past token positions for an attention query. A common strategy scores positions individually and keeps the highest-ranked K. Yet attention weights are renormalized over the chosen set: two individually important positions need not form the best pair. We ask whether independent ranking suffices even when each importance score measures the exact downstream change in next-token predictions caused by deleting that position alone. It does not. A four-position construction makes the ranked pair incur 133.2 times the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence of the optimal pair, and a parametric family proves that this multiplicative gap is unbounded. An exact exchange identity and preference reversals for every K >= 2 explain the interaction. In exhaustive frozen-model audits of SmolLM2, Gemma, and Qwen, exact-singleton Top-4 leaves at least 10% relative improvement within a fully enumerated 24-position candidate pool in 55/60, 49/60, and 60/60 cases, respectively. SwapCert, a same-budget one-swap audit, detects 157/164 such cases and returns directly checkable improving supports. A KL-margin certificate relates support fidelity to next-token stability; larger-budget, multi-layer cached-decoding experiments measure predictive fidelity at K = 128 and 256. These results establish joint support quality as an essential object of analysis for sparse attention and motivate downstream-aware support selection under inference constraints.
