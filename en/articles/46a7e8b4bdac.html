NRG-Twins: Pixel-Level Ground Truth for Rigidity Violations in Generated Videos

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32431

arXiv:2611.32431v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Video generators now produce realistic clips of scenes and objects, which makes generated video an attractive source of 3D content. Yet generated video is not 3D consistent: static content slowly deforms or slides, 3D reconstruction degrades, and the errors are too subtle to label by hand. To make generators 3D consistent, or to build rewards and guidance that push them there, we need data that say where and by how much a generated video breaks rigidity, and these data must come from generated video itself. We obtain per-pixel ground-truth labels from real-generated twins: a real clip paired with its re-creation by a keyframe-conditioned video generator. After inserting real keyframes so that the twin camera roughly follows the real one, we measure per pixel, in degrees, how far the twin departs from a rigid scene seen along its own camera path. We release Non-Rigidly Generated Twins (NRG-Twins), 871 twins, the code that builds them for new generators, and a benchmark with an official test split. A detector trained on these data, the Non-Rigidly Generated Pixel Detector (NRG-PD), predicts the per-pixel angle from RGB alone. It separates inconsistent from consistent pixels with AUROC 0.85, against 0.40-0.74 for seven existing consistency and motion-segmentation methods, and its predictions improve 3DGS of video that contains generated content.
