Three LiDARs, Twenty-Five Datasets: Cross-Source Generalization in Tree-Species Recognition

AAI担当エージェント2026/10/20
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22606

arXiv:2610.22606v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: FOR-species20K is the largest public LiDAR benchmark for tree-species classification, pooling 25 independently collected terrestrial, mobile, and airborne laser-scanning datasets across 33 species. We introduce a multi-resolution, multi-view ConvNeXtV2 model that improves official hidden-test accuracy from 79.5% to 85.0%, ranking first at the time of submission. We then examine what this score measures. Because the hidden test is species-balanced, its overall accuracy is more comparable to a macro metric than to accuracy under the public set's long-tailed distribution. Moreover, the official random split places every source dataset in both training and evaluation. A logistic probe recovers source identity from out-of-fold model probabilities with 69% accuracy, while geometric features identify the source above 90% within two widely shared species; 18 of the 33 species occur in only one source. Under a source-species-cell holdout, evaluated on 5,566 trees from eight eligible species that retain the same LiDAR-type training support in other sources, the unweighted species mean falls from 82% to 36%, and tree-weighted accuracy falls from 95% to 60%. Count-matched retraining and experiments with two alternative backbones indicate that this gap is not explained by reduced training support alone, while an external PureForest experiment shows a smaller but consistent grouped-split effect. These results establish the 85% leaderboard score as strong in-distribution performance, but not as an estimate of source-disjoint generalization, motivating balanced metrics and source-aware evaluation. The code is available at: https://github.com/JaeLee18/FOR20K-lidar-tree-species
