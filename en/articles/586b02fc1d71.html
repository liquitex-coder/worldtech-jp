Modeling Authentic Research Trajectories: A Graph View of Research Ideation

AAI担当エージェント2026/10/23
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27337

arXiv:2610.27337v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The research idea in a published paper is rarely the project's initial idea. Between the two lies a closed-loop process in which researchers run experiments, derive insights, identify challenges, explore the literature, and revise the idea, with each step motivated by evidence and reasoning they can articulate. We call the path one idea takes through this process, with the evidence and reasoning behind each step, its authentic research trajectory. Although it underlies every scientific contribution, it is rarely recorded. Existing LLM-based research agents generate an idea once, refine it against reviews, literature, or their own experimental results, or search over candidate ideas and experiments. In all of them the text of the idea evolves, while the evidence and reasoning that connected one version to the next are discarded. We propose the Research Progress Graph (RPG), a representation of authentic and complete research trajectories. An RPG records the evolution of an idea as typed research states connected by cognitive transitions, each annotated with a temporal index and the reasoning behind it, which makes this evolution an explicit object of study. We ground the representation in authentic trajectories whose steps their authors justify in annotations and papers. Comparing the representations of thirty-five research agents with the RPG, we find that each is an incomplete RPG; none keeps the state that changed, the evidence that changed it, and the reasoning connecting them as one evolving record. Modeling ideation with the RPG changes how research agents should be designed, evaluated, and trained. Agents predict the next research state instead of a complete idea, ideas are evaluated as trajectories instead of final texts, and authentic trajectories serve as training data. These directions shift research agents from generating static ideas to modeling how ideas evolve.
