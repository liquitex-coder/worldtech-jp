Sample-Efficient In-Context Adaptation via Sleep

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32802

arXiv:2611.32802v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: The ability to learn and generalize from limited experience, or sample efficiency, is a cornerstone of intelligence. How can we build language models that turn a few user interactions, a few snippets of unfamiliar code, or a handful of experimental results provided in context into knowledge that generalizes? Prior work has found that sleep, a process where the model performs $N$ recurrent forward passes to refine its representations of a chunk of context, can improve long-context reasoning (specifically, within SSM-attention hybrid models). We study whether this method also improves in-context adaptation on general, non-synthetic tasks for transformer language models. To this end, we pre-train language models up to 1.2B-parameters on up to 100B tokens with sleep mechanisms. Notably, longer sleep (i.e., using a larger number of recurrent forward passes $N$) enables the model to learn faster in-context on multiple few-shot algorithmic, question-answering tasks, while also achieving lower next-token prediction loss on out-of-distribution evaluations. On held-out code, models with $N=2$ and $N=4$ match the no-sleep baseline's loss using $2.10\times$ and $2.39\times$ fewer tokens in-context, respectively. More broadly, we view our work as a step toward building models that have better tradeoffs between in-context adaptation and decoding latency.
