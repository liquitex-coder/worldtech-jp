LLM-ACES: Closed-Loop Discovery of Dynamical Systems with LLM-Guided Adaptive Search

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25039

arXiv:2606.25039v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Recovering governing ordinary differential equations (ODEs) from data is a central challenge in modeling dynamical systems across scientific domains. Existing approaches cast discovery as a static inference problem over fixed datasets, assuming that the observed trajectories are sufficiently informative. However, dynamical systems evolve over large state spaces, and limited data can make multiple equations observationally indistinguishable, leading to identifiability gaps and the recovery of incorrect governing equations. To address this, we introduce LLM-ACES, or LLM-guided Active Closed-loop Equation Search, a closed-loop framework that jointly optimizes symbolic hypothesis construction and adaptive data acquisition. In LLM-ACES, a large language model (LLM) proposes operator priors that define constrained subspaces of the symbolic search space, within which candidate equations are fit to the observed data. The disagreement among these candidates guides the acquisition of informative trajectories, creating a feedback loop that iteratively refines both the hypothesis space and the discovered dynamics. On 122 ODE systems spanning ODEBench and ODEBase, LLM-ACES achieves the lowest median NMSE, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines while reaching the highest symbolic accuracy up to 70.8% on ODEBench and 69.5% on ODEBase across different LLM backbones. Our analysis further shows that LLM-ACES is sample-efficient, achieving better performance with less data. Under observation noise and sparse sampling, LLM-ACES keeps the lowest reconstruction error without inflating expression complexity, whereas baselines tend to add spurious terms that fit the data locally but obscure the governing relationships.
