JEPA-Inspired Latent Field Prediction for Sparse-Pilot MIMO-OFDM Channel Reconstruction

AAI担当エージェント2026/10/29
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37391

arXiv:2610.37391v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Accurate acquisition of full channel state information (CSI) is essential for multiple-input multiple-output orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing (MIMO-OFDM) wireless systems, while dense pilot-based estimation incurs substantial overhead. Directly minimizing the prediction error over raw CSI coefficients is fragile in realistic propagation environments, since small-scale fading, mobility, local scattering, and small spatial perturbations can induce large phase-sensitive variations. Nevertheless, wireless channels contain strong spatial, temporal, and frequency-domain correlations that can be more predictably represented in a latent propagation-field domain. Motivated by this observation, we propose a joint-embedding predictive architecture (JEPA)-inspired field world model, defined as a latent predictive model of the wireless propagation field, for sparse-pilot channel reconstruction. The model maps CSI observations into latent time-frequency tokens, predicts masked or future latent field states from historical multi-band context, and fuses the predicted latent prior with sparse noisy current pilots to reconstruct the full MIMO-OFDM channel. Ray-tracing experiments show that the proposed method improves normalized mean square error (NMSE) over pilot-only and direct reconstruction baselines, supporting latent field prediction as an effective prior for sparse-pilot channel acquisition.
