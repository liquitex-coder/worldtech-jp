Pairing AIS with RF: Analysis of a Markovian Approach for Maritime Data Fusion

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18563

arXiv:2611.18563v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Monitoring maritime activities is important for safety and security, particularly for detecting illegal activities, but remains challenging due to the size and complexity of the maritime domain. The Automatic Identification System (AIS) is commonly used for this purpose, despite its known vulnerabilities. In contrast, space-based radio frequency (RF) observations provide an independent source of vessel activity but lack ship identification and therefore cannot identify vessels on their own. Following the CRISP-DM process, this study applies a Pair Hidden Markov Model (PHMM) to align individual RF detections with AIS trajectories, using temporal and spatial information while modeling noise and timing discrepancies. We evaluate it on NOAA AIS data with synthetic RF detections against nearest-neighbor baselines and analyze its errors with bias tests on trajectory length, sampling density, stationarity and AIS--RF distance. The length-corrected Forward score reaches a top-1 precision of $0.7776$ on the validation split of multi-candidate detections, where errors are possible, and an AUC of $0.637$ for detecting dark vessels among the detections that pass the prefilter. With a single RF detection, the score mostly reflects the AIS trajectory itself, and its errors favor longer and more stationary trajectories. A log-odds score, which compares the full model with a null model without match transitions, raises precision to $0.8580$ and AUC to $0.852$. Its length-corrected variant no longer shows a significant bias towards stationary trajectories and short inter-message distances, but still favors candidates farther from the RF detection. A time-weighted nearest-neighbor baseline without fitting remains ahead (precision $0.9013$, AUC $0.904$), so this single-detection, hand-designed PHMM formulation approaches but does not beat it.
