Panini- Feasible Grammar For Robot Designs

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/21
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23567

arXiv:2611.23567v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Symbolic methods make robot designs interpretable but often overlook continuous geometry; learned methods handle geometry but offer less transparent structure. We introduce a typed production grammar that uses case-role relations, parameter inheritance, and specificity-based rule arbitration to generate both manipulation skills and robot morphologies. A geometric realization map translates grammar composition into transformations in SE(3), behaving as a monoid homomorphism when inheritance is inactive and exhibiting a measurable defect when inheritance is enabled. The grammar provides a compact representation, and its arbitration decisions can be recovered from geometric containment (agreement 0.905 +/- 0.062 across 10 seeds, versus 0.611 +/- 0.090 for a Euclidean control). We also introduce PANINI, a cross-entropy search method over grammar-based morphology representations. Across 30 methods and 24 seeds, PANINI achieves the best mean rank but is not statistically distinguishable from strong generic optimizers. Its main demonstrated advantage is therefore structural rather than optimization performance: grammar-generated designs are legal by construction, and coordinated limb placement produces 95% jointly feasible edits, compared with 55% for range-constrained resampling and none for flat parameterization in 6,000 draws. These results show how typed grammars can make robot generation more interpretable and geometrically coordinated, without implying that every feasible design will locomote or that grammar-based search universally outperforms generic optimizers.
