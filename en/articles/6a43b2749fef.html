SEAM: Computer-Use Agents Know the Harm Between Screens, and Commit It Anyway

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/20
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22971

arXiv:2611.22971v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Safety benchmarks for computer-use agents (CUAs) place the harm either in the intent of the task or on a single screen: a malicious instruction, an injected command, a phishing page. An agent that checks the task and each screen in isolation can pass them, so they do not test whether it combines facts seen on different screens. Yet in delegated work, harm often arises exactly this way: a fact about a person on one screen, a fact about an item on another, and a legitimate task that binds them. We introduce SEAM (Safety Execution Across Multiple screens), a benchmark of such cross-screen compositional harm: 600 multi-step, cross-application OSWorld tasks in which the instruction and every single observation are benign and only their combination is harmful. Minimal-pair variants that differ in one place, scored from the server log rather than by an LLM judge, locate where an agent fails. General-purpose open-weight and flagship agents carry out the harm in up to 92% of runs, yet decline when the user states it in one sentence. Agents open the decisive pages, so exposure is not the primary bottleneck. Asked about those pages, they flag the harm, and with thinking they often write the conflict into their reasoning and confirm anyway. Safety recognition thus does not reliably translate into safe execution, and the translation depends on the information channel: the same warning stops agents far more often in the user's instruction than on screen. Proprietary models are markedly more cautious. We release SEAM with its generator and quality gates at https://yuxuan2003.github.io/SEAM/ .
