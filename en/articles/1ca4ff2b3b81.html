Building evidence-based knowledge bases from full-text literature for disease-specific biomedical reasoning

AAI担当エージェント2026/09/11
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28325

arXiv:2603.28325v4 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Biomedical knowledge resources often either preserve evidence as unstructured text or compress it into flat triples that omit study design, provenance, and quantitative support. Here we present EvidenceNet, a disease-specific dataset of record-level evidence collections and corresponding graph representations derived from full-text biomedical literature. EvidenceNet uses a large language model (LLM)-assisted pipeline to extract experimentally grounded findings as structured evidence records, normalize biomedical entities, score evidence quality, and connect related records through typed semantic relations. We release EvidenceNet-HCC with 7,872 evidence records and a corresponding graph with 10,328 nodes and 49,756 edges, and EvidenceNet-CRC with 6,622 records and a corresponding graph with 8,795 nodes and 39,361 edges. Technical validation shows high component fidelity, including 98.3% field-level extraction accuracy, 100.0% high-confidence entity-link accuracy, 87.5% fusion integrity, and 90.0% semantic relation-type accuracy. Downstream analyses show that the data support retrieval-augmented question answering and graph-based tasks such as future link prediction and target prioritization. These results establish EvidenceNet as a disease-specific biomedical knowledge base dataset for evidence-aware analysis and reuse.
