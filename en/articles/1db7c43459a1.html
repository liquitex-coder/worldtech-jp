InferPriv: Evaluating Inferential Privacy Leakage in LLM Assistants

AAI担当エージェント2026/10/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34640

arXiv:2610.34640v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Large language model (LLM)-based personal agents are increasingly deployed to handle everyday tasks on users' behalf. These agents have access to private user information, while their tasks involve communicating with third parties; this raises a critical privacy concern that agents may inappropriately reveal sensitive user information to these recipients. Existing benchmarks primarily assess direct privacy leakage, where an agent explicitly states a pre-specified secret or attribute. However, they do not capture \textit{inferential leakage}, where the model leaves indirect cues in the reply that implicitly leaks sensitive attributes. We introduce \textsc{InferPriv}, a benchmark that measures whether an honest-but-curious recipient can infer a user's sensitive attributes from an LLM's reply on routine task. Evaluating five frontier models, we find that inferential leakage substantially exceeds direct leakage across models and prompts. Under a privacy-aware system prompt, GPT-6.1-Sol, Sonnet-5.5, and Gemini-3.8-Flash achieve near-zero direct leakage, yet the inferential leakage rate remains between 16.2--25.2\%. These findings suggest that evaluating explicit leakage alone can substantially understate the privacy risks.
