Agentic Computational Algebra

arXiv:2611.16073v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: We explore the use of AI agents for solving computational problems in algebra. For the problems we consider, the main challenge is not correctness but \emph{time}: general-purpose algorithms are guaranteed to provide correct solutions but can be prohibitively slow, while reformulations that exploit special structure often allow solving a problem much faster. We propose to use this setting to evaluate how effectively agents discover and exploit structure within and across tasks to reduce computational costs. More broadly, computational algebra supplies infinitely many problems with independently verifiable answers and systematically controllable structure and difficulty. To illustrate these possibilities, we use benchmark families of systems with controlled shared structure and evaluate agents in three settings: solving independent instances, solving online streams of related instances using persistent memory, and compiling a fast reusable solver for a known problem family. Our experiments show that (i) agents can extend the range of systems solvable within a fixed budget, although reasoning overhead can sometimes outweigh computational savings; (ii) memory can reduce the cost of repeated solving but may preserve suboptimal strategies; (iii) compilation produces faster reusable solvers but at substantial upfront cost. Overall, computational algebra offers concrete, measurable targets for improving how agents discover and reuse structure.
