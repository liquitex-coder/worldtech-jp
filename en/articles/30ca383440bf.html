See the Answer, Learn to Embed: Learning Universal Multimodal Embeddings from Hindsight

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32439

arXiv:2611.32439v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Universal Multimodal Embedding (UME) models map heterogeneous multimodal inputs into a shared embedding space, where pairwise similarity supports diverse downstream tasks. Recent reasoning-driven UME approaches use intermediate reasoning to generate additional context for embedding, but often incur extra computation at inference. In this paper, we explore the matched candidate (i.e., the paired target) as an alternative source of context, asking whether a model can "see the answer" during training and "learn to embed" without it at inference. However, direct conditioning on this privileged context can introduce candidate-specific information unavailable from the query alone or cause the candidate-conditioned query representation to collapse toward the candidate embedding. To address this, we introduce Hindsight-Enhanced Multimodal Embeddings (HEME), which learns query-grounded hindsight representations from such candidate context. HEME first trains a hindsight teacher with a hindsight attention mask, mismatched-candidate conditioning, and query-token alignment to limit excessive reliance on the candidate. It then freezes the teacher and distills its hindsight representation and similarity distribution into a standalone student embedder. At inference, the student independently encodes queries and candidates without candidate conditioning or the hindsight teacher. Experiments on MMEB-V2 and the agent and text tasks of MMEB-V3 demonstrate the effectiveness of HEME and show that it enables more efficient inference than explicit reasoning-driven methods.
