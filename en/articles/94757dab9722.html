Exercised, Rarely Recovered: What Public Reports Say About Recovery in 50 AI Agent Harnesses and Runtimes

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/24
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27090

arXiv:2611.27090v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: When an AI coding agent deletes the wrong files or a long agent run crashes, users fall back on recovery mechanisms: resume from a checkpoint, undo, ask for approval, read the log. Research runtimes promise stronger guarantees, such as saga-style compensation and transactional rollback. We ask what public evidence shows about whether either kind works in real use. Under a protocol frozen before data collection, we graded 12 persistent agent runtimes, 26 coding-agent harnesses and 12 research runtimes on eight recovery mechanisms, from not found to exercised in a real run, with two blind coding passes, adjudication and re-verification against archived sources. Recovery is exercised almost everywhere in practice: 11 of 12 runtimes and 25 of 26 harnesses have at least one real-use report. Yet of 128 such reports, only 19 describe the run fully recovering; 54 are partial and 45 failed. Checkpoint/resume recovered once in 27 reports, rollback once in 15. Research runtimes show the opposite profile: guarantees evaluated on benchmarks, none reported in operation, and compensation of external effects, their central guarantee, is offered by no harness at all.
