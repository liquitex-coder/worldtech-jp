What Does RL Post-Training Teach Text-to-Image Models? A Skill-Wise Analysis of Flow-GRPO

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33316

arXiv:2611.33316v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Reinforcement learning (RL) post-training has become a standard way to improve text-to-image flow models: Flow-GRPO lifts the GenEval score of SD3.5-M from 0.63 to 0.95, a gain read as the model having learned compositional generation. We ask how much of that gain the base model's own samples already contained, by measuring pass@k over shared noise seeds, one skill at a time, before and after RL, on prompts never used in training. The answer differs by skill. On four of GenEval's six skills, where 32 base samples already solve almost every prompt, the gain is sharpening: one post-trained sample is worth 4 to 10 base samples, and pass@32 barely moves. On spatial relations it is unlocking: pass@32 rises from 0.69 to 1.00, 128 base samples do not match one post-trained sample, and prompts the base model never solved in 32 samples are solved 93% of the time, as often as those it solved. The benchmark average, 31 base samples per post-trained sample, describes no skill. Relations carry 35% of the gain; of the other 65%, selecting among 8 base samples with the judge behind the reward recovers nine tenths. We propose an account of when each occurs: within a prompt, a group-relative update re-weights only the outcomes its samples contain, and a flow policy cannot widen the noise that produces them; across prompts, shared parameters let successes on some prompts repair a deficit common to many. The same anatomy applies to a text-rendering checkpoint. The account's predictions are tested with a checkpoint trained without KL, within-prompt diversity, and controlled training that holds object classes out, where sharpening comes first and transfers, and unlocking follows, on practised objects before unseen ones.
