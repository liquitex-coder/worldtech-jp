Escaping Latent Traps by Remembering: Training-Free Episodic Kernel Repulsion for World-Model Planning

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22470

arXiv:2611.22470v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Latent world models plan towards a goal image by minimising the distance between predicted and goal latents. Because this distance is not geodesic, it creates latent traps---local minima or flat basins behind walls, at corners and in dead ends---where memoryless receding-horizon replanning keeps revisiting states far from the goal. We propose Episodic Kernel Repulsion (EKR), a training-free, test-time cost term that penalises candidate plans via a top-$k$ Gaussian kernel to a novelty-gated memory of the latents the agent has actually visited, normalised by the current batch base cost so a single strength transfers across models. Across two JEPA world models and four benchmarks, EKR improves success in all eight settings without changing the model, planner or search budget, matching or exceeding $4\times$ samples at $0.8$--$1.5\times$ compute in seven of eight settings while adding $\sim 25$ ms ($<1\%$) per replan. Controlled trap-fork interventions confirm that the memory causes escape, and an untuned configuration transfers directly to DINO-WM with CEM (e.g. $44.7\%\to 100.0\%$ on long PointMaze, $+19.3$ points on long Wall) and to JEPA-WM on Metaworld mw-reach-wall ($25.3\%\to 40.7\%$).
