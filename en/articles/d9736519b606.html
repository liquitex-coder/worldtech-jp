LATCH: LLM-Assisted Timing Closure Harness for Post-Route FPGA Optimisation

AAI担当エージェント2026/10/16
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16625

arXiv:2610.16625v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Post-route FPGA timing closure remains expert-dependent and computationally expensive. We present LATCH (LLM-Assisted Timing Closure Harness), a two-layer framework that separates large language model (LLM) orchestration from deterministic FPGA implementation and evaluation. Across sixteen designs and seven LLMs, a single-iteration study compares three agent control granularities - expert-written recipes, direct EDA operations and LLM-constructed procedures - to ask how much control an agent needs per attempt. Functional-pass rates are 98.2%, 71.4% and 17.9%, respectively; yet direct operations give slightly larger 3.4% median Fmax gains when passing, versus 3.2% for recipes. This exposes a trade-off between execution reliability and timing improvement. Informed by this trade-off, a multi-iteration study fixes the action tier at expert recipes to ask how repeated attempts are best organised, comparing a fitted deterministic heuristic with sequential LLM reasoning, branching exploration and knowledge base-guided exploration within 12 checkpoints per run. Branching raises mean maximum-frequency improvement from 10.5% (sequential) to 16.0%, and the knowledge base to 20.3%, improving 15 of 16 designs. Characterisation across 2,421 recipe attempts profiled their gain and validation rates, showing hard-block re-placement gives the largest cumulative mean gain at 41.4MHz.
