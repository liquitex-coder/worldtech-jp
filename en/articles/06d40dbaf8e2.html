Keep or Roll Back? Out-of-Loop Judges for Self-Improving VLA and End-to-End Embodied Policies

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33794

arXiv:2611.33794v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Self-improvement is now a practical recipe for embodied policies: a policy collects experience, is retrained, and is kept when a judge rates it higher. However, the judge is usually trained or chosen inside the same loop, so its evidence is not independent of the policy. We introduce SealedLoop, an evaluation framework that adds a sealed judge: an evaluator outside the loop that the improver cannot see or optimize against. A new policy is kept only when a sequential test on the sealed judge confirms the gain, with a bounded probability of keeping a worse one. Running the same loop under each judge, we separate two sources of a false gain, which we call judge error (the loop's judge is wrong on its own data) and transfer gap (a real gain there does not carry over to unseen conditions). We evaluate on LIBERO manipulation, NAVSIM driving, and a full-scale real vehicle. In manipulation, a success classifier accepted 65 of 196 failures in one generation. In driving, policies that gain on the loop's own frames lose in an unseen city, so even an exact in-loop judge would keep 8/15 of them. When the sealed test decides, a driving loop built to improve ends with a better policy than under its own critic (0.674 against 0.649 PDMS, from 0.638), and on driving pairs with known sealed gain the test accepted 11 of 12 real gains and none of 59 losses. In a real-world field experiment on a published improvement step, the reinforced version drove further into a curve than the version it started from in all 6 blind pairs, though neither completed it. These results show that evidence from inside the loop, however accurate, is not enough to decide whether a policy has improved. SealedLoop offers a foundation for trustworthy self-improvement of embodied policies, in which progress is judged by evidence the improving system cannot influence.
