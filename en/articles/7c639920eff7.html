Red-Teaming Large Language Models in Low-Resource African Languages: A Case Study of Yoruba and Nigerian Pidgin

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/01
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39590

arXiv:2610.39590v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: As large language models (LLMs) become more widespread in Africa, their safety in low-resource languages remains largely untested since most red-teaming work focuses on English. This study evaluates six state-of-the-art LLMs using 400 explicit adversarial prompts in Yoruba and Nigerian Pidgin, categorised into four harm categories. The responses were scored by native-speaker annotators and reliability was assessed using Cohen's $\kappa$. While models that rejected up to 69% of English prompts rejected as few as 0% in Yoruba, this was not because they were safer, but because they failed to understand the prompts. Attack success rates increased for most models in the Nigerian languages, with GPT-4o Mini demonstrating the most significant cross-lingual safety discrepancy (+0.23 in Yoruba). When models did engage with adversarial prompts, many of the responses were harmful, with rates of harmful content reaching 0.63 in English and 0.50 in Nigerian Pidgin. The most difficult harm category to detect was cultural stereotypes and discrimination (ASR = 0.44 in Nigerian Pidgin). Comprehension in Yoruba was low across most models, and this directly suppressed apparent attack success without indicating actual safety. Gemini 2.0 Flash was the most consistent model across languages. Despite its explicit training in African languages, Aya 101 had the highest attack success rate in English (0.60) and one of the lowest Yoruba comprehension rates (0.28). These results demonstrate that English-centric safety alignment does not transfer to Nigerian languages, highlighting the urgent need for language-specific safety evaluations.
