Search From the Start: Search-Native Pretraining for Language Models

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26809

arXiv:2611.26809v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Modern language models (LMs) are increasingly deployed with access to search tools at inference. Yet pretraining remains predominantly focused on predicting static text, with search capabilities typically introduced during post-training. We propose Search-Native Pretraining as a practical route to teach search capability during pretraining from ordinary text corpora. Specifically, we augment pretraining corpus at scale with web-search queries and retrieve web response using a local index. Training on this augmented corpus teaches models to generate search calls and incorporate retrieved evidence during language modeling. Across Wikipedia and DCLM-Edu corpora and 360M to 3.3B parameter scales, search-pretrained models ("QueryLM") show improved performance on knowledge-intensive tasks and higher steerability toward the retrieved evidence. Crucially, their advantage persists after supervised fine-tuning of both search-pretrained and conventionally pretrained models, indicating that the tested post-training procedures do not fully recover the capabilities developed during pretraining. Throughout our experiments, we find that a 1.4B QueryLM consistently beats or matches the performance of a FLOP-matched 3.3B baseline model across all knowledge intensive tasks. These results establish search-pretraining as a practical way to develop foundational search capabilities during pretraining. We release the training artifacts, including model checkpoints and data.
