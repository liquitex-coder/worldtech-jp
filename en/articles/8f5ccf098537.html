Stable Dimensionality Reduction via Isometric Centroid-Encoder

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/10/29
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33872

arXiv:2610.33872v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Many supervised dimensionality reduction methods promote intra-class compactness and inter-class separation but do not explicitly constrain class geometry, leading to variability in the relative arrangement of classes across independent runs. Such variability limits the reproducibility and interpretability of low-dimensional embeddings. We propose the Isometric Centroid-Encoder (ICE), a supervised dimensionality reduction framework in which class centroids are learned directly in the latent space while preserving a prescribed pairwise class-centroid geometry. Unlike existing centroid- or prototype-based methods that primarily encourage intra-class compactness, ICE explicitly regularizes the relative arrangement of latent class centroids. Unlike multidimensional scaling and many metric-learning approaches that preserve sample-level relationships, ICE operates on class centroids, preserving inter-class geometry without requiring large numbers of sample-pair or triplet constraints. We further show that the intrinsic dimensionality required to represent the centroid-distance configuration of $M$ classes is at most $M-1$, explaining the observed saturation of distance-preservation measures as the latent dimensionality increases. To quantify embedding reproducibility, we introduce the Centroid Neighborhood Stability (CNS) metric, which measures the consistency of class-level neighborhood topology across independent runs and complements existing embedding quality measures. Experiments on image and biological datasets demonstrate that ICE produces stable embeddings with improved preservation of class-centroid geometry while remaining computationally efficient. Additional analyses of optimization dynamics, sensitivity, and preprocessing further provide practical insights into latent dimensionality and explicit geometry modeling for supervised dimensionality reduction.
