How Persona Conditioning Changes Open-Ended Task Execution: A Paired Evaluation of Behavioral Gains and Reliability Costs

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31264

arXiv:2611.31264v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Large language models (LLMs) are often prompted with role- or persona-based instructions, yet persona conditioning has mainly been evaluated through character fidelity or aggregate benchmark accuracy. We study persona conditioning as a task-execution steering signal that changes how models interpret, structure, and sustain responses. Using paired instances derived from real user role-play logs and expanded into simulated multi-turn continuations, we compare with-persona and no-persona conditions while fixing task content, constraints, user simulation, and evaluation criteria. Across eight models, persona conditioning improves expert content depth and procedural fidelity, and these gains survive length control. At the same time, it lowers strict instruction-following accuracy by 17 to 30 points in every model evaluated, and increases unsupported real-world specificity through persona-induced elaboration. These results suggest that persona prompting is not a general-purpose performance improvement technique, but a selective task-execution policy shift with both behavioral gains and reliability risks.
