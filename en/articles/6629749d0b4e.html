Can Its Own Misses Teach an MLLM to Find Small Brain Metastases?

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33836

arXiv:2611.33836v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) are becoming general readers of medical images, yet they remain weak at locating small abnormalities. Brain metastases expose this gap: at 384 px a 0.1 mL lesion covers less than a quarter of one visual token, and at one false positive per scan frontier MLLMs find fewer than 30% of lesions of 0.1-1 mL. Fine-tuning on one dataset helps but transfers poorly, and improving it further is slow trial and error steered by a single score. We introduce LANTERN (Lesion-finding AgeNt Through Error-driven Recursive learNing), a framework in which an MLLM improves recursively from its own misses. LANTERN recasts model development as an error-driven loop: a failure census attributes every missed lesion to one of four causes, an LLM mentor revises one of four levels (evidence acquisition, visual representation, learning policy, decision aggregation) per round, and a rule frozen before the first round decides what is kept, without test data and with every tool call audited. Over 15 unattended rounds, LANTERN raises a fine-tuned 4B MLLM from finding 75% to 94% of lesions at one false positive per scan on BraTS-MET, and 91% of those of 0.1-1 mL, compared with at most 51% and 29% for five frontier MLLMs read under the starting protocol; the gains hold on an external cohort (89%) and on unseen meningiomas (77%). These results show that an MLLM's own misses, attributed to their causes, can drive its reliable recursive self-improvement.
