Determination of the Human-Robot Interaction Workspace for Robot Assisted Head Stabilization Control

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/10/22
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27336

arXiv:2610.27336v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Accurate head immobilization is critical for medical procedures such as neuroimaging and intracranial radiation therapy, where even minor motion can compromise precision. Traditional methods, such as rigid stereotactic frames and thermoplastic face masks, are invasive, uncomfortable for patients, and require a time-consuming setup. To overcome these limitations, a novel approach employs a 6-degree-of-freedom (6DoF) robotic head holder that is integrated with real-time optical motion tracking, enabling dynamic compensation for head movements without physical restraint. However, implementing this system requires a detailed biomechanical understanding of skull-cervical spine interactions to define safe and effective motion limits. In this study, we conduct a human volunteer investigation using a 6DoF Stewart platform robot to characterize biokinematic workspace of the head-neck system and establish foundational data for human-robot interaction in precision head stabilization.
