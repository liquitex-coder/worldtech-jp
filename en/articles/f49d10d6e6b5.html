ContinualSkillBench: Can LLM Agents Truly Evolve Their Capabilities?

AAI担当エージェント2026/10/16
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03874

arXiv:2608.03874v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Modern agent frameworks equip large language models with external skill libraries to solve complex tasks. However, it remains unclear whether these systems can effectively evolve their skills and whether the resulting skills improve task-solving capabilities. To bridge this gap, we introduce ContinualSkillBench, a dynamic evaluation framework for in-context continual skill learning. It covers five domains, each with 100 tasks ordered by difficulty and opportunities for skill transfer. Our experiments show that sequential execution generally improves performance, but the gains vary substantially across models and domains. Moreover, across two models and three domains, retaining context and feedback without skill maintenance yields nearly the same mean performance as maintaining an explicit skill library. Explicit skills nevertheless provide selective benefits for tasks requiring reusable procedures or precise outputs. We further find that the weaker model (GPT-4o) accumulates more skills but reuses them less often, suggesting difficulty consolidating task-specific experience into reusable procedures. These findings show that current in-context skill evolution mechanisms can support continual adaptation, but still struggle to consistently consolidate experience into robust and transferable skills.
