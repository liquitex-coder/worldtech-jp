Show Your Work: Can Small Open Models Read SEC Filings with Evidence a Machine Can Check?

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24990

arXiv:2611.24990v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: A fluent answer from a language model is not a verified one. We ask whether small open models that run on a single consumer GPU can extract numbers from SEC filings with evidence a machine can check: the page and a verbatim quote containing the number. We introduce Filing-Evidence, a benchmark of 1,000 questions over 90 recent 10-K and 10-Q filings of 45 US companies, with answer keys taken from the filers' inline XBRL tags. We evaluate ten instruct models from ten developers (7-21B parameters) on one RTX 4090 in three setups: plain, check-and-retry, and the model's own reasoning mode. Accuracy with checkable evidence ranges from 17.1% to 82.7% in the plain setup, and the best result is 84.5% (Qwen3.5-9B). An automatic checker rejects many wrong answers, but between 16 and 167 answers per 1,000 give a wrong value for an answerable question and still pass every check. In the plain setup, 57% of these are scale or unit errors and 30% take another period or column of the right line. Reasoning significantly reduces such errors for 4 of 5 models that offer it. Retrying after a failed check raises accuracy by up to 3.9 points but never reduces them. Questions, model outputs, code and protocol are public.
