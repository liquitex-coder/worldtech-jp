A Face Robot which Imitates Human Musculoskeletal Skin System and Its Evaluation on Basic Movement of Facial Expression

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/26
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32413

arXiv:2611.32413v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Facial robots require many coordinated, smallamplitude motions, but accommodating these motions in a compact face remains difficult. This paper presents an anatomically inspired facial-robot platform that reproduces the layered organization of the human facial musculoskeletal system. The platform comprises bone, muscle, and skin components whose geometry, shape, and attachment locations are derived from three-dimensional anatomical data. Wire-driven, compliant muscle components are routed through PTFE tubes, allowing actuation to be placed away from the face. We describe the selection and fabrication of 65 muscles (34 muscle types) required to reproduce the 47 target action units (AUs) in the Facial Action Coding System (FACS). We then evaluate the reproducibility of individual AUs in two stages: muscle actuation without skin and actuation after the silicone skin is attached. Forty of the 47 target AUs were reproduced with the muscle components, and 30 were reproduced after skin attachment. These results demonstrate the feasibility of reproducing a broad range of FACS facial expressions using an anatomically inspired musculoskeletal structure. The proposed platform provides a foundation for the systematic development and evaluation of future anatomically inspired facial robots
