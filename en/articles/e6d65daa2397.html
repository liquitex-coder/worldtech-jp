Read and Ignore: What Self-Directed LLM Agents Do in Public Service Scenarios

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/15
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15460

arXiv:2611.15460v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Underspecified instructions leave LLM agents to decide what to do. In public services, staff write these instructions, but the agent's decisions affect residents, patients, and students. We build four expert-validated public-service scenarios with records and decision levers that can be checked in the files, collect $2{,}400$ trajectories from ten LLMs under four prompt conditions, and compare them with $1{,}000$ runs in a context-free sandbox. Agents read the records in $96.9\%$ of open-ended runs and produce an artifact or a self-chosen task in $18.7\%$; with an explicit task, $86.0\%$ produce. Domain context does not change how many runs produce ($18.7\%$ vs.\ $16.0\%$), and the production ranking of the models largely carries over (Spearman $0.79$), while the ranking by whether a model reads the records does not ($0.19$). Context cuts boundary-probing turns per run by $60\%$, and some artifacts contradict the records. We contribute a test that a public-service institution can run on its own records before procurement; its rule-based taxonomy needs no labeled data, and scenarios, code, and trajectories are released.
