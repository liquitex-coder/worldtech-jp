DiCT: Teaching X-ray Vision-Language Models to Reason via Imagining CT

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32344

arXiv:2611.32344v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Diagnosing with X-rays often requires reasoning about the 3D locations of abnormalities and their spatial relationships to surrounding anatomical structures, yet such reasoning remains challenging due to the loss of spatial information in 2D projections. To address this challenge, we propose Dreaming in CT (DiCT), a three-stage progressive CT-to-X-ray reasoning transfer framework that leverages a CT vision-language model (VLM) to guide an X-ray VLM. Through this CT-guided transfer, the student acquires CT-informed 3D reasoning capabilities, enabling it to reason directly from 2D X-rays without explicit CT reconstruction at inference time. The framework begins with instruct the dreamer, which equips the X-ray student with initial CT-oriented reasoning capabilities and establishes a structured interface that distinguishes X-ray observations from CT imagination. Building on this structure, visualize the dream introduces CT-guided on-policy distillation (OPD), where the CT teacher selectively guides the X-ray student's reasoning trajectories to transfer 3D anatomical knowledge. Finally, manifest the dream adapts the acquired reasoning capabilities from digitally reconstructed radiographs (DRRs) to real clinical X-rays. Experiments across two VLM backbones on 3D-RAD and ReXVQA demonstrate DiCT's effectiveness across radiological reasoning tasks, achieving a 1.66 percentage point improvement over a CT reconstruction-based baseline on 3D-RAD Abnormality Identification with 12.77$\times$ lower inference computation. Our code is available at https://github.com/HongYep/DiCT.
