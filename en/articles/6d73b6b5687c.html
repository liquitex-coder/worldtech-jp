Edge-Native Multimodal RAG: Hardware-Aware Quantization and Architectural Optimization for Consumer GPUs

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/15
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14924

arXiv:2611.14924v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Deployment of retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) for Edge-AI is constrained by memory and bandwidth limitations. A standard 7B parameter model consumes 14 GB Video Random Access Memory (VRAM) in half-precision (FP16), precluding co-location of vector indices and Key-Value (KV) caches. This paper presents an engineering integration and empirical evaluation of a multimodal RAG framework optimized for consumer-grade Turing hardware (e.g., NVIDIA Tesla T4). Our system-level analysis identifies a critical trade-off: while generative pre-trained transformer quantization (GPTQ) offers superior throughput (51.6 tok/s), it exhibits significant context instability at 2048+ tokens on Turing architectures. Conversely, NormalFloat 4-bit (NF4) maintains robust long-context performance with viable latency (81 ms/tok). By coupling NF4 with a decoupled Florence-2 vision integration, we achieve visual reasoning with only 430 MB VRAM overhead. Furthermore, empirical observations of prompt-induced search space pruning show that structured prompting reduces latency by 47% without altering computational intensity (1.88 GFLOPs/token). The integrated architecture maintains an 8.37 GB footprint, establishing a viable RAG pipeline for 12-16 GB GPUs. Finally, structured few-shot prompting effectively compensates for quantization-induced reasoning loss, restoring mathematical accuracy from 35.0% to 92.0% across 200 trials.
