Matching the Training-Time Weight Rule in Inference-Time Expert Deactivation

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/06
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05804

arXiv:2611.05804v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Expert deactivation at inference time changes both token routing and expert weights, and its effect on performance depends on how these changes are implemented. We compare removal, freezing, remaining-expert renormalization, and unnormalized re-routing on identical disabled sets across five instruction-tuned checkpoints from four model families, with a base OLMoE checkpoint as an additional comparison. We develop a weight-sum model that relates the loss of expert contributions and changes in branch scale to the weight rule used during training. With half of the experts deactivated according to activation-based rankings, remaining-expert renormalization yields the highest perplexity in models trained with raw router probabilities, whereas unnormalized re-routing yields the highest perplexity in models trained with normalized weights. Perplexity ranges from 1.4 to 73 times the intact baseline when the training rule is retained, and from 14 to 28,740 times the baseline under a rule mismatch. Task outcomes vary across evaluation types: on OLMoE, GSM8K strict-match accuracy falls to zero under all four implementations. Extending the model to changes in the number of active experts yields a further prediction, supported by the OLMoE experiments: the perplexity penalty from additional renormalization increases as fewer experts are activated.
