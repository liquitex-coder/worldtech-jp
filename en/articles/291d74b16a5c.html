A low-rank decoder bottleneck bounds reliability in foundation-model perturbation prediction

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/06
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05849

arXiv:2611.05849v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Foundation-model predictors of perturbation response are evaluated primarily by aggregate accuracy. Using their predictions to prioritize experiments requires assessing each prediction's reliability. We audit the state-transition (ST) decoder used in STATE across three encoders (SE-600M, scGPT, and Tahoe-x1) and four cell lines under CRISPRi. The decoder's first attention layer has a low-rank output. Projecting this output onto 4-32 fixed directions estimated from training activations preserves mean per-perturbation Pearson delta correlation (PDC). This low-rank attention bottleneck bounds the decoder's reliability by distance to training responses: PDC declines as the cosine distance from a perturbation's observed response to its nearest training response increases. We propose predicted-NN-distance, the cosine distance from a predicted response to its nearest training response, as a per-prediction confidence score. The score has a mean absolute Spearman correlation of 0.81 with PDC, close to the 0.88 obtained with the observed response. Thresholds calibrated on this score transfer across encoders and cell lines, and the score remains predictive of PDC for drug perturbations.
