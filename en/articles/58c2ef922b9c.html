When, Where, How: Active In-Context Learning for Tabular Foundation Models

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/07
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07886

arXiv:2611.07886v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Tabular Foundation Models (TFMs) adapt to a new task only through the labeled rows in their context. However, labels are expensive to acquire in real-world scenarios due to the need for highly specific domain knowledge to ensure high-quality data. Active in-context learning transfers classical acquisition rules to this setting, in which the model that guides acquisition changes with every label added. We ask how the value of diversity, predictive uncertainty and target-aware information gain changes as a frozen TFM acquires more context. For that, we study Target-Aware Nearest Neighbor (TANN) acquisition and the Expected Predictive Information Gain in Context (EPIC) and evaluate them against classical rules. Our study across twenty benchmarks with two well-established TFMs and classical GBDTs highlights three findings: (i) When: diversity-based acquisition is best during the first acquisition iterations; (ii) Where: labeling pool rows that serve the target inputs via a simple target-aware nearest-neighbor rule matters substantially; (iii) How: EPIC yields the best models at the label budget, retains on average 80--87% of the chance-corrected full-data performance with only 5-10% of the pool labeled on the eight benchmarks with the largest pools, and selects labels that also improve the predictive performance of gradient-boosted decision trees.
