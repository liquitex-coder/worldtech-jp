ReLoco: Unified Humanoid Locomotion and Fall Recovery via Behavior-Conditioned Motion Priors

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/20
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23916

arXiv:2611.23916v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Humanoid robots must recover from falls and resume commanded motion to sustain locomotion. Learning these behaviors in one policy requires supervision that accommodates both upright movement and ground-contact recovery. We present ReLoco, a framework that uses behavior-conditioned adversarial motion priors to learn locomotion, get-up from arbitrary fallen postures, and command resumption within one proprioceptive policy. During training, robot posture selects the expert pool and conditions a shared discriminator with two behavior-specific outputs. Task rewards and shared regularization support command tracking and recovery, while temporary assistance facilitates exploration. A simulation-based refinement stage supplies robot-executed motion references for this supervision. Ablations examine conditional supervision, output separation, and reference quality under matched settings. At a common training iteration, the full policy achieves $96.80\%$ independent get-up success and $96.96\%$ push-recovery success, including command resumption. Its torso planar velocity root-mean-square error is $0.1266$~m/s, $14.7\%$ lower than the conditional single-output variant, with similar joint-angle discrepancy measured by dynamic time warping. Deployment on the 64.5-kg Tiangong~3.0 humanoid demonstrates walking, running, get-up, and locomotion resumption after falls using one proprioceptive actor, without controller switching or on-robot policy fine-tuning.
