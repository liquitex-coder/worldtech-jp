Geo-SMILE: Statistical Model-Agnostic Interpretability with Local Explanations for Geospatial Machine Learning

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31731

arXiv:2611.31731v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Conventional post-hoc explanation methods were developed largely for non-spatial inputs and often provide limited explicit treatment of geographic context. We introduce Geo-SMILE, a model-agnostic framework that distinguishes substantive Feature explanation from geographic-position response through complementary perturbation branches. The Feature branch masks substantive predictors while retaining observed coordinates; the Geo branch relocates geographic units while holding substantive predictors fixed. Following the statistical-distance-weighted surrogate principle of SMILE, input-distribution Wasserstein distances determine coalition locality weights and weighted Ridge surrogates approximate expected black-box predictions. GeoSensitivity, signed in the keep orientation, quantifies fitted-model responses to geographic-player relocation under the specified design. Across crime prediction in Hull, housing-price prediction in Seattle and ride-hailing demand in Chicago, held-out weighted surrogate fidelity ranges from $R_w^2=0.910$ to $0.989$ in the Feature branch and from $0.842$ to $0.906$ in the Geo branch. The framework makes geographic position an explicit object of post-hoc explanation while requiring separate assessment of interpretation, stability and reference dependence.
