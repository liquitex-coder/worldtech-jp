PhysDial: Adapting Pretrained Video Models for PDE Simulation under Changing Physical Conditions

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/21
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23498

arXiv:2611.23498v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Simulating how physical systems respond to changing conditions is central to control, design, and scenario studies. To our knowledge, prior PDE foundation models have not been trained or evaluated on changes within trajectories. Our counterfactual test measures how far predictions move toward a changed solver branch from the same state. Models trained on fixed conditions follow changes only partly. A PDE foundation model given a condition input after pretraining barely responds. PhysDial adapts a pretrained video diffusion transformer to eight two-dimensional PDE families. Coefficients enter with the noise level, condition fields as reference frames, and equation text as a prompt. In Kolmogorov flow, switches within trajectories account for about 5% of the training samples. At matched compute, they halve the error after a change in forcing wavenumber or amplitude. A few trajectories at new amplitudes more than halve extrapolation error. Without physics pretraining, PhysDial matches or exceeds PDE foundation models under training conditions on two turbulent flows. At step 64 on the Kolmogorov test split, PhysDial has 1.07 times the solver's small-scale energy and Walrus 7.1 times. Our experiments suggest that a learned simulator uses a condition only as far as its training data make that condition necessary for prediction. Learning from trajectories that diverge under intervention can help scientific simulators predict what would happen if a system's forcing or parameters changed.
