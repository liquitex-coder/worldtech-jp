Double counting in LLM evidence fusion: exact posteriors separate rule compliance from posterior gain

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/24
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27138

arXiv:2611.27138v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Language models fuse reports in retrieval, multi-agent consensus and agent memory, where treating shared-source reports as independent can double count evidence. A common remedy is an instruction to count each source once. Its value depends on the receiver's raw baseline. Pricing it needs an exact reference. We build the graded-dependence exact reference: binary worlds with single-report reliability 0.65 and four dependence levels, whose disclosed parameters give every world an exact posterior and every rule a target risk. The paired gain protocol scores raw and instructed answers of four configurations on 2,976 worlds. Agreement and gain separate in both directions. At $\delta$ = 0, claude-opus-5-5 and gpt-6.1-sol agreed with the rule within 0.001 in 744 of 744, and the instruction raised their posterior mismatch by 0.044; gpt-6-luna agreed in 165 of 744 and gained 0.0140 (95% interval 0.0064 to 0.0219); claude-haiku-5-5's gain moved from -0.0295 ($\delta$ = 0) to +0.0302 ($\delta$ = 1) (descriptive). The prespecified first-child endpoint contrast, gain at $\delta$ = 1 minus $\delta$ = 0, was 0.0342 (0.0303 to 0.0382). In the preregistered tests, the raw source-weight contrast was positive (0.259, 95% interval 0.182 to 0.337; Holm rejected zero) and the grouping endpoint difference was not detected (0.0004, -0.0023 to 0.0031). This interval excludes differences larger than 0.0031 in magnitude and lies within the registered precision bound of $\pm$0.0046. The reference prices the rule before any model call. The paired gain, estimable as a Brier difference on labelled deployment items, decides adoption per receiver and prompt condition.
