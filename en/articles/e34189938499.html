High-Precision Docking of Differential-Drive Mobile Robots Through LiDAR and Vision Collaboration

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/20
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24191

arXiv:2611.24191v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Precise docking requires a differential-drive robot to correct lateral offset through coupled rotation and translation within a short approach distance. We combine global navigation with terminal control using complementary LiDAR and visual observations. A line fitted to the cabinet front gives normal distance and heading. An AprilTag identifies the workstation; intersecting its center ray with the LiDAR plane determines tangential position. Taught observations define the docking reference and can cancel repeatable fixed biases under unchanged mounting and geometry. Lateral pre-alignment and continuous visual servoing coordinate the approach, with predictive stopping to account for low-speed actuation lag. Across two sessions using the same navigation and docking code at five workstations on Robot 2, all 111 tasks completed, including 101 on the first attempt. Relative to the taught observations, onboard longitudinal, lateral and heading residual root-mean-square values were 1.51 mm, 5.93 mm and 0.238 degrees, respectively. Joint acceptance within plus or minus 10 mm per position axis and plus or minus 1 degree heading was 96.4% (107/111), or 95.5% (106/111) with a plus or minus 0.5 degree heading limit.
