MoE--LoRA Methods for Multilingual Adaptation: A Systematic Empirical Study

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/20
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23158

arXiv:2611.23158v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Routed low-rank adapters offer a parameter-efficient means to improve the performance of large language models (LLMs) across tasks. Hence, it is assumed that they can also be used to improve the support of low-resource languages in LLMs, which currently serve English far better than the thousands of other languages spoken worldwide. However, this assumption has never been verified empirically under a shared multilingual evaluation protocol. We address exactly this gap by comparing eight low-rank adaptation (LoRA) methods on a frozen base model, using 146 training runs across 200 unique languages and more than 10,000 evaluations. Our results suggest that a reduced-scale search can identify viable configurations of LoRA methods but does not reliably predict their relative performance, as the top-ranking configuration is never best at scale. LoRA reliably improves language-model fit on translation data, consistently degrades reading comprehension, and benefits low-resource languages most. These findings suggest that LoRA methods should be regarded as targeted tools rather than general-purpose means for improving the multilingual capabilities of LLMs.
