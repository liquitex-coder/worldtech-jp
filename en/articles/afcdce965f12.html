Teleoperated Haircutting with Delay Compensation and Passivity-Based Control for Unilateral Internet Teleoperation under Real Communication Uncertainties

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/20
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24883

arXiv:2611.24883v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Robotic haircutting requires accurate motion execution and reliable communication to safely reproduce the operator's commands. Internet-based teleoperation is a key enabling technology to remotely perform precise manipulation tasks such as haircutting. However, communication uncertainties, including variable transmission delays and packet jitter, degrade synchronization between the master and slave systems and affect tracking performance. This paper presents a unilateral Internet teleoperation framework for robotic haircutting that takes into account experimentally measured communication uncertainties. Three delay-compensation techniques, namely Zero-Order Hold (ZOH), First-Order Hold (FOH), and Low-Pass Filtering (LPF), are evaluated together with a classical proportional-derivative (PD) controller and a passivity-based synchronization controller. Unlike conventional studies based on synthetic delay models, the proposed framework is validated using communication data acquired from a real cloud-based teleoperation platform, where both packet delay and effective delay are characterized. The results demonstrate that combining delay-compensation techniques with passivity-based synchronization improves the robustness of unilateral Internet teleoperation under realistic communication uncertainties, providing a reliable control framework for robotic haircutting applications.
