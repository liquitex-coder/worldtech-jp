The Industrialization of Noise: Why Automated Proof-Solving is the Same Old Dead End

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31286

arXiv:2611.31286v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Nearly fifty years ago, DeMillo, Lipton, and Perlis argued that mathematical proofs are not static, isolated structures of formal logic, but the products of a vibrant, evolving social process of human critique, internalization, and shared intuition. In October 2026, OpenAI challenged this thesis by releasing a massive repository of 722 automated mathematical manuscripts, claiming full or partial solutions to 372 longstanding open problems. This paper examines the immediate crisis through the lens of the original DLP framework. We argue that the sudden, automated overproduction of dense, unreadable proofs represents an industrial pollution of the mathematical ecosystem. By examining the three fatal structural pillars of this automated approach (Specification, Regress, and Utility), we demonstrate that decoupling the act of mechanical solving from the human social process of understanding produces a sterile repository of dead logical artifacts. The resulting industrialization of unexamined manuscripts is self-fulfilling. Flooding the literature guarantees that the finite supply of human attention will lose the race to separate signal from noise. In this sense, posting a result is not the same act as submitting it to the community processes by which mathematics is vetted, digested, and incorporated into shared knowledge.
