Researcher-in-the-Loop Evolutionary Discovery of Entanglement-Assisted Quantum Code Families

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41183

arXiv:2610.41183v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: LLM-guided program evolution can produce mathematical constructions, but validity, generality, and reproducibility require distinct evidence. In this paper, we develop a researcher-in-the-loop discovery pipeline combining program evolution, exact evaluation, mathematical generalization, and independent verification. We apply it to entanglement-assisted quantum error-correcting codes (EAQECCs). Evolved programs produced finite qubit constructions from specifications that supplied no family formula. Researcher-directed analysis with a language model led to a general construction and proof drafts, which the researchers revised and checked. The resulting family $[[n,1,n-1;n-q-1]]_q$ exists for prime powers $q\ge3$ at $n\ge2q+1$ and for $q=2$ at odd $n\ge5$; Lean checks its formalized algebraic core. With entanglement lifting, it closes 54 listed table gaps. The evidence includes 115 verified code records, ten DRAT-checked nonexistence results, family implementations and preserved search programs. Controlled studies test how research proposals interact with program generation. Under a mutation-and-restart proposal, iterative evolution improves normalized success-curve area in all ten paired runs and raises success at excluded lengths $n=13,15$ from 13.4% to 62.5% and from 0.9% to 33.4%, respectively. The benefit depends on the proposal. Selected programs combine the proposed moves with objective and restart-counter changes. The pipeline connects finite computational discoveries to a proved family and independently checkable constructions.
