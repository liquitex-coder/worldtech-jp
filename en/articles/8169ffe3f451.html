DexWAC: A Unified World-Actor-Critic Model for Dexterous Robot Manipulation

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/26
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32654

arXiv:2611.32654v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Robot demonstrations provide more than actions to imitate: they reveal how actions change the world and how those changes contribute to task success. Recent world-action models (WAMs) extend vision-language-action (VLA) models with the first signal by jointly predicting actions and future observations, yet this predictive ability serves only as an auxiliary loss for policy learning. Meanwhile, most policy improvement methods learn dynamics and values in separate models, increasing training overhead and limiting representation sharing between these complementary objectives. We introduce DexWAC, a unified world-actor-critic model that jointly learns an actor, an action-conditioned world model, and a bootstrapped critic in a single robot pretraining stage. Our key insight is that visual prediction encourages control-relevant features, while value supervision promotes task-relevant representations; together, they shape a representation suited to policy learning. Built on an image-editing backbone, DexWAC predicts, for each action chunk, the visual latent and proprioception at its endpoint together with its reward and continuation, so the world model alone efficiently yields complete imagined transitions without generating intermediate video frames. After adapting the world model and critic on real rollouts, we improve the pretrained actor through either PPO on imagined rollouts or critic-guided best-of-$N$ selection. DexWAC surpasses the large-scale pretrained VLA and WAM baselines in simulation and achieves 75% average success on two real-world tasks, compared with 45% for $\pi_{0.5}$. Its world model is more accurate and faster than video-based baselines, and ablations confirm that learning dynamics and value together improves the policy.
