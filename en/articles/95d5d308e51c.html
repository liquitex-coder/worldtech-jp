Binarization as an Attack Surface in Weightless Neural Networks

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17646

arXiv:2611.17646v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Weightless Neural Networks (WNNs) are lightweight classifiers that binarize their inputs and learn by storing binary patterns in lookup memories, which makes them attractive for resource-constrained tasks such as intrusion detection. Because a small perturbation that crosses no binarization threshold leaves the binary input unchanged, WNNs may appear naturally robust to adversarial examples; a perturbation that does cross a threshold, however, can still change the prediction. We study this tension in WiSARD, the canonical RAM-based WNN, and in two variants that replace its memories with Bloom filters, Bloom WiSARD and BTHOWeN. A continuous perturbation reaches these classifiers only by crossing an encoding threshold, changing at least one binary tuple, and redirecting a memory query; we formalize this mechanism with a threshold-crossing bound and a response-margin certificate for standard Boolean WiSARD. Controlled MNIST experiments compare linear and data-driven thermometer encodings under model-agnostic sensitivity probes, a score-based Simulated Annealing attack, and surrogate-transfer attacks. Changing only the encoding while keeping each model otherwise fixed shows that encoding granularity can trade a small clean-accuracy loss for a large decrease in attack success. On the Bot-IoT, CICIDS, and UNSW-NB15 intrusion-detection datasets, adversarial perturbations lead WNN detectors to classify many attacks as normal traffic and to raise false alarms, even when their aggregate accuracy remains high.
