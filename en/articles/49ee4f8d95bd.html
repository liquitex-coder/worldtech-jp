ProTEUS: A mutation-resolved world model for closed-loop protein evolution

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/12
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13348

arXiv:2611.13348v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Protein engineering requires navigating rugged fitness landscapes, where small sequence changes can produce large functional differences, but experimental screening can only sample a small fraction of sequence space. Thus, a central challenge is to efficiently traverse the large combinatorial space of mutations in silico. We introduce ProTEUS, a mutation-resolved world model for closed-loop protein evolution. Pretrained on deep mutational scanning assays, ProTEUS learns a joint representation of sequence, evolutionary, and structural information using MutationFormer, a mutation-biased attention architecture. Variants are represented as states in this joint latent space, and mutations define actions whose functional consequences are predicted. To support exploration of many mutants, we developed a fast, physics-constrained all-atom predictor that utilizes explicit geometric constraint enforcement to achieve >10 fold increase in speed than AlphaFold3 with guaranteed physical validity. Lastly, ProTEUS refines its prediction and understanding of protein activity through closed loop experimental feedback to guide evolution toward a desired phenotype. ProTEUS surpasses established models in both zero-shot and supervised fine-tuning mutation prediction tasks. We experimentally demonstrate its utility in two closed-loop multi-objective engineering campaigns on aldehyde dehydrogenase and levansucrase, yielding variants with >10 fold higher activity than wild type. ProTEUS provides a world-model framework that connects virtual evaluation of mutational outcomes with experimental learning for efficient protein evolution.
