ISLA: Ensemble learning and domain adaptation for generalizable acute ischemic stroke lesion segmentation in diffusion MRI with clinical validation

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08732

arXiv:2601.08732v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Accurate segmentation of acute ischemic stroke (AIS) lesions on MRI is essential for clinical decision-making. Deep learning methods offer a promising approach to automating this task. However, they often generalize poorly to unseen clinical sites, and few AIS lesion segmentation methods have been externally validated. In this study, we propose $\mathit{ISLA}$ (Ischemic Stroke Lesion Analyzer), a framework for AIS lesion segmentation built on the design principles of $\mathit{nnU\text{-}Net}$. $\mathit{ISLA}$ combines attention modules, deep supervision, and ensemble learning across different input resolutions, convolutional block types, and loss functions. We also introduce $\mathit{ISLA\text{-}MT}$, a variant that leverages unlabeled target-domain images through semi-supervised learning. We trained both approaches on the SOOP and ISLES 2022 datasets and evaluated on a held-out internal validation set of 253 cases, as well as on 590 cases from an independent multicenter clinical cohort. We compared them with three state-of-the-art pre-trained models and with $\mathit{nnU\text{-}Net}$ trained on the same data. $\mathit{ISLA}$ outperformed the pre-trained models on the internal validation set, while both $\mathit{ISLA}$ and $\mathit{ISLA\text{-}MT}$ outperformed them on the external cohort. Although $\mathit{nnU\text{-}Net}$ performed slightly better than $\mathit{ISLA}$ internally, both $\mathit{ISLA}$ variants demonstrated better generalization to the external cohort. Furthermore, $\mathit{ISLA\text{-}MT}$ significantly improved performance over $\mathit{ISLA}$ on the external cohort. The proposed models performed particularly well on low-contrast lesions, and qualitative error analyses suggested greater robustness to acquisition artifacts. Both models are fast ($\sim$ 5 s per case), and their code and weights are publicly available at https://github.com/RocaVincent/isla_segmentation.
