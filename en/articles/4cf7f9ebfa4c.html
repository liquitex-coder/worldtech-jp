Collaborative Language Models for Semantic IoT Data Processing: Validation through Zero-Shot Human Activity Recognition

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33411

arXiv:2611.33411v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Internet of Things (IoT) systems generate low-level sensor observations that are not directly suitable for process analysis and must first be transformed into semantically meaningful events. This paper investigates collaborative language models as a zero-shot semantic abstraction layer for converting such observations into higher-level event and activity labels. We propose a five-stage workflow comprising semantic mapping, semantic aggregation, temporal and contextual aggregation, specialized reasoning, and final event abstraction. The resulting labels and contextual information can subsequently be represented as event records for downstream Process Mining (PM) tasks. The approach is evaluated through Human Activity Recognition on the CASAS Aruba smart home dataset using eleven workflow configurations. The experiments show that collaboration alone does not guarantee improved performance: general-purpose auxiliary SLMs can degrade recognition, whereas assigning complementary specialist roles leads to better results and allows a smaller final classifier to retain much of the performance of a larger model. The selected workflow achieves a macro F1 of 0.643 in the 1.5-day comparison and 0.567 (weighted F1 0.680) in a one-week evaluation. Although trained ML approaches remain more accurate on this dataset, the results demonstrate a different design point: modular semantic event abstraction using off-the-shelf models without task-specific parameter fine-tuning. The findings indicate that the structure of LM collaboration and task decomposition is more important than simply increasing the number of participating models.
