Generative artificial intelligence prompting strategies among Chinese university students across academic tasks

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/01
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39571

arXiv:2610.39571v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) is increasingly embedded in university learning, yet effective human-GenAI interaction depends not only on access to these tools but also on students' ability to formulate, evaluate, and refine prompts for academic tasks. This study examined university students' perceived GenAI prompting capability and reported prompting strategies. Questionnaire data from 234 students were analyzed using descriptive statistics, measurement modelling, scenario comparisons, structural associations, and qualitative coding of optional comments. Students reported using GenAI across diverse academic activities and generally expressed confidence in their prompting capabilities, particularly in providing relevant context and refining prompts following initial outputs. However, prompting strategies showed limited sensitivity to task types: specifying output format was more strongly endorsed in a creative workshop preparation scenario than in a research-oriented assignment, whereas other matched strategies showed no adjusted differences. Students also commonly endorsed both concise instructions and providing all relevant information at the outset, suggesting that effective brevity may depend more on selecting and organizing task-relevant information than on prompt length alone. By contrast, fixed prompt templates received lower endorsement than other prompting strategies. In the adopted factor model, perceived cognition showed the clearest unique association with perceived prompting capability, while open-ended responses highlighted prompt-intent alignment as a common challenge.
