TACTUS: Unifying Surgical Action Understanding in Time and Space

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/26
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31956

arXiv:2611.31956v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Advancing surgical automation requires a comprehensive spatio-temporal understanding of how instruments act on tissue throughout an action. Existing surgical benchmarks, however, largely define supervision through task-specific targets, leaving the relationships between action semantics, instrument motion, and tissue interaction incompletely specified. To address this limitation, we introduce TACTUS (Tool, Action, Contact and Trajectory Understanding in Surgery), a new dataset and benchmark that establishes a unified annotation framework for complete surgical actions. The framework jointly specifies tool and action identities, nested action and interaction windows, instrument-specific landmark trajectories, and tissue-region tracks. To construct these annotations at scale, we develop a two-tier pipeline combining human annotation, point tracking, and model-generated spatial prompts. Using this pipeline, we re-annotate 141 videos from five public sources, producing 22,995 action clips spanning 2.9 million frames. The dataset comprises 15,668 Gold clips with human spatial prompts followed by review and re-checking, and 7,327 Silver clips combining human semantic and temporal annotations with model-generated spatial prompts. We further introduce TactusNet, the companion reference network, to benchmark semantic, temporal, and spatial prediction from shared video features. Across TactusNet and 13 published baselines, tool-action accuracy reaches 95.8% with per-head encoder fine-tuning, while the best temporal recall and contact Dice reach 46.6% at temporal IoU 0.7 and 0.351, respectively. Moreover, adding Silver improves the default TactusNet's pair accuracy from 93.8% to 95.5%. Together, these contributions establish a foundation for learning and evaluating the instrument-tissue interactions underlying future surgical automation.
