Self-training for cross-centre coronary vessel segmentation in X-ray angiography using pre-contrast frame subtraction

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/01
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40302

arXiv:2610.40302v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Deep learning models for coronary vessel segmentation in X-ray angiography often perform poorly on data from centres that were not represented in training. Apart from differences in equipment, the labels of the largest public dataset (ARCADE) are incomplete, as only the segments defined in the SYNTAX score are annotated. In this study, we evaluated whether self-training with pseudo-labels can improve performance on external data without additional manual annotation. Pseudo-labels were generated from two sources, namely the predictions of an nnU-Net model trained on ARCADE and a contrast map obtained by subtracting a pre-contrast frame from the same sequence. The nnU-Net predictions were extended along the contrast map, and pixels detected only in the subtraction image were assigned an ignore label and excluded from the loss. Six models were trained and tested on ARCADE and on two external datasets (XCAD and XACV). All first-round models initialised with the baseline predictions performed better than both the baseline and a control model fine-tuned for the same number of epochs. The model trained on the XCAD pool improved the macro-averaged Dice score by 2.7 points (95% CI 2.3-3.2), while the control decreased it by 0.6 points. A second round of self-training did not lead to further improvement. When an external model trained on 50 fully annotated images was used to generate the initial labels, the XCAD Dice score increased from 0.623 to 0.745 without any XCAD labels, although performance on XACV did not improve because large main vessels were missed. These results suggest that the benefit of self-training depends largely on the sensitivity of the model used to generate the initial pseudo-labels.
