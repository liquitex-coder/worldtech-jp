TOMAS: Topology-Aware Operation Modeling and Adaptive Staging for Egocentric Hand Forecasting

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/21
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23970

arXiv:2611.23970v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Forecasting future hand movements from egocentric videos plays an important role in understanding human intentions and hand-object interactions. However, different manipulation intentions can lead to substantially different hand trajectories even when interacting with the same object. Existing methods have not sufficiently explored the intrinsic relationships between articulated hand movements and object semantics to capture such operation-dependent motion patterns. Therefore, we propose TOMAS, a topology-aware operation modeling and adaptive staging framework for egocentric hand forecasting. Specifically, we develop a topology-aware hand-object reasoning mechanism that associates hand skeletal structures and articulation dynamics with object semantics and spatial relationships to jointly infer potential interaction targets and latent manipulation operations. The resulting object-operation representations provide interaction conditions for future hand trajectory prediction, with supervision adapted to the annotations available in each dataset. Furthermore, we introduce an adaptive staging mechanism that organizes future hand movements into ordered latent stages with learnable temporal boundaries. Stage-specific geometric subgoals are then incorporated to guide trajectory refinement according to the underlying manipulation patterns. Experimental results on multiple publicly available benchmarks demonstrate that TOMAS achieves state-of-the-art performance in multi-dimensional and multi-target hand motion forecasting.
