Improving Building Energy Efficiency with an Indoor Environmental World Model and Counterfactual Adaptive Learning

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33764

arXiv:2611.33764v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Optimizing indoor climate systems plays a critical role in achieving the United Nations' Sustainable Development Goals 11 and 13. However, indoor delayed thermodynamic responses and partial observability severely hinder existing reinforcement learning (RL) methods, which are primarily limited by implicit thermal inertia, occupancy dynamic prediction, and cumulative prediction errors during long-horizon rollouts. Thus, we propose a novel occupant-centric indoor climate control framework. It develops indoor environmental world models to predict future physical thermal baselines and dynamic occupancy, explicitly capturing latent thermal inertia. By formulating adaptive regulation as counterfactual advantage learning against future environments via a Branching Dueling Q-Network, and integrating a Held-Action TD-{\lambda} target, it effectively mitigates delayed credit assignment. Additionally, a proposed physics-aligned smoothness regularization suppresses high-frequency actuator fluctuations, potentially extending equipment service life. Experiments on SemiBuildingSim and Sinergym demonstrate that our method achieves a superior multi-objective trade-off. Compared to the best-performing methods, it further reduces energy consumption by 11.2%, occupant discomfort by 44.2%, and action fluctuation by 47.3%, achieving better sample efficiency and cross-region effectiveness. This interdisciplinary study offers an energy-efficient, occupant-centric AI solution for control science, building science, and energy sustainability.
