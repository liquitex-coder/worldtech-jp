Decision Models Are General-Purpose Process Reward Models

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/24
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27068

arXiv:2611.27068v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Reinforcement learning (RL) from verifiable outcomes gives a language agent one reward per episode: every step is credited alike, and nothing about the task is learned when all rollouts fail. The last mile, step-level credit, usually requires step labels, a learned critic, costly LLM judges or hand-written rules. We introduce DM-PRM, which turns a general-purpose decision model -- one answering yes/no questions with probabilities -- into a general-purpose process reward model (PRM), zero-shot: no step labels, no fine-tuning, no critic. DM-PRM asks one question per step and adds the standardised answers to the advantage. It plugs into any policy-gradient method; one model, with one question text per environment, serves household tasks, web shopping and multiple RL methods. With Qwen2.5-1.5B it lifts GRPO by over twenty points on ALFWorld and over ten on WebShop, overtakes GiGPO on ALFWorld and learns in far fewer iterations; it also improves GiGPO and GraphGPO on both benchmarks and reaches the highest success of our comparison, ahead of critic-based PPO and of state-of-the-art GiGPO, HGPO and GraphGPO. Its scores locate useful steps in failed rollouts, and trained agents drop idle actions and explore instead of circling. DM-PRM shows that step credit need not be learned: it can be asked for.
