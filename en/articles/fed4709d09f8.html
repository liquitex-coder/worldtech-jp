Choosing Supervised Post-Processing for Frozen Person Detectors: Information, Object Changes, and Complete Cost

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/21
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24003

arXiv:2611.24003v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: A frozen detector computes hypotheses that can support better final outputs through supervised post-processing. Choosing a readout requires knowing which available information improves detection and what its processing costs. We compare candidate scores, class distributions, geometry, native-set summaries and adapted neighborhood meta classification under common candidate, supervision and operating-point conditions. On 320 held-out CrowdHuman images, adding native-set context to a 97-dimensional readout yields 275 more matches and 96 more unmatched predictions. On a separate 319-image cohort, Full100 matches 6004 objects,versus 5848 for source-selected raw WBF and 6002 for a MetaDetect-style adaptation. Its gain over raw contains 300 added and 144 lost discoveries. After execution-equivalent optimization, Full100 and the meta-statistics readout both take approximately 95 ms per complete warm request on a fixed 64-image workload. Source-frozen transfer gives positive point results on 223 MOT17Det frames from seven sequences, while the main MIAP result shows no improvement. The study supports using sufficiently informative output readouts while retaining optimized meta classification as a competitive option. It also shows why feature dimension and net detection gain alone are unreliable guides to adoption.
