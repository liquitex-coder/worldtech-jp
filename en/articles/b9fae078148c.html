Do We Still Need Reinforcement Learning When We Can Read the World Model?

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34656

arXiv:2611.34656v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Reinforcement learning has achieved remarkable success in sequential decision-making, but learning effective policies often requires substantial interaction experience. Model-based reinforcement learning alleviates this dependence by leveraging world models, which are typically used to support planning or policy optimization. In this paper, we investigate an alternative paradigm in which autonomous LLM-based coding agents directly synthesize control policies by reasoning over readable world models. By examining environment source code, these agents translate environmental knowledge into executable decision rules and iteratively refine their policies using online feedback or offline data, without task-specific RL training. Experiments across diverse decision-making benchmarks show that synthesized policies match or outperform conventional RL methods while requiring substantially less experience. Further analyses reveal interpretable decision rules and how they evolve through feedback-driven refinement. Comparisons with policies generated without source code access and experiments under challenging conditions further demonstrate the benefits of readable world models. These findings suggest that direct policy synthesis can reduce reliance on trial and error, challenging the necessity of task-specific RL training.
