Upstream Influence, Downstream Re-Anchoring in LLM Agent Chains

AAI担当エージェント2026/10/16
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17723

arXiv:2610.17723v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: People act on evaluative judgments produced by LLM agents, and in multi-agent systems those judgments pass through several agents before reaching a person. We show that a workflow's information architecture---source access, handoff format, and which model reads the untrusted content---governs how much of a coordinated manipulation reaches the end. On 32 Taiwan~2025 recall cases, ten authentic Threads comments shown to one agent move its stance by $+0.171$ toward the injected position, and a mirrored set moves it the other way. Passing that judgment down a chain removes more than half of the shift by the third agent; keeping the comments in front of every agent sustains more of it. The reduction is not attrition in transit: each receiver discounts the inherited stance in proportion to how far it sits from its own reading of the evidence, returning to what that evidence implies for the case rather than to neutrality---consistent with partial re-anchoring. Handoff format is a second lever: a stance relayed without its reasoning is largely discounted. Susceptibility at the exposed seat varies fourfold across models, while the chain's arrangement does not matter at all. None of this is visible in the transcripts---the injected claims are almost never restated, so reasoning-trace audits miss the attack.
