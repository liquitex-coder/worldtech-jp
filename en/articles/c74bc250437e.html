TriSAR-3D and PaintFusion: A Novel Trimodal LiDAR-RGB-Thermal Dataset and Method for 3D Person Detection in Search and Rescue

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/10/20
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22509

arXiv:2610.22509v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Reliable 3D person detection is important for rescue robotics to give first responders situational awareness. Yet most multimodal 3D detection research targets automotive scenarios in which pedestrians mostly appear in a narrow range of upright, near-road poses. It is therefore unclear how much of the reported performance transfers to rescue settings, as a person may be lying, partially occluded, or in unstructured indoor and outdoor environments. To investigate this gap, we introduce TriSAR-3D, a multimodal person detection dataset recorded with a high-resolution 128-channel LiDAR, an RGB camera, and a thermal camera. It captures varied poses in indoor as well as outdoor locations at different times of day to study their influence on 3D object detector performance. Furthermore, we propose a novel trimodal 3D detection pipeline, PaintFusion, that fuses PointPainting-based evidence using a simple MLP. We show that the derived method reaches 65.4 AP@50 and thereby outperforms strong LiDAR-only (CenterPoint, 53.4), RGB+LiDAR (BEVFusion, 60.5), and adapted trimodal (Tri-BEVFusion, 60.7) baselines on the dataset. At the same time, it is substantially faster than other fusion-based methods such as BEVFusion, with a per-frame latency of 51.6 ms compared to 101 ms. Additionally, we test how the performance of different 3D object detectors generalizes to unseen pose categories. Removing lying instances from training data collapses the lying-pose AP@50 to near zero across all evaluated detectors. The results indicate that dataset diversity exerts a large effect on 3D object detection performance. The dataset and code are available at https://github.com/MarkusKramer1/3D-Trimodal-People-Detection.
