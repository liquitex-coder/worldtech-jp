Beyond Self-Critique: Epistemic Convergence Control for Multi-Agent Research Verification

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/24
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27839

arXiv:2611.27839v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Multi-agent systems for scientific claim verification increasingly rely on role-structured LLM debate to pressure-test hypotheses. However, when debating agents are instantiated from the same underlying base model, they share identical latent representations, making self-critique bias structurally inevitable. Static fixed-iteration protocols compound this by either halting prematurely before evidence stabilizes or wasting inference compute after convergence. We present \sys{}, a multi-agent research verification framework that addresses both failure modes. First, we introduce an asymmetric role architecture comprising an Advocate, Skeptic, and Arbitrator with opposing epistemic directives, producing significantly lower role-embedding similarity (0.852 vs.\ 0.918 for symmetric Multi-Agent Debate [MAD], $p = 5.82 \times 10^{-8}$) and surfacing more contested claims (22.0\% vs.\ 15.4\%). Second, we propose the Belief-Delta ($\DB$) convergence criterion, an evidence-grounded stopping rule that eliminates over-iteration (0.0\% vs.\ 62.5\% for Fixed-$3$) while cutting LLM calls by 2.4$\times$. Third, each supported claim is anchored to a verbatim literature snippet audited by an independent NLI classifier, achieving a 21.7\% hallucination rate (comparable to 25.9\% for a Self-Refine critic, $p = 0.61$) while establishing auditable evidence grounding. Across 200 runs on 40 research hypotheses evaluated with DeepSeek-R1-32B, \sys{} balances compute efficiency with rigorous epistemic verification.
