TelePhysica: Agentic Reconstruction of Physically Interactive Scenes

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18078

arXiv:2611.18078v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Direct image-to-video diffusion models entangle pixel synthesis with implicit latent motion, precluding persistent 3D mechanical equilibrium, mid-rollout intervention, and counterfactual force control. Conversely, single-pass programmatic 3D reconstruction from an uncalibrated photograph leaves monocular depth and support clearance underconstrained, causing reconstructed scenes to interpenetrate or collapse under gravity. We present TelePhysica, a closed-loop framework that reconstructs an executable, multi-regime physical world from a single photograph to drive stateful 3D interaction and trajectory-grounded video synthesis. A vision-language coding agent synthesizes separable visual meshes and declarative physical models within a two-stage verification loop that pairs pixel-aligned silhouette critique with a zero-force gravitational equilibrium preflight. The verified world compiles into a unified solver coupling rigid bodies, articulated chains, co-dimensional cloth shells, and 3D tetrahedral elastodynamics, whose simulated rollouts condition pretrained video diffusion models through pixel-aligned structural streams. On a 100-scene single-image benchmark, closed-loop execution raises equilibrium-verified world recovery from $0\%$ in single-pass synthesis to $81\%$. Controlled constitutive ablations show over an order-of-magnitude larger localized corner displacement and out-of-plane pleating in finite-element cloth than in matched rigid-plate proxies. Conditioning video diffusion on simulated trajectories increases dynamic motion-mask overlap by $2{\times}\text{--}5{\times}$ and preserves motion direction under counterfactual force reversals where direct image-to-video models fail.
