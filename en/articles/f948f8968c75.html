From Bias to Positionality: A critical grammar for Large Language Models

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/12
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14079

arXiv:2611.14079v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Bias has become a default lens for understanding what machine learning systems do and how we should relate to them. Bias is at root a deviation from a standard; in decision making systems we can supply a standard from outside: a law, a protected attribute, a benchmark, a statistical property. However, even there, biased is often used as a shorthand, without stating what the position taken is - a silent ``relative to''. In a general use of language models, nothing supplies the standard: there is no decision to audit and no counterfactual output, so the shorthand fails. We argue that positionality -- the located, articulated, owned and contestable position from which anything is said -- is the better grammar for the question these models now force: who is speaking, from where? Against the vendors' answer of nowhere and the seductive possibility of everywhere, we argue that when a model is taken as-if speaking, it speaks from somewhere, and this means positionality can describe models, and the way they are believed beyond their warrant, even though they are not accountable speakers. A model's positionality is laminar -- laid down across corpus, tuning, system prompt, application and the live conversation. We separate three sets of factors: the positionality of a model's creators, the behaviour they intend for it, and the behaviour it enacts, and situate current work within this matrix. This lets us highlight what each stratum inherits from the one below, with ruptures and collapses where that inheritance is disavowed. We then return to where models speak from, and discuss the implications for how models are made, read and held accountable.
