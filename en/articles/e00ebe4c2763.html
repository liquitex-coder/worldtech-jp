Calibration of Neuromusculoskeletal Models Using Differentiable Physics: Recommendations for Improving Model Accuracy

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34428

arXiv:2611.34428v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Differentiable physics has shown potential to usher in a new paradigm in human neuromusculoskeletal (NMS) model calibration. Early results from pioneering works have demonstrated that auto-differentiable NMS models can greatly reduce NMS model calibration time while achieving comparable accuracy compared to traditional calibration methods. The efficient gradient-based optimizers have enabled calibration of increasingly sophisticated human models with a large number of parameters, which seek to narrow the gap between simulation and reality. Several modules have been evaluated separately in different studies, but have never been directly compared within the same study. In this work, these modules are compared on the same differentiable upper limb model with a 3-DoF shoulder, a 2-DoF elbow, and 11 superficial and 5 deep muscles in the calibration to find an optimal pipeline for improving model accuracy. The considered modules include muscle synergy, learnable bone parameters, optimizable geometry of wrap objects, and constraint torque of the differentiable arm model. To evaluate the effect and interplay between the modules on calibration accuracy, one or more modules were used in each of 16 calibration configurations, which were compared in terms of the error between two joint torques calculated by the forward and inverse dynamics with the calibrated model on 9 distinctive testing movements across 4 subjects. Results showed that torque loss was reduced by 23.6% on average using muscle synergy extrapolation to estimate deep muscle activities, and by 10.9% on average through bone dynamic parameter optimization, which are both recommended to be incorporated in the calibration.
