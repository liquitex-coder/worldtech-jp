Defining and Generating Compositional Structure

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/10/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20922

arXiv:2610.20922v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Compositional structure is pervasive in nature and across scales: atoms combine into molecules, molecules into cells, and cells into organisms, while further compositional hierarchies appear within each level. More broadly, compositionality, the reuse and recombination of parts into novel structures, is a fundamental source of interesting complexity in both natural and artificial systems. Yet despite its importance, and despite our apparent ease in recognizing it, mathematical accounts capturing what we intuitively mean by compositional structure remain limited. Here, we fill this gap by formalizing a simple idea: compositional structure emerges when parts, considered together, reveal shared structure that is not captured when those parts are modeled separately. We express this idea using algorithmic information theory, the mathematics of information and compression, which provides a natural way to identify structure in arbitrary data without specifying in advance what that structure must look like. Applied across scales, our definition informs where and how compositional structure emerges within an object. Using tractable approximations, we then invert the framework to generate compositional objects: we specify the desired form of their compositional structure without prescribing their particular content, and optimize directly over strings and images. The resulting objects exhibit readily recognizable hierarchical reuse and part-whole organization, suggesting that the definition captures compositional structure rather than merely complexity, regularity, or shared information. Finally, we discuss implications for machine learning, including a new perspective on depth in neural architectures and principles for constructing compositional data curricula capable of supporting open-ended learning.
