Exploring Multi-Player Social Games as Verifiable Training Grounds for Large Language Models

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31821

arXiv:2611.31821v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: The training of socially intelligent language models often relies on large language model (LLM) judges, which are expensive, noisy, and vulnerable to reward hacking. Multi-player social games offer a natural alternative: they require open-ended communication to coordinate with teammates and infer hidden roles, while their rules provide an objective outcome which can serve as a verifiable reward for training. Using three social games, we explore how much social intelligence Qwen3 models can acquire through supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR). Results show that against a much stronger Gemma-3-27B model, SFT and RLVR raise the win rate of Qwen3-4B from 33% to 81% on the three trained games, and from 39% to 54% on three additional held-out games. In contrast, in-context strategies such as prompting improve the win rate of Qwen3-4B by only about 6 pp across the same games we train and evaluate on. Furthermore, we show that the gains from SFT and RLVR hold across the model sizes 1.7B, 4B, and 32B. Qualitative analyses of agent behavior show signs of stronger social intelligence; for example, trained agents tend to share information more openly, check each other's claims against public evidence, and coordinate their votes. More broadly, our results demonstrate that training LLMs on verifiable outcomes in social games can cultivate generalizable social behaviors, which offers a scalable path for building socially intelligent agents.
