MEM-STRESS: Longitudinal Field Study of a Wearable-Informed AI Chatbot for Stress Management

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/21
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23691

arXiv:2611.23691v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Stress is an increasingly prevalent concern, creating a need for accessible and personalized support. Stress-management chatbots primarily rely on what users report, which may not capture their physiological changes. Wearables capture these changes, but chatbots rarely use them as memory across conversations. We therefore present MEM-STRESS, a stress-support chatbot that turns a wearable ring's physiological data into a layered memory of daily records, baseline deviations, LLM-generated summaries, and uses it to drive multi-session conversations grounded in Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT). In a four-week, non-randomized study with 60 adults, we compared MEM-STRESS (Treatment) with a chatbot remembering only past conversations (Control). Memory shaped the conversations: The Treatment chatbot referenced participants' physiological features (e.g., activity in 71.8% of sessions) and suggested more body-related DBT skills than Control (14.5% vs. 5.7%), with Treatment participants also using slightly more body-related words (1.76% vs. 1.45%). Treatment participants praised personalization three times as often (44.4% vs. 14.3%), more frequently reported reflecting differently on stress (69.0% vs. 46.4%, not significant), and described gaining insight into their stress signals. Their perceived stress fell 14.2% from baseline versus 3.6% for Control, though the group difference was not significant. Grounding stress-support chatbots in layered physiological memory is thus feasible and may improve perceived personalization, motivating larger randomized studies.
