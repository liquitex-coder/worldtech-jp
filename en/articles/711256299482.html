Reusing Locomotion Training Recipes Across Humanoids via Body-Aligned Virtual Joints

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18003

arXiv:2611.18003v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Reinforcement learning has enabled robust humanoid locomotion, yet training recipes (rewards, architectures, hyperparameters) remain hard to reuse across robots with different native joint coordinates, since policies output native joint angles while action scales, bounds, gains, and penalties are defined in those robot-specific coordinates. Existing transmission models convert native targets into actuator commands but do not standardize the coordinate interface exposed to the policy. We introduce a body-aligned virtual joint space upstream of this mapping: policy actions and observations follow a common anatomical convention, and a deterministic kinematic layer maps virtual targets to native ones, leaving the existing transmission and low-level control stack unchanged. Under a single unmodified recipe on three humanoids with different leg-axis layouts, it is the only interface that tracks accurately, keeps the feet aligned with the travel direction, and trains stably on all three, and on Unitree G1 hardware it reduces heading drift by nearly a factor of six.
