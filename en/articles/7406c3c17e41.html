What's in a Tree ? Hierarchical $n$-gram models as controlled approximators of language

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/20
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23093

arXiv:2611.23093v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Hierarchical models have been proposed as synthetic settings for studying language. However, their relevance to realistic language corpora remains underexplored. In this work, we introduce tree-grams, autoregressive generative $n$-gram models that build the conditional probability tensor $p(x_n \mid x_{1:n-1})$ through a hierarchical composition of order-2 and order-3 tensors. $n$ controls the maximal context window, whereas a rank hyperparameter $d$ controls model capacity, yielding an interpretable and systematically scalable family of approximators of language. We show how tree-grams outperform existing $n$-gram baselines, while remaining competitive with deep transformer-based language models. We demonstrate the utility of such controlled approximations by using tree-grams as generative models. In particular, we show that hyperparameter transfer under $\mu$P can be made more robust when narrower models are tuned on simpler data, generated by tree-grams with moderate $n$ and $d$. In addition, we show that entropy and effective range of correlations control performance gap after fine-tuning. This is because tree-grams allow us to explicitly control horizon and capacity. When allowed to route across candidate topologies, we observe emergent sparsity in tree-grams. Such concentrated routing permits pruning upto 95\% of topologies without significant change in performance.
