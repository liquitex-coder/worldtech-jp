Archetypal contexts in the neurons of language models: an architecture for intelligence?

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/07
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07234

arXiv:2611.07234v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: We validate our previous hypothesis that a language model holds relational patterns apart from the subject domains that instantiate them (Nordfors, 2026). These archetypal contexts are patterns of relations that people recognize across many fields, such as the tipping point or winner takes all, held once and applied across domains. We show this inside the model, Qwen3.8-27B. One set of neurons carries the archetype, say the tipping point; another carries the topic, say politics, ecology or cell biology; a third carries the language, such as English, Spanish or Chinese. Silence the tipping point's neurons, and the model no longer identifies the pattern but still knows what the text is about. Silence the topic's, and it loses the topic but still names the pattern. In the neuron maps the layers organize themselves into a sandwich: the outer layers carry languages, the layers inside them topics, the layers at the heart archetypes. The sandwich emerges spontaneously during pretraining and appears in every model we mapped. Archetypes are the architecture for analogy, and we strongly suggest that this structure of archetypes and topics is the mechanism of a language model's intelligence: we see it self-organize as the model is trained. The neurons, the texts and the code are released with this paper as its reproduction package, so that anyone can repeat the test inside the model.
