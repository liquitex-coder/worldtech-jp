Tractable Importance Sampling for Monte Carlo Privacy Accounting

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17596

arXiv:2611.17596v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Evaluating tight Differential Privacy (DP) parameters for DP matrix factorization with privacy amplification by sampling requires analyzing high-dimensional, correlated Gaussian mixtures. Because integrating these mixture distributions to find the exact privacy failure probability ($\delta$) is analytically intractable, these mechanisms are typically analyzed using Monte Carlo accounting. However, standard sampling suffers from prohibitive variance when estimating small $\delta$ associated with strict privacy guarantees, which in turn requires a large sample complexity, making Monte Carlo accounting inefficient. We demonstrate that Monte Carlo accounting can be made much more efficient via importance sampling. We give a shifted importance sampling proposal distribution parameterized by a stretch factor $\alpha > 1$. We prove a closed-form bound $B(\alpha) < 1$ on the support of the importance-weighted estimator that holds identically for both directions of the hockey-stick divergence, show that $B$ is log-convex so that the optimal $\alpha$ is a one-dimensional root-finding problem, and show that the resulting estimator seamlessly plugs into the KL-Hoeffding verification protocol used by past work, with a sample complexity reduced by exactly a factor of $1/B(\alpha^*)$.
