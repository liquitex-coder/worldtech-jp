Spokes, not a Ring: Revisiting the Weekday Manifold in Language Models

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/01
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37796

arXiv:2610.37796v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Recent work has hypothesised that large language models may represent some concepts as multi-dimensional manifolds instead of single directions. Using these manifolds to interpret or steer representations often assumes that moving along them corresponds to a continuous change in the represented concept. In this work, we test this continuous manifold assumption on the prototypical weekday manifold. Despite its striking circular geometry, we find that the weekday representations behave more like seven discrete states arranged in a circle than a continuous ring. First, we show that local sensitivity has seven minima aligned with the weekdays, producing a step-like mapping between representation and behaviour under on-manifold steering. Second, we test whether any concepts are represented along the manifold outside the seven discrete states, and find neither time of day, uncertainty, nor natural text populate the intermediate space. Together, these results challenge the continuous interpretation of the weekday manifold and motivate testing whether movement along other representation manifolds correspond to meaningful changes in model representations and behaviour.
