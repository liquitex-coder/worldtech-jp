Aligned, Conflicting, or Unspecified? A Diagnostic Benchmark for Multimodal Time Series Forecasting

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17680

arXiv:2611.17680v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Artificial Intelligence of Things (AIoT) systems increasingly combine sensed time series with external text such as expert reports and news, yet how reliably models use text that agrees or conflicts with the sensed history remains unclear. We introduce the first real-world diagnostic benchmark organized by text-history relationship: 1,762 text-window pairs across sensor telemetry (Weather), web-service traffic (Wikipedia), and market data streams (Finance), each labeled Aligned, Conflicting, or Unspecified by whether the text's direction agrees with the historical trend, opposes it, or is not stated. Across ten pretrained models evaluated zero-shot, we investigate text selection and combination under text-history relationships (RQ1) and error diagnosis and horizon-scaled revision control (RQ2). For RQ1, we compare two types of text on paired Weather windows: one directly forecasts temperature changes, while the other describes related weather conditions, such as winds and fronts. Under Aligned, the first type yields the lowest error for most models. Under Conflicting, it increases most models' error above the no-text baseline; we therefore add only its stated magnitude and timing to the second type. Compared with either type alone, this combination lowers mean absolute scaled error (MASE) for six models and raises directional accuracy (DA) for seven. For RQ2, an error decomposition attributes the error increase to oversized revisions, and a training-free rule that limits them outperforms forecasts both with and without text for the four models with this trade-off, lowering MASE by 4.49%-21.98% and raising DA by 2.99%-9.71%. Our dataset is available at https://github.com/Shelton1202/ACU-Bench.
