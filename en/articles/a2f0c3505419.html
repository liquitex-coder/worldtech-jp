Cross-Linguistic Acoustic Biomarkers of Cognitive Impairment Across Interactive and Single-Speaker Speech Tasks

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17710

arXiv:2611.17710v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Multilingual dementia detection is essential for scalable and equitable screening. In recent years, automated speech analysis has emerged as a promising approach for supporting dementia detection by enabling non-invasive and scalable assessment of cognitive function. However, existing studies that use automated speech analysis often rely on one type of task, such as picture description, and use black-box models. These studies often neglect the role of task and language. To address these gaps, we performed a cross-linguistic and cross-task acoustic analysis that integrates a conversational dataset with structured speech tasks across four languages (Spanish, Mandarin, English, Japanese). We applied statistical, linguist-/clinician-guided, and machine learning model-based feature selection to derive compact acoustic feature sets while maintaining classification performance. This enabled systematic comparison of cognitive impairment-relevant features across datasets and tasks. Our results revealed that acoustic features did not generalize uniformly across tasks and languages. Temporal features remained robust across all datasets, consistently capturing timing disruption regardless of language or task. In contrast, speech rate showed strong task dependence -- emerging as informative mainly in single-speaker tasks with externally anchored content and pacing, but becoming less reliable when speech content was highly individualized. Pitch-related features further exhibited language-specific patterns, favoring chroma-related features in tonal languages (e.g., Mandarin) and F0-related features in non-tonal languages. These findings caution against indiscriminate pooling of heterogeneous speech data and underscore the need for interpretable, context-aware approaches to multilingual dementia detection using automated speech analysis.
