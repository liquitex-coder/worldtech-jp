Modeling Event-Conditioned Emotional Expression Response with LLM Agents in a Synthetic Social Simulation

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31851

arXiv:2611.31851v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Emotions are central drivers of human behavior, yet most behavioral models fail to capture their role in shaping perception, decision-making, and collective action. Application areas such as emergency response and public health are especially constrained by the lack of emotion-aware modeling. While large language models (LLMs) are increasingly integrated into agent-based models (ABMs), little work has examined how to endow LLM agents with emotional reasoning or validated their ability to reproduce empirically observed emotional patterns. This study addresses this gap by introducing a hierarchical prompting framework that conditions LLM agents with demographic, psychological, and contextual cues to generate event-conditioned emotional responses to real-world events. We validate the resulting event-conditioned emotional responses against large-scale social media reaction data derived from the Social Science One Facebook URL Shares Dataset, complemented by demographic and personality benchmarks. Our simulation results reproduce both polarized and consensus-driven emotional patterns with significant fidelity, supporting the application of LLM agents for emotion-aware modeling in behavioral simulations. We also find, however, that systematic gaps in emotional expression of LLM agents and human populations exist, such as the underexpression of anger relative to human baselines. As such, these findings highlight the need for continued calibration if LLM agents are to serve as proxies for human populations in behavioral modeling. By quantifying their divergence from human populations in terms of emotional expression, we hope that our methods can aid in this effort.
