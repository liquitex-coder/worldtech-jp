Measuring What Matters: Synthetic Benchmarks for Concept Bottleneck Models

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/21
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04326

arXiv:2606.04326v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Concept bottleneck models predict using high-level concepts detected in inputs. Although these models provide a simple way to reap benefits from interpretability, they are hard to train and evaluate because very few real-world datasets include concept labels. In this paper, we develop synthetic benchmarks for concept bottleneck models. With our benchmarks, researchers can generate labeled datasets and control properties that affect performance, such as data modality, the choice of concepts, or annotation quality. They can thus find problems that suit these models and isolate the factors behind their failures. We demonstrate how our benchmarks can be used to evaluate major use cases for concept bottleneck models: decision support, i.e., helping human experts make better decisions; and automation, i.e., handling routine tasks without supervision. Our demonstrations show how the benchmarks can identify new failure modes and attribute them to either the architecture or the data.
