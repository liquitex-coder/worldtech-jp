PEACE-TEST-PROTOCOL: Polarization-Keyed Examination Papers and Mandatory Issued Eyewear that Displace AI Glasses and Extend Universal Design

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31299

arXiv:2611.31299v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Camera-equipped AI glasses turn an examination page into a query for a remote vision-language model. Device detection targets possession, and our companion work SEOYUN targets the model. PEACE-TEST-PROTOCOL instead changes who owns the eyewear. Every candidate wears authority-issued glasses for the whole session, and the question paper is printed so that it can be read only through them. As in cinema stereoscopy, a patterned laminate encodes the text in the handedness of circular polarization rather than in brightness. The page looks uniformly grey to the naked eye and to unfiltered cameras, and the issued circular analyzer reveals the text. Compulsory issued eyewear turns the smart-glasses form factor against itself. The face position is occupied by a device the authority supplies and collects, and a camera anywhere else needs its own analyzer. Spectacle wearers receive prescription inserts in the issued frame, low-vision candidates may use an offline assistive mixed-reality tier, and blind candidates keep braille, audio, and screen-reader formats. We derive the luminance and contrast budget and show why circular keying tolerates head tilt. A simulation study supports the analysis. A spectral Mueller model confirms tilt invariance and invisibility to linear-polarization cameras, but a 1 degree axis misregistration leaks a few percent of contrast. A capture-and-OCR experiment on English and Korean text shows that video averaging can recover leakage too weak to read in a single frame, so leakage must be bounded against the adversary's total photon budget. Data from 151 observers and 28 cameras show that spectral (metameric) keys are visible to all of them. The key carries no secrecy, and filtered covert cameras remain possible. All results are simulations; no prototypes, physical measurements, or human-subject results are reported.
