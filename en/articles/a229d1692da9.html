Between Obligations and Evidence: A Participatory Study of Translating the EU AI Act into Technical Requirements

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18439

arXiv:2611.18439v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: The EU AI Act imposes technical and organizational requirements for high-risk AI systems, but applying these requirements involves contextual choices about what to measure and what evidence supports a compliance claim. We investigate these challenges through a participatory workshop involving 32 researchers, policymakers, and practitioners from different disciplines, using realistic scenarios in healthcare, recruitment, and public transport to examine how regulatory obligations are interpreted, translated into technical requirements, and evaluated in practice. Through a thematic analysis of workshop data, we identify recurring tensions between how regulatory obligations are translated into measurable system properties, how responsibility and human oversight are exercised in practice, and how the validity of compliance evidence is affected by changing operational conditions and system uses. For example, participants highlighted how fairness metrics could conceal discrimination in recruitment, how time pressure and limited information could undermine human oversight in public transport, and how changes in patient populations could invalidate previously established evidence of system performance. Our findings suggest that technical, organizational, and evidentiary challenges are interdependent, and that establishing and maintaining compliance requires attention not only to technical assessments but also to how AI systems are used, governed, and monitored over time. We discuss implications for supporting context-sensitive and ongoing compliance.
