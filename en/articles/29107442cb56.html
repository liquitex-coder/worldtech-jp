What Is a Theory Worth to an LLM Solver? Measuring, Inducing, and Merging Theories in Context

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34751

arXiv:2611.34751v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Mathematicians solve problems faster when they know the right theory. We ask how much a stated theory is worth to a large language model (LLM) solver, and whether a model can write that theory itself. In a length-matched protocol, adding the right theory to the context of Qwen3-14B raises pass@1 on pattern waiting-time problems from 0.37 to 0.83 and cuts the median tokens of correct solutions from 10.2k to 6.3k, while worked examples of the same length do not help. The gain shrinks as the solver and its token budget grow (+0.46, +0.26, +0.07), and at a long budget it comes almost entirely from finishing within budget: solutions that finish are correct 98--100\% of the time in every condition. To separate induction from recall, we plant unpublished theorems in custom probability families and measure how much of the oracle theory's gain an induced theory recovers. A strong open model, required to explore the training problems with code before writing, recovers 60\% of the gain on one planted family, 48\% on a second, and almost none on a third, where it finds the right form but not the condition that makes the form computable. No single induced note is complete, but a strong model that merges several notes recovers 61\% of the gain on the classic family, above the best single note (47\%). Theories are worth the reasoning they save, and models can find their pieces before they can assemble them.
