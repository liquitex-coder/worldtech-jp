VIDE : Training-Free Video Anomaly Detection via Visual Intrinsic Distribution Estimation

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32138

arXiv:2611.32138v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: In embedding-based video anomaly detection (VAD), normal clips can have high similarity to anomalous descriptions, an anomaly bias that leads to false positives. Methods such as PRISM mitigate this bias by measuring deviations from a normality reference along anomaly directions. Both the normality reference and the anomaly directions are derived from text and shared across videos, although normal activity and the appearance of anomalies vary across scenes. The resulting scores are also sensitive to prompt selection. To address these limitations, we propose Visual Intrinsic Distribution Estimation (VIDE), a training-free framework that adapts text-derived scoring to each test video using frozen clip embeddings. VIDE estimates normal scene content from clips with low initial anomaly scores and uses this estimate to partially correct the textual reference. It then rescales category responses, penalizes clips with strong responses across categories, and refines the anomaly directions using high-scoring clips. VIDE averages scores across prompt views to reduce sensitivity to prompt selection and fuses scores from two frozen encoders. With both encoders, VIDE improves AUC over the best PRISM result by $1.5$, $1.9$, and $3.5$ points on UCF-Crime, XD-Violence, and MSAD, respectively, and AP by $6.5$ points on XD-Violence and $9.3$ points on MSAD. VIDE yields the highest AUC among the compared training-free methods on all three benchmarks and the highest reported AUC among all compared methods on XD-Violence and MSAD. Our code is included in the supplementary material and will be made publicly available.
