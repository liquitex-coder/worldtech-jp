SuperGlance: Driving at a Glance with a Vision-Language Model

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/20
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19138

arXiv:2611.19138v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Autonomous driving is turning to vision-language-action models (VLAs). They answer and plan by decoding, one step at a time. Each step is another forward pass, so a plan can take over a second of GPU time. Embodied AI often pairs such a slow, reasoning System 2 with a fast System 1 that acts. Human drivers plan at a glance, with System 1. Faster designs usually put System 1 outside the vision-language model (VLM), in a planner without its abilities. Yet before a VLM writes its first token, it has already read the scene into one hidden state. We show that, once trained, this hidden state is enough to drive and to answer questions about what the model sees. SuperGlance reads it to turn a decoder-only VLM into an encoder-style driving policy. Its linear head scores a fixed set of 5,273 candidate trajectories. With the set fixed, rewards are computed offline, so supervised targets can be reward-weighted and reinforcement learning maximizes the exact expected reward. Before and during policy training, multiple-choice training keeps the VLM's visual abilities in one pass, so the policy still answers questions about driving scenes. Training questions are built against shortcuts. Each must be answered right in two option orders. Against drift, a KL term keeps each stage near its starting model. One pass offers VLAs a more plausible path to System 1. SuperGlance answers visual multiple-choice questions as well as its base VLM does by decoding. It plans in 84 ms of GPU time, six times faster than Qwen-Drive-1.0, and at least as well on NAVSIM, PAI-AV and WOD-E2E. In AlpaSim closed loop, km per at-fault incident holds for both planners up to a 300-ms delay and drops beyond. With GPU time charged as delay, SuperGlance's lead over Qwen-Drive-1.0 grows from 2.8 to 3.9 times. Delayed by 1.2 s, our model falls below Qwen-Drive-1.0 at its own 518 ms.
