EgoHandWAM: Probing the Role of Hand Motion in Egocentric World Action Modeling

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/19
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22151

arXiv:2611.22151v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: How much does hand motion tell us about what a first-person camera will see next? EgoHandWAM forecasts three-dimensional wrist and fingertip trajectories and a future visual feature from the current image and recent hand states. We train on 751,065 EgoDex windows using a frozen DINOv2 encoder. On held-out development sessions, the action-only model reduces two-second wrist endpoint error from 60.26 to 56.48~mm relative to a stationary forecast. It still underestimates long reaches. Adding future-feature supervision gives nearly identical trajectory accuracy. With the recorded hand history, the visual head reduces smooth L1 error by 1.32\% relative to copying the current feature. Shuffling histories across sessions erases this gain. In a separate four-task probe, adding recorded future displacements to observed context and future tracking-validity masks reduces visual-feature error by 3.93\%. Masks alone give little improvement. A trajectory predicted from the same observations adds little. On two simulated RoboTwin tasks, substituting aligned human examples for an auxiliary batch of robot examples gives no consistent increase in task success across three training seeds.
