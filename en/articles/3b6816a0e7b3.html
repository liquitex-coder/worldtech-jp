LLM-Assisted Reranking to Operationalize Nuanced Objectives in Recommender Systems

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/21
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02883

arXiv:2606.02883v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Recommender systems shape what information we see, raising concerns about filter bubbles, radicalization, polarization, and social inequality. Large language models (LLMs) enable more powerful personalization that may intensify these dynamics, yet most recommenders are tuned for engagement or narrow accuracy metrics, with little attention to broader social effects. We investigate whether LLM-assisted reranking, while improving personalization, inadvertently amplifies exposure to ideologically congenial, extreme, or conspiratorial political content, a risk theorized but not yet empirically characterized. Using real YouTube trajectories, we rerank the platform's sidebar candidates by zero-shot prompting across three models, alongside an embedding baseline, and evaluate relevance, ideological alignment and diversity, and exposure to conspiratorial or extremist content. We find that (i) under a pure personalization instruction, reranking improves relevance while narrowing ideological exposure and, on two of three models, promoting problematic content for users with such histories; the third model, which declines most explicitly partisan requests, points to model-specific guardrails; (ii) lightweight prompt-level constraints substantially reduce problematic exposure on all three models, while the regularized rerankers still rank above the platform baseline on relevance; and (iii) synthetic experiments varying topical and partisan cues independently show no fixed preference between them: topic dominates on abortion and immigration, while partisanship takes over on elections, where the topical signal is weaker. Our results highlight the power of LLMs to operationalize contextual nuance in high-stakes political recommendation, and underscore the need to evaluate LLM-assisted personalization beyond accuracy and to treat prompt design as a value-laden choice rather than a neutral default.
