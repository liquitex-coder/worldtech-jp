Suspended-Fabric Robotic Manipulation Surfaces: Modelling, Design Space, and Multimodular Extensio

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/03
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41982

arXiv:2610.41982v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Robotic Manipulation Surfaces (RMS) reposition objects by deforming the surface on which they rest. A recent class of RMS suspends a continuous fabric sheet from a sparse grid of vertical linear actuators lowering the actuator density of such surfaces but how to design such surfaces so that objects can be controlled everywhere on it, and how to extend it beyond a single module so that objects can pass between modules and achieve parallel, remain open. This paper presents a separable catenary model of the suspended sheet that is fast to compute and provides both the surface shape for given actuator heights and the actuator heights for a desired local slope. The closed form inverse lets a simple PID controller position objects on the sheet, and the model relates fabric length, module span and actuator stroke to the reachable workspace, yielding a minimum fabric length bound and design charts for these surfaces. The approach is then extended to a multimodular platform in which neighbouring modules share actuators. On a $2 \times 2$ prototype, we demonstrate that centimetre-scale objects are transferred between modules and positioned within 0.03 m over a $1 m^2$ workspace with only nine actuators, enabling multi-object manipulation and opening the way to parallel manipulation.
