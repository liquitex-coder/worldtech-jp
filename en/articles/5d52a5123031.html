Embodied Science Agents: Making Embodied Experimental Design Learnable

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/12
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12958

arXiv:2611.12958v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Recent advances in large models have accelerated AI for Science toward agentic science, where AI agents autonomously conduct scientific discovery. Realising this vision requires closing the loop between computational reasoning and physical experimentation, positioning self-driving laboratories (SDLs) as critical enablers. However, existing SDLs exhibit a fundamental paradox: foundation model integration has increased linguistic sophistication while severing physical grounding. By treating models as disembodied planners that output symbolic workflows for separate robotic systems, current architectures inherently block the development of embodied experimental intuition. We argue that overcoming this paradox requires embedding \emph{learnability} along three interdependent axes: cognitive learnability (learning what to investigate), experimental learnability (learning where to search), and embodied learnability (learning how to act). Building on this view, we introduce the Embodied Science Agent (ESA), which unifies digital intelligence with physical embodiment through three synergistic components: a Digital Agent that provides cognitive and experimental learnability via memory-based reasoning and Bayesian optimisation, an Embodied Agent that provides embodied learnability via generalisable instrument control, and an Embodied Unified Interface for seamless coordination. We validated ESA in autonomous wastewater treatment experiments, navigating over 200 million possible conditions. Through 15 continuous rounds, ESA raised the final solution transmittance from 82\% to 99.7\%, corresponding to a 98.5\% reduction in residual dye concentration and near-complete decolourisation, while its learned visuomotor policies generalised across varying instrument placements, a promising step toward next-generation SDLs for autonomous scientific discovery.
