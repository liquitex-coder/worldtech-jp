Trained on Harm, Still Refusing: A Training-Time Defense for Code Generation

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/24
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27495

arXiv:2611.27495v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Fine-tuning enables safety-aligned code language models to adapt to diverse downstream tasks. However, training on untrusted data can severely undermine their ability to refuse harmful requests. Notably, we find that models comply with harmful requests even though harmfulness information remains highly decodable from their internal representations. Moreover, an initial preference for refusal does not necessarily translate into sustained refusal behavior throughout generation. Together, these findings reveal a critical gap between recognizing harmful requests and consistently refusing to fulfill them. To address this gap, we introduce SAFEHOLD, a novel provider-enforced defense framework that preserves safety alignment during harmful fine-tuning by treating refusal as a sustained generation process rather than a single response decision. Specifically, SAFEHOLD preserves the aligned model's full-vocabulary response distribution at the initial generation position through decision-boundary anchoring, while supervising the complete refusal sequence through refusal-path reinforcement. We conduct extensive experiments evaluating safety, utility, and internal mechanisms. SAFEHOLD reduces average ASR by 72.03 percentage points relative to undefended fine-tuning, outperforming five SOTA defenses by 24.32 to 70.87 percentage points. Meanwhile, it improves average HumanEval pass@1 by 10.24 percentage points over undefended fine-tuning, while maintaining an average XSTest over-refusal rate of only 2.73 percent. Overall, these results demonstrate that SAFEHOLD effectively mitigates safety degradation during harmful fine-tuning while preserving benign-task utility.
