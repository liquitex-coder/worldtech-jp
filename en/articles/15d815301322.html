Anti-AI in the Wild: From Toxic Discourse to Action

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33254

arXiv:2611.33254v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Online discussion of generative AI has become increasingly contentious, and communities devoted to supporting, opposing, or debating AI have emerged on social media. Some of these communities take anti-AI actions, such as deploying crawler tarpits and data-poisoning services to interfere with data collection and model training. Yet, the scope of these actions and their relationship to online discourse remain poorly understood. In this paper, we present the first measurement study of online conflict over generative AI, from toxic discourse to anti-AI actions. We collect 931,640 posts from 25 Reddit communities, label them with a large language model detector validated against human labels, identify 3,312 anti-AI actions, and trace the output of three deployed anti-AI tools. Our analysis reveals that 30.0% of posts in communities devoted to this conflict are toxic, compared to 4.4% in general AI communities. Anti-AI actions in these communities mainly target AI crawling or model training data, such as image cloaking and data poisoning. We further examine the implications of these anti-AI actions for AI training, by analyzing the text and code generated from deployed tools. Notably, 66.5% of the code samples generated by a data-poisoning service contain fabricated facts, misused APIs, or broken logic, yet 93.6% passes a public code-curation pipeline, close to the rate for real source code (95.0%). For crawler tarpit output, adding a period to the end of each paragraph increases the curation survival rate from 16.4% to 91.9%. We also find that crawler tarpit and data-poisoning output has already entered Common Crawl, a widely used public web archive, and that crawler tarpit output has also entered FineWeb, a released pretraining dataset. We hope our study can facilitate a better understanding of online conflict over generative AI and its implications for content moderation and AI training.
