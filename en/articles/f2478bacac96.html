Honesty is the Best Policy ... or is it? Demand Management for Probabilistic Service Options

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/10/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21351

arXiv:2610.21351v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: To enable well-informed customer choices and redistribute demand across service options, providers can display probabilities regarding predicted service levels at the time of booking, effectively offering probabilistic service options. In many service settings, the underlying truthful probabilities endogenously depend on the system state, rather than being static product attributes controlled by the provider. While customers rely on this information when making decisions, they cannot readily verify its accuracy. Consequently, providers may be tempted to manipulate the displayed probabilities to guide customers' choices in their favor, creating a novel lever for demand management. In this work, we model the resulting problem as a sequential decision process, define truthful probabilities recursively, and introduce transparency constraints that limit their distortion. Because the exact computation of truthful probabilities is intractable for realistic settings, we develop an adapted reinforcement learning algorithm to approximate them. Furthermore, we propose a sampling-based mixed-integer program to optimize the information communicated by the provider in each state. Our framework enables providers to gauge the benefits of dynamically disclosing probabilistic information at the time of booking and sheds light on incentives for manipulation. Our computational study is motivated by out-of-home delivery, i.e., the delivery to parcel lockers or shops, and reveals a trade-off between individual-level transparency and aggregate customer utilities.
