PreferenceScene: Personalized Indoor Scene Synthesis from Feedback

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32099

arXiv:2611.32099v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Text-to-scene methods turn a short instruction into a furnished 3D room, but the room that a particular person wants rarely comes across fully in words: many preferences surface only when the person sees a concrete room and can point to what they like and dislike in it. We therefore study personalized indoor scene synthesis under hidden user preferences: over several turns, a system shows a 3D scene, and the user, who sees only rendered images of the scene, replies with brief free-form feedback and a satisfaction score. The system must therefore bring the whole room toward the user's preferences from feedback that comments on only part of it at a time. It must also be able to change every part of the room, because a preference concerns not only single objects but the look and atmosphere that surfaces, lighting, textiles, furniture and their arrangement create together. We present PreferenceScene, in which a large language model keeps an explicit estimate of the user's preferences for every design attribute of the room and, after each turn, infers the taste behind the feedback and revises the whole estimate, including the parts that the feedback does not mention. A scene instantiation module builds every estimated value as its own part of the room, so that each of these parts can follow the user's preferences. We evaluate on the test set of a benchmark with 320 instances in ten room types, with a simulated user and a render-based evaluator. PreferenceScene matches the hidden preferences better than five published text-to-scene baselines adapted to the same interaction, and its match rises with every turn. It even surpasses them when they are given the preferences directly, and a vision-language model rates its scenes as the most realistic and functional.
