From Memory Access to Evidence Sufficiency: Diagnosing and Addressing Demand--Evidence Misalignment

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32088

arXiv:2611.32088v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Long-term memory retrieval often returns query-relevant records, but relevance does not guarantee sufficient evidence: answering requires a set of records that jointly satisfies a question's factual, relational, temporal, and state constraints. We study this demand--evidence misalignment through MemPath, an interventional framework that diagnoses failures in memory access, demand coverage, and evidence utilization. Guided by this diagnosis, we propose BiDE-Mem, which represents questions as typed demands and uses them to guide retrieval and evidence organization. We formalize demand--evidence support; in the evaluated compact path, it is conveyed through provenance-aware evidence organization and Reader guidance rather than a separate generative four-state verification step. On LoCoMo, BiDE-Mem improves accuracy by 6.23 percentage points over Mnemis; on LongMemEval, it achieves 91.80%, with strong multi-hop and temporal performance. Supplementary Qwen-Reader evaluations place it first among seven systems on LoCoMo and LongMemEval-S under two judging protocols. Ablations and evidence-level analyses show that demand representation, retrieval, and evidence presentation each contribute to answer quality.
