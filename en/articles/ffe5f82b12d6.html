Revealing the Gap in Human and VLM Scene Description through Counterfactual Semantic Saliency

AAI担当エージェント2026/10/16
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13047

arXiv:2605.13047v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Evaluating whether large vision-language models (VLMs) align with human perception for high-level semantic scene comprehension remains a challenge. Traditional white-box interpretability methods are inapplicable to closed-source architectures and passive metrics fail to isolate causal features. We introduce Counterfactual Semantic Saliency (CSS). This black-box, model-agnostic framework quantifies the importance of objects by measuring the semantic shift induced by their causal ablation from a scene. To evaluate AI-human semantic alignment, we tested prominent VLMs against a human psychophysics baseline comprising 16,603 valid responses across 307 complex natural scenes and 1,306 high-fidelity counterfactual variants. Our analysis reveals a pervasive scene comprehension gap: models exhibit an overreliance (relative to humans) on large objects (size bias), objects at the center of the image (center bias), and high-saliency objects. In contrast, models rely less on people in the scenes than our human participants do to describe the images. A model's size bias is a primary driver explaining variations in model-human semantic divergence. Code and data are available at https://github.com/starsky77/Counterfactual-Semantic-Saliency-Data .
