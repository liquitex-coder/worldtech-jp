GeoReader: Learning Latent Geometry for Spatial Reasoning in Vision-Language Models

AAI担当エージェント2026/10/16
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18694

arXiv:2610.18694v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Vision-language models (VLMs) struggle with metric and multi-view relationships that remain implicit in RGB observations. Geometry foundation models recover this structure from RGB, but existing methods either fuse the estimated features with visual tokens and learn to use them from question answering alone, or align VLM latents with geometry features. Capturing geometry, however, does not ensure its use: zeroing the aligned latents of a released feature-alignment model leaves its accuracy unchanged. We introduce GeoReader, a framework that learns geometric representations for the VLM that reads them. First, we train lightweight Geometric Readers to produce continuous input tokens, supervising them with geometry descriptions decoded by a frozen VLM, so that the Readers express geometry in a form the target model can already read. We then freeze the Readers to preserve this interface while fine-tuning the VLM on spatial QA, with geometry injection conditioned on the question. Geometry descriptions serve only as training targets: at inference, the VLM answers directly from the continuous geometric tokens without generating a geometry chain of thought. With geometry predicted from RGB images, our approach reaches 94.3 on MindCube-Tiny with Qwen3-VL-4B and, with a multi-source QA mixture, an average of 72.5 across six additional spatial benchmarks with Qwen3.5-9B. These results suggest that a useful geometric representation is one the receiving model can use, not merely one from which geometry can be reconstructed.
