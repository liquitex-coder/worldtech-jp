Once Upon a Bias: Gender Stereotyping in LLM-Generated Children's Stories

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/21
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24156

arXiv:2611.24156v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Large language models are increasingly used to create stories for children. Since bedtime stories can shape children's perceptions of social roles, this raises concerns about whether LLMs adapt narrative portrayals according to the intended recipient's gender. We conducted a large-scale study to examine gender stereotyping in LLM-generated bedtime stories. Using matched daughter/son prompts and a five-generation design, we analyze 62,873 retained stories from 16 LLMs across 35 country settings and 12 narrator roles. We introduce a multi-level evaluation framework combining context-adjusted lexical rate contrasts, relative stereotype alignment, narrator-recipient interaction analysis, human evaluation, and representation-level interventions to examine both the consistency and interpretability of these differences. Our results reveal systematic differences in narrative framing: son-directed stories emphasize higher agency and lower communion compared to daughter-directed stories. While narrator gender expresses limited moderation on these patterns, high sociability exhibits a more pronounced interaction. Human evaluations demonstrate consistent agreement, although correspondence with lexical measures varies across narrative dimensions. Additionally, hidden-state analyses reveal decodable recipient-gender information. These findings demonstrate persistent yet context-dependent representational disparities in LLM-generated children's stories and motivate narrative-specific bias evaluation beyond short-form benchmarks.
