Hybrid AI Patterns: Thirteen Ways to Couple Language Models with Rules, and How to Choose Among Them

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/01
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40036

arXiv:2610.40036v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Language models handle text well, and rule engines give conclusions with proofs. Systems that combine the two are usually described by example, and the same coupling turns up under different names in agent frameworks, guardrail toolkits and neuro-symbolic research. This document defines thirteen patterns for coupling a language model with an executable rule language, in the catalogue form introduced for object-oriented design: intent, motivation, applicability, participants, collaborations, consequences, implementation, known uses and related patterns. Four patterns concern what crosses the boundary between model and rules, five who controls whom, three how the model takes part in the reasoning, and one how knowledge is kept over time. Five invariants hold for every combination, and five failure patterns name the couplings that break them. We relate the catalogue to published pattern catalogues and taxonomies of hybrid and neuro-symbolic systems and show where they overlap with it. A decision guide leads from a problem to a pattern. It rests on our measurements, including the cases in which a model alone did as well as the hybrid or better, and it reports what each design cost.
