Node Differential Privacy via Node-Stable Graph Projections

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33672

arXiv:2611.33672v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Node-differentially private algorithms enable learning on network-structured datasets in which records reflect interactions among individuals. They protect the privacy of each individual's information, including all their associated records. A key challenge in the design of such algorithms is to exploit the structure of realistic graphs---where most nodes have low degree---to achieve high accuracy, without compromising on the worst-case privacy guarantee. In this work we give two new tools for designing accurate, computationally efficient node-differentially private algorithms. First, we give a nearly linear time, randomized, node-stable graph projection: the projection leaves graphs of maximum degree $D$ unchanged, maps arbitrary graphs to subgraphs of maximum degree $\hat D \approx D$, and ensures that changing one node of the input graph has only a small effect on the distribution of the edges selected among the remaining vertices. Unlike prior approaches, which abstain when the input violates the degree bound, our projection acts as degree clipping: even when $D$ is set well below the true maximum degree (trading truncation bias at high-degree outliers for lower privacy noise), it retains at least as many edges as a maximal $D$-bounded subgraph. Second, for graphs with bounded edge weights, we give a deterministic nearly linear time Lipschitz extension for the total edge weight that agrees with the original sum on graphs of maximum degree $D$ yet is $D$-Lipschitz on all graphs. Finally, we show how to apply these methods to design node-private algorithms for high-dimensional vector summation and gradient descent on datasets where records are associated with graph edges, such as email threads.
