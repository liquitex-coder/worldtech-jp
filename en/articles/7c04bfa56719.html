MuSeToM: Multi-Sensory Evaluation of Belief Revision with Textual Cues Across Six Senses

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/15
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16662

arXiv:2611.16662v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Theory of Mind (ToM), the ability to infer mental states in others, is pivotal for human social cognition. Existing evaluations of ToM in large language models (LLMs) mainly focus on static belief attribution and false-belief reasoning, leaving a critical aspect of social reasoning underexplored: can LLMs decide whether perceptual evidence is sufficient to justify another person's belief revision? To address this gap, we present MuSeToM, a human-validated, text-based benchmark for evaluating epistemic belief revision in ToM reasoning. MuSeToM contains 2,415 instances adapted from BigToM and OpenToM, with textual cues spanning six sensory categories, and tests whether models can determine when a protagonist should revise a belief and infer the resulting updated belief state. Using MuSeToM, we systematically evaluate state-of-the-art LLMs under direct and chain-of-thought prompting, revealing a pronounced dissonance: while models often detect that a belief may change, they struggle to identify the updated belief and frequently over-update on perceptually salient but epistemically insufficient cues.
