Validation Bias in Large Language Model Responses to Cancer Treatment Queries: A Factorial Cross-Sectional Study

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31251

arXiv:2611.31251v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Large language model (LLM) chatbots are increasingly used by patients seeking cancer information, but their safety when users present evidence-misaligned beliefs about treatment remains uncharacterized. We hypothesized that LLMs exhibit validation bias, affirming rather than correcting such beliefs, and evaluated this with a 2x2x2 factorial design crossing conversation format (single-turn vs multi-turn), treatment type (evidence-based vs alternative), and query valence (positive vs negative). Framing was evidence-aligned (supporting evidence-based treatments or questioning alternative therapies) or evidence-misaligned (promoting alternative treatments or questioning evidence-based therapies). We analyzed 1,272 conversations (6,360 interactions) across 14 LLMs from 6 vendors, scored by an LLM judge (GPT-5-mini) validated against human annotation on a 6-dimension safety rubric. The primary outcome was the unsafe response rate, defined as a Weak classification or any critical failure. Evidence-misaligned framing increased unsafe response rates compared with evidence-aligned framing (14.4% vs 5.1%; OR 3.14, 95% CI 2.07-4.78; P < .001), and the association persisted after accounting for clustering by LLM. Among 429 conversations classified as Strong under evidence-aligned framing, 22.6% were downgraded in their paired evidence-misaligned counterpart. Multi-turn conversations had lower unsafe response rates than single-turn conversations (5.2% vs 14.7%; P < .001). Unsafe response rates varied by vendor from 1.8% to 36.8%. LLMs exhibited significant validation bias in oncology, with unsafe responses nearly tripling under evidence-misaligned framing. Evaluations limited to neutral, fact-seeking queries may overestimate real-world safety, and safety testing of medical AI systems should include evidence-misaligned user framing.
