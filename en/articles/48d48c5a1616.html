Understanding Failure Patterns in LLM-Based Mathematical Optimization Modeling

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33351

arXiv:2611.33351v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Industrial optimization involves complex constraints, making optimization modeling difficult to automate. Large language models (LLMs) have been explored for generating optimization models from natural language, but their reliability in end-to-end settings remains limited, often producing non-executable, infeasible, or feasible but incorrect solutions. In this work, we study failure modes in LLM-based optimization modeling by decomposing the process into three components: mathematical reasoning, solver usage, and domain knowledge, and constructing datasets that target each aspect. Using these datasets, we analyze how different components relate to errors and find that they are associated with different types of failure, although these relationships are not always strong or consistent. Furthermore, while combining these components improves execution reliability, feasible but incorrect solutions remain the dominant failure mode, suggesting a remaining gap between executability and correctness. These findings highlight limitations of current dataset designs and suggest the importance of data that better capture constraint completeness and formulation accuracy to enhance reliability.
