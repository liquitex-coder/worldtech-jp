Movement Information from a Robotic Effector Influences Human Performance During Object Handovers

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/20
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.20299

arXiv:2511.20299v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Recent advances in robotics have increased opportunities for humans and robots to collaborate during object manipulation tasks. Effective coordination in such interactions depends on how humans use movement information provided by a robotic partner to organize and adjust their own actions. The present study examined how movement characteristics of a robotic effector influence human motor performance during robot-to-human handovers. Using a virtual reality-based handover paradigm, three sets of factors were investigated: (1) alternative modes of spatiotemporal control of object handovers, (2) robot velocity profile characteristics (minimum jerk, constant velocity, constant acceleration, and biphasic), and (3) the timing with which the required pickup orientation became visually specified. Across experiments, performance benefited when task-relevant action requirements were specified earlier in the interaction and when robot movements provided information that could be readily used for coordination. Smooth velocity profiles also improved performance, with benefits that appeared to be concentrated in the final deceleration phase preceding object acquisition rather than reflecting human-like motion more generally. Together, the results highlight the importance of movement information, action-requirement specification, and movement kinematics in supporting coordinated action. These findings contribute to understanding how humans organize and adapt their movements during interactions with robotic systems, while providing principles for the design of more effective human-robot collaborations.
