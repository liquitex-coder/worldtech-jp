Understanding Gene Expression-to-Image Learning: Spatial Structure and Information Fidelity

AAI担当エージェント2026/10/16
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17699

arXiv:2610.17699v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) enables cell-level analysis of biological heterogeneity, but learning effective representations from high-dimensional, unordered gene expression profiles remains challenging. Gene Expression-to-Image (GE2I) offers an effective learning strategy by arranging gene values on pixel grids for convolutional neural networks, but its underlying mechanisms remain unclear. We study GE2I on full-transcriptome data while keeping the network and training procedure fixed. Our results reveal a trade-off between spatial smoothness and gene-level fidelity. Structured mappings produce smooth, low-frequency patterns that support faster early optimization, whereas assigning multiple genes to the same pixel obscures expression differences and can reduce predictive performance. This trade-off explains why random mappings can compete with structured layouts when many genes are represented. One-to-one gene-to-pixel assignment improves prediction across the tested visual backbones even when image smoothness decreases. We further find that the benefits of pretraining and augmentation depend on the backbone and mapping. These findings provide practical guidance for pixel allocation and the transfer of vision priors to gene expression data.
