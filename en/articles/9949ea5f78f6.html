Labadain-MedQA: A Tetun Medical QA Dataset for Fine-Tuning and Evaluating Large Language Models

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/06
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00935

arXiv:2611.00935v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Advances in large language models (LLMs) have created opportunities for cross-lingual transfer of medical knowledge from high-resource to low-resource languages to support access to healthcare information. However, medical question-answering (QA) datasets for fine-tuning and evaluating LLMs remain scarce for Tetun. To address this gap, we translated MedAlpaca's Medical Flashcards and WikiDoc Patient Information collections into Tetun using Gemini 2.5 Flash, producing 39,077 medical QA pairs. Translation quality was assessed on 50 randomly sampled QA pairs (100 documents) using automatic metrics against human reference translations and evaluations by two native Tetun-speaking healthcare professionals, focusing on medical accuracy and Tetun fluency. Human translations achieved higher mean ratings than LLM-generated translations for both criteria (4.43/5 vs. 4.02/5), with statistically significant differences (Wilcoxon signed-rank test, p < 0.001). To assess downstream utility, we fine-tuned Gemma and Llama variants on the translated MedAlpaca dataset using parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) and evaluated them on 325 Tetun-translated United States Medical Licensing Examination multiple-choice questions. Gemma 4 12B fine-tuned using QLoRA achieved relative improvements in mean accuracy of 10.79% with 4-bit NormalFloat (NF4) inference and 10.42% after merging the LoRA adapters into the BF16 base model, compared with the corresponding base models. These results highlight the viability of automatic dataset translation for Tetun medical QA and the effectiveness of PEFT in enhancing model performance. The datasets, models, and evaluation resources are accessible at https://github.com/labadain/labadain-medqa to support reproducible research on Tetun medical QA and facilitate machine translation, cross lingual, and multilingual research.
