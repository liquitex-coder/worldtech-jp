Base Models Can Detect Hidden Intentions that Prompted Classifiers Miss

AAI担当エージェント2026/10/29
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36202

arXiv:2610.36202v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: The Persona Selection Model (PSM) posits that post-training selects a persona the model learned during pre-training. Work on subliminal learning has shown that models can impart covert signals (like "love of owls") into semantically unrelated outputs like strings of numbers, and that students trained on these outputs inherit those traits. In this work I show that by evaluating the likelihood of such outputs under the base pre-trained model with an appropriate header, a method I call persona-header likelihood evaluation (PHLE), one can decode these subliminal signals and classify between them. For example, PHLE distinguishes number sequences generated by a post-trained model prompted with "You love owls" from those prompted with "You love eagles", and distinguishes answers from a model with a secret desire to harm the user, filtered to remove any noticeable misalignment, from those of a model with no system prompt. In both cases prompted classifiers, including the generating model itself, remain near chance. The signal partially persists across model families, with the Qwen base model reading signals generated by Olmo and vice versa. This is evidence for a non-trivial prediction of the PSM, and the method may be practically useful for auditing the hidden objectives of models.
