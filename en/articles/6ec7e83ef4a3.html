PsyWorld: Profile-Conditioned World Modeling for Human Behavior Prediction

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/21
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24999

arXiv:2611.24999v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Understanding how stable psychometric traits and time-varying states jointly inform behavior is an important problem in computational behavioral science. Existing personalized agents commonly condition actions directly on a static profile, leaving realized state transitions unused as an explicit source of supervision. We introduce a profile-conditioned state world model that uses observed environment--behavior state transitions to learn transition-informed representations for behavioral prediction. During training, a posterior encoder quantizes realized transitions, while a prior predicts the resulting codes from the psychometric profile and pre-transition context. The prior code and predicted next state then condition macro-level behavior and simulator-grounded execution. Thus, the world model provides structured transition supervision instead of treating personality as a direct action descriptor. We evaluate the framework in VEET, a controlled behavioral simulator with human trajectories, and in AgentSociety as a structurally different synthetic environment. A participant-disjoint split of 240 training, 25 validation, and 48 test participants evaluates generalization to unseen people. On VEET, the model reaches 0.904 Top-1 accuracy (95\% CI: [0.895, 0.913]) and 0.0033 Macro KL. Ablations associate continuous next-state modeling with most of the Top-1 gain, while discrete codes and prior alignment mainly improve Top-3 and distributional alignment. These results establish transition-informed world-model supervision as useful for subject-generalized behavior prediction, without implying causal psychological mechanisms or calibrated long-horizon state dynamics.
