CF-Splat: Closed-Form Triangle Splatting with Mesh Priors

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/10/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22108

arXiv:2610.22108v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Splatting-based radiance-field representations have recently gained popularity in robotic perception, enabling high-quality novel-view synthesis (NVS) through explicit scene primitives. However, existing splatting methods typically rely on iterative nonlinear optimization that jointly optimizes millions of coupled primitive parameters, resulting in substantial computation time, peak GPU memory consumption, and susceptibility to local minima. In this paper, we propose CF-Splat, a novel closed-form triangle-splatting framework for efficient scene appearance estimation over a known mesh either pre-existing or provided by upstream mapping or reconstruction pipelines. Our key insight is that, by properly formulating the problem over the given mesh geometry and calibrated cameras, the rendered pixel colors can be expressed linearly with respect to the per-vertex spherical harmonics (SH) coefficients, allowing multi-view scene appearance estimation to be formulated as a global linear least-squares (LLS) problem without the need of iterative nonlinear optimization. To guarantee a well-posed solution under uneven multi-view observation coverage, we introduce adaptive SH regularization that constrains insufficiently supported high-order SH coefficients and yields a unique global minimizer. To ensure the scalability to large-scale scenes, we develop a triangle-structured matrix-free preconditioned conjugate-gradient solver that avoids explicitly constructing the large sparse normal system while producing the same LLS solution. Experiments on diverse real-world datasets demonstrate that CF-Splat achieves competitive or superior NVS quality compared with state-of-the-art splatting methods. While, CF-Splat requires approximately 27.5-378.0 times less computation time and 1.8 times lower peak GPU memory on average than 3DGS across scenes of different scales on a consumer-grade laptop.
