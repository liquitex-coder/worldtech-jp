One Confidence Threshold, Unequal Coverage: Language Gaps in Typed Decision Interfaces and How Translating Inputs Reduces Them

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/01
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37688

arXiv:2610.37688v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Deployed classifiers often act on a prediction only when its confidence clears a threshold and pass the rest to a person. We ask whether one threshold serves all languages equally on typed decision interfaces, APIs that return a probability for each option of a fixed multiple-choice question. In a confirmatory study with a written analysis plan, three vendors' interfaces classified 1,000 MASSIVE items, each in 51 languages, with a threshold of 0.9. Even when a request is answered correctly in both English and another language, the English version clears the threshold more often, by 8.6-11.7 percentage points on average. Observed coverage, the share of requests handled automatically, is lower than English's in every other language, while the requests the gate handles are rarely wrong. The gap remains when every language is reweighted to the same accuracy, and a standard calibration metric (ECE) does not detect it on one interface. In exploratory analyses, English's coverage stays higher when each language gets its own threshold fitted to the same 5% error target. Translating inputs into English nearly closes the gap at 0.9 but leaves English ahead at that error target. Requests written natively in African languages show a gap in the same direction when English is their human translation. We recommend reporting per-language coverage at the operating threshold.
