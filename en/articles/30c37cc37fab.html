EATS: Scaling Tool-Use Experience for Medical Reasoning Agents

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33897

arXiv:2611.33897v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Medical reasoning agents combine large language model (LLM) reasoning with clinical workflows and specialized neural tools. However, general-purpose LLMs receive limited training exposure to domain-specific medical tools, and the uncertainty and distributional shifts of neural tool outputs make selecting tools from simple descriptions significantly more challenging than in deterministic settings such as calculators or dictionary lookups. In this paper, we propose EATS, an experience-augmented tool selection strategy that leverages accumulated tool-use trajectories to improve tool selection in medical reasoning agents. EATS constructs a memory of past interactions, including queries, tool choices, arguments, and self-evaluated feedback, and retrieves relevant experiences to guide tool selection for new queries. As memory construction does not require ground-truth supervision, EATS scales naturally with test-time experience. We evaluate EATS on tool-augmented medical reasoning tasks, including closed-ended visual question answering and free-form report generation. Experiments on four public datasets, ChestAgentBench, CheXbench, MIMIC-CXR, and SLAKE-VQA, across three multimodal LLMs, Qwen3-VL 2B, Qwen3-VL 32B, and GPT-5.5, show consistent gains in tool selection behavior and downstream medical reasoning performance. Code is available at https://github.com/Limingyuan001/EATS.
