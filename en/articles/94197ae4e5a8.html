Response-Relevant Information Is Not Sufficient for Neural Training-State Equivalence

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/20
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23245

arXiv:2611.23245v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Response-relevant information is not sufficient evidence that two neural networks are in the same training state. We define training-state equivalence operationally: two checkpoints are equivalent, relative to an intervention family and horizon, if they respond alike when training is perturbed and continues. On fresh runs of a one-layer transformer learning modular addition, response to a weight-decay pulse is highly reproducible relative to between-checkpoint differences (rho_state = 0.934 at 500 steps), establishing the target as measurable. Fifteen descriptors of weight spectra, representation geometry and optimiser geometry carry within-run response-relevant information beyond weight norm and loss on a development dataset, predicting held-out response deviation across seed blocks. But their apparent 16.1% advantage at matching checkpoints by future response does not separate from a conditional placebo preserving each descriptor's fitted dependence on the conventional observables, and a calibrated positive control makes simple insensitivity an unlikely explanation. Information a decoder can extract need not be organised in the pairwise geometry that similarity judgments require.
