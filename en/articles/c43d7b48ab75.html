On the Limits of Observability for Evaluating Coverage in Machine Unlearning

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/24
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27543

arXiv:2611.27543v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Machine unlearning aims to remove the influence of any requested subset of training data while preserving utility on the remaining data. Empirical evaluations measure predictive accuracy and infer training-set membership (MIAs) from model outputs or intermediate activations. However, by focusing on selected forget sets, these evaluations can overlook Coverage, the range of requests a method can fulfill. To assess this coverage, we first conduct a stress test of observability with 502 retrained models, finding that hidden embeddings reveal known deletions missed in predictions. To characterize capacity limits on coverage, we show that distinguishing all $n$ singleton deletions with linear scores can require a shared representation of rank $\Omega(\log n)$. We further extend the bound to arbitrary subsets and show when partial deletion can appear complete. Building on this analysis, we propose CEO, a framework for evaluating unlearning coverage under limited observability. Its compositional color-shape benchmark makes forgetting and retained utility measurable from the full output distribution and provides a leave-one-out retraining oracle for each target. From a fixed number of oracle observables, we estimate empirical target coverage, the fraction of targets reliably flagged when deleted and rarely flagged under any other deletions. To recover missed deletions, CEO further expands each observable representation into high-dimensional activation space with the frozen encoder of a SAE trained on the original model. Extensive experiments of 20 baselines shows that even on this simple synthetic task, only retraining from scratch covers all deletion targets.
