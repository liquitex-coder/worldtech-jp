MOSAIC: Memory-Organized Search with Anti-Imitation Crossover for Training-Free Microarchitecture Optimization

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31733

arXiv:2611.31733v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Large language models can generate cache-replacement policies. The central challenge is to retain performance on workloads excluded from the search. In our adaptations of published search methods, generated code resembles established policies, with cosine similarities of 0.78-0.84. The wider comparison also shows held-out harmonic-mean instructions-per-cycle (IPC) losses of 1.0-2.4%. We present MOSAIC, a search controller that improves policies without updating language-model weights. It stores concise lessons from previous evaluations rather than repeatedly copying high-scoring code. When improvement stops, it selects two high-scoring policies whose code embeddings differ and asks the model to combine their mechanisms. Novelty prompts and model rotation support this search; controlled memory edits test how the stored lessons affect later proposals. We evaluate cache replacement using intervention-level cross-validation and a wider comparison on ten memory-intensive SPEC2006 workloads. MOSAIC reaches a 51.1% peak hit rate on the primary search workload and a 47.9% held-out mean in the intervention study. Removing crossover reduces these values by 7.8 and 21.8 percentage points, respectively, whereas the tested stronger model improves the held-out mean by 0.4 points. In the wider IPC comparison, MOSAIC has the smallest held-out gap (0.31%) and the highest held-out IPC among the evaluated LLM configurations. Adapting the same controller to data prefetching gives a 4.8% mean speedup over the LRU reference configuration. The accompanying corpus contains 10,687 generated policies with lineage and per-iteration prompt logs.
