Comparative Analysis of 3D Convolutional and 2.5D Slice-Conditioned U-Net Architectures for MRI Super-Resolution via Elucidated Diffusion Models

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14667

arXiv:2603.14667v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) super-resolution (SR) methods that computationally enhance low-resolution acquisitions offer a software alternative to higher-field scanners. We study an elucidated diffusion model (EDM) framework for 2x in-plane brain MRI SR and compare two U-Net backbones: (i) a 3D convolutional U-Net that processes volumetric patches and (ii) a 2.5D slice-conditioned U-Net that super-resolves each slice while conditioning on its two neighbouring slices. All models are trained from scratch to convergence on 500 subjects of the NKI cohort of FOMO60K, with a separate 6-subject validation set for checkpoint selection, and evaluated on 70 held-out subjects with subject-level statistics against baselines (EDSR, Swin2SR, Res-SRDiff) trained on the same data. Neither architecture is better overall. On the degradation used in training the two are tied (37.72 dB PSNR for the 3D model and 37.70 dB for the 2.5D model), 0.2 dB ahead of EDSR. When the test degradation changes to k-space truncation, EDSR matches the diffusion models, and with added noise the 2.5D model is the most accurate method. When the test cohort changes to 70 subjects of an external dataset (IXI), the 3D model is the most accurate, 0.4 dB ahead of the best baseline and 0.9 dB ahead of the 2.5D model in foreground PSNR.
