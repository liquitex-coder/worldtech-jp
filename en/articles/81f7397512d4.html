Auditable Clinical Support with Decision Agents

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/21
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23700

arXiv:2611.23700v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Clinicians reviewing a diagnostic work-up will ask what was considered at each step and why the assessment changed. Agents powered by free text can reach physician-level diagnostic accuracy, but their written reasoning does not always reflect the factors that went into the decision. Here, we present Decision2Care, a decision agent that works up the acutely ill patient without producing any text. A decision model assigns a probability to each permitted EHR action, and fixed rules carry out the most probable one. We evaluated Decision2Care on the records of 546 emergency admissions across 12 diseases. Decision2Care reached a diagnostic accuracy of 86.5%, compared with 86.1% for the MIRA-v2 agent, and required a median of 2.2 s per work-up, compared with 92-100 s. Each work-up tracked which options Decision2Care was considering at each step. In 10 of the 12 diseases, the differential diagnosis changed most at the test on which the guideline bases the diagnosis. Where Decision2Care was most confident after a single work-up, all but four of its diagnoses were correct (98.5%). We traced each of these four errors back to a single imaging report. Three of them were corrected when that report was removed. The same kind of decision was used to select the treatments, and Decision2Care's drug orders were judged guideline-adherent more often than MIRA-v2's. In emergency patients, its prediction of death was comparable to that of three intensivists on the same cases. In five of the eight intensive care cohorts, it exceeded the best of the three 24-h severity scores within 2-6 h of records. Our results show that decision agents can carry out clinical work-ups while recording the options, probabilities, and execution rules behind each action for audit.
