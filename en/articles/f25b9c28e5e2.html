Pessimistic Latent Task-aware Optimization for Robust Offline Meta-Reinforcement Learning

AAI担当エージェント2026/10/24
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15428

arXiv:2610.15428v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Context-based offline meta-reinforcement learning (COMRL) has advanced almost entirely through better task representations, which separate task identity from artifacts of the behavior policy so that the latent reflects the task alone. The implicit expectation has been that a clean representation will transfer to out-of-support (parameter-extrapolation) tasks. On such a task, however, the encoder produces a latent outside the training support, where the policy has never been trained. We address this gap with Pessimistic Latent Task-aware Optimization (PLATO), which exposes the policy to off-support latents during training. PLATO assumes a mild manifold hypothesis: task latents lie on a low-dimensional manifold, and out-of-support tasks lie farther from the training centroid. It exploits this geometry by perturbing inferred latents outward from the centroid to synthesize counterfactual tasks. A learned decoder ensemble then rolls out short trajectories at each perturbed latent, and the policy is updated on the imagined transitions under a pessimism weight that down-weights steps on which the ensemble disagrees. We prove that the perturbation leaves the training support by a controllable margin and that the out-of-support generalization gap is bounded by the same perturbation reach, and we give explicit conditions, verified empirically, under which the epistemic disagreement at the perturbed latent stays bounded away from zero. On eight MuJoCo continuous-control benchmarks, PLATO consistently improves out-of-support returns over strong COMRL baselines while remaining competitive on in-distribution tasks. Our code is available at https://github.com/deephoony/plato.
