Benchmarking Open-Ended Vulnerability Discovery in Windows Kernel Binaries

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33486

arXiv:2611.33486v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Frontier models are increasingly used to discover real-world security vulnerabilities, making it critical to systematically evaluate their cybersecurity capabilities. However, existing security benchmarks primarily focus on known vulnerabilities in open-source projects, largely overlooking closed-source binaries, which underpin much of the software industry. In this paper, we ask: how effective are frontier models at uncovering deep vulnerabilities in production binaries? To answer this question, we present DeepBinary, the first comprehensive benchmark for open-ended vulnerability discovery in Windows kernel binaries. Given a target binary, an agent analyzes it using a disassembler and kernel debugger in network-isolated VMs and reports each vulnerability with a crashing proof of concept (PoC). Unlike existing benchmarks, DeepBinary replays every report on fresh VMs and employs an LLM-based judge to assess its validity, uniqueness, and impact (estimated by bug bounty values). Beyond known vulnerabilities, DeepBinary also enables the discovery and validation of previously unknown (0-day) vulnerabilities. We conduct a comprehensive evaluation of representative open- and closed-weight models (including GPT-6 Sol, Claude Opus 5, and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash) across diverse coding agents (including Codex and Claude Code) on 12 target binaries. In total, they discover over 200 vulnerabilities with an estimated aggregate bounty of $661,000, including 146 distinct previously unknown vulnerabilities across all experiments. Our results further reveal substantial differences in models' ability to analyze binaries, conduct experiments, and sustain vulnerability discovery over long horizons. Overall, DeepBinary makes long-horizon, open-ended vulnerability discovery in complex binaries measurable, revealing fundamental differences in how frontier models analyze and explore such systems.
