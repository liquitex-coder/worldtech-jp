Lost in the Funnel: Selection-Induced Miscalibration in Ranking and Decision Systems

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/19
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22595

arXiv:2611.22595v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Modern ranking and decision systems combine components with divergent objectives, and scores calibrated at the model boundary are used as calibrated probabilities throughout the funnel, across filtering, reranking, business-rule enforcement, and auctions. Yet calibration is typically validated only where the score is produced, so its breakdown where the score is used is routinely overlooked. We ask when calibration survives this selection, how inherited error differs from selection effects, and what can fail for a perfectly calibrated score at a component boundary. We study these questions through a boundary-wise diagnostic framework of residual identities. We target rubric-judged relevance rather than presentation-dependent metrics such as CTR, so calibration is measurable at every funnel layer of interest. We first present an industry-wide case study that applies our diagnosis to settings where calibration deteriorates at decision time, tracing the degradation to selection rather than model error. This diagnosis motivates Funnel-Aware Recalibration (FAR): post-hoc, selection-policy-aware recalibration at the boundary where scores drive decisions. In a live two-region production A/B test, FAR cuts decision-time expected calibration error (ECE) by 60-80% and improves auction efficiency. We further adapt two public datasets to simulate industry settings, providing additional evidence for the mechanism. We plan to release the adapted datasets and their generation pipeline to support further research. Finally, we distill these findings into practical operating principles for calibrating multi-stage scoring systems.
