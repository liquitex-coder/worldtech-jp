InferSlice: A Reference Architecture for SLO-driven LLM Inference over a Telco Wide-Area Network

AAI担当エージェント2026/10/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32638

arXiv:2610.32638v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: LLM inference is increasingly deployed as a core service for enterprise and interactive applications, but sovereignty, compliance, latency and resource-locality requirements can limit the suitability of centralised cloud deployments. Telco operators offer geographically distributed compute and network infrastructure for addressing these constraints. However, existing LLM serving frameworks are designed for tightly coupled data centres and provide limited support for coordinating geographically distributed and heterogeneous resources. We present InferSlice, a reference architecture for Service-Level Objective (SLO)-driven LLM inference over a telco infrastructure, integrating network slicing, GPU partitioning, elastic serving and observability. Led by BT in collaboration with the University of Bristol and NVIDIA, and realised on the JOINER UK national-scale wide-area network (WAN), InferSlice is evaluated on service differentiation across tiers, elastic scale-out, client-edge bandwidth as a bottleneck, and the reach of tensor, pipeline, data parallelism, and prefill/decode disaggregation over the WAN. Our results show that, on a general-purpose telco network, tiers yield distinct latency-throughput regimes, and weight streaming shortens scale-out time. Replication is placement-insensitive but memory-bound; pipeline parallelism tolerates near- and mid-cross-site hops, while tensor parallelism and disaggregation fail beyond a single subnet hop. We share practical insights from operating SLO-driven inference on a telco network, lessons learned and open questions, and hope to encourage further research on distributed and heterogeneous LLM inference systems.
