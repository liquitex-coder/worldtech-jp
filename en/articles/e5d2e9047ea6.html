Bridging Neural Representations and Symbolic Semantics in Audio Classification

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/01
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40414

arXiv:2610.40414v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Deep audio classifiers can achieve strong predictive performance, but their hidden representations remain difficult to interpret. Existing audio explainability methods often focus on input-level attribution, such as waveform segments, spectrogram regions, or acoustic features, and provide limited insight into what hidden neurons encode. Interpreting hidden neurons at the concept level can provide a more meaningful view of what an audio model learns internally. This paper investigates whether hidden neurons in audio classifiers can be associated with human-understandable symbolic acoustic concepts. A model-agnostic neurosymbolic framework is presented for analyzing the final hidden layer of audio classifiers, containing 32 neurons, through symbolic concept induction. The framework is evaluated on two audio classification settings: music genre classification using GTZAN dataset and musical instrument classification using IRMAS dataset. Results show that several hidden neurons can be associated with meaningful induced concepts, and most confirmed neuron-concept pairs are statistically supported using the Mann-Whitney U test. These findings suggest that symbolic concept induction is a promising direction for explaining latent audio representations beyond input-level attribution.
