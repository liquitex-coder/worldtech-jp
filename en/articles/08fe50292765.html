Beyond Large Language Models: A Mental World Model Framework for Mental Modeling, Simulation, Evolution, and Interpretability

AAI担当エージェント2026/10/23
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23722

arXiv:2610.23722v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Understanding and simulating how subjective cognition and mental states emerge, evolve, and shape behavior is essential for advancing artificial intelligence toward deeper social cognition and general intelligence. Existing large language models (LLMs) and Theory of Mind (ToM) methods primarily infer mental states from linguistic information, lacking unified mechanisms for subjective world construction, cognitive-psychological interactions, dynamic evolution, and historical dependencies. This paper introduces the Mental World Model (MWM) and its implementation, the Multi-Branch Mental Evolution Architecture (MMEA), which explicitly constructs subjective worlds, models interactions between cognition and mental states, and simulates continuous mental evolution shaped by historical experiences. On DynToM and RecToM, MMEA achieves 96.48% and 94.00% accuracy, respectively, outperforming leading LLMs and specialized ToM methods. Additional experiments demonstrate improvements of 28.7, 39.3, and 16.6 percentage points in mental state and behavior prediction, long-sequence prediction, and implicit mental state inference, respectively. The results demonstrate that MMEA provides an interpretable framework for continuous mental modeling, enabling AI to move beyond surface-level semantic representations to infer individuals' underlying mental states from linguistic information and dynamically simulate their internal cognitive processes and evolution over time. MWM also provides a promising foundation for causal reasoning, personalized AI, sustained human-AI interaction, and future advances toward artificial general intelligence (AGI).
