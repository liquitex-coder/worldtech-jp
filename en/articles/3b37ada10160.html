Recursive Bayesian Monitoring of SLAM Failures for LiDAR-Based UAV Navigation

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/24
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10566

arXiv:2611.10566v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: SLAM failures remain difficult to avoid in autonomous robotic systems, and undetected failures can propagate to unsafe planning, control, and mission-level decisions. Existing monitoring strategies often depend on prior maps, estimator-internal variables, or redundant sensing, which limits their applicability to deployed and resource-constrained robots. This paper presents a scene-geometry consistency monitor for detecting geometry-manifested SLAM failures using only the geometric output of the SLAM backend. The targeted failures include cumulative drift-induced failures, abrupt misregistration failures, and backend map corruption when they become observable as inconsistency in reconstructed scene geometry. The proposed method represents revisited local regions with recursive latent voxel geometry and evaluates each current observation against a trusted continuation explanation and a weak non-propagated reference explanation. The resulting voxel-wise evidence contrasts are aggregated into a fixed-boundary monitoring signal for online failure declaration and downstream safety response. Experiments on self-collected sequences, GEODE benchmark sequences, multiple robotic platforms, and different SLAM backends show that the monitor responds to failures when they are manifested in backend scene geometry. It achieves 4/4 valid post-onset declarations with no pre-onset alarms on four self-collected sequences, detects geometry-manifested failures in all five corrupted GEODE runs while remaining alarm-free in five geometrically consistent runs, adds only 0.1--0.2\% absolute CPU load, and supports a closed-loop UAV detect--isolate--recover workflow.
