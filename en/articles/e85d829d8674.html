Safe Autoformalization: Sufficiency and Necessity with Entailment Graphs

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31571

arXiv:2611.31571v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Autoformalization translates a natural-language statement into a formula that captures its intent, which depends on the statement's context: the background that disambiguates it. LLMs make autoformalization practical, but their probabilistic output carries no formal guarantees. We propose techniques for safe autoformalization that certify one-sided logical adequacy: a formula sufficient for the intent is safe to enforce, and a formula necessary for it is safe to rely on. We use LLMs to generate candidate formulas and order them by logical strength in an Entailment Graph (EG), an ordering a solver decides. Each edge carries a residual and a co-residual, the two adjoints of the entailment order, all fixed by the candidate pool alone. In a context, two searches return the sufficient frontier, the weakest candidates that entail the statement, and the necessary frontier, the strongest candidates the statement entails; each step is one local check against an edge's residual or co-residual. The frontiers bracket the statement and yield the ambiguity residue, which holds exactly of the situations the candidates certify neither as satisfying nor as violating it; the statement is ambiguous precisely when the residue is satisfiable. For natural-language statements and contexts, an LLM entailment oracle decides the checks; it is sound if it accepts only checks that hold, and complete if it accepts every check that holds. We prove that a sound oracle makes the search safe regardless of pool quality, and that a complete one makes the frontiers tight. On security-critical AWS properties, EGs are safe in all 20 cases of an expert-adjudicated study, where majority vote over equivalence clusters and Universal Self-Consistency are unsafe in 25% and 10%. An ablation with the same oracle but no graph is unsafe in 25% and rejects every candidate in another 30%. We machine-checked our proofs in Lean.
