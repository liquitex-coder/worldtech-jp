End-to-End Image-Goal Navigation for Agile Quadrotors via Recurrent State Distillation

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34046

arXiv:2611.34046v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Image-goal navigation asks a robot to reach the viewpoint of a target photograph without a map or goal pose. In three dimensions, this requires not only recognizing the goal but also estimating 3D displacement and generating continuous control actions to approach and settle at the target view. This is difficult because the same visual difference between current and goal image can require very different actions depending on the robot's velocity, yet monocular images alone reveal neither metric scale nor body motion. As a result, existing image-goal policies are limited to planar action spaces and rely on a state estimator for flight control. Policies that map directly from images to low-level control commands have been demonstrated for agile flight, but not toward goal images in unseen environments. We present the first image-goal navigation policy that directly commands thrust and angular rates for agile quadrotor flight in unseen 3D environments, without relying on state estimation (e.g., SLAM, VO, or SfM) or a separate velocity controller. We train a recurrent vision-based policy through a four-stage pipeline that combines geometric and motion supervision with privileged-teacher distillation and end-to-end reinforcement learning fine-tuning. On 1000 held-out flights in unseen environments, the policy achieves an 82.3% success rate with a mean terminal position error of 0.10m across successful flights. Deployed zero-shot on a real quadrotor, the policy reaches an 84% success rate over 25 trials in an unseen indoor environment.
