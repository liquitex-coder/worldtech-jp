The Truncation Blind Spot: How Decoding Strategies Systematically Exclude Human-Like Token Choices

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/09/25
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18482

arXiv:2603.18482v4 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Why does machine-generated text remain detectable? We investigate a mechanistic explanation at the decoding stage: standard strategies such as top-$k$ and nucleus sampling restrict generation to high-probability tokens, while human writers routinely choose contextually appropriate words from deeper in the model's probability distribution. Truncation makes a measurable share of these choices unreachable; we call this the \emph{truncation blind spot}. Across five open models and three domains, 8--18\% of human-selected tokens fall outside common truncation boundaries. Linguistic analysis further reveals disproportionate exclusion of content-word tokens. In a benchmark comprising 1.8 million machine generations, classifiers using only predictability and lexical diversity achieve mean AUC-ROC near 0.97, with substantial variation across decoding settings and strong transfer across generators. Probability-floor samplers substantially narrow the blind spot, demonstrating that the choice of truncation criterion matters for retaining human-used tokens. Together, these findings characterize a source of human--machine distributional mismatch and motivate decoding methods that preserve contextually appropriate low-probability choices while maintaining generation quality. Code and data are available at https://github.com/EstebanGarces/human_vs_machine.
