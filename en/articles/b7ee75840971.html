Structure Across Voices: Comparing acoustic-event type accumulation and sequence dependence across four vocal corpora using frozen audio encoders

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/19
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16612

arXiv:2609.16612v3 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Comparing acoustic structure across very different vocal corpora is difficult because the recordings use different native event units, contain different amounts of sequence, and were collected under different conditions. We use the high-dimensional representations of frozen, pretrained audio encoders to measure acoustic diversity and temporal organization. Matched sampling gives each corpus the same opportunities to observe new acoustic forms and relationships between events, without changing its native event definition. We compare sperm-whale codas, human speech phones, Bengalese-finch syllables, and common-marmoset calls using 1,952 events and 1,700 adjacent pairs per comparison. Whale shows the fastest accumulation of encoder-defined acoustic groups, while Finch has the strongest repeated-sequence recurrence against ordinary within-block shuffles. Their neighboring-event dependence is closely matched under a source-and-position control. A second preceding event changes held-out prediction more favorably for Whale than for the other corpora, although Whale's standalone improvement remains uncertain. Waveform measurements recover several differences, but duration and acquisition-sensitive properties also contribute. Together, the findings show how pretrained representations can measure distinct forms of vocal organization, while waveform and source controls define the scope of the comparison.
