LingSante-Pulse: Unified Physiological Pretraining through Random-Feature Prediction and Cross-Modal Alignment

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/24
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27206

arXiv:2611.27206v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Clinical monitoring and wearable devices capture complementary physiological signals, but their partially overlapping modality coverage makes within-signal information and cross-modal relationships difficult to exploit jointly. We introduce LingSante-Pulse, a unified, general-purpose and flexible physiological representation framework that learns from these heterogeneous recordings. A shared, signal-conditioned encoder processes each physical channel independently and supports different numbers and combinations of signals for multimodal assessment and single-sensor inference. Pairwise cross-modal alignment, structured random-feature prediction (RFP) and masked waveform reconstruction jointly learn physiological relationships and preserve informative within-signal structure. Across 38 general physiological task configurations spanning PPG, ECG, EEG and EMG, the frozen encoder achieves the best dataset-balanced mean rank in each domain. In sleep assessment, multimodal staging on the HMC dataset and EEG-only staging on the Sleep-EDF dataset reach Macro-F1 scores of 0.7784 and 0.7941, respectively, while single-lead ECG apnea screening achieves an AUROC of 0.9183. Controlled experiments show that function prediction and waveform reconstruction provide complementary supervision for retaining temporal and rhythmic information. Representations learned under continually refreshed random supervision generalize to unseen teacher realizations, and correct auxiliary modality correspondence improves respiration-only representations. These results demonstrate that a single encoder can learn reusable physiological features across signal types, prediction targets and sensor configurations from partially overlapping recordings.
