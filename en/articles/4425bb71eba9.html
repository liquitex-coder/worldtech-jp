HAS: Hierarchical Adaptive Frame Sampling for Video Understanding

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34544

arXiv:2611.34544v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Due to context-length constraints, most MLLMs cannot process full-length videos and therefore rely on sampling a subset of frames as input. However, existing sampling methods, ranging from uniform sampling to relevance-based selection, are often driven by a single sampling principle and thus struggle to accommodate the heterogeneous evidential requirements of different queries: some demand a holistic understanding of how the video unfolds over time, while others hinge on fine-grained events within a short temporal window. To address this limitation, we propose \textbf{Hierarchical Adaptive Frame Sampling (HAS)}, a two-stage frame sampling framework. In the first stage, \emph{Backbone Frame Construction}, we apply a Determinantal Point Process (DPP) to sample frames that are both query-relevant and non-redundant. These selected frames capture key moments across the video and form the backbone of the sampling set, providing a foundation for subsequent enrichment. In the second stage, \emph{Adaptive Contextual Enrichment}, we analyze the temporal distribution of the backbone frames to infer the query's requirements and adaptively allocate the remaining frame budget between Local and Global Context. The Local Context enriches the backbone with fine-grained temporal dynamics and short-range causal relations, whereas the Global Context connects temporally isolated evidence and provides a holistic view of the entire video to support global understanding. Through such a hierarchical two-stage process, HAS effectively addresses diverse query requirements. Incorporated into three leading MLLMs, it demonstrates consistently superior performance across the Video-MME, LongVideoBench, and MLVU benchmarks.
