When Steering Shifts Discourse: Identifying and Intervening on Discourse-Level Changes due to Activation Steering in LLMs

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/04
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41661

arXiv:2610.41661v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Activation steering is an inference-time technique used to control or modify the behavior, tone, or safety properties of a large language model (LLM) without retraining the model's weights or editing its input prompt, but simply by adding a vector to its residual-stream activations. However, activation steering also changes the generated text in unintended ways, unrelated to intended behavior change, that are not well understood. Prior work has measured these changes only through coarse signals such as perplexity. In this work, we show that steering systematically produces significant changes in discourse dimensions of the generated text, such as its formality, use of evidence, and ambiguity. We present a way of countering this by adding a second, complementary vector that targets the affected discourse dimension. This intervention restores about 78% of the unintended discourse changes while preserving the intended steering behavior in about 93% of the generations.
