When the Proxy Is Wrong: Mismatch-Corrected Bandits for Adaptive Data Sampling

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34707

arXiv:2611.34707v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Data allocation methods often use a small proxy model to select training data for a larger target model. However, this proxy can rank data differently from the target, leading to suboptimal training performance. To address this problem, we model the mismatch between the proxy and target models and incorporate it into the allocation decision. To do so, the problem is cast as a multi-fidelity bandit, where each arm is a data intervention. The proxy reward is observed every round, whereas the target reward is revealed only on rare audits of the same update. These paired observations identify arm-wise mismatches without assuming a shared offset or preservation of rankings. Mismatch-corrected bandits correct the proxy with paired residuals, audit only uncertain decisions, and pace the audit budget over training. From a theoretical viewpoint, we prove a regret decomposition and lower bounds under stationarity. These bounds show that proxy-only allocation can incur linear regret and that a minimum number of paired audits is necessary. Under abrupt changes, using the audit budget early can also incur linear regret. A sliding-window policy that spreads audits over training achieves regret of order $\sqrt{\Gamma_T T\log T}$ where $\Gamma_T$ denotes the number of change points. Experimentally, we test the approach in a closed-loop proof of concept on CivilComments-WILDS, where BERT-Small guides the training of BERT-Large. Mismatch correction improves worst-group accuracy over a target-only policy with the same target-model budget. Spreading audits over training improves worst-group accuracy by 6.3 to 10.5 percentage points at the same audit count.
