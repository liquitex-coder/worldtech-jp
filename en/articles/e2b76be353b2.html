ChildAI-Gov: An Evidence-Linked AI System for Assessing EdTech Policies Against Children's Digital Rights

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/24
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27799

arXiv:2611.27799v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We present ChildAI-Gov, an explainable AI system for assessing whether education-technology (EdTech) policies respect children's digital rights. Generative AI is reaching children's education faster than its governance can keep pace, yet checking a tool against children's rights remains manual, expert-dependent, and slow. We propose the Child Rights Impact Score (CRIS), an evidence-linked measure across five dimensions: safety, privacy, transparency, autonomy, and non-discrimination. ChildAI-Gov reads an EdTech tool's privacy or AI policy and scores each dimension by combining a large-language-model judgement (gpt-4o-mini) with a deterministic rule check. A retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) component grounds every score in the governing provisions, retrieved from a curated knowledge base of children's rights frameworks (the EU AI Act, UNICEF guidance, and others), so each score is tied to the evidence behind it and an auditor can trace which provision it rests on. We also construct AIPolicy-EdTech, a dataset of 57 real EdTech policies. On it, the five dimensions cohered as a single measure (Cronbach's alpha = 0.79), and grounding made scoring stricter while preserving rankings; an ungrounded baseline was far more lenient, passing nearly half the policies where ChildAI-Gov passed none. It offers evidence-linked decision support on documented commitments, not observation of real-world behaviour or determinations of legal compliance.
