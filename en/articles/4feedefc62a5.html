Fault-Geometry-Aware Control Allocation for Emergency Quadrotor Landing under Single-Motor Loss of Effectiveness

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/24
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08259

arXiv:2611.08259v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Partial single-motor loss of effectiveness (LoE) changes the remaining control-authority geometry of a quadrotor and can change which control allocator performs best during emergency landing. We compare three allocators in Crazyflie software-in-the-loop simulation: bounded weighted least squares (reported as PINV), a finite-candidate residual allocator (QP-lite), and the same residual allocator with scoring parameters tuned offline by the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM). For this study, fault geometry is represented by the known failed-motor index. At $\eta = 0.496$ remaining effectiveness, matched development trials select CEM for M1, M3, and M4 and PINV for M2; the M3 selection is a one-trial margin (29/30 versus 28/30). After freezing this routing map, the policy achieves 117/120 safe first-contact touchdowns on fresh seeds and 115/120 in a separate randomized-order integrated-supervisor evaluation. For the critical M2/PINV branch, Firth regression gives a nominal $\eta_{50} \approx 0.4956$ with a within-model 95% bootstrap interval of approximately $[0.4955, 0.4956]$. One-factor-at-a-time $\pm 10\%$ plant perturbations shift $\eta_{50}$ by up to $0.05495$ for thrust coefficient and $0.04974$ for mass; the most frequent violated first-contact criterion also changes from vertical speed in many conditions to angular rate for mass $-10\%$ and thrust-coefficient $+10\%$. At fixed $\eta = 0.496$, PINV has a higher M2 safe count than frozen CEM in 9/11 tested plant states and ties in 2/11; the two ties are both 0/30. The experiments therefore distinguish motor-conditioned allocator selection from sensitivity to plant-model mismatch.
