Consensus Within, Competition Between: Decentralized LLM Teams for Perimeter Defense

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/19
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41044

arXiv:2610.41044v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: The use of large language models (LLMs) to coordinate multi-agent teams raises a fundamental architectural question: whether task allocation should be centralized in a single LLM controller or distributed across autonomous LLM agents, particularly when communication is unreliable. In a deliberately simplified perimeter-defense simulation against LLM-controlled intruders, we compared a central commander, an auction-based consensus team and independent agents under five network conditions. Breach rates were 15% for the consensus team, 32% for the commander and 29% for independent agents. Burst message loss, falsified reports and limited radio range raised the commander's breach rate by 10 to 18 percentage points (pp), whereas the consensus team's changed by at most 5. In the consensus team, giving a low-cost LLM more authority over the bid computation raised its breach rate by 38 pp. With birds and civilian drones in airspace, we added an image for each to the input text state. Only the near-frontier model treated them apart from intruders. Intruders disguised as existing taxidermy bird drones were mistaken for birds by both low-cost and near-frontier LLMs, being dropped from pursuit in 59-61% of near-frontier decisions (1-16% before). However, a one-sentence forewarning in the prompt lowered it to 0-25%. We find that the LLM team with a dedicated external conflict-resolution protocol had the fewest breaches. The code is available at https://github.com/the-kas-lab/consensus-within-competition-between.
