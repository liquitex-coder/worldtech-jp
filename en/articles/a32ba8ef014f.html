Learning Adaptive Defense Strategies: Fine-Tuned LLMs for Network Security Games under Network Mobility

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/24
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27180

arXiv:2611.27180v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Large language models(LLM) show promise for strategic reasoning, yet their ability to compute game-theoretic equilibria under dynamic conditions remains largely unexplored. This work investigates whether fine-tuned LLMs can serve as solver-free surrogates for online equilibrium computation in honeypot allocation, a setting in which network mobility continuously reshapes the underlying attack graph and forces repeated re-solving of a two-player zero-sum game. We construct a dataset of 270 security games on networks of 8--25 nodes with solver-grounded equilibrium labels, and evaluate four classes of predictor over 1{,}524 held-out mobility events: non-learned baselines, six open-weight architectures evaluated zero-shot, the same six after LoRA fine-tuning, and four proprietary models as an external reference point. Predictions are scored without post-hoc repair against exact game-theoretic criteria: exploitability against an optimal attacker best-response, equilibrium support accuracy, and distributional distance. Fine-tuning reduces exploitability across every architecture, and fine-tuned open-weight models of modest size outperform every zero-shot predictor, including proprietary ones; no zero-shot model at any tier reaches the floor set by uniform allocation. Stratifying by mobility type exposes a qualitative shift that aggregate metrics conceal: zero-shot models do not condition on whether a departing node lies on an attack path, while fine-tuned models recover this distinction. We further find that validation loss is negatively rank-correlated with task performance, and that output validity, rather than predictive accuracy, is the binding constraint on further progress.
