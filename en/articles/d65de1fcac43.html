From Per-Frame Search to Practical Attacks: Investigating Adversarial Robustness of Multi-Agent Heterogeneous Cooperative Perception

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17856

arXiv:2609.17856v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Heterogeneous cooperative perception (CP) lets connected agents with different sensors and encoders share bird's-eye-view (BEV) features and align them through learned translation modules before fusion and detection. A malicious collaborator can perturb its broadcast to erase real objects from a neighbor's view, yet prior CP attacks assume homogeneous agents and rely on per-frame gradient descent that needs test-time labels, online detector queries, and slow backward passes. Porting these homogeneous attacks to heterogeneous CP exposes two further architectural barriers. Translation modules often block direct gradients, and heterogeneous fusion stacks use per-location fusion and detection heads that leave the standard removal-loss gradient zero on over $99\%$ of the attacker's message, so per-frame updates reach only the sparse anchor footprint while neighboring cells still detect the object. We present HetPoison, a practical feature attack that trains a convolutional generator once, offline, on a frozen surrogate stack to map channel-observable features to a bounded perturbation in one forward pass, requiring no labels, detector queries, or gradients online. Because the generator parameterizes perturbations across the spatial neighborhood rather than per coordinate, HetPoison overcomes sparse anchor gradients while amortizing search across frames into a $\sim$2 ms forward pass. On OPV2V-H, HetPoison lowers STAMP detection average precision from $0.899$ to $0.063$, outperforms 500-step Adam-PGD on HEAL, STAMP, and CodeFilling at up to $14{,}000\times$ lower online cost, and remains effective on real-world V2X-Real fleets and under cross-family transfer, communication delay, quantization, and unseen collaborator encoders.
