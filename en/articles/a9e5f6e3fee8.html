Quantitative Measurement of Pavement Distress based on Vehicle Camera and Perspective Correction

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/24
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27728

arXiv:2611.27728v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Using image data is becoming an important method for highway agencies to automatically evaluate pavement conditions. However, most studies focus on improving detection accuracy rather than obtaining quantitative measurements, such as the length or area of pavement distress. Although field camera calibration can provide such measurements, it is impractical for large volumes of onboard images collected from different vehicles. This study develops a method to quantitatively measure pavement distress directly from in-vehicle camera images without on-site camera calibration. A quadrilateral region of interest is first identified based on lane detection and used to construct a perspective transformation matrix for image correction. Size correction is then performed using the proportions of road markings in the image and a specified scale, which also enables estimation of the actual width of the selected region. The corrected images are subsequently processed using object detection algorithms to obtain quantitative distress measurements. The proposed method can efficiently perform perspective correction for images collected by the same vehicle camera with minimal manual effort. The measured pavement patch lengths show a trend consistent with pavement friction data. By calibrating vehicle cameras using only image information, this study enables quantitative pavement distress detection without additional field experiments. The approach provides a practical solution for processing large volumes of vehicle-camera data, particularly as connected vehicles generate increasing amounts of uncalibrated roadway imagery.
