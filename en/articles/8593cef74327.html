Best-of-$N$ Test-Time Steering Can Lead to Reward Hacking in Vision-Language-Action Models

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33398

arXiv:2611.33398v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Vision-language-action (VLA) models have shown promise for general-purpose robotic manipulation, but adapting to changing environmental conditions still remains challenging. Recent test-time steering methods have shown promise in enabling adaptive VLA policies by utilizing additional computation during deployment. In this paper, we consider best-of-$N$ test-time steering for VLA models, where multiple action candidates are sampled from a fixed policy and a verifier is used to select the best one during deployment. We show that increasing $N$ does not always improve performance when the verifier is inaccurate, and can favor candidates whose scores exploit prediction errors, a form of reward hacking. We study this effect on 60 LIBERO-Safety task-level cases using a $\pi_{0.5}$ policy, with ten repetitions per case. We introduce controlled errors in a MuJoCo trajectory verifier and show that as $N$ increases, selected candidate rewards diverge from the accurate verifier rewards, leading to reward hacking. We also show that verifier calibration and softmax-based candidate selection can mitigate reward hacking in some settings.
