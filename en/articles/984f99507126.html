PROVE: Predict-to-Specify and Observe-to-Verify for Human-Aligned Evaluation of Mobile Manipulation

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33733

arXiv:2611.33733v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Judging open-world robot manipulation is hard because layouts, articulated fixtures, and valid goal configurations vary widely. On RoboCasa365, simulator predicates agree with human judgments on only 69.09\% of rollouts, and a frontier VLM judge on 86.12\%. We present \textbf{PROVE} (Predict-to-Specify, Observe-to-Verify), which first specifies what success should look like and then checks the outcome. PROVE refines success conditions from the instruction, the initial scene, a target-object reference image, and a generated goal image. The goal image only sharpens the conditions and never serves as evidence. On 6,500 human-annotated rollouts over 65 atomic actions, PROVE reaches \textbf{96.83\%} agreement. Applied to open-source policies, it reports far lower success than native checks and changes their ranking. To reduce evaluation overhead in policy learning, we distill PROVE into \textbf{PROVE-Lite}, a 4B evaluator that needs no reference images. In one forward pass, PROVE-Lite predicts the outcome and the terminal state of each task condition. It reaches \textbf{91.20\%} agreement while running about 38$\times$ faster than PROVE. Mapped to a severity-aware reward, its diagnoses improve PPO post-training over both simulator and binary rewards.
