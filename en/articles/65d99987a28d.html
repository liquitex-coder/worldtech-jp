FORK: Co-Evolving Safe Disclosure and Decision Skills Against Attackers Who Read Every Refusal

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33879

arXiv:2611.33879v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: When a guardrail rejects an LLM agent's action, its message reaches any attacker observing the trajectory: a fixed refusal leaves benign users unable to recover, whereas an explanation can tell the attacker what to try next. We study this as safe disclosure: explaining rejections so that authorized tasks recover without increasing adaptive attack success. Safe disclosure depends on both the decision and the message: what the guardrail blocks and what it reveals to the agent and the attacker. Because hand-written rules alone cannot anticipate how adaptive attackers probe decisions or use messages, we introduce FORK, which co-evolves a guardrail's natural-language decision and disclosure skills with an attacker that reads every public message. Each exploited message and permissive decision gives FORK evidence for a gradient-free revision of its natural-language skill library. We evaluate FORK on four benchmarks. Averaged over three agents on FORK-World, FORK cuts attack success rate (ASR) under adaptive attack from 78.4% to 14.1% and keeps utility above 85%, outperforming baselines on both ASR and utility. Notably, on MT-AgentRisk, whose environments and tasks are unseen in training, FORK lowers ASR from 70.6% to 9.1% with 93.1% benign utility.
