Baseline and Incremental: User, Item, and Request Debiasing in Instagram Feed Ranking

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/10/22
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27423

arXiv:2610.27423v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: A feed ranker scores candidates with a value model, a weighted sum of engagement predictions. Each prediction conflates a baseline, what this viewer, item or request would produce anyway, with the incremental amount by which this candidate exceeds it. The literature mostly removes the baseline. We argue that a multi-signal value model should keep the raw prediction and add the incremental part as a separately weighted term. On Instagram Feed, three debiasing families deployed independently, for viewer habit, item popularity and request context, are one parameterised operator, and a design procedure picks a point in it. Media-type (format) bias goes instead to a debiased label chosen by the same procedure. In a controlled four-arm ablation of deployed Feed terms (neither term, each alone, both), the two-term arm beats both single-term arms at production weights on point estimates in 11 of 13 judged (term, metric) pairs. Of the 26 direct contrasts, 17 are significantly for it and only 1 against it, on impressions. The ablation also suggests that the offline diagnostic should screen signals, not rank them. Such terms have served Instagram Feed production for most of a year. Beyond Feed, Meta, whose apps serve billions of people, has adopted the operator across surfaces, apps and both ranking stages. Its launches there drive sessions and daily active users, the core visitation and retention metrics.
