SwarmSafe-Delta: Isolating Message Effects on LLM Agent Safety

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/15
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16123

arXiv:2611.16123v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: When a tool-using LLM agent team violates policy, aggregate outcomes do not reveal whether an upstream message changed the executor's action. We introduce SwarmSafe-Delta, a paired executable protocol that freezes upstream computation, varies real, hidden, and format-matched placebo delivery, and scores a single committed action against deterministic benign and risky policy states under preassigned budgets. Across four models and constructed optional tasks, real delivery at $N=4$ produces 21.4% risky-world violations, versus 5.0% with placebo and 1.0% with hidden delivery (paired differences +16.4 and +20.3 percentage points). Real delivery also improves benign task success, illustrating that suppressing communication sacrifices utility. In a second regime constructed to require an upstream lookup route, real delivery improves safe completion by 74.2 points relative to hidden delivery. These effects quantify message-delivery contrasts under specified information dependencies, not naturally occurring failure rates. Exploratory action-level analyses show that the effect depends strongly on whether the upstream message recommends a prerequisite check or direct execution; model differences also largely track these message compositions. The contribution is a controlled, execution-grounded measurement protocol rather than a general claim that agent communication is harmful or protective.
