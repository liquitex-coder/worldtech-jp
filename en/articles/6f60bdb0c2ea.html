Self-Sensing Compliant Anthropomorphic Hand for Speed-Dictated Shared Force Control in Teleoperation

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/26
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32937

arXiv:2611.32937v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: In vision-based teleoperation of robotic hands, the operator receives no haptic feedback and therefore struggles to regulate the grasp force, while adding tactile sensors to the fingers increases wiring, cost and integration complexity. Compliant hands offer an alternative, as their mechanical compliance, usually treated only as a passive property for safe and adaptive grasping, can also serve as a source of information. Here, we show that the elastic transmission of a compliant, tendon-driven anthropomorphic hand can itself act as the sensor for shared-control telemanipulation. Series springs in the tendon routing are read by potentiometers in a forearm-mounted motor box, from which the hand estimates its joint states and finger forces without any external sensors. Building on this self-sensing, we develop a contact-aware shared-control framework for VR-based teleoperation. The robot hand is controlled through a mapping from the operator's hand kinematics until a contact is detected proprioceptively. Control then switches to a finger-level force controller whose set point is determined by the operator's grasping speed, and control authority can be allocated to individual fingers. Experiments validate the system's force estimation and increased grasp success rate compared with direct teleoperation for fragile and heavy objects. These results show that compliance can serve not only for passive adaptation but also as an intrinsic sensing resource for physical human-robot collaboration.
