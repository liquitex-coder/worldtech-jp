DForkTree: Accelerating Speculative Decoding with Fork-Aware Block Diffusion Draft Trees

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/15
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12356

arXiv:2611.12356v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Speculative decoding speeds up language models by having a lightweight drafter propose future tokens that the target model verifies in parallel. Block diffusion drafters such as DFlash predict an entire block in one forward pass, and DDTree builds draft trees directly from their per-position distributions. Each distribution implicitly assumes that the drafter's own greedy continuation is accepted, whereas a draft tree improves over a chain only on branches that leave this greedy path. Once a path forks, the drafter's top-1 accuracy drops from 89% to 18% and its top candidates often miss the true token. Re-running the drafter from the fork token largely restores its accuracy, showing that the errors stem from conditioning on an outdated prefix rather than from limited model capacity. In this paper, we introduce DForkTree, a training-free method that re-anchors each branch on its own tokens. It adjusts the drafter's scores with pointwise mutual information from target-generated n-grams, weighted more heavily after a fork, and a layer-wise GPU search recovers the exact optimal tree under these scores. On Qwen3.5-4B and 9B, DForkTree outperforms all baselines on every benchmark, by up to 41.5% over DFlash and 15.1% over DDTree, with average speedups of up to 4.75x over autoregressive decoding.
