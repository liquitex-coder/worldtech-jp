CENTRY: A Probabilistic Latent-State Model for Continual AI Risk Estimation, Monitoring, and Regulation

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/24
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26940

arXiv:2611.26940v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Artificial Intelligence (AI) systems are increasingly deployed in safety-critical applications, yet existing AI safety frameworks primarily define, categorize, or govern AI risk rather than proposing implementable models and methods for continually estimating, monitoring, and regulating risk throughout an AI system's operational lifetime. This paper proposes CENTRY, a probabilistic framework that enables continual risk estimation, monitoring, and regulation of a deployed AI system from its interactions with its environment. The framework models AI risk as arising from three intrinsic sources: datasets, hardware resources, and models/algorithms. A structured latent-variable model is developed in which the latent representation is decomposed into a fast interaction subspace that captures the evolving AI--environment dynamics and three slow latent subspaces that encode the long-term risk states associated with the dataset, hardware, and model components. Rather than requiring a ground-truth risk label at every time step, the component-risk states are treated as latent variables and are learned by maximizing a variational evidence lower bound, optionally informed during training and continual refinement by evidence-supported behavior--cause records. The resulting framework unifies continual probabilistic inference, online risk estimation, and adaptive risk regulation in an implementable architecture. Under the explicit assumptions stated for the corresponding results, we establish formal guarantees spanning safety, learning, inference, and operations, including finite-horizon and asymptotic risk-budget compliance, regret and convergence bounds for adaptive regulation, consistency and identifiability of component-risk ratings, and detection-delay, false-alarm, containment, and computational-complexity characterizations.
