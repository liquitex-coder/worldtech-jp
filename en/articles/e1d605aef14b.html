Towards Explainable Sleep Stage Classification using Single-Channel Electroencephalogram

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18434

arXiv:2611.18434v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Automated sleep staging has achieved strong bench- mark accuracy; however, clinical adoption remains limited due to a lack of interpretability. In this work, we fill the research gap by extending the SleepXAI sleep staging framework to systematically compare five different XAI methods. Further, attribution fidelity is evaluated using Spearman rank correlation with AASM-defined EEG frequency bands, serving as clinically grounded reference signals. In this paper, we focus on single- channel Electroencephalogram (EEG), which is more amenable to practical clinical deployment compared to full polysomnography setups. Experiments are conducted on the Sleep-EDF benchmark dataset and the DREAMT dataset. In DREAMT, we stratified subjects based on OSA into four Apnea-Hypopnea Index (AHI) severity groups. We observed that the SleepXAI model achieves strong classification performance on both datasets. Among the evaluated methods, Integrated Gradients (IG) consistently achieves the highest mean attribution fidelity across all five sleep stages on Sleep-EDF and across all AHI groups on DREAMT, outperforming all other evaluated methods under the Spearman rank correlation metric. These findings highlight the importance of attribution method selection as a key factor for ensuring reliable and clinically interpretable automated sleep staging systems.
