Geometrically Constrained Learning II: Agnostic Proper Learning of Lipschitz Functions

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/24
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27700

arXiv:2611.27700v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We give a proper learner for Lipschitz functions over the Gaussian distribution on $\mathbb{R}^d$. Given random Gaussian examples labeled by a $\lambda$-Lipschitz function, $\lambda \ge 1$, the learner returns a function $h:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}$ that is globally $\lambda$-Lipschitz and has loss (absolute or squared) at most $\varepsilon$. The sample complexity, running time, and representation size are \[ (d+1)^{\mathsf{poly}(\lambda/\varepsilon)}. \] The learning algorithm can be made \emph{agnostic}, given a known bound on the variance of the label marginal distribution. Some known control of label scale is information-theoretically necessary. For arbitrary labels with variance at most $V^2 \ge 1$, the complexity is \[ (d+1)^{\mathsf{poly}(V\lambda/\varepsilon)}, \] and the error is $\mathsf{opt} + \varepsilon$, where $\mathsf{opt}$ is the minimal error among Lipschitz approximators. Our approach combines $L_1$ polynomial regression under a convex gradient constraint with local correction of the Lipschitz property. The learner finds a polynomial that, after smoothing and clipping its range, is very close to a canonical Lipschitz correction of itself, the lower Lipschitz envelope. Our local corrector modifies the lower envelope by replacing distances to sublevel sets with a Gaussian sampling proxy, allowing the construction to be locally and stably estimated from samples.
