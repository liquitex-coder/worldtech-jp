When Perception Collides with Priors: Diagnosing Modal Polarization in Video Understanding Models

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/24
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26972

arXiv:2611.26972v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have achieved strong video comprehension, largely benefiting from extensive training on realistic video data. However, when video perception severely conflicts with internal priors or common sense, we identify a critical failure pattern termed Modal Polarization, where models fail to integrate modalities and instead bifurcate into two distinct extremes: Cognitive Inertia, where models rigidly adhere to priors despite contradictory visual evidence, and Perceptual Inertia, where they blindly trust video perception while ignoring logical inconsistencies. To investigate this, we introduce TheaterBench, a benchmark designed to expose Modal Polarization using counter-intuitive scenarios that violate physical laws or common sense - situations rarely encountered in standard training distributions. Extensive experiments reveal that current state-of-the-art (SOTA) models exhibit severe Modal Polarization, and many de-hallucination methods merely rebalance the trade-off between Perceptual and Cognitive Inertia rather than fundamentally resolving the conflict. This work serves as a foundational step for evaluating and enhancing the perceptual-cognitive equilibrium of future video understanding models.
