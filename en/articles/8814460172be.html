CrossProjection: Benchmarking Spatial Grounding across Projection and Section Views

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00473

arXiv:2608.00473v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Plans, elevations, and sections describe a three-dimensional structure through incomplete projections that change how an entity is drawn, not only where it is seen from. Reading them means deciding which entities correspond across views and recovering where they lie. We introduce CrossProjection, a benchmark that uses architectural drawings to test whether vision-language models (VLMs) can turn correspondence decisions into geometric answers. Its tasks cover cross-view matching, projection registration, and novel-section prediction, with outputs ranging from judgments and candidate selection to free region boxes and directed section cuts. For inverse section localization, we introduce, to our knowledge, the first VLM benchmark protocol that accepts any cut within an expert-adjudicated band of compatible positions and checks the viewing direction separately. Manually annotated drawings and BIM-derived views provide 33,788 questions from 46 buildings, and we evaluate 22 VLMs on a common set of 2,763. Strong selection or reproduction does not imply cross-view localization: on 100 matched region targets, the best model redraws 98 marked boxes but localizes only 59 across views. Section cuts remain far from expert level, with the leading model trailing two architects by 51.9 and 67.4 percentage points on shared Natural and BIM line questions. Incorrect choices favor mirrored positions, and many cuts face the correct way but violate local geometry. Curriculum fine-tuning on BIM supervision teaches a 9B model to localize regions it could not localize before, raising box accuracy from 0% to 25.3% on held-out BIM buildings and from 0.3% to 30.8% on real drawing sets not used for fine-tuning, whereas section cuts remain largely out of reach.
