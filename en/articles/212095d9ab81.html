Do Machine Learning Ensembles Beat Published Quotas? Target Parameterization, Censored Baselines, and Conformal Intervals Under Admissions Reform

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34104

arXiv:2611.34104v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Public admission capacity caps published prior to matriculation are widely used by ministries and institutional analysts as default intake projections in centralized postsecondary clearinghouses. Using an audited 2022 to 2026 Vietnamese undergraduate panel (26,895 program-year records across 309 universities) linked to strictly lagged t-1 occupational vacancy signals, this paper evaluates machine learning, multilevel, and censored econometric estimators against published quotas across 5,385 continuing 2024 programs (five-fold university-grouped cross-validation) and 3,348 matched (2,179 grain-consistent) 2025 programs strictly out of time. Because 62.8% of 2024 programs enroll their exact published quota, unanchored log-growth ensembles degrade accuracy, while the worst 5% generate half of total quota error via unfilled "phantom capacity." Controlled target ablations, including a single-factor test under widened non-binding clipping, show that parameterizing the log fill ratio ln(D_it / S_it) instead of log growth reduces 2024 MAE by 21.5% (14.86 to 11.67), aligns ML with Tobit, Cragg, REML HLM, and capped L1 baselines, and lifts combined tuition and institutional-tier TreeSHAP attribution from 2.5% to 45.8%. Hurdle stacking attains the lowest full-census 2024 MAE (H-Hard 11.29; H-Soft 11.32 vs. 12.35, p = 0.022, clearing 10% not 5% FDR; fold-overlap p = 0.134), whereas continuous QA-Stack outperforms H-Soft on mixed-reporting campuses, Tobit (median) ties H-Soft across both 90% MCS sets, and interior-only Cragg-ML lowers RMSE to 28.1. Under Vietnam's 2025 unified-clearing reform, exact filling falls to 10.4%, H-Soft and capped L1 tie quotas (p = 0.979), and 90% cluster-weighted conformal coverage drops from 89.3% to 74.6%. Across 257 reporting campuses, reconciled 2026 national totals (627,114 to 629,256) lie squarely within 604,533 to 662,044.
