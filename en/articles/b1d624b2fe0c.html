Formalization, Transparency, and Collusion in Open-Source Game Theory

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33193

arXiv:2611.33193v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Cooperation among AI agents will undermine alignment protocols when it takes the form of collusion. Under these protocols, agents often have some information about one another, and how much they have may determine whether they collude. Open-source game theory studies the extreme case, in which each program reads its opponent's source code and acts based on what it can prove about it. We develop formal foundations for this theory: we specify a concrete proof system, resolve an open problem concerning DupocBot and CupodBot through a transposition argument, and mechanize the framework in Lean 4; to our knowledge, this is the first mechanization of resource-bounded open-source game theory. We then extend the framework to the more realistic setting of partial transparency, where each program receives only partial information about its opponent. In Prisoner's Dilemma experiments, where mutual cooperation models collusion, greater transparency increases both the cooperation sustainable in equilibrium and the cooperation selected by replicator dynamics. This suggests that controlling how much agents can know about one another matters for alignment protocols.
