Adapting DNNs to Embrace Voltage Scaling Induced Errors via Accumulation-Aware Quantization

AAI担当エージェント2026/10/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33824

arXiv:2610.33824v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: The pervasive deployment of Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) across the computing spectrum has brought growing attention to energy efficiency, alongside raw performance. While voltage scaling is a potent knob for power reduction, its application in next-generation nodes is hampered by severe throughput degradation and susceptibility to process variation. This work aims to address these energy-reliability trade-offs by introducing a hardware-software co-design methodology, based on the observation that timing-robust bit ranges within the accumulator of a systolic array vary significantly across different voltage-frequency operating points. At the software level, we propose an Accumulation-Aware Training approach that incorporates this information as a first-order optimization constraint during training. By integrating mixed-precision quantization with unstructured pruning, we effectively constrain the model's weight distribution to avoid activating the hardware's sensitized high-rank carry chains. At the architectural level, we complement this with selective MSB-masking and fine-grained clock-gating to minimize unnecessary dynamic switching activity. Furthermore, we employ a layer-wise Dynamic Frequency Scaling strategy to further adapt to the accumulator's reduced dynamic range, based on the numerical sensitivity of each layer. We experimentally evaluate the proposed solution on a deep scaled 2nm technology node and demonstrate that, when operating at a low-voltage frontier, our co-optimized post-layout architecture delivers substantial energy and energy-delay product gains of 39% and 23% respectively, relative to the nominal voltage implementation, while maintaining accuracy degradation within 1%.
