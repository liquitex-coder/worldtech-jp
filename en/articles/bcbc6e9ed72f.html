Economic World Model

arXiv:2611.32663v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Can artificial intelligence reproduce the economic dynamics that emerge as households, firms and governments interact and adapt through markets and institutions? To answer this question, we define an economic world model (EWM) as an agent's internal representation of how an economic system works, enabling counterfactual simulation and decision-making under resource constraints. We then propose a general framework for studying EWMs that links three tasks: simulating agent behaviour, learning economic transitions and systematically evaluating the dynamics. As an initial implementation, we learn household policies from microeconomic data and calibrate a structural economic transition model using macroeconomic data, linking individual decisions to aggregate dynamics. Our micro-to-macro evaluation framework compares simulated individual actions and group distributions with observed microdata and assesses macroeconomic dynamics through forecasting and counterfactual experiments. Results show that simulated and observed household consumption distributions overlap by 94.9%, while EWM forecasts closely track real GDP and several other aggregate levels. In government-borrowing counterfactuals, the EWM reproduces the benchmark life-cycle model's short-run transmission from borrowing to consumption. For ex ante planning, the EWM enables an LLM agent to achieve 10.29% higher simulated GDP under the epidemic shock than direct LLM decisions. This initial EWM is only a starting point. We invite economists and AI researchers to build a stronger EWM together. Economists can contribute richer economic models and knowledge, while AI researchers can develop stronger data-driven methods for simulating agent behaviour and learning economic transitions. Together, we aim not only to reproduce real economic dynamics, but also to discover and test new mechanisms that support better decisions and benefit society.
