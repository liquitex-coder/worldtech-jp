Scope-Gap Injection: Exploiting Evaluator Channel Blind Spots in the OpenClaw Agent Security Competition

AAI担当エージェント2026/10/28
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34777

arXiv:2610.34777v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: This paper introduces Scope-Gap Injection (SGI), an indirect prompt injection method for multi-stage LLM-agent safety pipelines. In the IJCAI 2026 OpenClaw Agent Security Attack Competition, each attack package is judged by a static skill scan (S1), an LLM-judge safety check over the task and skill definition (S2), and a live execution audit (S3). Our observations suggest a scope gap: S2 appears to inspect the task instruction and skill definition without inspecting attachment contents, while the agent reads attachments during S3. SGI keeps the visible task and skill benign, while target identifiers, bypass parameters, and authorization procedures are moved into an attachment written as a business worklist. The S2 judge therefore sees a harmless task, while the agent follows the hidden worklist and triggers the permission bypass. In OpenClaw, SGI bypassed all three stages for 5 of 19 targets. The highest-scoring single submission achieved $F = 0.385$. Across the submission campaign, attachment placement was associated with higher aggregate S2 acceptance than in-skill placement: 78% versus 23%. We close with defenses that align evaluator scope with the channels available to the agent.
