What Must LLM Agents Communicate? From Information Loss to Communication Failures

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33318

arXiv:2611.33318v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: When evidence is distributed across LLM agents, a team may collectively possess all the information needed for a decision yet fail to use it effectively. What must agents communicate for accurate joint predictions, and when does interaction help? We study three requirements for effective communication: sufficiency of the information conveyed, selection of relevant evidence, and utilization of received information. Drawing on information theory and communication complexity, we develop a framework that combines theoretical benchmarks with controlled experiments and message-level analyses. We find that local probability reports can discard essential information, with theoretical information loss closely matching observed performance penalties. Query-first interaction improves predictions when relevant evidence depends on a private query, but compact one-shot representations can eliminate this advantage. Even when sufficient information is communicated, agents may fail to select or use it correctly. Together, these findings distinguish fundamental communication limits from failures in how LLM agents communicate and use information.
