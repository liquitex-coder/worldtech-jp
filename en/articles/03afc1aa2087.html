Acoustic Markers of Engagement in Children with Autism Spectrum Disorder and Their Caregivers

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/24
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26810

arXiv:2611.26810v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Characterizing user engagement is essential for developing effective human-computer interaction (HCI) applications, particularly for children with autism spectrum disorder (ASD). Because home-based HCI naturally involves caregivers as conversational facilitators, analyzing both child and caregiver vocalizations can provide critical insights into characterizing interaction dynamics. Using a dataset of home-based socially assistive robot (SAR) interventions, this work demonstrates how child and caregiver vocalization patterns can model engagement in HCI contexts. We employed linear mixed-effects models (LMMs) with child and caregiver acoustic data to identify acoustic features that significantly differentiated engagement from disengagement. The results demonstrated that children and caregivers share three significant acoustic markers of engagement: mean Alpha Ratio (AlphaRatio), mean Harmonics-to-Noise Ratio (HNR), and mean Spectral Slope 500-1500 (SlopeV500-1500). This reflects cleaner and smoother vocalizations during engagement across both groups. Furthermore, we evaluated a State x Group interaction model to compare the magnitudes of standardized state-dependent differences between children and caregivers. The results indicated that children exhibit significantly greater state-related variation than caregivers in three acoustic features: AlphaRatio, HNR, and SlopeV500-1500.
