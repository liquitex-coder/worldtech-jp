StreamEmbed: Real-Time Indexing from Pixels for Screenshot Retrieval

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18516

arXiv:2611.18516v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Screen history can serve as searchable visual memory for people and agents, but unlike an offline corpus, it must be encoded and indexed as each screenshot arrives. OCR-based indexing is fast but loses visual information; visual encoding takes over two seconds. Yet screens change incrementally: most pixels repeat within and across screenshots. This raises a question: do visual encoders need to recompute their representations from scratch for every new screenshot? We present StreamEmbed, a system for incremental visual embedding that exploits this redundancy. Guided solely by pixel differences, it combines spatial token pruning, which skips blocks that repeat a neighbor, with temporal KV reuse, which reuses cached keys and values for blocks unchanged across screenshots. A small learned adapter compensates for pruning and reuse to preserve fidelity to full-screenshot embeddings. On ScreenMemEval, our benchmark of 10,426 questions over 184 hours of screen histories from two humans and a computer-use agent, StreamEmbed sets a new accuracy-latency Pareto frontier on an M2 Pro GPU: it encodes up to 3.7x faster than full-screenshot encoding without sacrificing accuracy and improves accuracy over OCR-based indexing by up to 6.3 Recall@10 points at matched latency. A memory-efficient vector index stores a simulated year of screenshots in 0.13 GB of RAM. Together, these results make real-time indexing of screen history in its native visual form practical, laying a foundation for fully local visual assistants.
