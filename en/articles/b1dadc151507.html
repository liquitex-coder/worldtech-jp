Beyond the Benchmark: Localizing Released Autonomous Driving Models to Real Vehicles

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/26
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31814

arXiv:2611.31814v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Released autonomous-driving models achieve strong benchmark performance, yet deploying them on a new real vehicle remains challenging. We study this deployment gap and find that released models can remain coupled to assumptions from their source sensing, data, and execution processes even after conventional adaptation. We introduce Released-to-Real (R2R), a deployment-localization methodology that identifies such source-system coupling and corrects only the affected components while preserving the released model. For data and behavior adaptation, we find that the mismatch between trajectory-level optimization and receding-horizon execution enters through the ego state rather than the observation, and introduce Human-Anchored Reinforcement Learning (HA-RL), which corrects it on recorded target driving without the costly rollouts of generative closed-loop reinforcement learning (RL). For physical sensor adaptation, R2R localizes and corrects source-dependent spatial and temporal representations with frozen perception weights, requiring neither target annotations nor retraining. HA-RL improves the closed-loop success rate of a released diffusion-based planner from 82.2% to 95.2%, while sensor adaptation across multiple released detectors and three real sensor rigs improves the vehicle mean average precision (mAP) of a released multi-camera detector from 0.041 to 0.420 on a five-camera target rig. These results provide a practical path for preparing released models before deployment on new real vehicles. Our project page is publicly available at https://released-to-real.github.io.
