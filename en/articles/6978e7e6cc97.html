A Spectral Framework for Universal Proxy Regularization in Model Merging

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17728

arXiv:2611.17728v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Model merging constructs multi-task models without expensive joint training but often suffers from parameter interference. Some solutions mitigate this by constraining finetuning using data from the other tasks to be merged, violating the core independence and privacy assumption of model merging. Recent work offers preliminary empirical evidence that a universal, task-agnostic proxy distribution can substitute for such data, but leaves open when and why it suffices. To this end, we propose a \textit{Spectral Framework for Universal Proxy Regularization}, which characterizes the relationship between a proxy distribution and the distribution of other tasks to be merged through spectral coverage ($\alpha$) and leakage ($\beta$). Based on the framework, we derive a unified parameter interference bound: smaller $(\alpha, \beta)$ provably yields lower parameter interference through better spectral alignment. To exploit such a proxy, we augment the training objective with a proxy regularizer. Evaluating this regularizer during fine-tuning requires a concrete mechanism. We study three instantiations, including (J) an exact evaluation via Jacobian-vector products, (F) a frozen curvature approximation using the Fisher information matrix, and (O) an output-drift formulation that constrains the model's functional deviation on the proxy directly. We prove that all three coincide under the NTK linearization, where existing analyses of such regularizers stop, and go further to characterize standard non-linear fine-tuning, bounding how far each instantiation departs from its linearized reference and obtain a three-way hierarchy, zero for (J), proxy-independent for (F), and proxy-quality-scaling for (O). Empirical validation on the commonly used vision-language model CLIP confirms these theoretical predictions.
