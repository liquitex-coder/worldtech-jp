Uncertainty-aware damage identification in short-span bridges via physics-informed variational autoencoder

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/20
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05025

arXiv:2607.05025v3 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Vibration-based damage identification in civil infrastructure is a challenging, ill-posed inverse problem due to measurement noise, sparse sensor arrays, and modeling errors. While deep learning is powerful for system identification, deterministic approaches lack reliable uncertainty quantification and can yield physically inconsistent results. This work proposes a probabilistic Scientific Machine Learning framework for structural health monitoring: a physics-informed Gaussian copula variational autoencoder. The methodology introduces two main contributions. First, a numerical eigenvalue solver is embedded directly into the variational autoencoder as its decoder, which replaces data-driven surrogates, ensures that every posterior sample satisfies the governing equations of structural dynamics, and reduces the number of trainable parameters. Second, a Gaussian copula describes the posterior distribution of the stiffness factors, capturing cross-correlations between components with fewer parameters than Gaussian mixtures in real time. We validate the method numerically on a short-span bridge supported on elastic bearings, whose response is dominated by vertical bending, accounting for modeling errors, measurement noise, and a finer finite element discretization for testing. The method estimates the posterior distribution of the damage condition, i.e., the stiffness reduction factors of the deck zones and bearings, and these posteriors closely match the exact ones computed with sequential Monte Carlo sampling. The results reveal that the proposed methodology offers a robust and interpretable decision-making tool, able not only to indicate the presence and location of damage, but also to inform on the uncertainty associated with the diagnosis, for instance, when light damage is masked by the effect of noise or when the measured modal properties present limited sensitivity to damage.
