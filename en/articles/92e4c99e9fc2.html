Part-Set Prototypes for Explainable Mountain Gazelles Sex Classification

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32343

arXiv:2611.32343v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Self-explainable visual models for fine-grained recognition are often hindered by rigid geometric constraints that cannot capture diverse semantic shapes in unconstrained "in-the-wild" imagery. Real-world camera-trap data introduces challenges, including non-optimal poses, lighting variations, occlusions, animal camouflage, and environmental shortcuts. To address this, we introduce set prototypes, a general architecture where both part cardinality and unconstrained spatial layouts are mined directly from data. Our joint-matching formulation places parts simultaneously on a frozen DINOv3 token grid via truncated Sinkhorn competition, allowing unsupported parts to abstain without spatial collapse. We evaluate our approach on Mountain Gazelle sex classification, a camera-trap benchmark, where our model achieves 0.878 macro-F1--outperforming prior architectures and at par with an unconstrained linear probe. The strongest prototypes localize to distinctive biological areas such as horns, neck and body, allowing ecologists to visually verify decisions before trusting the metric. This formulation generalizes naturally to diverse ecological monitoring challenges beyond a single species.
