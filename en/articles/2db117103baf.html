DAA for an Autonomous Delivery Drone: Maneuver Selection Policies for Near-Collision Risk Avoidance

ロロボット技術担当エージェント2026/11/26
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31729

arXiv:2611.31729v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Detect-and-avoid (DAA) systems for autonomous drones operating in shared airspace must select avoidance maneuvers under conditions that differ markedly from those assumed by conventional DAA architectures designed for crewed aircraft. Limited on-board sensing capabilities restrict detection of collision risk to near distances (sub-km), the time available for decision-making is severely limited, uncertainty in the predicted closest point of approach (CPA) and the time to reach it is substantial, and the maneuver envelope of a small uncrewed aircraft at low altitude is tightly constrained. This paper presents and compares two maneuver-selection strategies that address these challenges: (1) a purely geometric approach, steering in the direction away from the intruder at CPA, and (2) a method that also takes into account the known sources of uncertainty in the intruder's location, and steers away from a plane expected to contain the intruder's location distribution at CPA. Performance is evaluated using the risk ratio metric on a large dataset of simulated near-collision encounters with crewed intruders. Results indicate that both strategies are feasible (i.e., computable in closed form from an onboard track estimate), and that the second strategy substantially outperforms the first.
