Blind Spots Travel, Knowledge Stays Home: What RL Inherits from an LLM Judge

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/21
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25191

arXiv:2611.25191v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: LLM judges serve as rewards where no answer key exists, in the hope that their knowledge passes to the trained model. In 814 RL runs that teach 7-8B models to answer closed-book questions or abstain, we ask what passes. What a grader knows about single answers stays home. A wrong answer that a judge rewards is memorised for that one question. A model trained with the answer key, or with a far stronger judge, chooses which new questions to answer no better than the untrained model does by sampling 32 answers and abstaining where they disagree; the grader's knowledge adds about one corrected answer per hundred questions. What a grader believes about a kind of question travels. A judge that rejects eleven of the twelve correct training answers about capitals of former states teaches the model to refuse 98% of such capitals on new questions, a loss that accuracy and abstention rates do not reveal. Changing only those twelve verdicts by hand moves the refusal from 5% to 90%, even for a grader that accepts as many answers as the answer key; at that leniency, two of four judges already cost 38-42% of these capitals. The capitals are not forgotten: with "I don't know" blocked, the model answers 94% of them correctly. The grader therefore needs no knowledge of its own: rewarding the model for giving the answer that most of its own untrained samples agree on, with no labels and no judge, earns the answer key's reward and keeps the category.
