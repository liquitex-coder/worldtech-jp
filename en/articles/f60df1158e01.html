Motion Without State: Probing Dynamical Consistency and Conditioning Bias in Video Models

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05135

arXiv:2610.05135v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Image-to-video models serve as visual world priors whose plausible motion departs from physical simulation. We ask what dynamical structure replaces forward state propagation in generated motion, and how it biases quantitative physics evaluation. Holding law and initial state fixed, we vary visible geometry, prompt wording, and clip length across nine models and compare tracked trajectories against simulator rollouts. Generated motion splits into local visual cues and global boundary control, omitting the propagating physical state between them. Locally, tempo-normalized speed follows track undulations with a 15{\deg}-41{\deg} phase lead toward steepest descent as mean slope barely accelerates the ball, showing short memory of past slope. Local responses fail to compose: no single contact law describes a model's bounces (prescribed, self-calibrated on single-deflector contacts, or fitted in-sample), and balls clear 43 of 49 barriers above release height under an outcome-free prompt. Globally, naming the catch tray lifts arrival 14-76 percentage points above post-contact simulator rollouts, and event duration scales near-linearly with clip length, making apparent gravity 5.6-7.3 times higher in 3 s than in 8 s clips. These patterns persist on photographed first frames, motivating neutral-prompt controls, exit-state rollouts, and tempo-normalized metrics.
