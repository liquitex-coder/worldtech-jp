Depth Enables Local Entropy: Quadratic Depth Dependence in Deep Variation-Norm ReLU Regression

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/27
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17434

arXiv:2608.17434v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: How small can a layer-sum variation budget be while deep ReLU regression keeps a statistical complexity quadratic in depth? We study Gaussian regression on the circle over compositions of $L$ vector-valued ReLU blocks of width $w$, whose second-order variation costs sum to at most $A$. With unit output bound and noise level, a budget $CLw^\varepsilon$ already forces minimax risk $\Omega(\varepsilon^2\min\{1,L^2w^2\log w/n\})$ for each fixed $\varepsilon\in(0,2]$ and $L\gtrsim1/\varepsilon$. This is far below the budget of order $Lw^2$ needed when every layer carries the full gain. When $L\gtrsim\log w$, a budget $CL$ forces risk $\Omega(\min\{1,L^2w^2/(n\log w)\})$, and over a wide range of sample sizes no budget below a small multiple of $L$ reaches this risk. Conversely, every function in the class has derivative variation at most $2(A/L)^L$, independently of the width, and in a low-budget regime the minimax risk matches that of a one-dimensional second-variation ball of radius $(A/L)^L$. The budget thus moves the class from the behavior of a one-dimensional smoothness ball to statistical complexity quadratic in depth, and a budget linear in depth already suffices for the latter.
