PRISM: A Post-Prediction Reliability Framework for Evidence-Guided Correction and Selective Abstention

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18127

arXiv:2611.18127v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: A model may produce a prediction with high confidence and still get the outcome wrong. This is a challenge in sequential applications, where the system evolves over time and deployment data may differ from the data used during training. To address this issue, we propose PRISM (Prediction Reliability and Intelligent Self-Correction Mechanism), a framework that evaluates prediction reliability after the initial output is generated. It estimates the risk of error and examines predictions identified as potentially unreliable. For these cases, PRISM analyses available signals, retrieves similar situations from historical data, and verifies whether an alternative prediction is supported by the evidence. It can then retain the original prediction, apply a verified correction, or abstain when the evidence is insufficient. We evaluate PRISM on second-innings match prediction in the Indian Premier League (IPL). Ball-by-ball records are converted into over-level observations to capture changes in match conditions throughout an innings. To preserve temporal order, earlier IPL seasons are used for training, IPL 2024 for validation, and IPL 2025-2026 for out-of-time testing. The dataset contains 8,665 over-level observations from 475 matches, with the test partition comprising 2,568 observations from 142 matches. In one experimental record, Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and HistGradientBoosting achieve accuracies of 65.2%, 68.4%, and 70.2%, respectively. The corresponding PRISM evaluation reports a selective accuracy of 82.4% at 88.5% coverage, with a Brier score of 0.126 and an expected calibration error (ECE) of 0.019.
