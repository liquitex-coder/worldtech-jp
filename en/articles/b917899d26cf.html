Deep Neural Networks Training Efficiency: Does Grokking Modify its Dynamics?

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/17
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18396

arXiv:2611.18396v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We study whether grokking, the delayed emergence of out-of-distribution generalization after apparent overfitting, alters training efficiency in deep neural networks, where efficiency is measured as accuracy per unit energy. Using two canonical settings, modular arithmetic and MNIST, we analyze two neural network models under 16-, 32-, and 64-bit precision and use Orthograd to test whether grokking produces noticeable deviations from the typically decreasing dynamics of training efficiency. We consider both short training runs of up to 500 epochs and long training runs of up to 20,000 epochs, and in both cases identify grokking as a sharp improvement in test accuracy after a sustained train-test gap. In the short-horizon experiments, applying Orthograd at progressively stronger levels during early training does not induce grokking, and efficiency remains smoothly decreasing. In the long-horizon experiments, Orthograd induces grokking, but the efficiency curves remain approximately monotonic and show no appreciable local increase at the grokking transition. These results suggest that, in our setting, grokking does not materially alter overall training-efficiency dynamics.
