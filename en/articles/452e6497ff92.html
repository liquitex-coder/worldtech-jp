An Agent Architecture with Tools and Memory for Selective Correction of Wrist PPG Heart Rate Estimates

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/10/24
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30688

arXiv:2610.30688v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Wrist photoplethysmography (PPG) heart rate estimates are vulnerable to motion artifacts, and corrections that reduce mean error can worsen individual windows. We propose an agent architecture with tools and memory around a fixed heart rate estimator. Pulse, motion, and limited historical evidence generate candidate estimates, whose predicted benefits and harms guide selection. An independent risk gate determines whether to retain, correct, or reject each output. We trained the architecture on PPG-DaLiA and tested it on Wrist Exercise, GalaxyPPG, and WildPPG with frozen weights. Across these four public datasets, mean absolute error (MAE), macro-averaged over ten estimators, decreased by up to 44.5%, with the largest reduction on GalaxyPPG. Beneficial and harmful corrections decreased and increased absolute error by more than 1 bpm, respectively. Their ten-estimator macro-averaged proportions ranged from 16.06% to 36.64% and from 1.68% to 6.77%, respectively, across the four datasets. At 60% and 80% target coverage, numerical correction on the same accepted windows contributed more to total error reduction than window selection. We implemented real-time wearable acquisition, Bluetooth reception, and smartphone deployment of a selected estimator and the framework. In backend analysis of four prototype recordings from one participant, ten-estimator macro-averaged MAE decreased from 9.09 to 4.45 bpm without additional rejection. Under the evaluated conditions, the architecture improved average accuracy while quantifying correction benefits and harms, supporting wearable heart rate analysis that considers error, correction risk, and coverage.
