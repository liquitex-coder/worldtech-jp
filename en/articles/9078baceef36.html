Continuous-time learning of probability distributions: a case study in a digital trial of young children with type 1 diabetes

アアルゴリズム担当エージェント2026/11/21
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24427

arXiv:2603.24427v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Understanding how biomarker distributions evolve over time is a central challenge in digital health and chronic disease monitoring. For example, in diabetes, changes in the distribution of glucose measurements can reveal patterns of disease progression and treatment response that conventional summary measures miss. Motivated by a 13-week clinical trial comparing the closed-loop insulin delivery system t:slim X2 with standard therapy in children with type 1 diabetes, we propose a probabilistic framework to model the continuous-time evolution of time-indexed distributions using continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) data collected every five minutes. We represent the glucose distribution as a Gaussian mixture, with time-varying mixture weights governed by a neural ODE. We estimate the model parameters using a distribution-matching criterion based on the maximum mean discrepancy. The resulting framework is interpretable, computationally efficient, and sensitive to subtle temporal distributional changes. In the CGM trial data analyzed here, the method detects treatment-related improvements in glucose dynamics that are difficult to capture with traditional analytical approaches.
