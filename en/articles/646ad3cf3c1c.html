Resolution as Attention for Efficient Agent Memory Perception

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/21
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24422

arXiv:2611.24422v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: LLM-based agents accumulate extensive interaction histories when solving complex, long-horizon tasks, making efficient memory management critical to their performance and scalability. While existing memory management methods primarily focus on what to retain or retrieve, they often overlook how much perceptual detail should be allocated to individual past interactions. We posit that effective agent memory depends not only on the information preserved, but also on the granularity at which each past interaction is perceived. Motivated by this insight, we propose Resolution as Attention (RAA), a memory perception mechanism that uses visual resolution as a means of attention allocation across interaction histories. RAA renders past interactions as images and dynamically adjusts their resolution at every step according to their relevance to the agent's upcoming decision, preserving fine-grained information for critical interactions while reducing visual-token consumption for less relevant ones. To adaptively determine the perceptual fidelity of each interaction, we train a lightweight relevance estimator with teacher-model guidance and further optimize it to preserve the agent's decision consistency at a reduced visual-token cost. Experiments across multiple tasks and backbones show that RAA reduces token consumption by over 50% without compromising task performance. Moreover, RAA can be integrated as a plug-in perception layer with existing memory management methods, yielding further token savings and revealing adaptive perception as a complementary dimension of agent memory.
