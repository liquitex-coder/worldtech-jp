Eight in Ten LLMs Leak System-Prompt Secrets: Nevan, an Agentic Capture-the-Flag Study

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/20
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21956

arXiv:2611.21956v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: We audit instruction-hierarchy enforcement across 363 text-in/text-out models from the OpenRouter catalog. The research question we answer with this experiment is which models disclose a protected secret embedded in the system prompt, and through which techniques. Each model guards a secret 16-character flag set in its system prompt, while an artificial intelligence (AI) agent (OpenCode, powered by DeepSeek V4.1 Flash), steered by a human researcher, tries to persuade it to emit the flag. The flag was exfiltrated from 296 of the 363 models, plus four models released after the initial catalog freeze, for 300 captures in total. Of these, 170 involved a non-English language pivot, most often French. Each capture shows that a model ranked a user request above its system instruction at least once. It does not show that the model provably resists untested attacks, nor that it would consistently leak semantically sensitive data, nor that it is vulnerable to harmful-content jailbreaks. For practitioners, we draw three lessons: do not store secrets in system prompts, enforce critical behavior outside the model with output validation and deterministic checks, and do not let a large language model (LLM) judge be the sole decider of consequential outcomes.
