Benchmarking LLM abilities on postdoc-level natural science problems

AAI担当エージェント2026/11/21
ENOriginal-language text (excerpt). 日本語訳は こちら。 Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24321

arXiv:2611.24321v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Scientific problems have no answer key, so evaluating an agent means deciding what counts as a better solution before any agent is run. Existing benchmarks either match a symbolic answer against a key or, where they require executable work, score it by unit tests, by a rubric, or by position on a competition leaderboard. None of these is the criterion by which a field decides that one published method beats another. STEMBench adopts that criterion directly. Each of its twelve research problems in high-energy physics, astrophysics and neuroscience is scored by its own field's figure of merit, and each comes with two measured anchors: a submission that uses no domain knowledge, and an expert reference reproducing a published method, both obtained by running code shipped with the benchmark. The interval between them states how much of a problem is crossed by scientific insight rather than by engineering. Across eight frontier models, task-level wins and domain-level leadership dissociate: a model that wins five of the twelve tasks leads no domain mean. In the high-effort cross-model comparison, at least one model's mean score exceeds the expert anchor on seven of twelve tasks. The remaining five span uncertainty estimation, latent and causal inference, and cross-site clinical classification. Numerical scores and official-submission validity capture different aspects of performance and are reported separately. We release the tasks, graders, anchors and harness.
