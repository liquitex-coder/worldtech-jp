ORIGINAL · ENarXiv:2611.25215v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Autonomous navigation in diverse real-world environments remains one of the greatest challenges in robotics. It demands both robust low-level motion control to handle diverse terrain and high-level reasoning to interpret environmental cues. We present ROAM (Robust Open-world Autonomous Mobility), a system that enables legged robots to perform kilometer-scale navigation in complex alpine and urban settings. ROAM combines the world knowledge of a Vision-Language Model (VLM), pre-trained on internet-scale data, with robust locomotion and local planning policies learned in simulation via reinforcement learning (RL). The VLM provides common-sense reasoning and semantic interpretation of the scene, assessing risks, interpreting signs, and producing semantic labels with associated navigation costs. An open-vocabulary segmentation module converts these labels into a dense, real-time risk map, which conditions two pre-trained RL policies responsible for agile locomotion and local navigation, effectively coupling high-level VLM reasoning with low-level physical control. Real-world experiments showed the system's ability to autonomously navigate a wide range of complex real-world environments. The robot completes kilometer-long hikes with minimal intervention, interprets signs to avoid danger, and evaluates its environment to identify safe areas on which to step. This work represents a step toward the reliable deployment of systems in unstructured environments. However, it also underscores the challenges that remain in VLM reliability and spatial reasoning, indicating that foundational models are a crucial component but not yet a complete solution for navigation in the wild.
