ORIGINAL · ENarXiv:2611.24644v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Muon updates each hidden weight matrix along the polar factor of its momentum, the steepest-descent direction under the spectral norm. That choice fixes two allocations without consulting the loss: every singular direction of the momentum receives the same step, and in a non-square matrix each neuron's step is set by its leverage score. Whether they are good is a question about curvature, which steepest-descent arguments do not answer; we measure it. Using held-out gradients and Hessian-vector products, we decompose Muon's step by singular-value band and by neuron group and compute each group's second-order optimal multiplier. In a 124M-parameter decoder trained on FineWeb-Edu, the multipliers rise monotonically from the top singular directions to the tail: Muon over-steps the high-curvature top of its spectrum. We prove in Lean when such a diagnostic licenses an intervention at Muon's own update norm. On the measured curvature, the criterion predicts, and held-out losses confirm, that moving step length off the top helps mid-training and early in the cooldown, but not at its end. We then test interventions under per-step norm matching, tuned baselines and paired seeds. Orthogonalizing Q, K and V separately rather than as one fused matrix lowers the loss by 0.0250 nats and accounts for all of the gain of block-wise variants. A matmul-only, rotation-equivariant spectral tilt $\sigma^{-1/4}$ applied in the cooldown lowers the loss of this stronger baseline by a further 0.0120 nats, additively with NorMuon (0.0158 nats together, three seeds; the baseline needs about 4.3% more tokens to match), and by 0.0188 nats at 355M parameters (three seeds). Energy-matched Schatten-1 weight decay adds a further 0.0052 nats. Geometrically principled changes that do not act on the measured miscalibration, namely exact polar factors, steepest-descent input whitening and top-heavy decay, do not help.
