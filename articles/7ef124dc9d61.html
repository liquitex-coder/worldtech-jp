ORIGINAL · ENarXiv:2611.26909v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Pit-stop strategy in Formula 1 is a sequential decision problem under uncertainty, in which strategists decide at each lap whether to pit and which tire compound to fit. We develop a data-driven simulator, calibrated with historical race data, that captures tire degradation, drivers' recent performance, overtaking, yellow flag events, and rivals' pit-stop decisions. We train agents with five Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) algorithms and compare them with a Quadratic Mixed-Integer Programming (QMIP) formulation that is optimal in deterministic single-driver settings. Without uncertainty or competition, all DRL methods except TRPO achieve near-optimal performance. With a full grid of competitors and stochastic race events, the ranking of DRL methods depends on the evaluation setting: Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and its recurrent variant are the only methods that improve on the QMIP both in fully simulated races and when the actual 2024 races are replayed. Averaged over eight circuits, PPO improves on the QMIP by 0.33 and 0.49 positions in the two settings and Recurrent PPO by less, and in the replay PPO finishes 0.66 positions ahead of the drivers' actual strategies simulated in the same races. The QMIP performs similarly to the other methods, even though it cannot anticipate yellow flag events. Finally, we use the trained PPO policy as a look-ahead decision-support tool that shows the strategist the distribution of race outcomes under each candidate pit-stop action. Applied retrospectively to eight 2024 races, the look-ahead identifies the laps where the actual decision, the recommendation, or a third action was preferable.
