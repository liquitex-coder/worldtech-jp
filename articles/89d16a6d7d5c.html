ORIGINAL · ENarXiv:2611.13059v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Languages of India need 1.6 to 14 times as many tokens as English on GPT-4o's tokenizer for the same content, which makes them more expensive and slower to serve. We train tokenizers from scratch on human-written text in about 200 languages. Havon v3 is a byte-level BPE tokenizer with 262,144 tokens and a pre-tokenizer that keeps combining marks with their letters and chunks scripts written without spaces. On FLORES-200 devtest it needs fewer tokens than GPT-4o's o200k_base on all 25 languages of India (median 0.74 of GPT-4o's count) and on 172 of 204 languages, but 7% more on English. Havon v4 adds a second, multi-word training stage (SuperBPE) to v3. At exactly GPT-4o's vocabulary size of 200,019 tokens, v4 needs 6.1% fewer tokens than GPT-4o on English and fewer on 161 of 204 languages and all 25 languages of India (median 0.74); at the same size, v3 needs 9.4% more on English and wins on 133. With 262,144 tokens, v4 needs 9.6% fewer tokens on English and fewer on 182 of 204 languages, and with 524,288 tokens it needs fewer on 203 of 204. The gains are largest for English, Hindi and the Romance languages; scripts without spaces, such as Khmer, Burmese and Tibetan, gain little and lose subword merges. Havon v5 replaces v4's Python-only code sample with permissively licensed code in 15 languages: on code it needs 1.03 to 1.22 times GPT-4o's tokens, against 1.11 to 1.29 for v4, while keeping all 25 languages of India (median 0.74, all significant under a sentence-level bootstrap) and English (0.94). These are compression results only: we have not yet evaluated a model trained with v4 or v5.
