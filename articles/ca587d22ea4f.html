ORIGINAL · ENarXiv:2611.27582v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Trunk-basis (TB) preconditioners build the coarse space of a two-level Schwarz preconditioner from the trunk functions of a trained deep operator network (DeepONet), restricted to subdomains. Their effectiveness has been attributed to spectral bias since networks learn low frequencies first, complementing a smoother that removes the high-frequency error. Spectral bias, however, is a property of training, whereas a randomly initialized trunk with a smooth activation is already smooth. We therefore ask whether TB preconditioners need training. In one dimension, we prove that on each subdomain the span of untrained trunk functions is close to the space of low-degree polynomials under suitable nondegeneracy assumptions. Numerically, for the Poisson equation and three-dimensional linear elasticity with constant coefficients, untrained TB and piecewise polynomial coarse spaces of equal dimension yield close conjugate gradient iteration counts, while training with the standard \(\ell^2\) loss sometimes increases them. Training can improve iteration counts when high-contrast coefficients make the low-energy modes non-smooth, and if the loss measures the error in the energy norm. For the Poisson equation with contrast \(10^5\), energy-trained TB coarse spaces need fewer iterations than piecewise polynomials of equal dimension, whereas \(\ell^2\)-trained ones do not. For a topology-optimized elastic cantilever with contrast \(10^6\), the energy-trained TB space enriched with the rigid-body modes gives the fewest iterations at larger coarse dimensions. This suggests that for neural operator-based hybrid solvers, a network's inductive bias and training objective should match the solver.
