ORIGINAL · ENarXiv:2610.05533v3 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: A harness search around a frozen longevity-tuned 1.7B model reported a rescue. An audit found that the gain came from rows the start left unparsed, that the caller sent each row's reference answer as an assistant turn, and that replication through the same pipeline missed it. Is a harness's gain more than a constant answer would give? We preregistered three studies and report every endpoint. In the first two, thinking off raised test accuracy over a capped thinking setting in 7 of 12 cells, mostly on rows the cap left unparsed. It was not meaningfully worse than a rescue in 7 of 9 small-model test cells or than four GEPA harnesses at 1.7B. Replication reproduced an injected defect's distortion in 9 of 20 evaluable cases (7 fixed by design). At 14B, two GEPA harnesses beat thinking off by 34 points on test but were not detectably above the strongest constant label, and answered "Yes" in 94.6% and 98.2% of parsed mortality answers on test. In the third study (SmolLM3-3B, base Qwen3-1.7B), GEPA beat the constant on FPB (confirmatory, both partitions); thinking off was not meaningfully worse than any BoolQ harness, and 3 of 4 MMLU-Pro searches kept their seed (the fourth inconclusive on test). On Autocast (exploratory), the harnesses were within 0.7 points of the constant and gave one label in 93.1% to 96.8% of parsed answers, both on test. On questions about the input or about knowledge (FPB, BoolQ, MMLU-Pro), GEPA beat the constant or found no clear gain over thinking off; on questions that ask for a prediction (14B mortality, descriptive; Autocast, exploratory), the harness ended near the constant and gave one label almost throughout while its gain over thinking off looked large. The constant label told the two apart wherever the gain was large. This rests on few tasks that also differ in label skew, number of labels and task type, so it is not shown to depend on the kind of question.
