ORIGINAL · ENarXiv:2611.33234v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Large, well-annotated datasets are essential for developing medical artificial intelligence models, yet data sharing is often restricted by privacy regulations. Synthetic images may help address this limitation if they preserve diagnostic information while reducing the risk of reproducing patient data. We developed a diffusion autoencoder-based framework for class-conditional synthetic chest CT generation that incorporates latent-space distance filtering to reduce the likelihood of regenerating training samples. A diffusion autoencoder was trained on the public RSNA 2020 Pulmonary Embolism (PE) dataset. PE-positive and PE-negative images were synthesized by sampling latent representations from class-specific kernel density estimates. Samples with a nearest-neighbor latent-space distance below a predefined threshold were rejected. To assess diagnostic utility, ResNet-50 classifiers were trained across 10 independent runs using real, synthetic, or mixed real-synthetic datasets. Performance was evaluated on a held-out real test set using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), with DeLong 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Models trained on synthetic data achieved an AUC of 0.862 (95% CI, 0.851-0.874), only slightly lower than models trained on real data (AUC = 0.872, 95% CI, 0.861-0.883) and mixed real-synthetic data (AUC = 0.865, 95% CI, 0.855-0.876). These differences were statistically significant (p < 0.05) but small in absolute terms. A nearest-neighbor copy-detection analysis of 38,000 synthetic slices found no direct copies of real training images. These findings indicate that the synthetic CT images preserve diagnostically meaningful information for image-level PE classification while incorporating measures intended to enhance privacy awareness and reduce similarity to real patient data.
