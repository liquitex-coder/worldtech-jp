ORIGINAL · ENarXiv:2608.14590v3 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Large Language Model (LLM) agents take irreversible real-world actions, yet no system gives formally grounded, task-level safety guarantees for their plans. In a PRISMA 2020 systematic review, we searched six databases (20-29 May 2026) for work on LLM agent specification, verification, and enforcement published in 2022-2026. We narratively synthesized 38 studies, assessed risk of bias per study, and graded evidence certainty with GRADE. Three authors independently screened a repeated search to validate the single-reviewer screening (agreement: Fleiss' $\kappa = 0.81$). First, specification is the bottleneck: on one benchmark, only 24.8-34.2% of LLM translations from natural language into Planning Domain Definition Language (PDDL) are semantically correct. Second, runtime monitoring is the most mature enforcement strategy; it reduced unsafe actions by 65.37% in one household-agent study, but its guarantees cover only single steps or short horizons. Third, in one $\tau$-Bench study of transactional tool-use agents, blocking 94% of unsafe actions still left fewer than 5% of tasks completed both safely and correctly in most settings, because agents found other unsafe routes (the verifier tax). Fourth, no approach simultaneously achieves soundness, completeness, scalability, semantic grounding, and utility preservation. For seven of the nine principal claims, GRADE gives the lowest certainty rating (Very Low), meaning further studies are likely to produce substantially different figures; each rests on one or two studies in narrow domains. Two of the open problems we rank highest are translating requirements into formal specifications that keep their meaning and enforcing safety over whole plans rather than single actions. We contribute a three-level taxonomy and an agenda of ten open problems.
