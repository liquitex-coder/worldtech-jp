ORIGINAL · ENarXiv:2611.20452v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We present XMimic and XSoma, which learn humanoid motion tracking and locomotion on rough terrain in under a minute of training. Both follow SuperMimic, which learns a world model of the robot and optimizes the policy with analytic gradients through short rollouts imagined in this model. Existing implementations of this method do not make use of larger GPUs, as every training iteration is a long chain of small GPU operations that run one after another. XMimic and XSoma therefore shorten this chain and run the steps of every iteration at the same time on up to four GPUs. We compile the complete training loop with JAX, including the MuJoCo Warp simulator. For locomotion, XSoma leaves only the dynamics of the robot to the world model and computes everything else exactly, including the terrain height under the feet. It learns with a horizon of one second and without a critic. On four H100 GPUs, XMimic learns to track all 40 LAFAN1 clips with the Unitree G1 in 0.86 minutes, 3.7 times faster than BlinkMimic on one GPU. XSoma reaches the tracking target that Holosoma's tests set for FastSAC in 0.77 minutes, 12.3 times faster than FastSAC on one GPU, while needing only an eighth of its environment steps.
