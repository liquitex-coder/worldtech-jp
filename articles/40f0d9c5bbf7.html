ORIGINAL · ENarXiv:2611.29989v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: We tested whether SHAP improves protein-based Horn rules for distinguishing lung adenocarcinoma from adjacent-normal tissue. CPTAC supplied 213 samples (111 tumour, 102 adjacent-normal) from 111 patients, including 102 matched pairs. Per-protein effects and tests used the same complete pairs with Benjamini-Hochberg correction. Sparse logistic regression used five stratified patient-grouped outer folds, with preprocessing and tuning confined to training data and feature selection repeated within inner folds. A separate five-fold patient-grouped evaluation of inductive logic programming used training-derived evidence and discretisation. Of 9,518 proteins passing quality control, 378 differed in abundance (40 up, 338 down) at FDR below 0.05 and an absolute mean paired log-ratio difference of at least 1. Classifier ROC-AUC, average precision, accuracy and balanced accuracy were 0.9997, 0.9998, 0.9906 and 0.9906, respectively. On the original held-out rule splits, accuracy and F1 were 0.9765 and 0.9774, respectively. One clause appeared in two folds and the others appeared once. On common folds across all 111 patients, F1 was 0.9600 with SHAP, 0.9910 without SHAP and 0.9909 for L1 logistic regression. The inclusive SHAP cutoff retained all 100 ranked proteins per audited fold. Absolute logistic-regression coefficients reproduced the SHAP-guided candidate sets, clauses and held-out predictions in both the all-patient and paired-only analyses. A single-protein threshold reproduced full-rule predictions in the paired-only cohort. SHAP guidance did not improve held-out F1 on the common folds. Diagnostic utility, early detection, prognosis and generalisability beyond this cohort remain unestablished. Biomarker and clinical claims require independent validation, rule-stability testing under repeated resampling and further biological annotation.
