ORIGINAL · ENarXiv:2611.31916v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Extreme-mass-ratio inspirals (EMRIs), stellar-mass compact objects spiralling into massive black holes, promise to measure the masses and spins of the central black holes, reveal the strong-field environment around them and gauge cosmic distances via millihertz gravitational-wave observations. Yet most forecasts for the parameter inference of these systems assume known source parameters, and broad-prior analyses have addressed only simplified versions of the problem. The joint inversion of all 14 parameters of a spinning source from the complete prior has remained unsolved. Here we solve it for the augmented analytic kludge model: generative networks narrow the complete prior and reveal a ring of degenerate spin directions, and the exact detector likelihood determines the posterior. For a simulated one-year LISA-Taiji observation, within one ring sector we obtain, for the first time, a 14-parameter posterior of a spinning EMRI from the complete prior, the most precise yet obtained for such a source from broad priors. The redshifted mass and spin of the massive black hole are determined to two parts in $10^5$, the mass two orders of magnitude beyond any electromagnetic measurement. The compact object's redshifted mass is measured to one part in $10^4$, and its eccentric, retrograde orbit discriminates between capture channels. The 0.8% distance and sub-square-degree localization are sufficient for dark-siren cosmology. Year-long coherent analysis resolves the inspiral driven by radiation reaction itself.
