ORIGINAL · ENarXiv:2611.16477v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Laya is a small model that answers a multiple-choice question about a text in one pass: the question, the options (each with a name and a description) and the text form one sequence of at most 512 tokens, the window, in the English release. A longer text is silently cut at the end, and we measure what that costs. Test documents place a held-out row's text, the evidence, at a chosen token offset among distractor text; 177 items, five tasks, three checkpoints (released, short-row fine-tune, long-document fine-tune), four window sizes and 93,275 scored requests. When the evidence lies beyond the window, accuracy falls to 26.4% for the released checkpoint (27.2% for both fine-tunes), near the 27.0% of guessing and below the 35.6% of always giving the commonest label. Confidence gives no warning (AUROC 0.50 to 0.55 for telling cut from read requests; 0.5 = chance), and the runtime's truncation flag also fires on requests whose evidence was visible. The released checkpoint reads visible evidence badly once text precedes it: 38.5% pooled against 60% when the evidence comes first, so a larger window alone gains +6.2 points. Fine-tuning on long documents adds +27.6 points on visible evidence over the short-row fine-tune (95% interval +22.3 to +33.1) and nothing on cut evidence. That is an in-family result: training rows and test documents share generator and question types, and the main filler is of the training kind. With encyclopedic filler never seen in training the gain is +13.2 (+9.9 to +16.6), with rows of other tasks as filler -2.0. Among training-free rearrangements a 312-token window scan helps most (+11.9 points over the default for the released checkpoint) at six to eleven forward passes. Documents are synthetic, labels are LLM-made and one backbone is studied, so these diagnose this model, not long documents in general.
