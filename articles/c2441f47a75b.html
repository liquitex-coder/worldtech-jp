ORIGINAL · ENarXiv:2611.18484v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: AI-enabled remote patient monitoring is evolving beyond passive data collection toward agentic AI systems that interpret patient states, coordinate care, and initiate action across domestic and clinical settings. This structured scoping review finds that mature agentic care has not yet emerged: Most systems remain human led, while stronger decision-action coupling and embodied AI remain largely confined to prototypes and pilots. Healthcare robotics makes the transition from monitoring to action especially visible by translating AI-generated recommendations and decisions into speech, movement, prompting, physical assistance, escalation, and care coordination, making embodiment, clinical integration, human oversight, and governance fundamental design requirements. Further progress requires safe and interoperable care architectures in which intelligent components operate through clinically validated and accountable workflows. We propose agentic robotics as a future direction for supervised systems embedded in the care ecosystem, connecting AI agents and robots with clinicians, caregivers, and patients through auditable workflows that can be interrupted and remain subject to human oversight and explicit AI governance.
