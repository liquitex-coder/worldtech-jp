ORIGINAL · ENarXiv:2608.08049v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: This paper presents a parameter-dependent linear matrix inequality (LMI) synthesis framework for trajectory tracking of nonholonomic mobile robots subject to multiplicative wheel slip. A sampled polytopic convex formulation jointly targets four design objectives on the variational dynamics in Kanayama coordinates: a prescribed exponential decay rate, an H-infinity disturbance-to-error gain, regional pole placement, and a weighted gain bound acting as a design-time actuator budget. The inverse metric and the auxiliary gain are parameterized affinely in the reference linear and angular velocities. A Slip-to-Disturbance Lemma provides an explicit closed-form upper bound on the additive disturbance equivalent to bounded multiplicative wheel slip in Kanayama coordinates, connecting the physical slip mechanism to the convex synthesis. The dissipation inequality is established at the sixteen vertices of the parameter-rate polytope, and a Lipschitz-based residual condition is given as a sufficient criterion for extending feasibility to interior points; the regions where the criterion succeeds for the synthesized controller, and where it does not, are both reported. An integral representation of the Kanayama residual lifts the variational certificate to a pointwise exponential-plus-ISS bound on the trajectory error directly from the LMI. The certified envelope is the semi-global ball at sigma_bar = 0.10; variable-terrain tests at slip ratios up to plus-minus 50% are reported as empirical observations outside that envelope. In the certified regime, the LPV controller and a fixed-gain LMI baseline produce quantitatively similar tracking records; on the severe-slip probes, the parameter-dependent design retains modest reproducible margins over both the fixed-gain and the classical Kanayama baselines.
