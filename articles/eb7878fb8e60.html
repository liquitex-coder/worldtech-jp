ORIGINAL · ENarXiv:2610.17818v3 Announce Type: replace Abstract: We give the first adversarial linear bandit algorithm based on Follow-the-Perturbed-Leader (FTPL) that achieves $\sqrt{T}$ regret over general action sets. Our algorithm attains $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(d^{5/4}\sqrt{T})$ regret using only linear optimization oracles, and the near-optimal $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(d\sqrt{T})$ regret when an optimal-design exploration distribution is further available. This improves on the $T^{2/3}$ regret of the FTPL algorithm of Dani and Hayes (2006) for general action sets, and generalizes the $\sqrt{T}$ regret of L\'evy et al. (2026) over two special action sets (hypercube and Euclidean ball). Compared to previous oracle-efficient linear bandit algorithms based on continuous exponential weights, the FTPL approach generally has better computational efficiency, and is much simpler to implement. The key algorithmic element is a perturbation distribution that adapts to the past reward estimators. Its simplest form, an adaptive Gaussian perturbation, is straightforward to implement and already attains the regret bound above. We further extend it to two general constructions, random-weighting and elliptical perturbations, each covering distributions from light tails (Gaussian) to heavy tails (Student-$t_2$). On the technical side, we develop a systematic way to analyze the stability term in the FTPL regret through the Fisher information of the perturbation distribution. This builds a general framework for FTPL beyond existing ones such as the hazard rate and the FTRL-FTPL duality.
