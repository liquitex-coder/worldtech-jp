ORIGINAL · ENarXiv:2611.32302v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Cloud-hosted vision-language models (VLMs) require sending images beyond the user's local trust boundary, potentially exposing sensitive information the query does not need. We study query-guided visual minimization: a trusted local transformation preserves query-relevant visual content and reduces unnecessary disclosure before transmission. Unlike query-agnostic anonymization, it preserves the same sensitive content when the query requires it and suppresses it otherwise. We introduce QGVM, a framework that combines query grounding with an independent sensitive-item inventory, determines disclosure necessity at the type, instance, and text-content levels, and renders a minimized image for a black-box downstream VLM. We evaluate QGVM on complementary benchmarks measuring task utility, directly recoverable sensitive information, and inferential geolocation leakage. With OpenAI GPT-6.1 Sol, QGVM achieves 85.3% DoxBench answer correctness versus 77.5% for PGP, while a separately prompted Sol geolocation attacker succeeds within 1 km on 5.8% of QGVM images versus 25.7% of unprotected images. On Scene-Privacy, QGVM reduces face re-identification from 27.7% (PGP) to 4.5%. On held-out BIV-Priv-Seg, unnecessary private-text recovery falls from 33.9% (PGP) to 15.3%. To verify that disclosure is query-dependent, we evaluate paired queries that either require or do not require the same sensitive information. QGVM achieves 69.6% correct disclosure switching, compared with 29.1% for grounding-only cropping. These findings highlight the value of distinguishing visual relevance from disclosure necessity, while revealing the privacy-utility trade-offs of selective visual disclosure.
