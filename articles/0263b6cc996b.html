ORIGINAL · ENarXiv:2611.27211v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Personalized long-form generation asks a language model to write new text the way a specific user would. The standard recipe, retrieval-augmented generation, places a few of the user's past texts in the prompt but leaves the model to decide what to take from them. We introduce StylePrism, a taxonomy of 28 stylistic aspects in seven categories, and SP-Refine, an inference-time method that makes style extraction an explicit step: the model first fills StylePrism with the user's patterns, quoted evidence, and habits to avoid, then drafts from the user's samples and revises the draft twice against this profile, guided by its own style scores. On the three long-form tasks of LongLaMP with three LLMs, SP-Refine outperforms LongLaMP's retrieval-augmented baseline in 25 of 27 ROUGE and METEOR comparisons, by 10.2% on average and by up to 35.8%. Annotating users' own texts with the extracted profiles shows that StylePrism captures concrete, checkable habits, from the order of rhetorical moves to a reviewer's sign-off. Ablations show that the profile helps as revision guidance, not as a replacement for the samples: writing from the profile alone is worse in all 27 comparisons, while a single implicit-inference call is a cheaper alternative that comes close. Oracle variants that let the reviser see the reference, together with reference-free controls, show that larger refinement gains require information unavailable at inference time.
