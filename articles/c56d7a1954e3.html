ORIGINAL · ENarXiv:2611.27778v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Identifying scientific computations that could benefit from quantum algorithms requires both domain knowledge and fluency in quantum algorithm design, expertise that rarely resides in one person. We report results from QuASAR, an experimental, two-agent, LLM-driven pipeline intended to bridge this gap. One agent screens scientific literature for computational kernels with a plausible quantum-algorithmic analogue and proposes candidates, each backed by citations. A second agent independently verifies those citations. For candidates that survive verification, it reconstructs the source's computational structure, formulates a quantum mapping, builds and executes a prototype, and benchmarks it against classical baselines. We applied this pipeline to ten candidate computations drawn from the astrophysics literature. Nine candidates were carried through to a small-scale, benchmarked prototype. We report three in detail, chosen as the clearest examples with the least redundancy across the set. They span combinatorial optimization, stochastic simulation, and Bayesian inference. The pipeline attempted to supply quantum-design expertise a domain scientist might lack, reconstructing source algorithms where one existed and building original, disclosed mappings where none did. None of the three case studies achieved quantum advantage at the tested scales, and their astrophysical framing has not yet undergone expert validation. What the experiment does show is that the pipeline can turn a published idea into a runnable prototype, which a scientist can then inspect, test against classical methods, and decide whether to pursue. QuASAR is an exploratory framework, offered to the community for evaluation, critique, and further development, with an approach that could broaden the use of quantum computing in scientific research and extend to computational improvements more generally.
