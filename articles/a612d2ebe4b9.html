ORIGINAL · ENarXiv:2611.32304v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models advance robotic manipulation, but their reliance on demonstrations exposes them to backdoor threats. Among these threats, trajectory-level targeted backdoors are particularly challenging and may pose greater safety risks than behavior-disrupting attacks: rather than merely causing task failures or local action deviations, they must sustain closed-loop redirection to complete an attacker-specified multi-step task. To reliably induce such behaviors, existing methods typically rely on high poisoning rates to obtain sufficient target-trajectory supervision. However, high poisoning rates may compromise clean-task performance, whereas low rates leave target-trajectory supervision scarce and backdoor learning vulnerable to interference from dominant clean-task updates. To resolve this trade-off at low poisoning rates, we propose a two-stage co-optimization framework guided by vision, language, and action. Stage I freezes the policy and uses differentiable rendering and multimodal supervision to optimize a 3D trigger's texture, translation, and scale, strengthening trigger-target associations from limited poisoned demonstrations. Building on the optimized trigger, Stage II applies source-selective dual-LoRA adaptation to decouple clean and backdoor updates and mitigate their interference, followed by learned column-wise calibration and analytic fusion into a unified policy. Experiments on OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, $\pi_0$, and $\pi_{0.5}$ across four LIBERO suites show consistent gains over all evaluated attack baselines. With only 1% poisoned data, our method achieves 86.4% target success and 95.2% clean-task success on OpenVLA-OFT in LIBERO-Spatial. Real-robot trials also demonstrate trajectory-level hijacking.
