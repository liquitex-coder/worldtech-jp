ORIGINAL · ENarXiv:2611.13055v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: A document preserves the visible result of professional work but often discards the research, calculations, assumptions, evidence, and authorization boundaries behind it. We study a different unit of delivery: a Portable Agent Format (PAF) package that carries a work product together with compiled context, evidence, provenance, and rules for subsequent interaction. To test whether this context remains useful beyond the process that produced it, we evaluate four new packages, created after the hosted and offline readers were fixed: an investment proposal, fund research, financial-statement analysis, and an engineering incident review. Two packages were produced by specialized agents and two through a general-purpose assistant's packaging skill. Across 400 frozen questions and 12,000 evaluated responses, a hosted governed reader succeeds on 96% of questions versus 86% with instructions plus raw-file retrieval (Holm-adjusted p < 10^-7). On the 260 answerable questions per condition, success is 94% versus 79%; the gap varies by package. Source-material chat and instructions alone reach 48% and 74% overall. A separate 0.8-billion-parameter offline reader succeeds on 69% overall, but 54% on answerable questions. Its -1 response rate is 2.9%, combining materially wrong and unauthorized answers, versus 56% with the source and instructions alone. Ablations suggest advantages for the integrated selector and package-adapted writer, without isolating representation from training effects. The same run exposes a failure: offline retrieval resolves none of the 40 historical-record questions in one financial-data package. These findings support treating compiled knowledge as part of the delivered work product, while leaving independent standards interoperability and end-to-end governance conformance unproven.
