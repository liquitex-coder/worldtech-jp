ORIGINAL · ENarXiv:2611.21945v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Making automated vehicles accessible requires consideration of vulnerable road users, including wheelchair users. To offer a more tailored experience that supports independence for this group, vehicle design needs to accommodate the wide range of wheelchair configurations. However, wheelchairs are individualized, varying in frame geometry, propulsion, and visual appearance. Moreover, occupants or accessories may obscure visual cues. Existing mobility-aid datasets only label the presence of a wheelchair, rather than describing its specific configuration. To fill this gap, this paper introduces the Wheelchair Configuration Image Dataset (WCID), comprising 467 face-blurred images across 192 capture groups solely for evaluation. Each image is annotated with (i) a two-class label of manual or powered, (ii) a chair subtype, (iii) occupancy, viewpoint, setting, lighting, occlusion, propulsion, and control, with a 273-image validation split and 194-image test split. Three convolutional neural network backbones and a frozen vision-language probe are trained on an external image dataset and evaluated on WCID to measure cross-dataset generalization, while also testing robustness using Gaussian blur. Convolutional models do well on clean images, but performance drops under strong blur, leaving a gap before deployment. Code, split assignments, labels, trained weights, and the face-blurred images is available at: https://github.com/Mi3-Lab/MAID
