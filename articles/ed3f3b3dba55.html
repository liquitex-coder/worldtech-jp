ORIGINAL · ENarXiv:2611.24124v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Autoregressive neural networks exhibit spectral bias in long-horizon spatiotemporal prediction. As rollouts progress, high-frequency details decay, blurring the predicted fields and destabilizing long-range forecasts. Ocean dynamics offer a particularly stringent test because kinetic energy is concentrated in mesoscale eddies and fronts spanning tens of kilometers. This degradation stems from coupling large-scale phase evolution and small-scale energy retention in a shared feature space. Evolution on a coarse grid smooths out high-wavenumber eddies, whereas evolution on a fine grid amplifies small-scale phase errors and triggers rollout instability. We introduce QuantAtlas-OM (QuantAtlas Ocean Model), a scale-spectral decoupling framework that confines deep temporal evolution to a low-wavenumber latent manifold for long-term stability while routing full-resolution eddy and front residuals through an outer bypass to the decoder. Across physical regimes, the evolution core pairs non-local attention with resolution-invariant dilated spatial gating. Across three western boundary currents, QuantAtlas-OM retains $43.7\%$ to $86.5\%$ of mesoscale spectral energy along the $k^{-3}$ cascade at Day $120$, sustains anomaly correlation coefficients of $0.81$ to $0.88$ at Day $60$, and reaches $0.66$ at Day $120$ in the Kuroshio ($2.5\times$ to $7.7\times$ the best baselines). Trained at $0.25^\circ$, QuantAtlas-OM transfers zero-shot to a global $0.125^\circ$ grid ($0.81$ Day-$10$ spatial correlation) and leads global $0.25^\circ$, $23$-level 3D ocean simulations ($120$ days) and coupled ocean-atmosphere forecasts ($60$ days), ranking first on $15$ of $16$ metrics and cutting $60$- to $120$-day salinity RMSE by $13\%$ to $27\%$ across global ocean basins.
